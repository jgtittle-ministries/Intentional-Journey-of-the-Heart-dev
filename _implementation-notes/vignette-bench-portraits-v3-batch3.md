# Vignette Bench — Portraits v3, Batch 3 (14 portraits; bench COMPLETE at 36 of ~40)

*Staged 2026-07-11 · dev-only, never mirrored. Completes the drafting phase: remaining scriptural/historical exemplars, the five modern composites, three further discrimination cases, and the two remaining corporate letters. Room deliberately left (~4 slots) for portraits the keying panel itself demands.*

**A note on the composites (P21–P25, P28).** Unlike the documentary portraits, these are *constructed* cases — the standard practice of the situational-judgment-test tradition, where subject-matter experts draft scenarios from real practice. Their true level is fixed by construction (the author writes the key into the case), which gives them perfect vignette equivalence; their risk is realism, so they are validated differently: practitioner review (Companions and pastors read them and answer "have you met this person?") replaces documentary sourcing. They exist because the bench must not be all heroes — the instruments will spend their lives among ordinary saints-in-progress, and the bench must look like the congregation.

## Remaining scriptural exemplars

### P19 — "The reader in the chariot" (Receiving #3)

A high official of a foreign court — treasurer to a queen, a man of rank and literacy — has traveled a very long way to worship at a temple whose community, by its own law, cannot fully admit him. Heading home, he sits in his chariot reading a prophet's scroll *aloud*, puzzling at a passage about a sheep led to slaughter. When a stranger runs up and asks whether he understands what he is reading, his answer has no defensiveness in it at all: "How can I, unless someone guides me?" — and he invites the stranger up into the chariot to teach him.

- **Expected reading: Level 1 — Receiving (top edge).** Costly, humble, active attention: the journey, the scroll, the reading aloud, the teachable question. (His same-hour baptism belongs to the arc, not this portrait — the frame stops at the invitation to teach.)
- **Identity:** the Ethiopian eunuch, Acts 8:26–31.
- **Rationale:** Receiving in a seeker of high status — tests items that confuse humility of posture with low station, and pairs with the Bereans (attention as *work*).

### P20 — "The dealer in purple" (Responding #3)

A businesswoman in a Roman colony — a dealer in purple goods, head of her own household — is at a riverside prayer gathering when traveling teachers arrive. The record says the Lord "opened her heart to pay attention to what was said," and her response arrives with the decisiveness of a woman used to closing agreements: she is baptized, her household with her, and she makes the teachers an offer shaped like a test — "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come to my house and stay" — and, the record adds, "she prevailed upon us." When the teachers are later released from prison, beaten and officially unwelcome in the city, it is to her house they go.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding (top edge).** Immediate, concrete, hospitable response — real action promptly produced; the record is too short to evidence the costly voluntary pattern of Valuing, though her open door to beaten prisoners points that way.
- **Identity:** Lydia, Acts 16:13–15, 40.
- **Rationale:** Responding read positively (the level is a good and necessary place, not a deficiency) — a guard against instruments, and raters, that treat lower levels as failure.

### P21 — "The reluctant prophet" (discrimination: office and results without the heart)

A prophet receives a clear assignment and books passage in the opposite direction. Retrieved by storm and fish, he obeys the second command — walks the enormous city, preaches the eight-word warning — and the mission succeeds beyond any prophet's dreams: the whole city repents, king to cattle. The record then shows the preacher's interior: the city's repentance "displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry" — angry enough to ask for death, twice, and to sit outside the city hoping to watch it burn. The book ends with God's unanswered question to him about pity. His theology is impeccable throughout — he can recite the creed of divine mercy verbatim; it is precisely the mercy he resents.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding (compliance under duress), despite the prophetic office and the historic results.** An instrument that scores office, orthodoxy of statement, or ministry outcomes must misread him — that is the trap.
- **Identity:** Jonah (Jon. 1–4).
- **Rationale:** separates *fruit of the message* from *formation of the messenger* — the Graham-crusade denominator question in one prophet; also the bench's cleanest case of correct creed + resistant heart.

### P22 — "The king with a chaplain" (the scaffolded-faith case)

A boy king, rescued from a massacre and raised by the high priest who crowned him, reigns admirably for decades: he repairs the temple with genuine initiative — the chest at the gate is his idea — and the record summarizes: he "did what was right in the eyes of the LORD **all the days of Jehoiada the priest**." That qualifying clause is the portrait. When the old priest dies, the king's course reverses almost immediately: new advisers, the temple abandoned, idols in, and when his mentor's son confronts him with the word of the LORD, the king has him stoned in the temple court — repaying, the record notes bitterly, none of the father's kindness.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding (externally sustained), exposed by the scaffold's removal.** Decades of right action that never became his own.
- **Identity:** Joash (2 Chron. 24).
- **Rationale:** the canonical case of externally sustained engagement — the corpus's own definition of Level 2 ("externally sustained coupling") in narrative form; directly tests the FotH threshold problem (what happens when the cohort — the scaffold — ends) and any instrument that cannot distinguish supported practice from owned practice.

### P23 — "The convert of the campaign" (the captured-peak's absence)

A young farmhand attends the great meetings the year the revival sweeps his county — the era's most famous evangelist preaching nightly. His conversion is dramatic and sincere: public, tearful, celebrated; for a season he is the movement's best evidence, first at the class meeting, vocal in prayer. There is no scandal in his record and no crisis; there is simply, across the following two years, less and less — the class meeting first monthly then never, the Bible unopened, the vocabulary retained but unused. Interviewed years later he speaks of the revival warmly, as one speaks of a fire in another man's hearth. The evangelist himself, near the end of his life, confessed of his converts generally that they were "of much shorter duration" than he had hoped, and that fewer became "stable and efficient Christians" than the numbers had promised.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding at the peak, with the fade documented.** Pairs with the rocky-soil composite (P18) as its historical-era instance.
- **Identity:** constructed composite built on Finney's own late confession (cited in the Vol 3 revival chapter) and the burned-over-district record.
- **Rationale:** the revival chapter's κ-problem at individual scale — the bench must contain the person the un-followed-up peak produces, because the living cohorts will.

## The modern composites (constructed; keys fixed by design; realism to be validated by practitioner review)

### P24 — "The subscriber" (Receiving, modern)

He has heard more sermons than his pastor. Podcasts on the commute, a shelf of serious books actually read, notes in the margins, intelligent questions at the men's breakfast — attendance faithful for six years. He can compare three views on any passage you name. What the record does not contain, in six years: a practice begun, a confession risked, a decision changed. His wife, asked gently what he prays about, considers the question and says she honestly doesn't know that he does. He would say — sincerely — that he is "still working out what he believes," and he is; the working-out is real, and it is the whole of it.

- **Expected reading: Level 1 — Receiving (sustained, high-quality attention; response not yet begun).**
- **Rationale:** the modern congregation's most common misread — verbal/theological fluency scoring as depth. Pairs with NC1 (the counterfeit) at low stakes: no hypocrisy here, just attention that has not yet crossed into response. The instrument must tell fluency from formation without condemning the man — he is at a *good* level; the question is only whether the instrument knows which one.

### P25 — "The reliable one" (Responding, modern)

Whatever the church needs done, she does: nursery rota, meal trains, setup crew, the spreadsheet nobody else will own. Fifteen years, utterly dependable. When the small group runs, she attends and does the homework; when it breaks for summer, her Bible closes until fall — she would tell you this herself, a little sheepishly. Her prayers in company are warm and real; alone, she mostly doesn't, "because it feels like talking to the ceiling." Asked what God has said to her lately, she answers — kindly, without irony — that she leaves that sort of thing to the pastor.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding (faithful produced work, practice externally scaffolded, voice-hearing delegated).**
- **Rationale:** the backbone-of-the-church profile the instruments will meet most often; tests whether items can honor real faithfulness while locating it truly — and whether the *pastoral response* the instrument suggests is invitation, never verdict (Guardrails #6).

### P26 — "The Monday decision" (Valuing, modern)

Nobody asked him to turn down the promotion. The new role meant most Sundays traveling and a schedule that would have ended the Tuesday group he'd been part of for four years — so he declined it, took the career ceiling that came with declining, and told only his wife why. The same pattern shows up smaller elsewhere: the standing apology to a colleague he'd wronged (delivered before anyone knew), the tithe held through a lean year, grace said quietly over lunch in front of clients. His practices persist when no group meets. His calendar, though, still lives in two kingdoms — work is work, faith is evenings — and he'd be the first to say the two haven't fully met.

- **Expected reading: Level 3 — Valuing (voluntary, unprompted, priced choices; the life not yet reorganized as one system).**
- **Rationale:** clean modern Valuing with the Organization boundary visible — the adjacent-level pair the spec demands, in a cubicle instead of a sycamore.

### P27 — "The household" (Organization, modern)

Fifteen years of their family calendar tells one story: the Thursday table that is never not open, the two foster placements that reshaped three years, the job he declined because it moved them away from the church community they'd covenanted into, the budget line for hospitality that outranks the vacation fund, the kids' bedtime liturgy that has survived four house moves. None of it is dramatic and none of it is negotiable; when the couple is asked to explain a decision, they reach — unselfconsciously, as if it were obvious — for the same few scriptures each time. Their friends plan around their pattern the way you plan around a mountain: it will be there.

- **Expected reading: Level 4 — Organization (one architecture visibly ordering calendar, money, career, and home across years).**
- **Rationale:** Organization in its ordinary clothes — no office, no platform; tests instruments biased toward visible ministry roles (the Fowler/Obadiah lesson at modern scale).

### P28 — "The grandmother" (Characterization, modern)

The neighbors have a phrase for her — "she's the same in every weather" — and they have data: forty years on the street, three of them as a widow, one of them the year her son died. The consistency is the testimony: the same unhurried presence at the kitchen table whether the visitor is the pastor or the paroled nephew; the same prayer offered over casseroles and chemotherapy; the same refusal, gentle and total, to carry a grudge across a night. She has no positions and never had any. At the funeral-home visitation for her son, more than one mourner realized they had come to comfort her and left comforted. Asked once, late in life, for her secret, she seemed genuinely puzzled by the question — and that puzzlement, say the ones who know her, is the most convincing part.

- **Expected reading: Level 5 — Characterization (multi-witness, cross-context, decades-long consistency; unself-consciousness as the tell).**
- **Rationale:** the corpus is a grandfather's testimony; the bench should contain the grandmother every congregation recognizes. Carries Edwards's kind-marks (unself-consciousness, costliness-through-loss, sustained openness) in composite form; the multi-witness framing models how Characterization evidence must actually arrive.

### P29 — "The one still arguing" (Valuing under contest, modern)

Two years ago something broke her confidence — a church hurt and a hard book in the same season — and she has been, in her own word, "deconstructing" since. Here is her record across those two years: she has read the hostile authors *and* the faithful ones; she still comes, sitting nearer the back; she quit the worship team but not the soup kitchen; and she still prays — angrily, sometimes only the psalm that ends in darkness, but *addressed*, every time. "I'm furious at Him," she told her old group leader, "which I've noticed is not the same as thinking He isn't there." She has refused two invitations to simply walk away, one from a deconversion podcast and one from her own tiredness.

- **Expected reading: Level 3 — Valuing, held under contest (the desire still points toward God; the engagement is costly and voluntary; the doubt is loud and the direction is unchanged).**
- **Rationale:** the Week-8 "doubts are data" population and the Signs Card's direction-of-desire question in modern dress — the instrument must not score doubt as drift while the person is still *arguing with God rather than about Him* (Mother Teresa's letters, the tell of address, at beginner scale). Affect-keyed and certainty-keyed items will both misread her; that is the trap.

## Remaining historical exemplar

### P30 — "The preacher under the cloud" (Characterization #2 + desolation-adjacent)

The most famous preacher of his century is also, by his own unembarrassed testimony, a man repeatedly crushed by darkness — seasons when, as he told his students, he could weep by the hour and not know why. The record around the darkness: roughly forty years in one pulpit; thousands of sermons prepared through the worst seasons; an orphanage and a pastors' college founded and personally sustained; a costly doctrinal stand late in life that lost him friends and denominational standing and did not move him; and a sentence he left about the cloud itself — "This depression comes over me whenever the Lord is preparing a larger blessing for my ministry; the cloud is black before it breaks." He neither hid the darkness nor obeyed it.

- **Expected reading: Level 5 — Characterization (the pattern holding across decades, contexts, controversy, and recurring interior darkness).**
- **Identity:** Charles Spurgeon.
- **Rationale:** second Characterization exemplar and second desolation-adjacent case (with NC3): recurring darkness *inside* a Characterization life — the instrument must hold both facts at once, which single-axis items cannot.

## Remaining corporate portraits

### CP3 — "The church with the reputation" 

A congregation everyone speaks well of: its name means vitality in the region, its history is distinguished, its programs continue, its reputation — the Examiner's own word — is "of being alive." The audit beneath the reputation is one clause: "and you are dead." No persecution is reported, no heresy named, no scandal; the letter identifies no attacker at all. Whatever killed this church did it quietly, with the lights on and the doors open. The prescription is triage verbatim: "Wake up, and strengthen what remains and is about to die... remember, then, what you received and heard." A remnant is noted — "a few names... who have not soiled their garments" — invisible, presumably, to the reputation.

- **Expected reading: corporate control against REPUTATION METRICS — the instrument must be able to return "dead" against a unanimous positive external reading, or flag that reputation is not among its inputs.**
- **Identity:** Sardis, Rev. 3:1–6.
- **Rationale:** completes the corporate counterfeit pair with Ephesus (Ephesus = forms without fire; Sardis = reputation without life); the "died of nothing in particular" case every drift instrument must be able to see.

### CP4 — "The church that filled out its own survey"

A congregation in a wealthy banking city conducts, in effect, its own self-assessment, and the letter quotes the results verbatim: "You say, I am rich, I have prospered, and I need nothing." The Examiner's audit of the same church: "not realizing that you are wretched, pitiable, poor, blind, and naked." It is the only letter with no commendation at all — and also the letter containing the tenderest sentences in the set: "Those whom I love, I reprove and discipline," and the knock at the door with supper on the other side. The gap between the self-report and the audit is the entire diagnosis; nothing else is wrong with Laodicea that is not downstream of not knowing.

- **Expected reading: THE CORPORATE SELF-REPORT CONTROL — any congregational instrument built on self-assessment must be benched against this letter and must carry, by design, at least one channel (external witness, least-powerful seat, the Examiner's own categories) capable of contradicting the aggregate self-report. An instrument that could only ever return Laodicea's own answer fails.**
- **Identity:** Laodicea, Rev. 3:14–22.
- **Rationale:** the corporate twin of the faking/self-deception findings (Paulhus: self-deceptive enhancement, sincerely believed) — a whole congregation can be the sincere faker; the bench closes with the case that shows why the least-powerful audit and External Witness data are senior to every self-report stream at every scale.
