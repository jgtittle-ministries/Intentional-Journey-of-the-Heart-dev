# Peer Review of TA — *Theological Anthropology of the Heart, Spirit, Soul, and Mind*
## A seven-author charitable-strengthening review

> **What this is.** A *simulated* peer review: each section reasons in the documented theology of seven figures, in the two panels used on Vol 1 — **academic**: **Timothy Keller** (Reformed), **John Stott** (evangelical/expositor), **Gordon D. Fee** (Pentecostal NT scholar), **Dallas Willard** (contemplative); **companion**: **Richard Foster** (contemplative/Renovaré), **John Eldredge** (heart/desire), **Derek Prince** (Word-and-Spirit/Pentecostal). Posture: **charitable strengthening** — make the paper the strongest it can be by each tradition's lights, naming what each affirms, where each presses, and a concrete fix. Scope: the whole TA paper. It is a stress-test, **not** an endorsement.
>
> **Two wrinkles unique to TA.** (1) TA is an **academic paper**, not a popular book — so the reviewers engage its *argument and exegesis*, not its register. (2) **Willard and Foster are cited *sources* in TA** (Willard heavily — the model borrows his *Renovation of the Heart* schema; Foster's *Celebration of Discipline* is a formation resource) — so their reviews carry the extra weight of "the author you're building on, telling you where you bent him."

---

## Headline verdict (all seven): **commend with revisions — sound spine, four clustered presses + one genuine fault line.**

The spine is genuinely good and all seven affirm it: a careful, sources-first, **both/and** (Trinity-development) anthropology that refuses the either/or, aimed at the *useful* end of formation training, honest about its own provisional status. Nobody asks you to dismantle it. The presses cluster into four, plus one real disagreement only you can settle:

1. **The gospel/grace/sin frame is thin** — the anthropology is described somewhat *statically* (the parts and how to train them) without the fall → regeneration → glorification arc as its frame. (Keller, Stott, Foster, Eldredge — and it is the *same* "de-mechanize / name grace and the Spirit as agent" fault the Vol 1 panels flagged as #1.)
2. **The model is a Willard–Nee *hybrid* that isn't reconciled, and it over-reads the tripartite proof-texts** (1 Thess. 5:23; Heb. 4:12). (Keller, Fee, Willard.)
3. **Systematizing/measurement humility** — the nested-circles model (Figure 1) and the "build a Bloom's taxonomy for heart/soul/spirit" plan risk reifying *functional* biblical terms into measurable *compartments*; guard the unity of the person. (Willard, Foster, Keller.)
4. **Primary exegesis is light** — the case is argued largely *through authors* (Mitchell, Kilner, Grudem, Nee, Willard) rather than direct engagement with the load-bearing texts. (Stott, Fee.)

**The one fault line you must adjudicate:** *how hard to lean on the spirit-vs-soul (trichotomy) distinction.* **Fee and Keller (with Grudem, whom you cite) press you to soften it** — Scripture's anthropology is largely holistic/functional, and 1 Thess. 5:23 / Heb. 4:12 aren't meant to partition. **Prince presses you to strengthen and ground it** — for Pentecostal formation the regenerated human spirit as the organ of God-contact is load-bearing. Your **both/and method is itself a reasonable mediator** — but right now the paper *borrows Nee's trichotomy while citing Grudem's dichotomy* without owning the choice. The fix is the same either way: **name the decision and defend it.**

## Part 1 — Cross-panel synthesis

### Where the panels converge (treat as settled)

**(A) Put the anthropology inside the gospel.** The paper's inner-man is described as a set of parts with training targets, but the *condition* those parts are in goes mostly unnamed: the **fall** (the heart is not neutral — Jer. 17:9; the noetic effects of sin on the mind; the deadness of the unregenerate spirit, Eph. 2:1), **regeneration** (the new birth makes the human spirit alive), and **grace** as the *engine* of all formation. Without that frame the formation program reads as **faculty-training technique** — the very thing the Vol 1 reviews flagged. Keller, Stott, Foster, and Eldredge each arrive here from a different door. *Strongest fix: a short "The Inner Man Under the Gospel" section between the model and the formation-implications — fall, regeneration, union with Christ, the Spirit as the personal agent of formation — so every "training" downstream is Spirit-empowered transformation, not self-improvement.*

**(B) Reconcile the Willard–Nee hybrid; stop leaning the model on the tripartite proof-texts.** TA fuses two *incompatible* schemas: **Willard** (spirit = will = heart, all the same innermost component) and **Nee** (spirit ≠ heart; spirit = intuition/communion/conscience, mind/will/emotion = soul). Figure 1 (spirit *inside* heart; heart and mind *inside* soul) is a third thing again, and the paper even notes Nee never places the heart, then *adds* it from "other authors + Kahneman." Keller, Fee, and Willard all want this **owned and defended**, not assumed. And Fee/Keller note the model can't rest on **1 Thess. 5:23** ("spirit and soul and body" = a comprehensive *whole-self* benediction, not an ontology) or **Heb. 4:12** ("dividing soul and spirit" = the Word's *piercing penetration*, not a clean faculty seam). *Fix: a paragraph that (a) states plainly which schema you adopt and why, (b) defends the heart-as-distinct move on its own scriptural feet, and (c) reads 1 Thess. 5:23 / Heb. 4:12 as holistic/penetrative rather than partitioning.*

**(C) Keep the systematizing humble; guard the unity.** The nested-circles diagram and the proposal to "develop Bloom's Taxonomy for each of heart, soul, and spirit" are the paper's most ambitious move — and its most exposed. Willard, Foster, and Keller all warn: the **mind** may submit to a cognitive measurement taxonomy; the **heart and human spirit may not**, and a model that reifies functional terms into nested, separately-measurable parts can quietly become **faculty psychology** at odds with the unity of the imago-Dei person the paper itself affirms. *Fix: explicitly flag Figure 1 as a heuristic (you already say "diagrams are always incomplete" — promote that caveat), and frame the per-faculty taxonomies as **provisional aspirations**, with an explicit "the immeasurable parts stay partly immeasurable" guard (1 Cor. 8:1, knowledge puffs up).*

**(D) Add primary exegesis of the load-bearing texts.** The argument currently runs *through* secondary voices. Stott (the expositor) and Fee (the NT scholar) want direct engagement with the texts the thesis stands on: the *leb* range (you have Mitchell — go to the texts), **1 Thess. 5:23**, **Heb. 4:12**, **Rom. 12:2** (mind), **Prov. 4:23** (heart as wellspring), **1 Cor. 2:11–16 / 6:17** (human spirit and the Spirit). *Fix: one "primary-text" pass so the model is shown to *arise from* Scripture, not just be consistent with authors who read it.*

### The divergence only you can settle — the trichotomy fault line

| | Pushes to **soften** the spirit/soul line | Pushes to **strengthen** it |
|---|---|---|
| Who | **Fee, Keller** (with Grudem, your own cited source) | **Prince** (and Nee, your backbone) |
| Why | Hebrew/Pauline anthropology is holistic; the proof-texts don't partition; over-distinction drifts toward the body-bad dualism the paper rightly critiques | The regenerated human spirit as the organ of God-contact is load-bearing for Spirit-led formation; 1 Cor. 6:17, Rom. 8:16 |
| Foster / Eldredge | mostly indifferent to the spirit/soul seam — they care about the **heart** | same |

Your **both/and (Trinity-development) method is the right instinct** for exactly this kind of standoff — but it currently *reads as* unintentional (you cite the dichotomist Grudem and then build a trichotomy-leaning model). Make it *intentional*: "I adopt a functional both/and — the terms name real, distinguishable *aspects* of one unified person, not separable *substances* — for the practical purpose of formation training." That single move satisfies Fee/Keller (it's not a substance-trichotomy) **and** Prince (the spirit is still really distinguishable) at once.

### A note on the heart (Eldredge's solo press)

The paper's heart is defined *functionally* — seat of will/emotion/thought/life-center, mapped to Kahneman's fast System 1. Eldredge affirms the **recovery of the heart as central** (against "humans as primarily thinking animals") but finds the definition **clinical**: Scripture's heart is the **wellspring of life to be guarded** (Prov. 4:23), the seat of **desire/longing that points to God**, and a thing that is **wounded and needs healing** — not just a fast decision-processor. Not a convergence (the academics are content with the functional definition), but worth your judgment because the heart is the paper's signature contribution.

## Part 2 — The seven reviewers

### Timothy Keller (Reformed)
**Affirms:** the sources-first rigor; the both/and humility; Kilner's image-as-connection-and-reflection (Reformed-congenial); the refusal to let formation collapse into mere doctrine-transfer.
**Presses:** (1) You adopt a **trichotomy-leaning** model via Nee while citing **Grudem**, who rejects trichotomy on the ground that Scripture uses "soul" and "spirit" near-synonymously and stresses the *unity* of man — engage that tension head-on rather than noting it and moving past. (2) Watch the **Nee "soulish vs. spiritual" wire**: pressed too far it re-imports the very body/soul-bad dualism the paper critiques (a quasi-gnostic drift). (3) The formation program needs the **grace engine** named — sanctification is the Spirit's work received by faith, not a training regimen the operator drives.
**Fix:** a paragraph positioning the model relative to the dichotomy/trichotomy debate and declaring it a *functional* both/and (aspects, not substances); a guard against the soulish/spiritual hierarchy denigrating body/soul; name regeneration and grace as the engine before the training taxonomies.

### John Stott (evangelical / expositor)
**Affirms:** Scripture as the reference base; the practical, teachable aim; the disciplined engagement with a wide source set.
**Presses:** (1) **The cross and salvation-history are nearly absent.** A formation anthropology should be framed by fall → redemption → glorification, not presented as a static parts-list. (2) **Sin is underweight** — if the heart is fallen (Jer. 17:9), "training the heart" is the *renewal of a corrupted faculty by the Spirit*, not the cultivation of a neutral one. (3) **Exegesis is mostly secondary** (through Mitchell, Grudem, Nee). The expositor wants primary work on 1 Thess. 5:23, Heb. 4:12, the *leb* texts.
**Fix:** add a gospel/salvation-history frame; name the fall's effect on each part; do primary exegesis of the load-bearing texts.

### Gordon D. Fee (Pentecostal NT scholar)
**Affirms:** the serious attention to the **human spirit** and its communion with the Holy Spirit; the "hearing God" thread; the *truth-testing* discipline (Powlison/Trentham) applied to Kahneman.
**Presses:** (1) **Exegetical overreach on the partition texts** — 1 Thess. 5:23 is a comprehensive benediction ("may he sanctify you *wholly*"), not a tripartite ontology; Heb. 4:12 is about the Word's penetrating power, not a faculty seam. Don't rest the model's weight on them. (2) **Name the Spirit as the personal agent**, not the human spirit as a quasi-autonomous receiver — and remember the Spirit forms the **whole person and the community** (1 Cor. 12–14), not an isolated "spirit compartment." (3) The **Kahneman System 1 = heart / System 2 = mind** identification is a *heuristic analogy*; flag it as such so it isn't quoted as equivalence.
**Fix:** soften the proof-text reliance; foreground the Holy Spirit as agent (and add the corporate dimension); explicitly mark the Kahneman mapping as heuristic, not identity.

### Dallas Willard (contemplative — *and your principal source*)
**Affirms:** he'd recognize his own *Renovation of the Heart* schema as the relational backbone, and endorse the whole-person aim and the "hearing God" emphasis (his own book with Johnson).
**Presses:** (1) **You diverged from me and should say so.** In my model spirit = will = heart are *one* innermost component; you **split** them (spirit *within* heart; heart *outside* spirit) and graft that onto Nee, whose spirit ≠ heart. That's a hybrid of two schemas that don't agree — defend the heart-as-distinct-from-spirit move or it looks like an inconsistency, not a synthesis. (Moreland, also cited, would press the same.) (2) **Formation is relational transformation into Christlikeness, not a measurement taxonomy** — be careful proposing a Bloom's-style grid for the heart and spirit; the danger is that "the apparatus's sheer density becomes its own hazard" (1 Cor. 8:1).
**Fix:** an explicit reconciliation of the Willard-vs-Nee schemas (which you take from each, and why the heart earns its own ring); keep the per-faculty taxonomies provisional and relationally framed.

### Richard Foster (contemplative / Renovaré — *and a cited source*)
**Affirms:** the disciplines as formation means; the multi-part, balanced aim; the recovery of the heart.
**Presses:** (1) You cite my *twelve disciplines* but **flatten them into a faculty-targeting grid** (inner→heart/spirit, outer→…, corporate→community). The disciplines are **means of grace that place the whole person before God** — not technique inputs to train compartments; the fruit is God's. (2) **The contemplative stream is thin as a *practice*** — silence, solitude, the prayer of the heart, the dark night (your "soul integrating conflicting voices" gestures at it) deserve real place, not just the cognitive/heart-language and analytic tracks. (This is the *same* contemplative-practice gap the Vol 1 review flagged.) (3) Honor the **breadth of the streams** (Streams of Living Water), not just the analytic.
**Fix:** present the disciplines as means of grace rather than taxonomy inputs; add the contemplative practice explicitly; balance the streams.

### John Eldredge (heart / desire)
**Affirms:** strongly — the **central thesis that the heart is distinct and central**, and the explicit critique (via Smith) of "humans as primarily thinking animals." This is his whole project.
**Presses:** your **heart is too clinical**. Defined as a fast decision-processor (Kahneman System 1), it misses the biblical heart's drama: the **wellspring of life to be guarded** (Prov. 4:23), the seat of **desire/longing that points to God**, and the place that is **wounded and needs healing** — formation is *awakening and healing* the heart, not only training it. And Nee's **spiritual warfare** (which you cite in passing) belongs nearer the center — the heart is *contested ground*.
**Fix:** deepen the heart from a faculty into the biblical wellspring (desire, woundedness, guarding, the contested heart); frame heart-formation as awakening + healing, not only training.

### Derek Prince (Word-and-Spirit / Pentecostal)
**Affirms:** he is your **most sympathetic** reviewer on the very point Fee/Keller resist — the **spirit/soul distinction** and the regenerated human spirit as the organ of God-contact; the Nee backbone (Prince admired Nee); the "hearing God" aim.
**Presses:** (1) **Strengthen and ground** the distinction in the new-birth and Word texts you underuse — 1 Cor. 6:17 ("joined to the Lord, one spirit"), Rom. 8:16 ("the Spirit bears witness with our spirit"), Eph. 2:1 (the unregenerate spirit dead), Eph. 4:23 (renewed in the spirit of the mind). Formation of the spirit *presupposes regeneration* — say so. (2) **Foreground the Word as the formative agent** (Heb. 4:12 — the Word is what divides soul and spirit; the sword), not only the disciplines and taxonomies. (3) **Deliverance, not only training** — Nee's warfare implies the soul/mind can be *oppressed* and sometimes need deliverance, not just discipline.
**Fix:** ground the spirit/soul distinction in the regeneration + Word texts; name the Word as a formative agent; acknowledge deliverance alongside training.

## Convergence with the Vol 1 reviews (worth noting)
TA's #1 press is the **same** as Vol 1's #1: *de-mechanize / name grace and the Spirit as the personal agent.* If you carry one habit from the Vol 1 work into the Formation Documents, it's this — make the gospel and the Spirit the frame, so every model and taxonomy reads as describing how *God* forms a person, not how an operator runs a program. The contemplative-practice gap (Foster) also recurs. Both are cheap to fix and high-value.
