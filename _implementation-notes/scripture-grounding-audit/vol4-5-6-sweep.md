## The finding (MEDIUM — real)

**`corporate-person-structure-scriptural-definition.md` over-claims its systematic six-part parallel as "direct scriptural treatment, not inference."**

- **Line 9:** *"The articulations are not metaphorical extensions or theological inferences; they are direct scriptural treatments of the church as having a corporate spirit, a corporate heart, a corporate soul, a corporate mind, a corporate body, and a corporate structural frame."*
- **Line 126 (echo):** candidates are to be evaluated against "the scriptural corporate structure rather than against an assumed parallel."

**The problem (Standard Tests 2 + 4):** the *individual* elements are genuinely scriptural — corporate heart (Acts 4:32 καρδία μία), corporate body (1 Cor 12:13), corporate spirit (Rev 1–3; Eph 4:4), corporate soul (Phil 2:2 σύμψυχοι) are named in the texts, and the exegesis on each is careful (it attends to the Greek). **But the systematic claim that these scattered articulations compose a single six-part corporate person-structure precisely mirroring the individual one is a structural *inference*** — a good one, but an inference, not a datum the text hands over whole. The doc's own headings concede this ("The Person-Structure **Parallel**"; "Does the Existing Group Structure **Map onto** the Scriptural Corporate Structure?"), which makes line 9's "not inference… direct scriptural" an internal contradiction. This is the same fault profile as the Sower #8 fix (a mapping over-claimed as taught).

**Proposed fix (calibration, not rewrite):** at line 9, keep the strong claim where it's earned (the individual elements *are* direct scriptural treatments) but name the systematic six-part parallel as the disciplined inference it is — held and tested, not handed over whole. Light touch at line 126 to match. **Preserve** the body-of-Christ "more than metaphor / ontologically robust" claim (lines 57, 190) — that one is legitimate mainstream Pauline theology (Eph 5:30), not an over-claim, and the body is the one element Scripture does treat as more-than-simile.
