# Peer Review: "Soul and Spirit Taxonomies for Spiritual Formation" (SST)
## Reviewer: Dallas Willard — Foundational-Assumptions Review (Document 3, continuing from the TA review)

---

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Ontological trichotomy.** Spirit and soul are "genuinely distinct ontological realities, not just functional labels," each with "its own developmental logic, its own scriptural vocabulary, and its own relationship to the work of the Holy Spirit."
2. **My concentric model warrants (or at least accommodates) that trichotomy.** Willard (2002) is cited for spirit-at-center and soul-as-integrating-faculty, and the trichotomist framework is announced in the next breath.
3. **Each distinct faculty admits its own five-stage taxonomy** with its own assessment possibilities — soul from recognition to shalom; spirit from regeneration to union.
4. **The Holy Spirit is the agent; the disciple's part is cooperation.** Formation is received, not manufactured; the taxonomies are "the map," not the engine.
5. **The three inner taxonomies are one interdependent system** — growth in each conditions the others; stagnation in any one limits all three.
6. **Mind and body are condition and overflow** of the inner three, mapped by their own taxonomies but not reducible to them.

## 2. Affirmations

Let me be specific, because much here deserves it. Assumption 4 is the theological heart of the paper and it is held with real precision: Stage 3 of the soul taxonomy names renewal as "not a product of the disciple's effort but the fruit of the Spirit working in a soul that has become receptive" — that is exactly the grammar of grace, and it is rarer than it should be in frameworks with numbered stages. The use of Willard (1998) on the disciplines as bodily practices that are "both the means and the evidence of inner formation" is an accurate use of my work; I have no complaint there. The Psalms as "a school in accurate soul-naming before God" (Substage 1.2) is pastorally exquisite. Soul-care and soul-restoration are real — *He restores my soul* — and a framework that gives the anxious, appetite-driven, fragmented interior life its own vocabulary of attention is doing something the church needs. The dark-night material (4.2) is handled with both mystical honesty and Reformed sobriety, and the paper's repeated guardrail — Word-tested experience, gifts for service never pride — shows an author who knows where the ditches are. Finally, the closing admission that "testing the interdependencies… is the natural next step" is honest self-critique, and I credit it.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — My model does not say what it is enlisted to say.** *Assumption at stake: 2 (and 1).* In *Renovation of the Heart* I distinguished six *dimensions* or *aspects* of one human person, and I explicitly identified spirit, heart, and will as **one component under three names** — the executive center viewed by its origination, its centrality, and its power to choose. The soul, on my account, is the deepest dimension precisely because its job is to *integrate all the others* — including body and social context. That is an aspect-anthropology, not an ontological trichotomy. SST quotes my concentric picture accurately and then, in the same paragraph, declares an ontology I did not hold — "genuinely distinct ontological realities" — without marking the seam. This repeats, and now compounds, the unargued departure I flagged in the TA review: the framework has moved from one taxonomy premised on component-distinctness to three. If the departure is deliberate, it deserves an argument; if it is not deliberate, my name should not sit under it. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 2 — The equivocation on "spirit."** *Assumption at stake: 1, 3.* Whose spirit is being staged? My spirit is the will — the executive center. Nee's spirit is an organ of communion, intuition, and conscience. SST's spirit taxonomy — indwelling, discernment of promptings, fruit, contemplative union — is Nee's spirit wearing my citations. Notice what happens at Spirit Stage 2: the paper cites *Hearing God* (2012) for "the conversational relationship with God" — but in that book the conversation addresses the *whole person*, chiefly through the mind's thoughts, tested by Scripture and character; I never located it in a separate spirit-faculty. The taxonomy is largely sound as a map of the *relational life with God*; calling that life a faculty's development is the equivocation. Name the spirit you mean, and say plainly it is not mine. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 3 — The discriminating observation is still owed.** *Assumption at stake: 3.* Carry the practical test forward: what could a coach *observe* that says "soul development" rather than "heart development"? Soul Substage 4.1, "Volitional Coherence" — the intention-action gap narrowing — is will-formation, which is heart-formation on any account including the paper's own first document. Soul 3.1's affective reordering overlaps the Affective Taxonomy's valuing-to-characterization arc almost point for point. And the paper concedes the problem in its own words: Substage 5.2 is "the soul-level analog of the heart taxonomy's Stage 5 endpoint." If every soul observable has a heart analog, the two taxonomies are measuring one growth twice under two names — and the ontological distinctness of Assumption 1 does no operational work. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4 — A staged soul-track is in tension with the soul's own definition.** *Assumption at stake: 1, 3, 5.* If the soul is the *integrating* faculty — the paper's stated position, and mine — then the soul's condition is a function of the whole system's order, not a parallel lane with its own mileposts. The paper half-knows this: Stages 4 and 5 ("Integrated Coherence," "Spirit-Governed Wholeness") describe *whole-person* integration, not soul-specific gains. The rescue is available and would strengthen the framework: reframe the soul taxonomy as the **integration index** of the entire five-dimension system — the gauge, not a fifth engine. That would be more faithful to Scripture's usage, to my account, and to the paper's own interdependence thesis. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 5 — Union staged as curriculum.** *Assumption at stake: 3, 4.* Mostly the spirit stages honor gift over technique. But Stage 4 slips: "Deep Union Pursued," contemplative capacity as a developed skill, and the dark night sequenced as Substage 4.2 — as though desolation were a module one *navigates* on schedule. The dark night is not a competency; it is a severe mercy that arrives uninvited. Staging it risks turning relationship into syllabus, the very thing *Hearing God* warned against. **Severity: Minor** — because the surrounding prose repeatedly corrects the drift.

## 4. Confirmations

The Spirit-as-agent grammar holds throughout and is the paper's glory. The interdependence thesis — stagnation in one dimension limits all — holds, and holds *better* on an aspect-anthropology than on trichotomy; the paper's best instinct testifies against its stated ontology. The heart-as-Reformed-guardrail architecture is wise and workable. Spirit Substages 2.1–2.2 (discernment tested by Scripture, obedience to promptings at personal cost) genuinely track the maturing conversational relationship I described — that is my book, used well. Stage 5.1's habitual communion — Brother Lawrence, the with-God life — is exactly right as the horizon. And the mind-body section is the most Willardian page in the paper.

## 5. My single biggest concern

My concern is that the framework's ontology and its wisdom are pulling in opposite directions, and the ontology is winning the citations while the wisdom wins the substance. Everything true and pastorally powerful in this paper — the interlocking growth, the soul as integrator, the Spirit as sole agent, the whole person moving toward shalom — flows naturally from seeing spirit, heart, and soul as *dimensions of one indivisible life* being reorganized under God. Yet the paper doubles down on distinct ontological realities, borrows my concentric picture as warrant for a position I spent a chapter declining, and thereby writes three promissory notes for assessments that Challenge 3 suggests cannot be independently cashed. Dear brother, the map is good; it is the legend that misleads. Call these taxonomies three *vocabularies of attention* trained on one formation — the will's allegiance, the interior life's integration, the relationship's deepening — and nothing of pastoral value is lost, the debt from the first paper is paid, and a grandfather's testimony to his children rests on ground that will not shift when a sharp-eyed grandchild asks how we know the soul grew and not the heart. The life with God you are mapping is real. Let the map confess that it is one country.
