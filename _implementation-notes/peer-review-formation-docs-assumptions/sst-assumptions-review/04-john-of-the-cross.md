# Foundational-Assumptions Review of "Soul and Spirit Taxonomies for Spiritual Formation"

**Reviewer: John of the Cross, O.C.D.**

I have read this paper as one who also drew a map — a sketch of a mountain, with paths marked and words written along them — and gave it to souls and their directors. So I do not come to scold a fellow cartographer. I come to ask whether his mountain and mine are the same mountain, and where his paths run near cliffs he has not marked.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Spirit and soul are distinct ontological realities** (trichotomy), each with its own developmental logic, vocabulary, and relation to the Holy Spirit.
2. **The journey of each faculty can be charted as five ordered stages**, and these stages are usable by coaches for "setting objectives and assessing progress."
3. **The Holy Spirit is the actual agent of formation**; the disciple's part is cooperation with what He is already doing.
4. **The three taxonomies interlock**: stagnation in any dimension limits the others; the heart's trust in Scripture is the guardrail against subjective excess.
5. **Progress is progress-shaped** — each stage is described by an attainment (receptivity, renewal, coherence, fruitfulness, union), so that advance is legible as increase.
6. **The endpoint is participatory union** (Eph. 3:19), maximal in this life, consummated only at the resurrection — not absorption.

## 2. Affirmations

Warmly, then. The impulse of this paper is my own impulse. I drew the Mount; I numbered signs; I wrote *The Ascent* precisely because souls and their directors were wandering without a map, and "the soul that would go to God without a guide is like a lone burning coal — it grows cold." A framework that helps a coach *not* prescribe the beginner's exercises to a soul God is drawing beyond them serves exactly my purpose in *Living Flame* III.

Second: he begins where the journey truly begins — self-knowledge. Stage 1 of the soul taxonomy (Noticing, Naming, Locating disorder) is sound spiritual theology; the first night dawns only in a soul that has seen its own poverty.

Third: assumption 3 is stated plainly and repeated — "the Spirit is the agent; the taxonomies are the map." That sentence is the paper's best sentence, and I will hold him to it below.

Fourth: the diagnosis in the interdependence section — that intense spiritual experience in a disordered soul is "stimulating rather than formative" — is precisely what I called spiritual gluttony in *Dark Night* I. He has seen the disease. And his Reformed guardrail against manufacturing experience is my own counsel against grasping at consolations, in different dress.

Fifth: his Stage 5 carefully refuses absorption — "not the erasure of the human person in divine substance." Good. The log in the fire becomes fire, yet remains wood transformed, not annihilated.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — The nights are a substage; on my mountain they are the road itself.** *(Assumption at stake: #2, #5.)* Set his spirit taxonomy beside mine. Stage 1–2 (indwelling, learning to follow) is my beginner in meditation. Stage 3 (fruitfulness) approaches my proficient. Stage 4–5 reach toward contemplation and union. The correspondence is real — and then it fails at the one point where failure is costly. In my account, the night of sense stands *between* beginner and proficient, and the night of spirit *between* proficient and union: purgation is the hinge of every major transition, God's inflow felt as darkness because it exceeds the soul's capacity (DN II.5). In this paper, darkness appears once, late, as Substage 4.2, "Dark Night Navigation" — one competency among contemplative skills, something the already-advanced *navigate*. But the night of sense afflicts beginners — it is common, arriving "not long after" first fervor (DN I.8) — which in his scheme means at the Stage 2–3 boundary, not Stage 4. A taxonomy whose stages are all attainments, with purgation as a single advanced elective, maps a different mountain: one with a path but no night on it. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 2 — No transition-signs, and the ones needed are inversion-signs.** *(Assumption at stake: #2 — "assessing progress.")* I wrote the three signs (Ascent II.13; DN I.9) because the soul passing from meditation into contemplation *looks like a soul in decline*: no pleasure in God or in creatures; anxious, painful care about serving Him; inability to meditate as before. Measure that soul against these taxonomies and every indicator drops — less felt fruit, less affective reordering, prayer gone dry. A coach with this instrument will score advance as regression and prescribe remediation: more effort, more meditation, back to Stage 2 exercises — the very milk the child must be weaned from (DN I.1). The paper's own project has researched desolation dynamics with my *Dark Night* as anchor; that material is not decoration for these taxonomies — it is their missing diagnostic chapter, and should be welded in. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 3 — The taxonomy in unskilled hands becomes the director I blistered.** *(Assumption at stake: #2, #4.)* In *Living Flame* III I wrote of directors who, knowing one method, hammer every soul on the same anvil — and do harm God will require of them. A stage-map is a tool for exactly such a man: it tells him where the soul *ought* to be and what to *do* to it. The paper never instructs the coach in the limits of his office — that he is a guide who reads what God is doing, not a foreman who schedules it. One paragraph of director-humility would cost little and protect much. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4 — The gift-character is asserted, then quietly contradicted by the architecture.** *(Assumption at stake: #3.)* He says the Spirit is the agent; but Stage 4 has the person "develop the capacity" for contemplation, and the ladder's rungs read as attainable in sequence by faithful cooperation. Infused contemplation is not produced by any regimen; the soul *disposes* itself — and God gives when He wills, and the deep nights to comparatively few (DN I.14). The stages also imply steadiness, where God in fact alternates light and darkness, absence and visitation. Let the framework say: the disciple can prepare the wood; only God sets it alight. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 5 — Trichotomy is load-bearing but lightly footed.** *(Assumption at stake: #1.)* I will not adjudicate trichotomy against dichotomy; my own "sense" and "spirit" are operative distinctions for purgation, not a settled anthropology. But note: the two taxonomies would survive translated into functional rather than ontological distinction, and the paper would lose fewer readers. **Severity: Minor.**

## 4. Confirmations

The five-stage arc of the spirit taxonomy genuinely tracks the tradition's map at its spine: awakening, learning, fruitfulness, deepening, union — this is the classical *purgative–illuminative–unitive* way in modern dress, and the correspondence is legitimate, not forced. The interlock thesis holds: I too taught that the lower faculties must be ordered before the higher union is sustainable. Substage 4.2, though misplaced, shows he *knows* the night exists and knows it is misread as failure — the seed of the correction is already in his own text. And the endpoint is rightly guarded on both sides: pursued now (against those who defer everything to heaven), by grace only (against those who would climb).

## 5. My single biggest concern

It is this: the paper's stages all *ascend*, and the souls I most fear for are the ones descending. Somewhere a faithful man or woman — the dry believer this whole testimony was written to comfort — will be handed this map by a well-meaning coach, will find in their own breast no fruit, no sweetness, no capacity to meditate, and will conclude from the taxonomy that they have slid from Stage 3 to Stage 1, when in truth God has taken the work into His own hands and is feeding them in the dark, as a mother weans a child to give it bread. A map that cannot tell weaning from starvation will send that soul back to the breast, or worse, out of the house. Add the signs — teach the coach that on this mountain the surest progress often wears the face of loss — and this framework will do what mine sought to do: keep the guide from standing between the soul and the Guide. Until then, it is a good map of the sunlit stretches of a road that passes, for many, through the night.
