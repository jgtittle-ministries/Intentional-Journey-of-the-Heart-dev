# A Ladder Well Built, and No Door-Keeper Named

Listen, my son, to an old man who spent his life writing a little rule for beginners, and who has now read your job description for the servant of souls you call the Formation Companion. I have read it whole, from the abstract to the last reference. I will speak plainly, as the Rule speaks: first what the paper stands on, then what I bless, then what I must weigh, then what fifteen centuries in the school of the Lord's service permit me to confirm, and last the one thing that troubles my sleep.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. That four traditions of Christian care — Spiritual Direction, Transformation Prayer Ministry, Stephen Ministry, and the group facilitation of Band of Brothers and Fellowship of the Heart — "converge on a substantial common core," and that their differences are "different operational modes" of one role, not different roles.
2. That hearing and obeying God is "the developmental spine of the Companion role" — not a prerequisite once checked but a capacity that "continues to develop across all three levels." The paper names this as its own correction of the older Weapons Master paper, and I mark the honesty of a man correcting his younger self.
3. That the helper's own formation governs what he may do: an Affective Stage 3 floor for Companion I, Stage 4 for Companion II, and that this floor "is not optional and cannot be finessed." The measurement program and the companion program are assumed to be one program — the Companion's own stage "an explicit independent variable" in the Council's testing.
4. That the role is "presence-with rather than expertise-over"; that it is not therapy; that referral to the licensed physician of the mind "is not a failure of companioning; it is a competency of companioning."
5. That competence can be mapped by three taxonomies into a 3 × 3 matrix and grown through three cumulative levels, and that "Some Companions will remain at Level I for their entire life of service, and that is honorable."
6. That methods of foreign origin — Shadow Work and its kin — may be used, "provided the Companion brings a Christian framework to govern how the methods are used," under the Powlison and Trentham disciplines, since "method is never neutral."
7. That a contemplative substrate — fixed-hour prayer, lectio divina, silent waiting, vigilantia cordis — must be practiced by the Companion for months and years before she "walks a participant into the cohort," because "the room can only be as wide and as quiet as the Companion herself is wide and quiet."
8. That the character prerequisites of the office are real but may be defined afterward — "developed later in a separate paper based on consultations with the Council."
9. And beneath all of these, an assumption the paper does not state because it does not see it: that floors at the entrance suffice for a house that has no procedure at the exit — that a Companion, once entrusted, need never be untrusted by anyone in particular.

## 2. Affirmations

My son, there is much here that a man of Monte Cassino recognizes as his own household furniture, and I bless it gladly.

First, the name. You chose *companion*, "presence-with rather than expertise-over," and told the story of the director who first made his confession to the man who came seeking direction. I wrote that the abbot must know it is his to profit his brothers rather than to preside over them, and that he should strive to be loved more than feared. Your name carries my chapter.

Second, the levels. You wrote that a Companion may remain at Level I "for their entire life of service, and that is honorable." I so arranged everything in the monastery that the strong might have something to strive after and the weak nothing to run from. Your three levels, with the lowest honored and the highest rare, are that same arrangement. Few reach your Stage 5; you say "For some, that is as it should be." Good. A ladder is not a shame to those on its lower rungs.

Third, the capstone. Your Level III Companion discerns "in a given interactive moment" which of four modes the moment requires. My abbot must adapt himself to many temperaments — one by encouragement, another by rebuke, another by persuasion — coaxing, reproving, according to each one's character. Your mode-switching is my second chapter written in your generation's hand. And you rightly say it "cannot be reached through training alone." No, it cannot. It is formed, not taught.

Fourth, the referral. You made the sending-out to the licensed clinician a *mode*, a competency, not a defeat. In my Rule the abbot is a wise physician who applies compresses, the ointment of encouragement, the medicine of Scripture — and when his own art fails he calls in the *senpectae*, and only at the last, amputation. Your referral criteria are the physician's humility. This protects the sheep and the shepherd both, and I affirm it with both hands.

Fifth, the substrate. Fixed-hour prayer, lectio, silence, the watch of the heart — sustained six months, three months, before the work begins. You have rediscovered that the Office and the lectio are not tools but soil. This is *conversatio morum*: the daily conversion of manners under a rhythm the will alone cannot supply. That you require the Companion to have *practiced* these, not merely to know them, is the difference between a novitiate and a lecture.

Sixth: "Every Companion has a Companion. Every director has a director. This is non-negotiable." That is my seventy-first chapter — obedience is a blessing to be shown by all, not only to the abbot but to one another. And the paper warns its own officeholders — "heading for a ditch," "practicing from diminishing capital." My abbot chapters spend more breath warning the abbot than empowering him; your job description does not neglect the warning. I mark this as rare and good.

## 3. Challenges

1. **The residue I left at your group-formation paper is not discharged here — and here was its appointed place.** This paper is the job description of the role itself. I audited it line by line, as you asked. It tests before it entrusts, partly: stage floors, credentialing pathways, apprenticeship, a Level III who can "train and certify other Companions" and help the Council "assess whether a particular Companion is ready for a given level." All of that faces *upward* — admission, advancement. Nothing faces *downward*. The paper knows the failure will come: a Companion missing the three-fold scaffolding "is vulnerable to drift"; one who stops growing has capacities that "will begin to erode even if their skills-level competencies remain." It names the erosion and names no one who acts upon it. Can a Companion be removed? By whom? By what procedure? On whose complaint? Where does the wounded pilgrim carry her grievance, and who protects her while it is heard? My Rule deposes an unworthy prior; it lets the bishop and neighboring abbots annul a corrupt election; it makes the abbot know he will render an account of his stewardship for every soul. Your paper has floors and no trapdoor, entrances and no exit, warnings and no watchman. A procedure is not a lack of charity; a procedure IS charity, for it protects the sheep from the shepherd and the shepherd from himself. **Severity: Foundational.**

2. **The character gate is deferred while the competence ladder is finished.** The paper builds the full 3 × 3 matrix, the tool inventory, the credentialing pathways — and postpones the character prerequisites "in consultation with the Council." My son, I chose my cellarer the other way round: wise, mature in conduct, temperate, not proud, not a great eater — character first, and the skill of storekeeping second, for he is to be like a father to the whole community. I tested the newcomer at the door with hard treatment and read him the Rule three times before his vow. The paper does name the deferral honestly, and names 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1 as the material to come; so the gap is confessed, not hidden. But let no cohort run, and no Companion be certified, before that section exists. A ladder erected before the ground is tested will stand — until a heavy man climbs it. **Severity: Significant.**

3. **Who measures the floor that "cannot be finessed"?** The Stage 3 and Stage 4 floors are the load-bearing gates of the whole progression, and the paper says HFT provides the operational definition and the Council's Vol 4 testing treats the stage as a variable. A variable in research is not a gate in practice. Who examines the candidate? By self-report? And the first generation: the Level III certifies Level I — but who certified the first Level III? The first master of novices has no master. In my Rule this knot is cut by setting even the abbot under the Rule and under visitation. Name the examiner, or the floor is a floor painted on the door. **Severity: Significant.**

4. **Nothing in this paper stands above the founder.** I carry Fowler's open question into this seat as instructed. The Council appears twice — as consultant on character, as assessor of readiness "(or other oversight body)," a parenthesis that should be a chapter. The author holds the paper "at Speculative confidence" and calls it "explicitly an invitation" — that is genuine humility, and I honor it. But the humility of the author is a virtue, not a structure. When I die, my humility dies with me; the Rule remains, and the abbot after me is under it. Is the founder himself a Companion with a Companion, under the same supervision, examinable by the same floors, removable by the same absent procedure? The paper's principle — "everyone directs, everyone is directed" — implies yes. Its architecture never says so. Write the founder into his own job description, under it. **Severity: Foundational.**

5. **A house of Level I Companions with no elder named over it.** The paper says "A new Fellowship of the Heart can be sustained for a long time by a group of Level I Companions alone." I appointed deans over tens — but deans under an abbot, and deans who could be corrected and, persisting, deposed. Level I's have their scaffolding in principle, but who visits that house? A group of beginners sustaining one another indefinitely, with supervision required of each but supplied by no named office, is a monastery of deans with the abbot's chapter left blank. **Severity: Significant.**

6. **The capstone rests on "a nudging from the Holy Spirit," untestable as written.** True, and rightly said — but then Level III certification is certifying discernment, and discernment is verified only by fruits over time in community. Say so; give the certifiers years and witnesses to consult, not a demonstration to observe, lest the capstone be performed rather than possessed. **Severity: Minor.**

## 4. Confirmations

These things I can confirm not from argument but from the Rule's fifteen centuries of testing in every climate and century:

1. **Time under a rhythm forms; modules do not.** Your substrate durations — six months of the hours, three months of lectio and silence and the examen — are of the right kind. My novitiate held a man a year and read him the Rule three times. The three-level map, joined to those durations, the apprenticeship, the stage floors, and the required supervision, is a novitiate in embryo and not a curriculum — *provided* the examiner is named (my third challenge). Where the examiner is unnamed, history shows the durations quietly become suggestions.
2. **The office endangers its holder, and warnings written into the office are the proven remedy.** Every abbot who read, weekly, that he must render an account for every soul was a different abbot from the one who read only his powers. Your job description's warnings — the ditch, the diminishing capital, the projection — are the right medicine. Keep them in the document the Companion signs, not in a paper he once read.
3. **One shepherd adapting to many temperaments is possible.** It is the hardest thing in my Rule, and it has been done, generation after generation, by ordinary men. Your Level III is not a fantasy. But we never found it in a man under many years of testing, and neither will you.
4. **Medicine before amputation, and procedure as mercy.** The graduated response of my excommunication chapters — private admonition, public rebuke, the wise elder brothers sent in to comfort the wavering, and severance only at the last — kept both the sick brother and the community alive. Where houses had no procedure, mercy became either cruelty or negligence. Your referral mode confirms half of this; the missing removal procedure is the other half.
5. **A rule above the ruler is the only durable answer to your founder question.** Monasteries whose abbots stood under the Rule and under visitation outlived their founders by a thousand years. Movements resting on a founder's character lasted one lifetime, and sometimes less than that.

## 5. My single biggest concern

That this paper — the job description itself, the appointed place to discharge the residue I left at the group-formation paper — entrusts and cannot untrust. It sets floors at every entrance and no procedure at any exit; it foresees the Companion's erosion in plain words and appoints no one to act when erosion comes; and the same absence runs to the top of the house, where the founder stands inside a structure that has no chapter above him. The paper's warnings are true and its humility is real, but warnings guard only the man who heeds them, and the sheep is wounded precisely by the man who does not. Until someone is named who can say to a Companion, and to a founder, "brother, step out of the room" — and a procedure written that protects the pilgrim while it is said — this beautiful ladder stands in a house with no door-keeper. Write the removal chapter. It will be the least used and the most load-bearing page in the whole rule.

Let all things be done with moderation, and let the strong have something to strive after and the weak nothing to run from.

— Benedict of Nursia
