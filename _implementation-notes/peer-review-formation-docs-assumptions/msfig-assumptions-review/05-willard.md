# Peer Review — Dallas Willard (simulated)
## "A Model of Spiritual Formation for Individuals and Small Groups" (MSFIG) — Foundational Assumptions

Having reviewed the anthropology paper and the soul-and-spirit taxonomies, I come to this integrating paper with my central question already on the table: when the pieces are assembled, do we find one formation seen through three lenses, or three formations run in parallel? The paper answers — though perhaps not in the way its author intended. Let me proceed carefully, because this is in my judgment the healthiest document of the set.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Hearing and obeying God is the meta-discipline.** Mark 4:13 is read as Jesus' own claim that receptive hearing is the foundation on which every other discipline and all discernment rest.
2. **The Affective Taxonomy describes the observable human side of the Spirit's work.** It is descriptive, not causal; the Spirit remains "the irreducible primary agent," free to do sudden and permanent work at any level.
3. **Internalization, not compliance, is the currency of formation.** Stage 2 behavior can perfectly counterfeit Stage 5 character — the Pharisee counter-example is built into the model as a permanent warning.
4. **Formation is corporate as well as individual, under one map.** The same five stages describe a group as a body — collective hearing and obeying — not merely the average of its members.
5. **One taxonomy suffices in practice** (implicit but load-bearing): the affective map, integrated with the cognitive and psychomotor domains, carries the entire working model. The soul and spirit taxonomies appear only in the bridge paragraph and never again.
6. **Progress is a spiral, not a staircase.** Apparent regression may be Brueggemann's disorientation — an invitation deeper, not a fall backward.

## 2. Affirmations

The principle of indirection — the heart of *The Spirit of the Disciplines* — appears here in almost my own words: "The human role is to put ourselves in situations where we are willing to listen more deeply and obey more quickly." That is exactly right. We do not achieve transformation by direct effort; we do what is in our power (place ourselves in the way of grace) to receive what is not. The paper never confuses the discipline with the transformation, and its repeated insistence that the taxonomy "is not the agent" keeps grace and effort in their proper relation: grace is not opposed to effort, only to earning.

The Pharisee counter-example is the paper's finest structural feature. Building the counterfeit into the model itself — perfect Stage 2 mistaken for Stage 5 — is precisely the guard against what I called the gospel of sin management. The humility marker ("the closer one gets to Christ, the more aware one becomes of the gap") is pastorally exact. And the resolution of the Willard–Friesen dispute is genuinely deft: locating the disagreement at different taxonomy levels turns a standoff into a conversation, and its spirit matches my own final chapter of *Hearing God*.

Section 6.3 (spiral, not staircase) deserves specific credit as self-critique: the author identifies a real defect in Krathwohl's linearity and repairs it scripturally.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — The integrating paper does not integrate the trio it was supposed to integrate.** *Assumptions 4 and 5 at stake.* The "Trio of Taxonomies" in Section 4 is Bloom's trio — cognitive, affective, psychomotor — not the corpus's trio of heart, soul, and spirit. The soul and spirit taxonomies, developed at length in SST, appear in the bridge and then do no work whatsoever. Every operational claim — the stage table, the group extension, the preaching and worship tests, the church-wide designs — runs on the affective map alone. Notice what this means for the redundancy question that survived my SST review: when the author sits down to build the *working* model, one taxonomy carries it. The system runs on one lane. Either the corpus should say this plainly (the affective taxonomy of hearing-and-obeying is the master map; the soul and spirit taxonomies are lenses or gauges upon the same formation — my proposed integration-index rescue), or the integrating paper must show the other two lanes bearing load. As it stands, the corpus's architecture claims three parallel maps and its own integration uses one. *Severity: Foundational — for the corpus architecture, though I note it is a confirmation, not an indictment, of MSFIG itself.*

**Challenge 2 — Person-staging returns.** *Assumption 3 at stake.* "A Stage 4 person," "a leader cannot help the group reach Stage 5 without having been there themselves." The Sower parable — the paper's own scriptural ground — grades *soils*, and soils change. The taxonomy should describe the current receptivity of a heart toward a particular word, not affix a grade to a person. The paper's spiral section already knows this; its language elsewhere forgets it. Souls are not staged; receptions are. *Severity: Significant.*

**Challenge 3 — The leader ceiling sits in tension with the divine conspiracy.** *Assumption 2 at stake.* Where is God's agency at group scale? The paper's best answer is 1 Corinthians 14:26 at Stage 2: "The Spirit distributes; group learns to receive from multiple sources." That is the divine conspiracy exactly — God acting through ordinary relationships, not solely through the ordained channel. But the leadership constraint ("cannot... without having been there themselves") makes the leader's attainment the rate-limiter of the group's formation, which quietly contradicts both the distribution principle and the paper's own affirmation that the Spirit does sudden work at any level. As ordinary pedagogy the constraint is sound; as a law it makes the leader, not the Spirit, the binding constraint. Write "ordinarily cannot," and the conspiracy is restored. *Severity: Significant.*

**Challenge 4 — My conversational relationship is placed too low.** *Assumption 1 at stake, mildly.* Casting the practice of *Hearing God* as training for the Stage 2→3 transition undersells it: conversation with God is not a scaffold to be outgrown but the fabric of the friendship at every stage — at maturity it changes character (less directive, more communion) rather than ceasing. The resolution's instinct is right; its placement clips it. *Severity: Minor.*

## 4. Confirmations

The Spirit's primacy holds throughout without wobble. Indirection holds. The inner/outer distinction holds and is structurally protected. Suffering as engine (Brueggemann, John of the Cross, 1 Peter 1) holds and is well-sourced. Measurement humility — "The LORD sees, not as man sees" — holds, and the honest admission that instruments capture Stages 1–2 while the deeper stages are "partially opaque by design" is exactly the right epistemic posture. And the redundancy question is, in practice, confirmed in my direction: three lenses, one formation, one map doing the work.

## 5. My single biggest concern

My concern is architectural, and it is for the grandchildren. This paper, working alone, quietly demonstrates what the corpus has not yet confessed: that there is one formation of one person — the progressive internalization of hearing and obeying God, wrought by the Spirit through Word, community, and suffering — and one map suffices to describe its human side. Yet the corpus around it still promises three parallel five-stage taxonomies, and a child of the family reading the whole set will inherit a three-lane dashboard that the family's own integrating document never drives. Say the simpler, truer thing out loud: the affective taxonomy is the map; the soul and spirit papers are ways of gazing at the same pilgrim from other angles, gauges of integration rather than separate engines. A grandfather's testimony is best served not by the completeness of its instrumentation but by the clarity of its one road — hear Him, obey Him, and let Him make it who you are. This paper already walks that road. Let the architecture follow it.

— D.W.
