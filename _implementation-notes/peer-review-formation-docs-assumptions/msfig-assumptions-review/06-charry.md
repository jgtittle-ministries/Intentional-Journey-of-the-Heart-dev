# Peer Review: "A Model of Spiritual Formation for Individuals and Small Groups" (MSFIG)
**Reviewer: Ellen T. Charry — Document 4 of the series, continuing from my reviews of TA and HFT**

---

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Krathwohl's internalization continuum describes the human side of the Spirit's formation work** — at individual and now group scale — without distorting what it describes.
2. **The Parable of the Sower warrants the five stages**: the four soils represent progressive receptivity, with the 30/60/100-fold harvest grading the upper stages.
3. **Hearing-and-obeying God is the meta-discipline** (Mark 4:13) from which all other disciplines derive — the model's true theological center.
4. **Divine agency and human observables are cleanly separable**: the Spirit causes, the taxonomy describes, and description never quietly becomes prescription.
5. **Formation is isomorphic across scales**: a small group ascends the same five stages an individual does, and Stage 5 culture is "portable."
6. **The deep stages are partially opaque yet meaningfully measurable** (Hubbard), provided we ask with humility.

## 2. Affirmations

John, this is the best paper of the four, and I want to say plainly why. Twice — for the first time in the series — I watched Scripture *amend the instrument* rather than merely populate it. The spiral-not-staircase section (6.3) lets Brueggemann's disorientation force an explicit concession: "the taxonomy needs extension to account for this." And the Pharisee counter-example is the Gospel exposing the taxonomy's blind spot from inside: perfect Stage 2 compliance can counterfeit Stage 5 undetectably. That is the direction of judgment I asked TA and HFT to run, and here it runs. Credit where it is due.

Three more things deserve warmth. The **humility marker** — that nearness to Christ increases awareness of the gap — is deeply Augustinian and quietly anti-metric; it means the best-formed people will *under-report*, which subverts every survey in your bibliography, and you say so. The **Spirit at every transition**, with formation as "dying and rising" rather than accumulation, gives the Spirit a structural role at last — HFT named Him; MSFIG builds Him into the joints. And the **Willard–Friesen adjudication** (6.1) is the finest use of the taxonomy in the whole series: theology sets the question, and the taxonomy serves as a modest level-discriminator that dissolves a false disagreement. That is what a junior partner is *for*. Finally, the small group as formation site has the whole catechetical tradition behind it, and the integrated map a grandfather hands his family remains the Enchiridion genre I honored before.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — Who structures the integration? (Assumptions 1, 3)**
The paper claims Powlison's step one: "Scripture establishes the framework (Sower parable)." But the framework — five levels, their names, their ordering logic — is Krathwohl's, 1964, pages cited. The parable is then partitioned to fit it. Section 2's biblical theology (covenant, Ezekiel 36, the *syn-* ecology of Paul) is genuinely rich, but it functions as *warrant and vocabulary* for a skeleton built elsewhere; and Powlison's step two — examine the theory's own assumptions — is still never run on Krathwohl, three papers in. The mitigations are real: the corporate column is theologically generated (Krathwohl has no group taxonomy — Paul does), and the spiral amendment shows Scripture pushing back. But the senior-partner problem survives a third time, now in weakened form. **Severity: Foundational** — with the honest note that this paper contains the series' first working escape routes from it.

**Challenge 2 — The Sower mapping has an unacknowledged mismatch (Assumption 2)**
You asked the parable to test the taxonomy; I looked for a fail condition, and found one the paper smooths over. In Jesus' telling, the **thorny soil is unfruitful** — it is a failure mode, a heart choked. MSFIG maps it to Stage 3, "Valuing — real growth, but distractions still compete." That inverts the parable to preserve the grid: five stages need four soils plus a split good soil, so a soil Jesus pronounces fruitless becomes a stage of progress. Likewise the 30/60/100-fold: in the parable *all* good soil hears, understands, and bears fruit; Jesus does not rank the yields as maturity grades. And the soils are four different hearts receiving one seed — differential reception — not four stations on one heart's journey. The mapping is ingenious, but here the parable judges the taxonomy and the verdict is not recorded. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 3 — The group's sufferer, and Liebig's law at person-scale (Assumptions 5, 6)**
Section 6.3 handles the grieving *individual* beautifully — apparent regression as disorientation-invitation, not failure. But the small-group column has no such row. The group taxonomy describes only ascent: discernment, corporate obedience, portable culture. What does a Stage 4 group *do* with the member in the dark night, the chronically ill member whose formation never yields visible contribution? Brueggemann is cited, but corporate lament appears nowhere in the group practices. Worse, the minimum-factor logic imported from Schwarz — find the rate-limiting factor and intervene — is benign for church systems and dangerous for persons: it makes the sufferer the group's bottleneck. The desolate member is not the group's limiting factor; she may be its formation. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4 — The tradition as extension, not architecture (Assumption 5)**
Section 7.2 consults the traditions as downstream "extensions" that "could validate or correct" the framework. But the church has run staged communal formation for centuries: the catechumenate, the monastic rule, and — nearest of all to your model — Wesley's class meetings and bands, a graded small-group hearing-and-obeying architecture with its own progression. None appears. Had the class meeting supplied the group column's skeleton, history rather than Krathwohl would have structured the corporate half, and Challenge 1 would largely dissolve. A missed ally, not a wound. **Severity: Minor.**

## 4. Confirmations

The hear-and-obey meta-discipline (Assumption 3) holds and is the paper's true center — Deuteronomy 28, John 15, Mark 4:13 converge on it independently of any taxonomy. The corporate priority of formation (OT and Pauline) holds and genuinely corrects evangelical individualism. Suffering as engine rather than interruption holds. The descriptive-not-prescriptive caveat, the any-level sovereignty of the Spirit, and the humility marker all hold — indeed they hold *against* the grid, which is their virtue.

## 5. My single biggest concern

It is the thorny soil, and what it stands for. When Jesus says a heart is choked and fruitless, and the model records "Stage 3 — real growth," the instrument has overruled the Lord's own exegesis of the Lord's own parable — gently, without anyone intending it, because the grid needed a rung there. This is how theology becomes psychology's junior partner: not by denying the Spirit, whom this paper honors more structurally than its predecessors, but by letting the framework's *shape* decide what Scripture is permitted to mean at the point of friction. And the stakes are pastoral, not merely methodological: a leader holding this table will stage his people, and the grieving woman who has fallen silent, the man whose thorns are a diagnosis he cannot pray away, will be read as Stage 2 problems to be moved along — when the tax collector who cannot lift his eyes goes home justified. The paper already contains its own cure: the Pharisee counterexample, the humility marker, the spiral. Let those insights govern the grid instead of footnoting it, let the parable keep its own severities, and this model becomes what John clearly wants it to be — a grandfather's map that tells his family the truth about the country, including the valleys the map cannot chart.
