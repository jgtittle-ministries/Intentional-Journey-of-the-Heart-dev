# Foundational-Assumptions Review: ATB — Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad

**Reviewer: Klyne Snodgrass** · Document 5 in the series · continuing from my MSFIG review

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The Sower's question admits stacked complementary answers.** Why does the same Word land differently? ATB assumes the developmental answer (Affective Taxonomy), the structural answer (Foundational Laws), and its own relational answer can coexist without competing — "a third complementary answer... that does not replace the first two."
2. **There is a relational substrate beneath receivability.** Attachment history conditions *how receivable* formation is; the substrate belongs to sanctification's terrain, never to justification's ground (§4, §13).
3. **The four soils and the four attachment patterns are parallel typologies**, mapping region-by-region within one heart, and the relation is correlation, "not one-to-one or causal" (§8).
4. **Earned security is real, and the church is God's ordinary ecology for it** — the gospel's prior love is precisely the secure-base experience the insecure patterns need (§5, §7).
5. **Integration-not-replacement extends to attachment**: maturity is all four sensitivities held under a secure baseline, not the eclipse of three by one (§11).
6. **Attachment theory is common-grace description**, one true lens among several, normed by special revelation (§2, §13).

## 2. Affirmations

First, the structural verdict I was asked to carry forward, and I give it plainly: **soils-to-attachment-patterns is more exegetically defensible than soils-to-ascending-stages.** The soils are a typology — four kinds of hearing-response, three unfruitful and one fruitful, with no developmental arrow between them. Attachment patterns are likewise a typology — four kinds of relational reception, three insecure and one secure. Type-to-type is structurally honest in a way type-to-sequence was not; MSFIG had to invert the parable's own interpretation to make thorny soil "real growth," and ATB commits no such inversion. Indeed the formal parallel is striking: both schemes are 3+1; both concern the reception of an initiating word or love; in both, the "good" category is the outlier that bears fruit. The parable will bear this shape.

Second, the **region-specific reading** ("the same heart contains multiple soil conditions") matches both the domain-specificity findings in contemporary attachment research and, frankly, honest pastoral experience. It rescues the mapping from stamping whole persons with a soil.

Third, **Section 13 is the best cautions section in this series.** Saving faith vs. psychological trust capacity; the Creator-creature distinction ("God is not a bigger parent"); cultural variability; the refusal of prosperity-healing narratives. The author has done his own adversarial review before I arrived, and I credit it.

Fourth, the core intuition deserves confirmation on biblical grounds, not merely psychological ones — see Confirmations below.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — The path and the birds.** *Assumption at stake: #3.* The parable's own interpretation names the mechanism at the path: "Satan immediately comes and takes away the word" (Mark 4:15), "lest they believe and be saved" (Luke 8:12). The agent is external and personal. ATB's table cell renames the mechanism internally: "the relational system is deactivated before the Word can land; the defense is 'I don't need this.'" The text will bear the claim that a hardened, deactivated substrate is *what the enemy exploits* — and the corpus has exactly this resource in V2.Exp2B, which ATB itself cites ("the enemy's exploitation of attachment wounds"). But the table cell as written omits the birds entirely. Matthew's "hears and does not understand" (13:19) gives the hearer-side purchase the mapping needs; the fix is to write Satan into the cell as the one who snatches what deactivation leaves exposed. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 2 — Disorganized thorns.** *Assumption at stake: #3.* This is the weakest assignment. The text's thorn-mechanism is *rival loves*: cares of the age, deceitfulness of riches, desires for other things, pleasures of life. These are attractive competitors for allegiance — a divided-heart problem Scripture treats under idolatry and mammon (Matt 6:24). Disorganized attachment is structurally different: approach-avoidance conflict toward the *same* figure, born of caregiving that was simultaneously the source of fear and safety. Rivalry is not ambivalence. The paper senses the strain — it hedges "most often appear here" and re-describes the thorns as "oscillating cares, deceitfulness of competing securities, desires for substitutes," which is reading the trauma dynamic *into* thorns the text explains otherwise. A worldly, comfortable, securely-attached hearer can be thoroughly thorny; the parable requires no trauma history to explain riches choking the word. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 3 — The table's cells forget the table's caption.** *Assumption at stake: #3 and #6.* The hedge beneath the table is exactly right: "not... one-to-one or causal... correlates." But the cell prose speaks causally: tribulation breaks the rocky connection "*because* the working model could not sustain it"; anxious patterns "*cannot supply*" felt security (§8, implication 4). The parable's interpretation gives its own three causes — Satan, tribulation on account of the word, rival loves — and none of the first and third is dispositional. Where the framing paragraphs supplement, the cells locally supplant. **Severity: Significant** — because this is where a reader of the table alone would take away the wrong claim.

**Challenge 4 — Good soil and the fruitful insecure believer.** *Assumption at stake: #2, #3.* The paper guards the justification line admirably. But §13 concedes — rightly — that "some believers will struggle with attachment wounds while growing in godliness and service for decades." That concession is true, pastoral, and quietly in tension with the table: it names good soil *without* secure pattern. The region-specific hedge partially absorbs this; the paper should own the tension explicitly rather than leave it for a reviewer to find, for it is actually the strongest internal evidence that the correlation is loose. Note also the rocky-soil assignment (the strongest of the four — immediate joy, no root *in himself*, collapse: anxious hyperactivation fits well) shows the mapping *can* be tight; the paper should say which assignments are tight and which are loose. **Severity: Minor**, because the guards hold.

## 4. Confirmations

The Sower's question is legitimate and profound, and "the variance is also relational" has genuine biblical company: the covenant's grammar is relational before it is legal; Israel is the adopted child taught to walk (Hos 11:1–4); God is "father of the fatherless" (Ps 68:5); the Spirit's first word in the believer is "Abba" (Rom 8:15). The saving-faith/sanctification distinction holds throughout. The 3+1 typological parallel holds. And one strengthening note: the parable describes soils but does not narrate their tillage — yet Scripture elsewhere does (Jer 4:3; Hos 10:12; Ezek 36:26). "Earned security" should anchor there, not in the parable, and the anchor is solid.

## 5. My single biggest concern

Jesus interpreted his own parable, and he named his own agents: Satan at the path, tribulation at the rock, rival loves among the thorns. My concern is not that ATB is wrong to look beneath these to a relational substrate — the corpus takes the enemy seriously enough that "the enemy exploits attachment wounds" is a coherent, even illuminating, synthesis. My concern is that the synthesis lives in the framing paragraphs while the table — the artifact readers will remember and reproduce — quietly performs a substitution, re-explaining each failure in the hearer's psychology and letting the birds fly out of the story. The text will bear a substrate the enemy exploits; it will not bear a substrate that replaces him. Write the parable's own mechanisms into the four cells, mark the rocky assignment as tight and the thorny one as loose, and this becomes the most exegetically responsible use of the Sower in the whole Formation series — better grounded, I would say, than the mapping I reviewed in MSFIG, because here type answers to type.
