# Foundational-Assumptions Review — ATB: Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad
**Reviewer: Pehr Granqvist, Stockholm University**

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The attachment system is genuinely engaged in religious life**: God functions as an attachment-like figure (safe haven, secure base), so attachment history shapes reception of the Word.
2. **Four categorical patterns (secure, anxious, avoidant, disorganized) exist as identifiable adult profiles**, diagnosable at "region of the heart" grain and targetable by pattern-specific practices.
3. **Earned security through religious experience is empirically established** — attributed to my research — so church, healing prayer, and disciplined practice can durably repattern insecure working models.
4. **The Sower soils map onto the four patterns** (hard path→avoidant, rocky→anxious, thorny→disorganized, good→secure), such that soil condition and attachment signature co-vary within life regions.
5. **Lay-operable instruments** (practice cards, 8-week trackers, facilitator observation, SIP extensions) can measure and shift the substrate well enough to test assumption 4.
6. **Attachment insight is common grace, not gospel**: it names the substrate of sanctification, never the ground of justification (Section 13).

## 2. Affirmations

This paper stands squarely on the central claim of my research program, and stands on it correctly: religion is not merely *like* attachment — believers' relationships with God satisfy the functional criteria (proximity seeking, safe haven under distress, secure base for exploration, separation distress) to a degree that no other symbolic relationship does. The paper's use of safe haven/secure base to read Psalm 23, Hebrews 4, and the Abba cry is exactly the criteria-based mapping I would defend. Section 13 deserves genuine credit: the Creator-creature distinction ("God is not a bigger parent"), the cultural-variability caution, the refusal to let secure attachment become a soteriological credential, and the anti-prosperity paragraph ("some attachment patterns will not fully heal in this life") are cautions many applied treatments omit and this one states plainly. The insistence on collaboration with licensed clinicians, on consent, and on "tendencies, not destinies" is responsible. The bibliography is real and current — Davis, Granqvist & Sharp (2018) is indeed where I would send a reader. And Section 8's acknowledgment that adult attachment is more domain-specific than first appreciated is a sophistication most popularizations lack.

## 3. Challenges

**C1. "Granqvist's research demonstrates that earned security through religious experience is empirically real" (Section 2) — this overstates my findings.** What my work with Kirkpatrick demonstrates is *compensation*: sudden religious conversions and turning to God as a surrogate attachment figure concentrate among the insecurely attached, and this relationship can regulate distress and predict improved wellbeing. That is not the same as *earned security* in the technical sense — a coherent, secure state of mind despite adverse history, established via the AAI. Whether religious compensation generalizes into revised internal working models for human relationships, and whether it endures, remains largely open; some evidence suggests compensation-based religiosity can be emotionally unstable precisely because the old models persist. The paper's theological hope may be true; my data do not yet certify it. *Severity: Significant* — soften to "compensation is empirically real; durable earned security is the plausible but still-under-tested extension."

**C2. Correspondence is misdescribed as purely working-model matching.** Section 2 presents correspondence as insecure history → insecure God-image. The stronger empirical finding is *socialized* correspondence: religiosity is transmitted mainly to securely attached children of religious parents, who adopt the parents' faith gradually. This matters for ATB's ecclesiology: the secure arrive by socialization, the insecure by compensation — two different doors into the same church, requiring different formation logic. The paper intuits this but doesn't name the socialization pathway. *Severity: Minor* as summary; *Significant* for the formation design it feeds.

**C3. The disorganized-attachment apparatus is the paper's most serious empirical liability.** I must be blunt here, because this is the issue on which I led a consensus statement (Granqvist et al., 2017, *Attachment & Human Development*) signed by the field's principal researchers. Disorganized attachment is an infant classification from the Strange Situation. It is **not a diagnosis**, not a reliable individual-level predictor of pathology, and — crucially — there is no adult trait "disorganized attachment." The adult analogue (unresolved/disorganized on the AAI) is a *state of mind regarding specific losses or traumas*, not a personality type; self-report instruments like the ECR have no disorganized category at all. ATB treats "disorganized believers" as a stable congregational type, maps thorny soil to "disorganized patterns most often," and proposes training lay facilitators to *recognize the four patterns in group dynamics and record observations*. This is precisely the over-attribution pipeline our statement warned against: well-meaning practitioners labeling trauma-exposed people with a quasi-diagnostic category that the science cannot support, with real stigma consequences. The pastoral *posture* prescribed (consistency, consent, predictable structure, referral) is excellent and needs no label to justify it. *Severity: Foundational* for the operational program; the fix is to keep the care and drop the classification.

**C4. Categories versus dimensions.** Adult attachment research has largely abandoned four-type language for two continuous dimensions — anxiety and avoidance (Fraley & Waller's taxometrics settled this). A typology at "region of the heart" grain reifies categories twice over. The tidy soil-to-pattern table (one soil, one pattern) inherits this fragility: compensation research predicts dramatic, rocky-soil-like conversions across *all* insecure configurations, not only anxious ones. The paper's own disclaimer ("not one-to-one or causal") is right; the table's rhetoric outruns it. *Severity: Significant.*

**C5. Measurement realities.** Testing the Section 8 mapping would require validated instruments — ECR-type dimensional self-report for human attachment, the Attachment to God Inventory (which the paper cites) for the divine relationship — administered psychometrically. Untrained-facilitator observation, ipsative 100-point forced-choice items, and 8-week practice trackers cannot carry that load; correlating them with Sower diagnostics would produce numbers, not evidence. *Severity: Minor* if Future Work is reframed as instrument-adoption rather than instrument-invention.

## 4. Confirmations

The deep architecture holds. That the attachment system is engaged in relating to God; that felt security toward God predicts wellbeing beyond human attachment (Pirutinsky et al.); that prayer practices shape attachment to God longitudinally (Bradshaw & Kent); that reliable, boundaried, attuned community is the context in which surrogate attachment relationships do their work — all consonant with the evidence. The distinction between saving faith and psychological trust capacity is theologically the paper's business, not mine, but it maps cleanly onto our distinction between religiousness and attachment security, and it protects believers from a cruel implication. A formation framework that takes the relational substrate seriously, humbly, and with referral pathways is the applied use those of us in this field hoped someone would attempt.

## 5. My single biggest concern

My concern is not the theology and not the spiral — it is the moment a small-group facilitator, armed with Section 8's table and the Group SIP extension, looks at a struggling congregant who flinches at closeness and writes "disorganized" in a notebook. We spent years documenting what happens when that word escapes the laboratory: in courtrooms, in child-protection files, in parenting advice, it becomes a verdict. It is not a verdict; it is an infant research classification with no adult trait equivalent, and its adult cousin is a discourse phenomenon about unresolved loss, not a kind of person. ATB's pastoral instincts are genuinely good — patient, consent-based, referral-friendly — and none of them require the label. Keep the care; retire the taxonomy to the footnotes; let the practices be offered to *anyone* who recognizes the struggle rather than assigned to those a facilitator has typed. The gospel this paper preaches does not need a four-cell diagnostic to reach the substrate, and the science cannot underwrite one.
