# Peer Review — ATB: Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad
**Reviewer: Ellen T. Charry — Document 5 of the series**

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The variance of Word-reception is partly relational.** Beyond the developmental (HFT) and structural (V1) accounts, the capacity to receive, keep, and fruit the Word is shaped — "though not determined" — by attachment history.
2. **The gospel, mediated by reliable church community, is the ordinary means of what psychology calls "earned security."** Repatterning the substrate is sanctification's proper business.
3. **The biblical triad of faith, love, and hope supplies the theological architecture**; attachment theory is received as common-grace description of the creaturely substrate. "The laws are universal; the relational pathway varies."
4. **Saving faith is categorically distinct from psychological trust capacity.** Justification is insulated from the substrate; sanctification engages it.
5. **Maturity is the held integration of all four attachment sensitivities with secure as regulating baseline** — a believer who is "always secure" is insulated, not mature.
6. **The Sower soils correlate, region by region, with attachment-pattern signatures**, and clearing the soil includes addressing the substrate.

## 2. Affirmations

Three things in this paper made me put my pen down.

First, Section 13's firewall between saving faith and psychological trust capacity is exactly the distinction I have demanded of this series since TA, stated with a precision I could not improve: "attachment as part of the substrate on which sanctification operates, not as part of the ground on which justification rests." A profoundly wounded believer is no less saved. That sentence protects the sheep.

Second — and this answers my third challenge at HFT — the implicit learner of this project is finally no longer the able-bodied, unstalled progresser. The disorganized believer, the trauma-carrier, the one for whom the same prayer regulates one week and dysregulates the next, stands at the center of this paper. "Some attachment patterns will not fully heal in this life. Some believers will struggle with attachment wounds while growing in godliness and service for decades." That is aretegenic realism of the kind I wrote *God and the Art of Happiness* to defend. I record the answer as given.

Third, the "insulated, not mature" move of Section 11 is a genuinely theological insight arrived at through the psychological material rather than surrendered to it — the mature believer *retains* the anxious grief of distance from God. Augustine would recognize that grief. Which brings me to my challenges.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — The tradition consulted or decorated? (Assumption 3.)** The triadic spiral of Sections 3 and 7 is built explicitly on Romans 5:1–5 — and I will credit that fully below. But the church did not leave Paul's triad lying untouched for nineteen centuries until Bowlby arrived to explain it. Augustine's *Enchiridion* is a handbook *organized by* faith, hope, and love; the *Confessions* opens with the West's founding attachment claim — *inquietum est cor nostrum, donec requiescat in te* — the restless heart seeking its secure base; the Psalms are not a quarry for proof-texts (Ps 23, 18:2, 56:3 appear as anchors) but a complete secure-base *grammar*, the church's training manual in crying toward the haven; Julian of Norwich did disorganized attachment before the category existed. None of this appears. The bibliography runs from Ainsworth to Willard with Calvin and Edwards as doctrinal ballast, but the aretegenic literature that *practiced* this paper's thesis for sixteen centuries is absent. The cost is not ornamental: without the tradition, the paper implicitly teaches its readers that the authority for these insights lives in Guilford Press. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 2 — Which partner supplies the skeleton? (Assumptions 3, 6.)** I have asked this of every paper in the series. Here my answer must be divided. The paper's *dynamic* — the spiral, encounter to enactment — is scriptural (see Confirmations). But the paper's *diagnostic anatomy* is Ainsworth's fourfold grid, everywhere: every section's pastoral bullets come in four attachment flavors; the soil-mapping runs soils onto patterns; the practice cards are issued per-pattern; even Section 11's maturity is defined as the integration of the four. The triad names the destination; the categories name the *person*. When the operational machinery asks "who are you?", it answers in Ainsworth's vocabulary, not Paul's. The paper's own best defense — "the laws are universal; the relational pathway varies" — keeps the categories descriptive rather than constitutive, and I accept it at the doctrinal level. But at the practice-card level the four patterns function as the working anthropology. **Severity: Significant** (not Foundational — because the spiral genuinely governs the whole).

**Challenge 3 — "Earned" security and the anxious reader. (Assumption 2.)** The paper knows the danger: "Hope, finally, does not rest on achieved secure attachment." Good. But consider the Reassurance Cooldown through anxious eyes: rate your distress, wait ten minutes, ask once, do not re-ask, track for eight weeks. To the very reader this card targets, that is a syllabus for one more performance — security as attainment, with SUDS scores as the grade book. The grace-language ("grace is patient with the substrate") is present and sincere, but it must *govern* the machinery, not caption it: each card needs grace built into its failure condition — what the anxious person hears from God when she re-asks anyway. And the term "earned security" should carry a standing theological flag: what psychology calls earned, theology calls given slowly. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4 — The soil-mapping's pastoral hazard. (Assumption 6.)** Mapping disorganized attachment to thorny soil and avoidant to the hard path means telling the abused and the neglected that their heart-regions are the Sower's bad ground. The paper's disclaimers (correlational, regional, not causal) are correct and will not survive lay transmission. The one who was made disorganized by another's sin must never hear her substrate described in the parable's language of failure. **Severity: Significant**, curable with one paragraph of explicit pastoral fencing.

## 4. Confirmations

My senior-partner challenge, which survived TA and HFT and was first weakened at MSFIG, is here **substantially weakened — the most theological result in the series**. The structural spine of Sections 3–7 is Romans 5:1–5: Scripture supplies the sequence, and attachment science supplies only the friction points where each pattern resists it. That is the right order of operations, achieved for the first time. The justification/sanctification firewall holds under pressure. The Creator-creature caution ("God is not a bigger parent") holds. The sufferer is centered, answering HFT Challenge 3. And Section 13's self-critique anticipates half of this review — I credit it.

## 5. My single biggest concern

That this paper, having finally found the church's own spine — Paul's triad, the Romans 5 spiral, the God who is haven and base — still reaches for Bowlby to say what Augustine said first, better, and on his knees. The restless heart, the Psalms' cry from the depths toward the rock, Julian's wound that becomes worship: this is the church's own attachment literature, sixteen centuries deep, and it is aretegenic through and through — it does not merely describe the substrate, it *exercises* it. A grandfather writing testimony for his grandchildren should hand them that inheritance by name, lest they grow up believing the deepest things about their hearts were discovered in 1969. The theology here is sound; the genealogy is truncated. Restore the genealogy, and this becomes not an integration of psychology and Scripture but what it already almost is: the church remembering, with psychology's help, what she always knew.
