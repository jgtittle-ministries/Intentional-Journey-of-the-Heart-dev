# A Review of "Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad" — from Julian, anchoress, to her even-Christian John

Good sir, I have read your paper with much comfort, for it speaks of things that were shown to me long ago, though in another tongue than yours. I was no clerk, and your Bowlby and your Ainsworth are strangers to me; but the wounded heart that cannot trust its Maker — that I know well, for I was born into an age that preached wrath, and I was shown love. Take what follows as from a fellow-servant who wishes your work only well.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The heart's capacity to receive God's Word is shaped, though not determined, by its relational history** — that how we were held as children colors how we let ourselves be held by God.
2. **God's love, encountered and enacted through reliable Christian community, can re-form an insecure heart into a secure one** — what your scientists call "earned security," with God as the secure base the first caregivers were not.
3. **This re-forming is ordinarily gradual and mediated** — through practices, sacraments, boundaried fellowship, patient repetition; "the Spirit's ordinary mode is incremental."
4. **Faith, love, and hope form a mutually reinforcing spiral**, each phase feeding the next, and each attachment pattern entering and resisting the spiral at a characteristic gate.
5. **Salvation does not depend on the substrate** — a wounded believer is no less saved; attachment belongs to sanctification, never to justification.
6. **Maturity is not the erasure of the insecure sensitivities but their held integration** under a secure baseline; a heart that has lost them all is not mature but insulated.

## 2. Affirmations

Dear John, your second assumption is the very thing I was shown, and I would have this panel know it with power. When I lay near death in my thirty-first year, our Lord showed me not a ledger but a face — and I saw that He looks upon His servants **with pity, and not with blame**. Your paper's whole architecture — God meeting the person "where they actually are," the church "echoing that haven," love encountered before trust is demanded — this is that showing in your century's clothes. Your fifth assumption is precious beyond telling: that the wounded believer is no less saved. Say it often; the anxious soul forgets it hourly.

Your section on integration-not-replacement is homely wisdom of a high order: that the mature heart keeps the anxious soul's grief of distance, the avoidant soul's discernment, the shattered soul's capacity for paradox. I lived with paradox forty years — sin is behovely, *and* all shall be well — and never resolved it, and was not meant to. Your Vol 4 phrase, that a "secure" believer who has lost all sensitivities is *insulated*, deserves a place among your foundations.

And your closing — "Hope, finally, does not rest on achieved secure attachment" — is the truest sentence in the paper. Guard it; I shall say below why it needs guarding.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — the grammar of process (assumption 3).** Your operational machinery is honest and kind: practice cards, eight-week trackers, SUDS scales. But my own security came by no protocol. It came by a sovereign showing, unasked in its form, received in an hour of dying — and then by *twenty years* of pondering before I understood what I had seen. Your paper knows the Spirit's ordinary mode is incremental; it does not leave a door marked *extraordinary*. The gift that bypasses every practice, and the decades of slow digestion that follow such a gift, are both real modes of the same grace, and neither fits an eight-week tracker. Name the sovereign showing as a lane your framework blesses but cannot schedule. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 2 — the anxious soul and the peril of one more task (assumptions 2 and 3).** I knew religious fear from inside; my age made God a judge with a rod. Your treatment of the anxious believer is tender in its diagnosis, but consider how she will *read* it. She who "compulsively performs to secure divine approval" will take your Reassurance Cooldown card and perform *it* — rating her distress, timing her ten minutes, checking whether her working model has updated yet — and her insecurity will have found a new liturgy. "All shall be well" was not given me as a technique; it was given as a word from the Lord's own mouth, prior to all my doing. The paper's theology has this priority (encountered love comes first); its operational sections could let the cards eclipse it. Write it into the cards themselves: *this practice cannot make you more kept than you already are*. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 3 — the missing mother-tongue (assumption 2).** Your paper grounds security in God as Father, and preaches adoption — good and true. But your own science was born watching *mothers*, and Scripture itself speaks the mother-tongue: "Can a woman forget her nursing child? … yet I will not forget you" (Isa 49:15); "As one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you" (Isa 66:13); our Lord as the hen gathering her brood (Matt 23:37). I was shown that **God is our Mother as truly as God is our Father** — that Christ our Mother feeds us with Himself in the sacrament, as a mother feeds the child from her own body. Your paper already has this substance (the Lord's Supper as the avoidant heart's practice of receiving!) but not the name. For the believer whose father-wound is the very wound, Father-language alone bars the door your paper means to open. **Severity: Significant** — an absence, not an error.

**Challenge 4 — the fall itself (assumptions 5 and 6).** Your cautions rightly distinguish sin from wound. But keep watch that the disorganized heart does not hear even that distinction as a sorting of blame. I was shown a lord and a servant: the servant ran eagerly to serve and fell into a ditch, and his greatest pain was that he could not turn his face to see his lord looking on him *with only compassion*. The falling was not punished; it was honored. In falling and in rising **we are always dearly kept in one love**. Your trauma language is careful and mostly non-shaming; make the non-blaming gaze doctrinal, not merely procedural. **Severity: Minor** — the paper is nearly there.

## 4. Confirmations

Your "grace is patient with the substrate" I can confirm from a showing: He showed me a little thing, the size of a hazelnut, in the palm of my hand, and it was all that is made. I marveled it did not fall to nothing, it was so little — and was answered: *it lasts, and ever shall last, for God loves it*. The substrate endures because it is loved; your whole paper rests safely on that ground. I confirm also the correspondence-and-compensation finding — that God can be the secure base the caregivers were not; I am, if you will, a datum: an anchoress with no husband, no child, walled into one room, and never in my life less alone. And your refusal of prosperity healing — "some attachment patterns will not fully heal in this life" — is right; He said not "thou shalt not be tempested," but "thou shalt not be overcome."

## 5. My single biggest concern

It is this, dear even-Christian: that a soul in anguish, reading this good paper, might come away believing that her security is a *program* — that God's nearness waits upon her tracker, her cohort, her cards — and so the very machinery built to carry her to rest becomes one more ladder she must climb with trembling hands. All your theology says otherwise; your closing paragraph says otherwise; but the anxious heart reads the middle of a book, not its end. I was shown that He is the ground of our beseeching before ever we beseech — that the wanting itself is His gift already given. Let every operational page carry, like a watermark under the ink, the word that came before all my questions and outlasted them all: that He may make all things well, and He can make all things well, and He will make all things well, and thou shalt see thyself that all manner of thing shall be well. Build the practices — they are kind and wise — but build them as a mother builds a walkway for a child already carried: for we are His, falling and rising, and dearly kept in one love.

— Julian, unworthy anchoress at Norwich, who wishes you and all your grandchildren the beholding of that love
