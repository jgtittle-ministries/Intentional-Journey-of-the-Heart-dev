# Peer Review: "Bearing Fruit with Patience" (BFP)
**Reviewer: Dallas Willard — seventh seating**
**Review discipline: charitable-strengthening; foundational assumptions**

I have now sat with this project seven times, and this is the paper I have been waiting for, because Characterization — the person who routinely and easily does what Jesus would do — is the subject to which I gave my working life. Let me do my work carefully, and then do a little summing, since a series that ends deserves an accounting.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The receding cultivator.** As the levels rise, deliberate cultivation does progressively less, until at Characterization "the worker mostly waits." The boundary sentence — tools tend conditions; the Spirit gives growth — here reaches its sharpest form.
2. **Characterization is given, not made.** It cannot be aimed at directly ("the aiming is itself a deliberation"); it is the fruit of three engines no technique operates: time-through-obedience, suffering (the dark night), and the Spirit working toward an eschatological asymptote.
3. **The diagnostic of the summit belongs to others.** Both upper levels are certified externally: "others describe them primarily in terms of their Christlikeness"; "no gap between public and private life as reported by external witness."
4. **The chief danger at the top is rigidity, not stagnation.** Maturity is integration of all five levels, each later one adding without subtracting; "a Level 5 person whose Level 1 Receiving has died is not at Level 5; they are rigid."
5. **The upper levels are corporate and reproductive.** Organization's signature is a body that hears together and weighs (1 Cor 14:29); Characterization's fullness is fruit that reproduces fruit — a lineage, not an institution.
6. **The gravest pastoral error is misreading depth as regression** — applying Level 1–2 remedies to a person on the threshold of Level 5.

## 2. Affirmations

**The description of Characterization is right, and I say that as its long student.** "They do not deliberate about whether to obey; they obey as a matter of who they are" — this is precisely the renovated heart: the person for whom the deeds of the kingdom have become the natural expression of who they are. I spent decades insisting that Christlikeness under crisis must be *automatic*, because crisis does not wait for deliberation; the paper's summit is that person.

**The humble regress is now architecture.** At MSM I demanded a transformed observer and warned that no instrument sees hearts. BFP does not merely accept the point; it builds on it twice — the summit diagnostic is *given over to others*, and the dark-night differential "requires the discernment of someone who has walked the road." The observer is inside the design. I rule my MSM finding answered structurally.

**The rigidity thesis is the paper's finest original work.** "Pharisee-Characterization collapse is the settled rigidity whose spiritual life no longer surprises anyone" — that sentence earns its place beside anything in the literature of formation. The most advanced disciple's most important act is to go on receiving like a beginner: exactly right, and rarely said.

**The honesty is exemplary.** "Going Out aims at producing Level 2 leaders — not Level 5." "Some leaders never reach Level 5; that is honest." A body's level "set by the lowest stable common practice, not by the average." This is the opposite of the church-growth inflation I spent years lamenting.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1: Receding cultivation must not become receding intention.**
*Assumption at stake:* #1 and #2 — the cultivator recedes; Characterization is the cessation of deliberation.
*Argument:* The paper is careful about the *worker's* posture — "not idle waiting, but the active patience that keeps the conditions." But it never makes the same distinction for the *disciple*. "One cannot aim at Characterization, because the aiming is itself a deliberation, and Characterization is the cessation of deliberation" — read quickly, this makes the mature person passive, and that is false to every saint we have records of. The person at the summit has not lost intention; they have lost *strain*. Paul at Miletus — whom the paper itself invokes — is maximally intentional: he plans, resolves, presses on. What has ceased is inner conflict, the deliberation *between* competing wills, not willing itself. The disciple cannot aim at Characterization, true; but they can and must aim at obedience today, with their whole will, forever. The strain recedes; the will does not. One sentence would repair it, but without that sentence the summit reads as quietism, and quietism is the counterfeit nearest to the truth here.
*Severity:* **Significant.**

**Challenge 2: The Vision square remains half-painted — at the costliest location.**
*Assumption at stake:* the summit as described will draw people up the mountain.
*Argument:* At CSM I found Vision and Intention missing from a pedagogy otherwise strong on Means. BFP partially answers: the reproductive turn finally shows what the summit is *for* — the Antiochs, the lineage no institution owns, formed people forming people. That is genuine vision, and I credit it. But the Characterized life itself is still rendered almost entirely from the outside, in diagnostic language: what others report, what witnesses observe, gap-measurements. Where is the view *from* the summit — the joy of it, the ease, the "settled background awareness of divine presence" not as a stage-descriptor but as something a reader closes the paper *wanting*? The kingdom life is the eternal kind of living begun now, God's cooperative friends doing work that lasts; people will not walk decades toward a diagnostic profile. A summit paper is the natural home of the view, and the view is gestured at (union, shalom, the downhill mission) but never painted. The gap has narrowed since CSM; it has not closed, and here it costs the most.
*Severity:* **Significant.**

**Challenge 3: The body is missing from the integrated life.**
*Assumption at stake:* Organization = "the word governing every domain."
*Argument:* The domains named are vocation, finances, relationships, daily decisions — and, admirably, the social body. But the *physical* body, my most-neglected aspect, never appears. This matters doctrinally at exactly this paper's summit: non-deliberative obedience is a *bodily* achievement — righteousness come to reside in the members, habits laid down in flesh through long practice. Naming the body would actually demystify engine one: time-through-obedience forms Characterization partly *because* the body learns. Without it, reflexive obedience looks like pure mystery where there is also mechanism.
*Severity:* **Minor** (for this paper; worth a line in the corpus's foundations).

**Challenge 4: The dark-night differential names the discerner but not the signs.**
*Assumption at stake:* #6 — the distinction between depth and collapse can be made.
*Argument:* The paper rightly says behavior alone cannot decide, and hands the judgment to a seasoned observer. But John of the Cross, whom it cites, gave *three positive signs* (no consolation in God or the world; anxious care toward God rather than indifference; inability to meditate discursively despite desire). Even the transformed observer needs the saint's checklist; asserting that discernment exists without transmitting its marks leaves the gravest error named but not yet guarded.
*Severity:* **Minor.**

## 4. Confirmations

The boundary sentence holds at its point of maximum pressure — the paper never once claims the tools produce the summit, and its final paragraph gives the growth to God in so many words. The corporate turn is confirmed sound: what multiplies is *formed people*, "not by following a manual but by having been formed by it." Check what multiplies — I did, and it is discipleship, not program. This is the Great Omission answered structurally: a design in which making disciples who make disciples is the architecture, not an optional appendix. Grace is not opposed to effort — the lower levels do real work — but it is opposed to earning; the paper holds that line across all five levels.

**The ledger, across seven seatings.** Answered: *indirection* (raised at TA, resolved at CSM, held here in its sharpest form — the boundary sentence survived the summit); *the humble regress* (MSM — now load-bearing architecture); *the one-map confession* (MSFIG — and footnote 29's disambiguation of the three parallel taxonomies is exactly the discipline I asked for); *the discriminating observer* (the dark-night section presupposes and thereby ratifies the transformed observer). Still open for the corpus: *the Vision square* (narrowed, not closed — Challenge 2); *the anchor slide* (4Cs — intimacy and character are here held together in "union" and "shalom," but the corpus should say explicitly, somewhere foundational, that felt intimacy is not the measure of maturity, or the dark-night teaching itself will be undermined by the corpus's own devotional vocabulary); and now, newly opened, *intention at the summit* (Challenge 1). Three answered and held, two open, one opened — that is honest work over four papers, and better than most series I reviewed in my lifetime.

## 5. My single biggest concern

That in guarding so faithfully against engineering the summit, the paper leaves the reader unsure what the person climbing is to *do* with their will. Everything in me applauds the receding cultivator — I taught for forty years that we cannot make the tree grow. But I also taught, just as long, that the tree is not passive: Vision, Intention, Means, and the intention is the disciple's own, exercised daily unto death. "They do not deliberate; they obey as a matter of who they are" — yes, but they got there by ten thousand acts of deliberate obedience, and they remain, at the summit, the most intentional people alive, only now without inner war. The paper knows this — its engines are obedience sustained over decades — but it never says it in the summit's own vocabulary, and so the strongest sentence in the series ("at the top, the worker mostly waits") is left one clause short of the truth. The worker waits; the disciple wills, easily. Say both, and the series will have said what I most wanted said before I was done saying things: that the renovated heart is not an emptied one, but one whose whole intention has finally come to rest in a single direction — and finds, at last, that His yoke was easy all along.
