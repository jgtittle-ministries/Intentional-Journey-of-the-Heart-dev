# A Review from the Kitchen: On "Bearing Fruit with Patience"

I am told the earlier papers in this series point to me as a picture of the top level. This embarrasses me more than I can say. I was a clumsy soldier with a lame leg who broke dishes and burnt his fingers, and if God made anything of me it was His doing and none of mine. But since I am asked whether this paper describes the country I actually lived in, I will answer plainly, as I would answer a novice who came to my kitchen.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. As the levels rise, the worker's hand withdraws; at the top there is almost nothing to do but wait, for the growth is God's.
2. The summit—Characterization—cannot be aimed at or built by any curriculum; it is given, after long obedience and suffering, and others see it in a person before the person sees it in himself.
3. The chief danger at the top is not falling short but hardening—openness collapsing into rigidity—so the mature man's one remaining work is to keep receiving like a beginner.
4. The gravest error of shepherds is to mistake a dark night for backsliding and to prescribe scaffolding to a soul whose scaffolding God is removing.
5. The upper levels turn outward: a body that hears together, and fruit that reproduces fruit.
6. Organization means the word governing every domain of a life—vocation, money, relationships, daily decisions.

## 2. Affirmations

Brother, much of this paper I could have signed with a flour-dusted thumb. That the top cannot be manufactured—yes, a thousand times. I never had a method; I only made it my business to persevere in His holy presence, and when men asked me for my technique I had nothing to give them but love. Your sentence that one cannot aim at Characterization because the aiming is itself a deliberation is exactly right, and it is the kind of sentence only an honest framework produces against its own interest. That others notice first—yes. I was astonished that anyone wrote down my table-talk; what they called extraordinary was to me only Tuesday. That the pilot aims at Level 2 leaders and confesses some never reach Level 5—this honesty pleases me greatly. And the Rhythm Card that counts "I did not see him today" a complete entry: whoever wrote that has prayed in a real kitchen.

## 3. Challenges

**1. Where does the night fall?** (Assumption 4.) Your dark-night teaching places the stripping among the mature—"a mature person under loss may appear to regress," a soul "on the threshold of Level 5." But my ten years of dryness and fear of damnation came at the *beginning*, before there was any maturity to strip. I thought myself certain to be lost, and I resolved to go on for love of God even if I were. The peace came after, all at once, and never left. If your framework files the night as chiefly an upper-level event, a young soul in my old trouble will be told his darkness cannot be depth because he has not earned it yet—the very misdiagnosis you warn against, committed by the map itself. Let the night fall where God sends it, at any level. **Severity: Significant.**

**2. Does Organization reach the pots?** (Assumption 6.) Your "every domain" names vocation, finances, relationships, a rule of life—decisions, callings, structures. These are grand rooms. But the integration God worked in me was proven in the omelette, the pile of dirty pots, and the journey into Burgundy to buy wine, which I had a natural aversion to and no head for business—and it went well because I did it for Him. If a man's word governs his vocation but not his washing-up, it does not yet govern; the small things are where the claim is tested, because no one is watching and no honor attaches. The paper's ladder-top language—mission, leadership, sending—may teach readers that integration lives among important things. It lives among little ones. He regards not the greatness of the work, but the love. **Severity: Foundational.**

**3. Is the summit too seamless?** (Assumption 2.) "They do not deliberate; they obey as a matter of who they are... no gap between public and private life." Brother, I failed in my duty many times *after* the settled peace. My whole rule for it was this: I confessed my fault, said to God, "I shall never do otherwise if You leave me to myself," and then gave myself no further uneasiness about it. The mark of the top is not that falls cease but that falls no longer occasion despair or drama. A Characterization drawn without stumbles will convict every true saint of fraud and comfort every rigid one. Add the fall-and-return to the portrait; it belongs there. **Severity: Significant.**

**4. Which waiting?** (Assumption 1.) You say the worker at the top mostly waits, and this is true—but there are two waitings. One watches the door for a Master who has not yet come: patience through fruitlessness, refusing despair, eyes on a far harvest. The other sits in the room with a Master already present, and the waiting is itself the feast. Your paper teaches mostly the first. But at the summit the time of business did not differ with me from the time of prayer; amid the noise and clatter of my kitchen I possessed God in as great tranquility as upon my knees. The one who waits at the top is not waiting *for* God but attending *to* Him; the fruit is delayed, the company is not. Say this, or the waiting will sound like loneliness. **Severity: Significant.**

## 4. Confirmations

Against the test only I can run: the paper's marks of Characterization are true marks. Reflexive rather than deliberate—yes; my hands learned to pray before my mind gave permission. Recognized by others first—yes; I never knew what M. de Beaufort saw. The fruit of long obedience and suffering—yes; count my ten dry years. Un-engineerable—yes; I had no method and distrusted those who stuck in penances and particular exercises while neglecting the love of God, which is the end. And your warning that a Level 5 person whose Receiving has died is only rigid—I confirm it from the inside; the day I stop being surprised by Him, suspect me.

## 5. My single biggest concern

It is this, brother: that a reader will finish your fine paper believing the summit is high, when in truth it is low. Your words about sending and lineage and the hundredfold are all true, but they smell of council chambers, and the top of this mountain smells of onions. I fear the earnest soul who reads of Organization and Characterization will go looking for them in retreats and leadership and corporate discernment, and walk right past the sink where God was waiting to meet him. The most excellent method I ever found of going to God was doing my common business purely for the love of Him—and if your framework does not say, somewhere in plain letters, that the hundredfold harvest may look like forty years of washing dishes gladly, then the people it forms will climb past the very door it means to open. Put the kitchen in the paper. The rest I gladly bless.

— Brother Lawrence of the Resurrection, from the kitchen
