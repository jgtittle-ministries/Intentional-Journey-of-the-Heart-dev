# Peer Review: "Spiritual Formation: Four Connects" (FotH Teaching Deck, July 2025)
**Reviewer: Dallas Willard — Document 6 in series (following TA, SST, MSFIG)**

---

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Spiritual formation is sanctification** — the deck's work is not an elective enrichment program but the very thing Scripture calls being sanctified (slide 7, grounded in slides 3–4), and it aims at maturity, even theosis (slides 46, 50).
2. **Formation proceeds through connection** — the operative anthropology is relational: a person grows by connecting with self, others, God, and mission, in that pedagogical order.
3. **The heart is the source, and it can be accessed** — Luke 6:43–45 governs slide 8 onward: what comes out of you reveals the treasury within, and guided experiential exercises (Garden of the Heart, anchoring, the Four Questions) can reach that treasury where lecture cannot.
4. **Powers are trained by constant practice** — Heb 5:14 is quoted early and meant seriously: discernment is a trained capacity, and the deck supplies the training regimen.
5. **Formation must survive re-entry** — the ecology check assumes that a change which does not hold in the valley, against opposition and within one's actual life-system, is not yet formation.
6. **Community is the context** — individual process always flows into pair-share and group process; the deck itself exists to launch a fellowship (slides 44–50).

## 2. Affirmations

This deck gets right what most formation curricula never touch. First, it opens with **Romans 12:2 and the renewal of the mind** — not vague inspiration but the transformation of how a person actually thinks, "that by testing you may discern." Second, it puts **Hebrews 5:14 on the third slide**: powers of discernment trained by constant practice. That is the entire thesis of *The Spirit of the Disciplines* compressed into one verse, and the deck does not merely quote it — it builds a practice regimen to honor it. Third, **slide 7's equation of this work with sanctification** is exactly correct and pastorally brave; most churches treat sanctification as something God does to you while you wait, and this deck refuses that passivity. Fourth, **slide 28** contains the two sentences I would most want in any curriculum on hearing God: "Obedience to what you have heard opens you up to hearing more," and "What if I am 'bathed' in the Father speaking all the time?" That is conversational relationship, not oracle-hunting. Fifth, the discernment standards — Scripture, reason, witness of the Spirit, peace, community, circumstances — are sound and guard the exercises. Sixth, the **shadow mission / gold mission** sequence (slides 32–37) is genuinely wise: it takes sin seriously as misdirected gift rather than mere rule-breaking, and redemption as redirection rather than amputation.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — The definition targets intimacy of relationship rather than formation of character.** *Assumption at stake: #1.* Slide 7 defines the focus as moving "into a deeper, more intimate relationship with the Father, with others, with yourself and your mission." My definition: the Spirit-driven process of forming the inner world of the self until it takes on the character of the inner being of Christ. These are not the same target. Intimacy is a relational state; formation is a change in what the person *is* — what they would do when no one is guiding the exercise. The deck's Scripture slides point at my target (Rom 12:2, Eph 4:13 "the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ"); its stated definition points at experienced closeness. A participant can grow in felt intimacy while the will, the habits, the readinesses of the body remain unformed. The deck's *practices* often do reach character (the Four Questions, shadow mission) — so the definition undersells the work. State the target as Christlikeness of the whole inner life, with intimacy as its natural climate. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 2 — The Four Connects are the corpus's real working anthropology, and the corpus should say so.** *Assumption at stake: #2.* In TA I asked what "within" meant; in SST I found two taxonomies measuring one growth; in MSFIG I found the working model runs on the affective taxonomy alone. Now I find that the deck people actually *use* runs on neither faculty taxonomy. It runs on four *relationships*. My six aspects (thought, feeling, will, body, social, soul) are faculties of the person; the connects are directions the person faces. Both are legitimate maps — but the corpus's papers labor over faculties while its operational curriculum navigates by relationships. This extends the MSFIG finding: one formation, one master map, and here is the evidence that the *relational* map is the one doing the driving. The papers should confess this — either derive the four connects from the anthropology or admit the anthropology is descriptive commentary on a practice that was relational all along. **Severity: Significant** (for the corpus; the deck itself is innocent).

**Challenge 3 — VIM arrives means-heavy and vision-light.** *Assumption at stake: #4.* Vision, Intention, Means — the deck is superb on means (anchoring, garden exercises, questions, processes on nearly every slide) and honest on intention (the ecology check asks "Do you want something different?"; the social contract names intentionality). But vision of the kingdom — the compelling picture of life with God that makes the means worth wanting — appears as one late bullet ("Theosis is the goal," slide 46) and the mission criteria of slide 36. A tool-rich process with a thin kingdom vision produces technicians of experience, not apprentices of Jesus. The remedy is small: move the vision forward, dwell on it, let slide 36's "it lights your heart up" language describe the whole life with God, not only the mission component. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4 — The anchoring method risks training state-reproduction rather than attention to a Person.** *Assumption at stake: #3.* Slides 20–27 teach recalling a felt connection, anchoring it, and importing it into disconnected places. Used well, this is memory serving faith — Israel's stones of remembrance. Used carelessly, it teaches participants to manufacture a feeling and call it God, and the dry believer who cannot summon the feeling concludes God has left. Slide 28's obedience-loop and the six-fold discernment test are the safeguards; make them load-bearing, not appendices. **Severity: Minor**, given the safeguards present.

**Challenge 5 — Grace is present in the proof-texts but absent from the process.** *Assumption at stake: #4.* "Unusual tools for unusual results" is fine — grace is opposed to earning, not to effort, and I commend the effort. But the Spirit's agency lives on slides 3–4 (2 Thess 2:13: "sanctification by the Spirit") while the process slides are wall-to-wall human activity. One sentence would fix it: *these exercises do not produce formation; they place us where the Spirit produces it.* **Severity: Minor** in a deck; Foundational if the omission migrates into the corpus's self-understanding.

## 4. Confirmations

The **ecology check** (slides 38, 41) holds completely, and I want it on record: "How many times have you been to the mountaintop — but nothing changed back in the valley?" is the question every retreat ministry avoids asking. Formation happens in the whole of life or it does not happen; asking what will oppose the new behavior, what will support it, and counting the cost (Luke 14) is precisely the systems-honesty formation requires. The **community assumption** holds: every exercise ends in sharing, and the deck culminates in covenant community with confidentiality and shared intention. The **practice assumption** holds: Heb 5:14 is not decoration here; it is the architecture. And the **sanctification equation** holds — this deck rightly refuses to let formation be an optional track for the especially interested.

## 5. My single biggest concern

My concern is not with any exercise but with what the definition trains people to seek. If participants learn that spiritual formation *is* deeper, more intimate experience of connection, they will measure their growth by the vividness of their sessions — and the sessions here are vivid enough to sustain that mistake for years. But the aim of formation is a person who routinely and easily does what Jesus would do, whose mind is renewed for discernment on a Tuesday afternoon when no anchor fires and no group is watching. The deck's own Scriptures know this — Romans 12:2 ends not in feeling but in *testing and discerning*; Hebrews 5:14 ends in *distinguishing good from evil* — and the deck's best moments (obedience opens hearing; the ecology check; the Four Questions asked of someone you have wounded) already reach past experience into character. Let the definition catch up with the deck's own best instincts: intimacy with the Father is the atmosphere of formation, but the *product* is Christ formed in you — a self whose ordinary outflow, from the good treasury of the heart, has become good.
