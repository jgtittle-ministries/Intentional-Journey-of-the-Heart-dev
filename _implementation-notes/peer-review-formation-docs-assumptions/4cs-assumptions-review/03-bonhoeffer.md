# Foundational-Assumptions Review: "Spiritual Formation: Four Connects"
## Reviewer: Dietrich Bonhoeffer — Document 6, continuing from my MSFIG review

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. Spiritual formation is sanctification — the deck says so plainly (slide 7) — and it is God's work by the Spirit through the Word, into which the community enters intentionally rather than accidentally.
2. Formation is irreducibly communal: individual process is always completed in group process; the body grows "when each part is working properly" (Eph 4:15–16, slide 5).
3. The heart speaks a language of image and story, so imagery and parable — Scripture's own instruments — are legitimate tools for reaching it (slide 7, and the double weight given the Sower).
4. Connection proceeds in an order: self, then others, then God, then mission; self-knowledge is the gateway.
5. Felt experience of God's presence is recoverable and instructive: one can "anchor" a remembered connection and carry it into the disconnected place (slides 20–22, 26–27).
6. The mountaintop must be tested in the valley: an "ecology check" with dates, goals, and named opposition guards against experience that changes nothing (slides 38, 41–42).

## 2. Affirmations

Let me say first what gladdened me. Slide 5 knows what most modern programs do not: the Philippian church's first members were "radically socially different." The community is created by God in Christ, not assembled from natural affinity — Lydia, the jailer, the slave girl had nothing in common but the Lord. That the deck grounds its communal claim there and not in the psychology of small groups is exactly right.

Slide 16 contains a sentence I could have written at Finkenwalde: "The irritation point is there for a reason – your sanctification – ignore at your peril." Yes. God does not give us the brother we wish for; He gives us the brother who breaks our dream of community, and precisely he is the gift. The deck refuses to let the irritating person be avoided, and pairs this with Matthew 7's log and speck. This is spiritual realism.

The Four Questions of slide 17 — above all the third, "How have I wounded you?" — bring the participant to the actual injured brother, not to a journal. That is a costly question. And slide 28's "Is there something you heard and did not obey?" with its axiom that obedience opens further hearing — this is *Nachfolge*: only he who obeys believes. I did not expect to find it in a slide deck, and I honor it.

Nor will I fault the imagery. I set my seminarians a half hour daily of meditation on a single text until its images did their work. "Start with Scripture" governs every connect here; the tools are unusual, but the foundation named in slide 43 is Christ, and the fire will test the building. Good.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — Confession of sin is still not ordered into the process.** Assumption at stake: that formation is communal (2). I carry this forward from my prior review, and the deck partially answers it: the Shadow Mission (slides 32–35) is a genuine doctrine of sin — the self curved in upon itself, using God's gifts "to build yourself up," damaging others. This is *incurvatus in se* taught to laypeople, and I commend it. But watch what happens at the moment of disclosure: "Share whatever you wish with your partner"; report "whatever they have permission to say." The sinner remains sovereign over his own exposure. Confession that costs nothing — where I select the presentable portion of my darkness — is not the breakthrough to community; it is its counterfeit. The pious fellowship permits no one to be a sinner, and a fellowship where sharing is always optional permits everyone to remain concealed. What is missing is the moment where sin is named as sin before a brother and the brother speaks Christ's forgiveness aloud. The deck walks to the very door (slide 17, question 3!) and does not open it. Severity: **Significant** — the materials are all present; only the ordinance is lacking.

**Challenge 2 — The self is consulted before it is judged.** Assumption at stake: (4). The sequence begins with connecting to yourself in the Garden of the Heart, and only later with others and God. But the deck itself quotes the verdict: "the heart of this people has become dull" (slide 24), and Luke 6 teaches that the heart is known by its *fruit* — externally, publicly — not by its self-report. My ordering ran the reverse: the day together grounds the day alone, and I come to know myself truly through the brother's word and the Word's judgment, not through introspection. To the deck's credit, the Garden is not left autonomous — Jesus is invited in (slide 11), and the brother hears the account. But the architecture still assumes the heart's reports are readable by their owner. Slide 8's opening question — "Are you ever surprised by what you do?" — concedes self-opacity, then prescribes more self-attention as the remedy. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 3 — The brother as mirror rather than as bearer of the Word.** Assumption at stake: (2). "Share in pairs… ask for their feedback and suggestions" (slide 15). Feedback is human love: it helps me toward *my* self-discovery. Spiritual love speaks Christ's word to the brother — Scripture, admonition, absolution — and is willing to be unwelcome. The group here listens, reflects, suggests; it rarely *declares*. Slide 28's tests (aligns with Scripture, community, etc.) gesture toward mediated discernment, and I credit them. But the pair-share is structurally a mirror. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 4 — The anchored feeling as the measure of connection with God.** Assumption at stake: (5). The process locates connection in remembered sensation — look, sound, feeling — and teaches its retrieval. But what of the brother for whom there is no such memory, or from whom God has withdrawn all sensation for years? He will conclude he is disconnected when he may be closest. God is not more present when felt; the dark night is not a malfunction to be re-anchored away. Christ on the cross felt utter abandonment and was never more at one with the Father's will. The technique will serve many; it must not become the criterion, or the desolate believer is catechized into despair. Severity: **Foundational** — not because the practice is illegitimate, but because the deck nowhere says this.

**Challenge 5 — The day alone is not architected.** Assumption at stake: (2, 6). Solitude appears only as eyes-closed minutes inside the meeting. Between sessions there are goals and dates, but no ordered rhythm of Scripture, prayer, and silence alone. He who cannot be alone should beware of community. Severity: **Minor** — the ecology check is a real bridge to the valley; extend it inward.

## 4. Confirmations

The MSFIG concern about the group as *Wunschbild* does **not** carry into this deck: slides 47–49 are sober — a test bed, a social contract, confidentiality, an explicit exit ("decide if this FotH is for you"). No idealized community here. Costly grace is genuinely present in outline: obedience-gated hearing (slide 28), action dates (slide 16), the fire of 1 Corinthians 3 (slide 43), the counted cost of Luke 14 (slide 38). And theosis-in-community-expressed-as-love (slides 46, 50) is a wholly orthodox triad.

## 5. My single biggest concern

It is the anchor. This fellowship, whose instincts about obedience, irritation, and the valley are so sound, has quietly taught its people that connection with God has a phenomenology — a look, a sound, a feeling — that can be stored and retrieved. For the young in faith this is scaffolding; I do not begrudge it. But the God of the gospel gives Himself in Word and sacrament and brother precisely so that we are not thrown back on our inner states, which the deck's own Scriptures call dull and deceived. The day will come for every participant when the anchor holds nothing — when God is silent and the garden is winter — and on that day the process as written offers only re-anchoring, which is to say, offers the man his own past. Let the fellowship teach now, while the sun is out, that the absence of feeling is not the absence of God; that the brother's spoken absolution is more certain than any remembered warmth; and that the disciple in the dark who obeys anyway is bearing fruit the anchor cannot measure. Then the tool will serve the gospel instead of quietly replacing it.
