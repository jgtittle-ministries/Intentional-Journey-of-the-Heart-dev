# Review of "The Heart in Formation: Affective Taxonomy as a Framework for Heart Training"
**Reviewer: David R. Krathwohl**

Mr. Tittle has read us carefully, and he has read us for the right reason. We wrote in 1964 of the erosion of affective objectives — the way schools state them in commencement rhetoric and then grade only cognition — and this paper is an attempt to arrest exactly that erosion in a domain (church formation) where the affective objectives are not decorative but constitutive. I take the project seriously. My task is to say where he is standing on our framework as we built it, and where he is standing on ground we never surveyed.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. That the internalization continuum (receiving → responding → valuing → organization → characterization) describes "a genuine phenomenology of how people actually come to own a value" — his words — i.e., a developmental sequence in persons, not merely a classification of objectives.
2. That an individual disciple can be assigned a *stage position* on the continuum, and that measurement instruments can be built on that position ("the language to say what stage a disciple is at").
3. That "trust in Scripture" is a value of the kind the taxonomy was built to classify, and that its deepening is "affective more than cognitive."
4. That characterization by a value complex is a workable operationalization of the endpoint of Christian formation (Barna's worldview outcome; the Job 42:5 movement).
5. That a two-track assessment — self-report Likert plus external-witness behavioral observation — can detect stage transitions in a small-group setting.
6. That the taxonomy generalizes from school curricula to church-based, Spirit-attributed formation without loss of validity.

## 2. Affirmations

The author's rendering of the scheme is substantially accurate, and I want to say so plainly, because most secondhand uses of Handbook II are not. His subcategory citations are correct: 1.1 awareness; 2.1 acquiescence in responding through 2.3 satisfaction in response; 3.1 acceptance of a value through 3.3 commitment. Two small slips: our 1.3 is *controlled or selected attention*, not "selective attention" (a tolerable paraphrase), and our fifth level is *Characterization by a Value or Value Complex*, not "Value Set" — a trivial relabeling, but in a document meant to found further taxonomies, the canonical labels should be exact. His mapping of PROAPT phases onto levels is genuinely well-made pedagogy: low-risk factual questions as an entry condition for receiving, the shift to interpretation as the responding transition, application as the valuing threshold. That is precisely the kind of objective-writing the Handbook was written to enable, and it is done here with more care than most curriculum committees ever gave us. The choice of internalization as the organizing thread of formation is also apt: we chose internalization *after* rejecting other organizing principles, and his intuition that it maps onto Willard's renovation language is not foolish.

## 3. Challenges

**C1. Objectives, not persons.** (Assumption 1, 2.) We were emphatic — and I remained emphatic in later methodological writing — that the taxonomy classifies *educational objectives*, not stages of personality development. We deliberately declined to claim a theory of how persons grow. The paper's phrase "genuine phenomenology of how people actually come to own a value" asserts what we hypothesized but never established. Moving from "this objective sits at the valuing level" to "this disciple *is at* the valuing level" is a category shift with real measurement consequences: persons occupy different levels simultaneously with respect to different values, and even with respect to the same value in different life regions. A disciple may organize her finances around Scripture and merely receive it regarding forgiveness of a particular enemy. Stage-position scoring of a whole person is outside the design envelope. **Severity: Foundational** — not fatal, but it must be named and designed around (score value-by-domain, not person-by-stage).

**C2. The ordering was logical-hypothetical.** (Assumption 2.) The sequence's cumulative, hierarchical character had, in 1964, limited empirical validation, and I said so. The intervening decades produced less confirmation than we hoped. A program that builds *instruments* on ordinal stage position inherits that unvalidated ordinality. If the pilot treats the continuum as a hypothesis to be tested — do disciples in fact traverse receiving before responding before valuing on this specific value? — the pilot becomes a contribution. If it treats the ordering as settled, it builds on sand we ourselves flagged. **Severity: Significant.**

**C3. "Affective more than cognitive" overstates the separation.** (Assumption 3.) We insisted every affective objective has a cognitive component and vice versa; the two domains are deeply intertwined. Trust in Scripture as "a trustworthy guide" is saturated with cognitive content — beliefs about provenance, interpretation, warrant. The Barna finding he cites is itself a *belief* inventory. The paper's own instincts are better than its rhetoric here; the design should model the cognitive-affective interaction rather than assign trust to one domain. **Severity: Significant.**

**C4. Measurement cautions bite harder in church than in school.** (Assumption 5.) We warned about faking, social desirability, and the gap between expressed and operative values — and noted affect changes on long time horizons. A small group is a community where *displaying* trust in Scripture is socially rewarded weekly; the self-report Likert track will be contaminated in exactly the direction of the desired outcome. External witnesses help, but observers see behavior and infer value; the inference is the weak joint. Also: his claim that the external witness "introduces the metacognitive dimension" of my 2002 revision misreads that revision — metacognitive knowledge is a *knowledge* category (self-knowledge about one's own cognition), not third-party observation, and it belongs to the cognitive taxonomy. **Severity: Significant** (the misreading itself, Minor; the faking problem, Significant).

**C5. Characterization and sanctification.** (Assumption 4.) We conceived level 5 as the point where a value complex controls behavior so consistently that the person is *described* by it — and we noted candidly that formal schooling rarely reaches, or even aims at, levels 4–5; those are the work of a whole life. We did not conceive it as sanctification, and religious formation appears in the Handbook chiefly in our discussion of why schools *cede* value formation to home and church, and in our worry about indoctrination versus education. Equating level 5 with a scriptural endpoint imports theological content the instrument cannot carry. **Severity: Minor as framing, Significant if instruments score "Stage 5 attainment."**

## 4. Confirmations

The core move survives scrutiny better than one might expect. The church, unlike the school, actually possesses what levels 4–5 require: long time horizons, a total-life scope, and a community licensed to care about operative values. Our erosion problem existed partly *because* schools lacked these; this application restores the taxonomy to an institution structurally suited to its upper reaches. That is a legitimate extension, and an interesting one. The objective-writing use (PROAPT mapping) is squarely inside the envelope. The two-track assessment, whatever its contamination risks, honors our insistence that expressed values be checked against behavior. And the indoctrination worry we raised is dissolved rather than violated here: a church is candid about which values it intends to form.

## 5. My single biggest concern

If I may speak plainly as the instrument's maker: my taxonomy was a filing system for statements teachers write, and this paper — like many before it — wants it to be a map of the soul's progress. It is a good filing system and it may even turn out to be a serviceable map, but nobody has yet demonstrated the second thing, and a formation program that scores disciples by stage will be reifying a hypothesis I published with explicit reservations. My advice is procedural, not theological: run the pilot as a *test of the ordering itself*, score values within life-domains rather than persons globally, triangulate every self-report against operative behavior over intervals long enough for affect to actually move, and hold "characterization" loosely — whether it names sanctification is a question for theologians, and I was never one. But the impulse to stop grading only what the head knows and start attending, carefully and honestly, to what the heart has come to own — that was our impulse too, and I am glad to see someone taking it this seriously.
