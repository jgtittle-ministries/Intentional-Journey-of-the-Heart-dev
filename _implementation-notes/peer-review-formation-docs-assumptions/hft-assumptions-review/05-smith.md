# Peer Review: "The Heart in Formation: Affective Taxonomy as a Framework for Heart Training"

**Reviewer: James K. A. Smith**

Let me say at the outset that I read this paper as a friend. Tittle has understood what I've spent twenty years trying to say — that the heart is prior to the mind "in the order of transformation" is a sentence I wish more of my own readers could write — and he's trying to do something I've only gestured at: give coaches an actual working language for the formation of desire. So what follows is pressure applied to a structure I want to stand.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The heart — the seat of motivation and desire — is formation's real target, and it is trainable.** Not the intellect alone; not generic "discipleship."
2. **Heart formation proceeds by progressive internalization that can be mapped as an ordered sequence of stages** (Krathwohl's receiving → responding → valuing → organization → characterization), and this sequence describes "a genuine phenomenology of how people actually come to own a value."
3. **What can be formed can be objectified:** stated objectives and staged assessment are appropriate instruments for heart-work, just as they are for classroom work.
4. **A small-group reflective Bible study (PROAPT) is a sufficient practice-site** for producing and observing this internalization.
5. **Trust in Scripture is the affective gateway,** and its terminus is Barna's "biblical worldview" — a life reorganized around Scripture, evidenced by assent to specific foundational commitments.
6. **The Holy Spirit, not the pedagogy, is the agent of growth** (via Rynsburger and Lamport); the method only structures the listening.

## 2. Affirmations

- The core anthropology is right, and the paper knows *why* it's right. This is not a footnote-citation of *Desiring the Kingdom*; assumption 1 is load-bearing, and the paper draws the correct consequence: "The heart's orientation determines what the mind will do with what it learns." Amen.
- The paper refuses to collapse formation into doctrine-transfer. It takes Hall's finding — prayer up, Bible engagement down — as an alarm rather than a statistic, and it distinguishes *knowing what the Bible says* from *trusting it*. That distinction is the whole ballgame, and most curricula never make it.
- The two-track assessment is genuinely wise. Self-report alone would ask the intellect to give testimony about the heart — a notoriously unreliable witness, since we rarely know what we love. Adding external witnesses (spouse, close friend) who watch for *behavior* is a quiet acknowledgment that the heart shows itself in what we do before it shows itself in what we say. Keep that. Strengthen it, even.
- The paper credits practice where it counts: Hawkins and Parkinson's finding that *reflective* engagement with Scripture — a repeated discipline, not a content dump — catalyzes every stage. And "Receiving begins simply with showing up" is a better sentence than the author may realize: showing up is a bodily act, a micro-liturgy, formation happening before a single idea is exchanged.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1: The classroom smuggled into the sanctuary.** *Assumption at stake: #3.* The Affective Taxonomy was built in 1964 for schools — for institutions whose native ritual is the lesson, the objective, the assessment. Import the framework and you quietly import the picture of the person that comes with it: a learner who ascends by increasingly sophisticated *engagements with content*. But if we are liturgical animals — if loves are trained by repeated, embodied, communal practice below the level of reflection — then an objectives-and-assessment grid doesn't just *measure* formation; it risks re-describing formation as a curriculum, which is the very intellectualism the paper's anthropology rejects. To be fair: I don't think intentionality is the problem. I've argued we should *audit* our liturgies and redesign them deliberately. The question is whether the intentional design targets *practices* or targets *stages of conscious appropriation*. **Severity: Foundational** — not because it sinks the project, but because it sits under everything and deserves explicit acknowledgment.

**Challenge 2: The taxonomy narrates the conscious lane; habitus runs in the other one.** *Assumption at stake: #2.* Receiving, responding, valuing, organizing — these describe a person *deliberately taking up* a value: noticing it, engaging it, preferring it, systematizing it. That's a real process, and it happens. But Bourdieu and Merleau-Ponty taught us that most of what we love got into us without ever passing through the valuing stage at all. Nobody "receives, responds to, and then values" consumerism; the mall's liturgy installs it directly into the body's know-how. If the deepest heart-formation is pre-reflective, then a taxonomy of *conscious* internalization maps the visible ten percent of the iceberg. The paper's own endpoint hints at this: characterization is precisely the stage where the value *no longer requires deliberation* — which suggests the taxonomy's summit is the habitus's ground floor. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 3: Is talking about trust a liturgy of trust — or a liturgy of talking?** *Assumption at stake: #4.* A weekly discussion group is itself a formative practice; the question is what it forms. Its repeated bodily routine is: sit, discuss, venture opinions, apply verbally. Run for years, that liturgy may train articulate *commentators* on Scripture more reliably than it trains people who *lean their weight* on Scripture at 2 a.m. Now, credit where due: PROAPT opens and closes in prayer, the Tell is embodied testimony, and the assessment looks for behavior outside the room. But the retraining of trust in Scripture would seem to want practices thicker than discussion — daily immersion, psalm-singing, memorization until the words are in the body, communal reading aloud, the long slow marination of lectio. The paper gestures at "reflective study" as the catalytic practice; I'd want the pilot to weight the *practices* as heavily as the *conversation*. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4: Barna's worldview as the summit reintroduces the propositional.** *Assumption at stake: #5.* Defining characterization by assent to six biblical statements is worldview-talk — and "worldview" is itself a residually intellectualist frame, formation pictured as installing correct beliefs. The paper's better instinct is Job 42:5: from hearing *about* to *seeing*. Trust that instinct over Barna's checklist. **Severity: Minor** — the paper's own language already points past it.

## 4. Confirmations

The staged picture is not wrong, only partial: internalization *does* deepen, and coaches genuinely need language for "where is this disciple, and what's next." The two-track assessment holds. The communal grounding holds. The Holy Spirit as agent (assumption 6) is the right theological firewall against technique-idolatry — provided it's remembered when the Likert data comes in. And the anthropology — heart first, desire formable, bobble-head discipleship refused — is not merely defensible; it's the recovery of the church's oldest wisdom.

## 5. My single biggest concern

Here it is, plainly: the taxonomy describes how a *student* comes to own a value, and I worry that the heart is not, finally, a student. The heart is more like a garden, or a gut — it is cultivated, marinated, habituated; it learns the way hands learn a piano, by ten thousand repetitions that never once pass through "valuing." My concern is not that the map is false but that coaches will mistake the map's staircase for the territory's soil — that they'll shepherd people through *discussions about* trusting Scripture and read the fluent talk as formation, while the actual retraining of trust happens (or fails to happen) in the unglamorous daily liturgies nobody assessed. The paper's grandfather-heart already knows this — it's there in "showing up," in the prayers that bracket PROAPT, in the spouse who watches for changed behavior. Let the pilot be built so the practices carry the freight and the taxonomy merely reads the gauges. Then I think this framework doesn't just survive my critique; it does something I couldn't — it hands the next generation an instrument panel for the pedagogy of desire.
