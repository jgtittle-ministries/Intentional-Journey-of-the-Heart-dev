# Review of "The Heart in Formation: Affective Taxonomy as a Framework for Heart Training"

**Reviewer: Ellen T. Charry — Foundational-Assumptions Review (Document 2), carrying forward my review of the anthropology paper**

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **The heart is trainable by objectives.** Heart formation can be specified as defined objectives with measurable progress, and doing so serves rather than distorts the formation itself.
2. **Krathwohl's continuum transfers.** The 1964 internalization sequence (receiving → characterization) describes "a genuine phenomenology" of coming to own a value, and this phenomenology holds when the value in question is wrought by the Holy Spirit rather than a curriculum. The paper's warrant is that the continuum "maps remarkably well" onto Willard's renovation of the heart.
3. **Trust in Scripture is the affective gateway.** Deepening trust in Scripture is not merely *a* heart outcome but the tractable proxy for heart formation generally — "the affective gateway to spiritual development broadly."
4. **Heart change is observable.** A seven-point self-report plus witnessed behavior change (group members, spouse, close friend) can detect movement along the continuum.
5. **The instrument is theologically neutral.** An apparatus built to grade classroom value-acquisition can be carried into sanctification without altering what it measures.
6. **Barna's endpoint is the taxonomy's endpoint.** Characterization (Stage 5) is identified with Barna's propositional biblical-worldview profile.

## 2. Affirmations

Let me say first what delighted me. The Smith material (pp. 5) is not decoration; it is the paper's true foundation, and it is Augustine's: we are what we love, and the heart's orientation is prior to the mind's work. "Training the heart is not supplemental to training the mind; it is prior to it in the order of transformation" — that sentence could have been written by any of the pastoral theologians I have spent my life reading, and I receive it gladly. This is the aretegenic instinct: teaching exists to form lovers of God, not repositories of information.

Second, the communal architecture. The external-witness component — that a spouse or friend, not only the self, testifies to change — is quietly profound. Sanctification is ecclesial; the self is a poor judge of its own heart (Jer. 17:9), and the author has built that suspicion into his method. Most assessment schemes do not.

Third, honesty. The Hall finding — prayer rising while Bible engagement declines — is faced, not managed. And the paper's closing concession that the taxonomy has "limitations and need for updating" shows the author knows he is holding a borrowed tool.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1: The senior-partner question — faced or dodged?** *(Assumptions 2 and 5; Severity: Foundational.)* Partially faced; substantially inherited. I raised in my prior review that the discernment protocols were never actually run on Krathwohl and Bloom. HFT was the natural place to run them — the taxonomy is now the *subject* — and it does not happen. The paper's validation runs in the wrong direction: Willard is commended because he fits Krathwohl ("maps remarkably well"), when the theological order requires that Krathwohl be tested against the church's account of the heart. The single gesture toward limitation comes in the final paragraph, unspecified. What are the limitations? Do any of them touch the *structure* — the assumption that internalization is learner-driven and monotone? I credit the Rynsburger and Lamport citation, which names the Holy Spirit as "the agent of growth" — that is the right confession. But the confession sits beside the apparatus rather than inside it: nothing in the five-stage mapping or the assessment design changes because the Spirit, not the pedagogy, is the agent. Theology supplies the content; psychology still supplies the grammar. My challenge stands, now with a sharper edge, because this paper had the opportunity and the obligation to answer it.

**Challenge 2: Is trust in Scripture an adequate proxy?** *(Assumption 3; Severity: Significant.)* Trust — *fiducia* — is a relation to a *person* before it is an attitude toward a text. The Reformers were careful here: *notitia*, *assensus*, *fiducia*, and the last is trust in the God who promises, with Scripture as the trustworthy form of his address. The paper knows this at moments (Willard's hearing God requires "a person whose heart is oriented toward God") but the instrument measures an attitude toward the Bible on a Likert scale. Two risks follow. First, a subtle displacement: one can score high on trust-in-Scripture while trusting the text as a possession rather than the Lord who speaks in it — the Pharisees of John 5:39 would have scored splendidly. Second, the endpoint problem: Stage 5, the deepest *affective* internalization, is defined by Barna's *propositional* checklist. An affective taxonomy capped by a cognitive endpoint has quietly conceded that the heart's destination is a set of beliefs. Job 42:5 — the paper's own better instinct — points elsewhere: to sight, to encounter, to the relation itself.

**Challenge 3: Does the internalization continuum secularize sanctification's grammar?** *(Assumptions 2 and 4; Severity: Foundational.)* Krathwohl's continuum is smooth, cumulative, and agent-monotonic: the learner deepens by degrees. Sanctification's grammar is not. It includes rupture (repentance is not a deeper stage of the old value but its death), gift (grace arrives; it is not accumulated), and desolation (the dark night, Psalm 88, Mother Teresa's fifty years). Consider what the pilot's instrument will do to a believer whom God is weaning from consolations: her felt trust drops, her Likert score falls, her witnesses see her struggle — and the framework can only read this as regression to Stage 2. Yet John of the Cross would say she may be closer to God than ever. A taxonomy with no category for grace, no mechanism for repentance-as-rupture, and no way to distinguish desolation from decline has not merely simplified sanctification; it has re-described it in a foreign grammar. The Spirit is *named* (Rynsburger and Lamport) but not *structural*.

**Challenge 4: The tradition's own maps are absent.** *(Assumption 2; Severity: Significant.)* The church has native developmental taxonomies: Bernard's four degrees of love, Teresa's mansions, the *triplex via*, Cassian's account of the ascent from fear to love. Every one of these was built for exactly this purpose — naming stages of the heart's formation — and several anticipate Krathwohl's structure while retaining grace and desolation. That the paper reaches for a 1964 educational handbook without testing it against Bernard is the senior-partner problem in historical form. The comparison would likely *strengthen* the project: where Krathwohl and Bernard agree, the borrowing is vindicated; where they diverge, the divergence is the finding.

## 4. Confirmations

The Augustinian anthropology holds: desire is prior, and the paper's whole rationale rests on it soundly. The communal-witness design holds and should be kept whatever becomes of the taxonomy. The PROAPT mapping is genuinely illuminating as *description* — the account of a newcomer crossing the threshold of Receiving simply by sitting in the room is pastorally true and tenderly observed. The choice of reflective Scripture engagement as the pivot is well-warranted by the MOVE finding and by the whole tradition of *lectio*. And the paper's modesty — a pilot, a "simpler self-report" flagged as provisional, follow-up research named — is real self-critique and earns real credit.

## 5. My single biggest concern

Instruments catechize. Whatever this paper intends, the disciple who is handed a seven-point trust scale every few weeks will learn from the scale itself a doctrine no one preached: that formation is a monotone competence, self-assessed, always meant to rise. And the believer I worry for most is the faithful one in a dry season — the one whose number falls while her fidelity deepens — who will conclude from the instrument that she is failing God when in fact He may be forming her most severely and most lovingly. The old teachers understood that doctrine shapes the people who live under it; so do measurements. Until the framework itself can say *grace*, *repentance*, and *desolation* — not in an adjacent citation but in its own structure — I would not put this scale in that woman's hands. That is not a reason to abandon the project. It is a reason to let the church's own psychology, which is older and wiser about the heart than 1964, finish becoming the senior partner.
