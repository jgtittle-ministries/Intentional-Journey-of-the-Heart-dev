# Peer Review: "Theological Anthropology of the Heart, Spirit, Soul, and Mind: Implications for Training in Spiritual Formation"

**Reviewer: J. P. Moreland — Foundational Assumptions (Analytic Rigor Check)**

Let me say at the outset that I am glad to be seated at this table. The author cites my "Tweaking Dallas Willard's Ontology of the Human Person" (2015) accurately and charitably, and the project he is undertaking — getting the anthropology right *before* building the pedagogy — is exactly the order of operations I have argued for over forty years. You cannot train what you cannot define. Most spiritual formation literature skips this step; this paper does not, and that deserves credit before a single criticism is raised.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Scriptural consistency assumption.** Scripture's uses of *leb/kardia*, *nephesh/psyche*, *ruach/pneuma*, and *nous* are accurate and mutually consistent, so a single coherent model can in principle be extracted from them.

2. **Real-distinction assumption.** Heart, spirit, soul, and mind are "scripturally different" — genuinely distinct referents, not four names for one thing under different modes of presentation.

3. **Nesting assumption.** The distinctions are structured containment-wise: spirit within heart, heart and mind within soul, soul expressed through body (Figure 1), with the entailment that predicates true of an inner layer are true of its containers ("scripture can say the soul decides, the heart decides, and the mind decides; all can be true simultaneously").

4. **Differential-trainability assumption.** Each element admits its own developmental logic, so distinct taxonomies (Bloom-style) and distinct training practices can target each element separately and measurably.

5. **Both/and methodological assumption.** Apparent scriptural tensions (dichotomy vs. trichotomy) are to be resolved on the Trinitarian-development model — hold both truths, accept residual mystery.

6. **Communication assumption.** The Holy Spirit really speaks to the human spirit, and hearing Him is a learnable skill (Willard & Johnson) — this is the functional core the whole IJH project rests on.

## 2. Affirmations

First, the paper takes the soul seriously as *real*. In an academy where the mind is routinely reduced to the brain and the soul dismissed as folk ontology, this author simply proceeds as if the inner man exists and can be studied — and he is right to. The implicit anti-physicalism here is not naïveté; it is fidelity to the biblical data and to what each of us knows from the first-person perspective. Thompson's definition of mind as "an embodied and relational process" is the one place the paper flirts with property-functionalism, and I note with approval that the author uses Thompson for insight without adopting his ontology.

Second, the trainability thesis is exactly right. Faculties of the soul are clusters of *capacities*, and capacities are strengthened by habituation under grace. This is the entire logic of the spiritual disciplines — Willard's, Foster's, and mine in *Kingdom Triangle*. A formation curriculum that distinguishes what it is training is superior to one that does not.

Third, the paper treats hearing God as a real, two-way, learnable communicative relationship. I have defended this publicly and I will defend it here: the Willard-Johnson material is sound, and making it the target of spirit-training is legitimate.

Fourth, the Powlison/Trentham truth-testing protocols are a genuinely good methodological move — the author does not baptize Kahneman; he screens him. That is intellectual discipleship of the kind I called for in *Love Your God with All Your Mind*.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1: What does "distinct" mean? (Assumptions 2 and 3.)** The paper never says whether heart, spirit, soul, and mind are (a) distinct *substances*, (b) distinct *faculties* of one substance, (c) distinct *capacities* grouped functionally, or (d) one substance under distinct *modes of presentation*. These are not stylistic variants; they generate different theologies. If (a), the paper courts a four-substance anthropology no church council would recognize. If (d), the differential-trainability thesis collapses, because there is nothing distinct to train. The paper needs (b)/(c) — and I believe intends (b)/(c) — but it must *say so*, because several sentences ("the heart is measurably different from the mind") read as (a). Severity for IJH: **Foundational** — the taxonomies inherit whatever ambiguity sits here.

**Challenge 2: Can immaterial entities nest spatially? (Assumption 3.)** "Within" in Figure 1 cannot be literal containment; the soul is not extended, so nothing is spatially inside it. The nesting must be *mereological* (part-whole), *functional* (dependence-ordering), or *metaphorical* (Willard's own caveat that such diagrams "are always incomplete"). Each reading has different consequences. On a mereological reading, the author owes us an account of immaterial parthood — a notoriously hard problem; simple souls have no separable parts. On a functional reading — which I recommend — "spirit within heart within soul" means: the capacity to commune with God is exercised *through* the heart's orientational core, which is itself a faculty *of* the soul. That is coherent, and it is very nearly my own view. But the paper's inference "a statement about the spirit is also true of heart and soul" is only valid on a specific reading (predication transmits from faculty to substance, not between coordinate faculties — what is true of the spirit is true of the *soul*, but not automatically of the *mind*). Severity: **Foundational** as stated; **Minor** once the functional reading is made explicit.

**Challenge 3: What individuates the faculties? (Assumptions 2 and 4.)** Kahneman's System 1/System 2 is doing the individuating work for heart vs. mind, but System 1/2 is a *processing-speed and effort* distinction within cognition, not an ontological one — Kahneman himself calls the systems expository fictions. Mapping heart = System 1 risks defining the biblical heart as merely "fast sloppy cognition," which flattens Mitchell's own finding that *leb* is polyvalent (will, emotion, thought, life-center). The faculties should be individuated by their *proper objects and acts* (mind: truth, by reasoning; will: goods, by choosing; spirit: God, by communing), not by their speed. Severity: **Significant** — the heart-taxonomy in SST will inherit whatever Kahneman distortion enters here.

**Challenge 4: Unity of consciousness and agency. (Assumptions 2, 3, 4.)** If four elements each "decide" and each develops on its own staged track, who is the agent? There is one "I" who hears, deliberates, and obeys. The paper's language occasionally makes the faculties into little agents ("the mind needs to learn how to submit its will") — a homunculus regress. The fix is available and cheap: *the person* (the soul) acts through its faculties; the faculties do not act. Four trainable capacity-clusters can each have developmental stages without four agents, exactly as an athlete's strength, flexibility, endurance, and coordination each develop on distinct curves while remaining capacities of one body. But the paper must adopt this locution consistently, and the IJH measurement instruments must measure *the person's maturity with respect to* each faculty, not the faculty as a free-standing measurable entity. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 5: The both/and method needs a stopping rule. (Assumption 5.)** The Trinity analogy is suggestive but disanalogous in one respect: Nicaea's both/and was forced by *explicit, non-negotiable* scriptural predications on both sides. Dichotomy-vs-trichotomy is a dispute about *lexical overlap* (Grudem's point that soul and spirit are used near-synonymously). A both/and applied to lexical data risks affirming distinctions Scripture merely fails to deny. State the criterion: a distinction enters the model only when Scripture *predicates differently* of the terms in the same context (1 Cor 14:15; Heb 4:12), not merely uses different words. Severity: **Minor** — the paper's actual distinctions mostly survive this test; the method just needs the guardrail.

**On the "laws of spiritual cause-and-effect" catalog (IJH Vol 1).** Is a laws-catalog coherent with libertarian freedom and divine personal agency? Yes — *provided* "law" means what natural moral law means: a normative-teleological regularity grounded in how God designed persons to flourish, whose "effects" flow through free response and faithful divine character, not mechanical necessitation. Galatians 6:7 is lawlike in exactly this sense. What "law" must *not* mean is an impersonal mechanism that binds God or bypasses the will — that is magic, not covenant. My reading of IJH's hearing-and-obeying core is that its laws are of the covenantal-teleological kind, which is philosophically respectable; but Vol 1 should define "law" once, precisely, and flag which entries are promises (person-backed), which are proverbs (ceteris paribus regularities), and which are constitutive design-truths. Those are three different modal strengths.

## 4. Confirmations

Here is the happy news: the strongest available repair of this paper is a position I have already published, and the author has already cited it. On my view the soul is one immaterial *substance*; heart, mind, will, spirit are *faculties* — natural groupings of capacities *of* that one soul; the faculties are individuated by their proper acts and objects; and the soul and body are deeply interdependent in functioning (my mirror-neuron and CD arguments, which the paper summarizes correctly). Read Figure 1 as a *dependence map among faculties of one soul* — the communion-capacity (spirit) exercised through the orientational core (heart), heart and reasoning-capacity (mind) both faculties of the one soul, the soul expressed through the body — and every one of the paper's formation claims goes through: real distinctness (of faculties), differential trainability (of capacities), predication-flexibility (soul decides / heart decides / mind decides, because the *soul* decides *by* those faculties), and unified agency. In other words: the paper is not wrong; it is *underspecified*, and the specification it needs is the Willard-Moreland substance-with-faculties model it already gestures toward. I would also gently note that the author's instinct that Nee's silence on the heart is a real gap is correct — Nee's trichotomy lacks precisely the faculty-level resolution the author is supplying.

## 5. My single biggest concern

My single biggest concern is this: the entire IJH edifice — taxonomies, developmental stages, measurement instruments, forty-eight laws — is being erected on the word "within," and that word is never defined. I have spent my career watching good theology wobble because a load-bearing term was left doing three jobs at once, and "within" here is doing at least three: parthood, dependence, and metaphor. Brother, the fix costs you two paragraphs: say plainly that there is *one* soul, one substance, one "I" standing before God; that heart, mind, and spirit are faculties — structured capacities — *of* that soul, not things *inside* it like nested boxes; and that training a faculty is the person, by grace and habituation, growing in the exercise of a capacity God built into him. Do that, and the nesting diagram becomes a teaching aid instead of an ontology, the four taxonomies become four lenses on one maturing person instead of four report cards for four little selves, and your grandchildren inherit a model that will withstand the physicalist headwinds they will surely face. The soul is real; you know it, I know it, and Scripture assumes it on every page — so let the model say clearly what the soul *is*, and everything you have built on it will stand.
