# Peer Review: "Cultivating Spiritual Maturity in Hearing God" (Document 8)
## Reviewer: Teresa of Ávila — third seating, continuing from HFT and MSM

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. Formation fails chiefly by *misfit*: the intervention is right for a transition the soul is not at, and the wrong prescription does not merely waste effort — it hardens the ground it means to soften.
2. Each level-boundary has one characteristic barrier (hardness, inertia, extrinsic motivation, disordered loves, immaturity), and naming the barrier specifies the remedy.
3. The remedies are the means of grace, not techniques: the taxonomy adds no power, only "a map for the gardener's hand"; the Spirit alone gives growth.
4. The duty-to-desire transition (Level 2→3) is the hinge, and it can only be cultivated *indirectly* — by offering rather than demanding; force compacts the soil.
5. The four traditions plateau at characteristic levels and are complementary specialists; formation advances by borrowing across streams.
6. A group has a collective soil, its norms set early and hard, and the leader is a deliberately receding presence whose final act is sending.

## 2. Affirmations

My son, you have written my book. I do not say this to flatter you; I say it because I spent chapters of my *Life* laboring over exactly this map and called it four waters. The beginner hauls from the well with aching arms; then the waterwheel, where the labor lessens; then the stream, where the garden is watered almost without us; and at last the rain, which is pure gift and no labor at all. The whole point of that figure — the point my confessors took twenty years to grasp — is that *the labor changes by stage*, and that to command a soul in the third water back to hauling buckets is not rigor but cruelty and ruin. Your central claim, that formation fails by pressing one level's discipline on another level's barrier, is a thing I proved on my own hide. When the paper says that the wrong intervention "does not merely waste effort; it can harden the very ground it means to soften" — yes. I watched souls compacted exactly so, by directors who knew one technique and called it faithfulness.

And your prescriptions, tested against mine, mostly hold. For beginners you prescribe scaffolding, fixed hours, accountable friendship — I prescribed determinada determinación and the company of those who help, and I anchored meditation in the humanity of Christ, a concrete place to set the mind. For aridity you prescribe perseverance without forcing sweetness, exposure to goodness without demanded response — I told my daughters never to abandon prayer in dryness, to do works of love, and never to strain after consolations, which flee the grasping hand. For the deep waters you prescribe receiving, not manufacturing — that "the person who tries to manufacture settled character produces only the self-conscious performance of it" is a sentence I would have paid a confessor to understand in 1560. And your restraint at the hinge — "the gardener waters and waits; the Spirit gives the taste" — is the fourth water's own doctrine. Best of all, the boundary sentence: formation tends conditions; the Spirit gives the growth. He gives the rain when He pleases, and often when the gardener has done least.

Your group chapter, too, knows something I built convents to enact: that the community is itself a soil, and that its founding matters more than its repair.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1 — the paper knows when to stop prescribing technique, but not what the soul passes *into*.** Assumption at stake: #2 and #4. At the 3→4 and 4→5 transitions your prescriptions are weeding, then patience and the dark night — and these are right so far as they go. But when God begins to give the prayer of quiet, the danger is not only *more pressure* but *more meditation*: directors who set the soul grinding discursively when God is giving stillness. I fought half-learned confessors over exactly this. Your Level 4–5 pedagogy says "wait," and cites John's night, which is good; but it never tells the director the positive sign — that the soul should *let itself be held*, that receiving is itself the new labor, that trying to work the old way now is disobedience. The paper rules out wrong technique without describing the new posture. A director using this map could still mistake infused quiet for laziness and prescribe the well-rope. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 2 — humility and self-knowledge are not named as the floor of every room.** Assumption at stake: #2–3. I wrote that while we are on this earth nothing matters more to us than humility, and that self-knowledge is the room the soul never leaves, however high the Castle. Your interventions are correctly matched but they float: nothing in the pedagogy says that the plowed heart, the scaffolded heart, the desiring heart, and the ordered heart must all, continuously, be growing in self-knowledge — or the whole ascent becomes a subtler pride. Your Pharisee problem gestures at it; a standing rule would secure it. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 3 — the group pedagogy begins with norms of disclosure; I began with virtues.** Assumption at stake: #6. Before I wrote one word about prayer in the *Way*, I gave my sisters three things: love for one another, detachment, humility. Your founding-norms insight is true and I confirm it — but your norms are *practices of interaction* (honest question, disclosure, shared discernment), where mine were *virtues of common life*. A group can master vulnerable disclosure and remain unmortified, undetached, each sister still clutching her honor. Disclosure without detachment breeds a warm, thorny fellowship. The community virtues come before the prayer techniques; the paper has the order half-right. Severity: **Significant**.

**Challenge 4 — the prescriber, doubled.** Assumption at stake: #1. In my MSM review I asked who forms the watcher; here it is worse, for the watcher now holds a prescription pad. Your own survey says skilled directors are scarce; your pedagogy then requires a diagnostician who can distinguish infused quiet from sloth, over-obligation from neglect (your own composite admits this is "a discerning, situational judgment"). I preferred a learned confessor to a merely pious one, but the mismatch of a confident, half-learned one destroyed souls. The paper needs one honest paragraph: this map in unformed hands is a sharper instrument of the very harm it diagnoses. Severity: **Foundational** — carried forward, and doubled.

**Challenge 5 — aridity appears only at the top.** Assumption at stake: #2. You place the withdrawal of consolation at Level 4–5. But dryness visits beginners in the first water constantly — it was most of my first eighteen years. A reader could conclude that a dry Level-2 soul is diagnosed as "no intrinsic desire" (rocky ground) when in truth God is already teaching her to serve without pay. Your affect-keyed blind spot from MSM re-enters here as a formational misdiagnosis. Severity: **Minor** to **Significant**, depending on the director.

## 4. Confirmations

The four-waters structure of the thesis: confirmed by one who lived it. The refusal to force sweetness at the hinge: mine exactly. Perseverance in prayer as non-negotiable through dryness: present in your "still be in the field." The dark night rightly received, not programmed. The traditions as toolkit — I borrowed Dominicans and Jesuits by turns and was saved by both. The boundary sentence — the Spirit gives the growth — kept honestly, restated at the close, and never once violated in the body. That discipline is rare and I credit it.

## 5. My single biggest concern

Daughters, when I wrote my *Way*, I knew the book would fall into hands I could not choose, and so I put humility on every page like salt in every dish — not as one course among the others but as the thing without which the rest poisons. This paper's map is truer than most of the direction I received in forty years, and that is precisely what frightens me about it: a true map makes a confident guide, and a confident guide who has not himself been through the rooms will march souls briskly to the door of the fourth water and then, finding God doing something he has never suffered, prescribe the well-rope. The paper says who does what at each transition; it never asks what has been done *in* the one prescribing. Add that — require the director's own self-knowledge and mortification as the license for the pad — and I would hand this to my prioresses. Without it, I would hide it from half my confessors.
