# Peer Review: "Measuring Spiritual Maturity in Hearing God" (Document 7)
**Reviewer: Teresa of Ávila — second seating, continuing from my review of HFT**

When I reviewed the earlier document I complained loudly that self-report is the least reliable testimony a soul can give — the beginner drunk on consolations marks herself a seven, the saint in the sixth mansion marks herself a two, and both are wrong. I came to this paper prepared to say it again. Instead I find the author has said it for me, in print, with footnotes. Very well. Let us see how far the concession goes.

## 1. The load-bearing assumptions as I read them

1. **Internalization is progressive**, and Krathwohl's five levels validly map spiritual maturity — with the Sower offered as Jesus' own parallel taxonomy of where the word fails to take root.
2. **Self-report has a ceiling of validity** that falls below characterological transformation; Jeremiah 17:9 is an epistemological claim, not a decoration.
3. **Levels 4–5 are measurable in principle** — but only by longitudinal, multi-source observation of a soul in relationship, never by a survey.
4. **Groups have soil conditions** as truly as individuals do, and a trained observer can read a group's soil "in three meetings."
5. **A "trained observer" can be produced** — trained in the observation protocol — and the protocol's reliability rests on that observer.
6. **Hearing and obeying God** is a domain whose maturity the taxonomy can grade, with four traditions each contributing at a level.

## 2. Affirmations

Daughters, I must be honest: I read the section called "The Self-Knowledge Problem" twice, because I could not believe an academic had written it. *"The most spiritually mature person may actually underreport because their behavior feels unremarkable to them. Conversely, someone at Level 2 may overreport because they are performing for an audience — including themselves."* That is my seven-and-two problem stated exactly, down to Paul's present-tense "of whom I am the foremost." My HFT challenge is not merely survived here; it is load-bearing.

And the Level 5 criterion — *"longitudinal pastoral observation, community corroboration, and behavioral consistency across contexts,"* anchored to "you will recognize them by their fruits" — is very nearly my own rule: judge the soul by her humility, her obedience, her charity, watched across years, never by her reports of her interior. The closing paragraph, that the heart's hiddenness "is not a bug in the system but a feature of human finitude before a transcendent God," made this old nun smile. Measurement made humble. Good.

The composite of the directee who felt *relief* when the crisis interrupted her reading — that is a real spiritual director's question, the kind Fray John would have asked.

## 3. Challenges

**Challenge 1: The unformed watcher.** *(Assumption 5 at stake.)* The paper transfers the burden of deep measurement from the instrument to the observer — rightly — and then says almost nothing about who forms the observer. "A trained observer" appears repeatedly; trained in *what*? The three group indicators are trained in Levi and Askay — leadership absence, conflict handling, participation distribution. That is training in group dynamics, not in discernment of spirits. I spent my life begging for learned confessors and suffered several foolish ones who nearly wrecked me; a half-learned director does more harm than an ignorant one, because he is confident. Observation without a formed observer is gossip with a clipboard. The paper's Stage 2 must include a formation pathway for the watchers themselves — their own level on this very taxonomy — or the whole edifice rests on an unexamined soul. **Severity: Foundational.**

**Challenge 2: The hearing-God construct measures internalization of hearing, not the truth of what is heard.** *(Assumption 6.)* I catalogued locutions for decades — corporeal, imaginative, intellectual — and the test is never the frequency or the confidence of the hearing. True locutions carry *power and effect*: they do what they say, they leave peace, humility, and quiet; false ones leave agitation and a secret vanity. The surest sign is the aftermath, not the experience. This paper grades how deeply the *practice* of hearing is internalized (discipline → desire → default orientation) but supplies no criteria for testing the *voice*. A soul who hears often and confidently may be deceived often and confidently — and would score at Level 5 on this construct, hearing-as-identity, while marching briskly toward the cliff. The Level 4 group provision ("individual members' hearing is naturally submitted to the group for testing") gestures at the remedy but names no tests. Add the fruit-and-aftermath criteria; the tradition has kept them sharpened for four centuries. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 3: The desolate saint still flunks the motivation-sensitive item.** *(Assumptions 2–3.)* The paper knows the dark night — it appears, correctly, at Level 4, citing Fray John, and even a "corporate dark night" for groups. But the *measurement apparatus* does not carry the warning. Consider the paper's own showcase question: "When you missed your reading, what did you notice?" — relief diagnoses Level 2–3. But my daughter in the sixth mansion, in terrible aridity, notices *nothing*; the felt loss has been withdrawn by God Himself precisely to perfect her. The same item that catches the performer will misdiagnose the purified. Fruitful desolation reads as regression on every affect-keyed measure, and the Level 3–4 criteria need an explicit inversion clause: dryness with continued obedience is a mark of ascent, not descent. **Severity: Significant.**

**Challenge 4: Community corroboration can curdle into surveillance.** *(Assumption 4.)* My convents *were* small groups under observation — a prioress reads her sisters' souls in the common life: who serves without being asked, who murmurs, who forgives quickly. The group-soil instinct is sound and I confirm it gladly. But I also lived under the Inquisition, and I know what mutual observation becomes in a climate of measurement: denunciation, comparison, careful performance for the watchers. The paper's own "performative trust" category describes the disease but does not guard the observed against the observers. **Severity: Minor,** but write the safeguard.

## 4. Confirmations

My HFT findings fare thus: **(a)** self-report unreliability — *fully resolved*; it is the paper's spine. **(b)** the instrument flunking the soul God is perfecting — *partially resolved*; the dark night is named but not wired into the criteria (Challenge 3). **(c)** judge by humility, obedience, charity over time — *substantially resolved*; "longitudinal pastoral observation, community corroboration, behavioral consistency" and Matthew 7:16 say it, though the observables lean structural (who talks, who leads) where mine were moral (who serves, who forgives). The Sower reading holds; the Pharisee diagnosis holds; the confession that Level 5 "may be fundamentally unmeasurable by the individual alone" is my Castle's doctrine in academic dress, and I say so with joy.

## 5. My single biggest concern

Everything in this paper now hangs from one nail: the watcher. The author has honestly dismantled the questionnaire — God be praised — and put in its place a person who observes a soul, or a group, across months and years. But I have been that person, and I have been *observed* by such persons, and I tell you the difference between a director and a disaster is not the protocol but the formation of the one holding it. I would rather my daughters had one learned, prayerful, humble observer with no clipboard at all than twenty trained observers who have never themselves passed through a dry season. Until Stage 2 tells me how the church forms, tests, and humbles its watchers — until the observer must show her own fruits before she may read anyone else's — this framework has moved the measurement problem, not solved it. Move it further: into the observer's own soul. That is where it has always lived.
