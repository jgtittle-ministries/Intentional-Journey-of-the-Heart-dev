# Review — "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart"

**Reviewer:** M. Elizabeth Lewis Hall, Rosemead School of Psychology, Biola University
**Stage:** C (spiritual formation / psychology of religion desk)
**Recommendation:** Major revision. The design is publishable and, in places, genuinely original. It is not yet publishable as a preregistration, because several of its stated thresholds cannot be reached, cannot be failed, or point at instruments and sessions the article does not contain.

---

## Preliminary assessment

I want to say at the outset what I think this paper has, because most of what follows is corrective and I do not want the corrections read as a verdict on the whole.

The central conceptual move — that the forming and the observing are one activity seen from two sides — is not a rhetorical flourish. It is a real answer to a real problem in our subfield, which is that we have spent thirty years building self-report instruments for a construct that self-report demonstrably cannot reach past its middle range, and then treating the ceiling as a psychometric nuisance rather than as information about the construct. The author's diagnosis is correct and the response is the right shape. The aging of the observational instrument to the developmental level of the room (Table 2's "first form / growing form / full form") is, as the author claims, the paper's most useful contribution to practice, and I would like to see it survive review. The candor of the limitations paragraph is above what this journal usually receives; the sentence "Nothing in the design is positioned to notice what all of us take for granted" is the kind of sentence most authors cannot write about their own work.

My findings cluster in four places, which correspond to the four questions I was asked.

The first is that this design has systematically arranged for certain people to be unable to say a true thing, and then built its instruments so that the silence is unreadable — it comes back marked identically to contentment. The second is that the design has no category for suffering. It has a category for lapse, and it rewards the honest report of one; it has no category for a person who practiced faithfully and heard nothing, and its ladder scores such a person at the floor of its primary sign, by definition, for a whole year. The third is that Tables 2 and 3 do not survive close reading as a measurement architecture: the promotion rule and the missing-data rule are mutually undefined, two conditions cannot be failed, one points at an instrument described nowhere else in the article, and one points at sessions the schedule does not contain. The fourth is that the prototype disclosure is handled at the wrong level of analysis — the mitigation is applied to individuals and every reading that matters is a property of the room.

I have also flagged three places where the paper claims considerably less than its own data could carry. Those are the cheapest fixes in the review and the ones I would most like the author to take.

## HIGH

### 1. The second disconfirmation condition is read at a participant level the article says it cannot reach, on an instrument the article never describes. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** Table 3, row 2, states the disconfirmer as "The where-they-differ row left empty at all four profile reads for more than half the participants," read at "The four participant profile pages."

These "participant profile pages" appear exactly once in the manuscript, inside the disconfirmation table. They are not in the measurement architecture section. They are not in Table 1's measurement-occasion column, which schedules every other reading in the year by week. They are not in the family-facing inventory, which the article closes off explicitly: "That is the whole family-facing corpus." They are not in the team's paperwork inventory, which is likewise closed: "One debrief page per session, filled within a day in about ten minutes," feeding "the companion team's one weekly forty-five-minute call."

Worse, the unit is individual, and the article has already told us it cannot go there: "nothing here establishes an individual's level; the claims available at year's end are about the room." A page carrying a per-participant "where-they-differ row," read four times a year, is precisely an individual longitudinal dossier synthesizing self-report against observation — the artifact the "rooms, never people" commitment exists to forbid.

**Why it matters.** One of two things is true, and both need fixing before publication. Either the profile pages exist, in which case the article has concealed its most individually invasive instrument from a measurement architecture it presents as complete, and has violated its own stated claim-ceiling and arguably its seventh constraint; or they do not exist, in which case a preregistered disconfirmation condition names a source of evidence that will not be there in June, and the condition is dead on arrival. For a paper whose entire warrant is that it published its failure conditions in advance, an unfalsifiable-because-unbuilt condition is the most damaging kind of defect.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Resolve the disjunction in the text. If the pages exist: describe them in the measurement architecture, place them in Table 1's third column at their four occasions, state who writes them, who may read them, what is on them, how long they are kept, and reconcile them explicitly with "Rooms, never people" — I would accept the reconciliation that a Companion's per-person notes are a shepherd's lens rather than a rating, which is the argument the article already makes for named notes, but it must be made *about this artifact* and not left for a reader to construct. If they do not exist: rewrite row 2's unit as the room and its source as the debrief series plus the self-read verbatims, and set a threshold that a room-level design can actually read (for example, a preregistered count of recorded self/observed discrepancies at the room level across the year, with a floor).

### 2. The promotion rule and the missing-data rule are mutually undefined, and Table 3 uses "full form" in a different currency than Table 2. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The marking rule reads: "The team reads the rung the room is on, marks each sign seen, not yet, or no occasion (an event-contingent sign with no event is missing data, not failure), and promotes a rung after three consecutive weeks of seen."

Set that beside: "First forms are readable every week because the liturgy schedules their occasions; the higher forms are event-contingent."

The rule is therefore undefined at exactly the point where it carries weight. Does a "no occasion" week break a consecutive run? The article never says, and both answers break something:

- If "no occasion" breaks the run, then the full forms of the two event-contingent signs are arithmetically unreachable in twenty-two weeks. The full form of the sought stray has its occasion "Whenever someone goes quiet"; there cannot be three *consecutive weeks* of an event that is by construction occasional. The full form of costly telling is carried by "testimony nights," of which the year contains a small and unstated number. The top rung of this instrument cannot be climbed.
- If "no occasion" does not break the run, then "three consecutive weeks" does not mean three consecutive weeks; it means three consecutive occasions, which could span October to May. A promotion in May would then certify nothing about a trend, and the article's claim that the promoted rung is a trace of internalization loses its temporal content.

Compounding this, Table 3 row 1 predicts "full forms at least occasionally by spring." "Occasionally" is an occurrence criterion. "Promotes a rung after three consecutive weeks of seen" is a promotion criterion. The table and the marking rule are denominated in different currencies, so in June the author will have to choose which one row 1 was about — which is precisely the post-hoc latitude the article cites Nosek to foreclose.

**Why it matters.** The aged-forms instrument is this paper's best contribution. If its ascent rule is undefined, the contribution cannot be evaluated, replicated, or corrected by anyone else — and the article has committed to publishing its materials so that it can be. This is not a quibble about wording; it determines whether the pilot's headline finding is possible.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** State the rule in two lines on the teaching pages that already sit at the front of the pack. I would propose: (a) promotion at first form requires three consecutive *sessions* marked seen, since first forms are scheduled weekly; (b) promotion at growing and full form requires three consecutive *occasions* marked seen, with "no occasion" weeks passed over rather than breaking the run, and with the count of intervening no-occasion weeks recorded beside the promotion so a reader can see how thin the base was; (c) Table 3 row 1 is restated as an occurrence prediction with a number ("at least one full-form occasion recorded for at least two of the three signs by the final quarter"), and (d) a rule is added for what happens when a full form has no occasion all year — under the article's current rules that outcome is neither passed, nor failed, nor eligible for the "reported untested" exemption, which is reserved for conditions "written about a part of the curriculum that then changed." It should be reportable as *no occasion arose*, which is itself a finding about the year.

### 3. The design predicts full forms of "response to load" but schedules no load. School breaks are not load, the liturgy contains no lament, and the families who meet real load are the ones who leave. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The three vital signs are declared to be "three readings of one underlying construct, confidence in God under the load of ordinary weeks." The second sign's full form is "Real load carried toward God in the room's first ten seconds," and Table 2 names where the year schedules its occasion: "Every opening; the three breaks are its scheduled load tests."

The three breaks are the host school's holidays. A school holiday is not load. It is the *removal* of scaffolding, which is a fine test of practice survival — and the article uses it well for exactly that, in Table 3 row 3 — but it is not adversity, and adversity is what the construct names. The design has therefore scheduled zero occasions for the full form of one of its three signs and left the sign's ascent entirely to whatever grief happens to walk in the door.

This is compounded twice. First, the twenty-two-week liturgy has no lament move anywhere. The arc runs "container, story, being known, confession... hearing God in Scripture... doubts... mission, rhythm, family night... consolidates, sends, and commissions." Doubt is scheduled (Table 1, weeks 7–11). Confession of sin is scheduled. Suffering is not, in a curriculum whose central construct is confidence in God under load, in a tradition whose largest single genre of prayer is lament. Second, the design cannot read the load it does encounter, because the article concedes: "nor does the design currently read attrition as safety data, though a family that quietly stops coming may be the most informative reading available to it." A family hit by a serious loss in February is, in my experience of both church groups and school communities, the family most likely to disappear from a Wednesday-afternoon commitment. So the participants who instantiate the construct exit the instrument, and their exit is recorded as absence.

**Why it matters.** This is the finding I care about most, and it is not a methodological complaint dressed up in theological clothes. A design that names suffering as its organizing construct and then encounters suffering only as attrition will produce a year of readings drawn entirely from families for whom the year went reasonably well. That is a selection process operating precisely along the dimension being measured. And for this journal's readership, an instrument that cannot see lament will be adopted by ministries that then cannot see it either, which is a pastoral harm at scale and not merely a validity problem.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting, and it fixes both halves at once).** Convert one week of the third quarter into a scheduled lament practice — the psalms of lament walked through the same six-step hearing practice the room already knows, parents first, one practice for the week. This costs nothing: the one-practice-per-week rule is preserved, no family is divided, no new paperwork is added, and no target is announced. What it buys is the only *scheduled* occasion in the design for the full form of sign two, which currently has none, and it puts a socially sanctioned form on the table for a participant whose year is hard — which is the difference between a room where a grieving mother can speak and a room where she can only be quiet. Second, add attrition to the Thursday call's existing decisions-log discipline: when a family stops, the follow-up under the forty-eight-hour rule already happens as care; record on the team's side, in a short fixed vocabulary, what the team learned about why, so that a departure enters the record as data rather than as a hole. This requires no participant-facing page and therefore no permission; it is shepherd-side watching, which the article's own third constraint places outside the permission requirement.

### 4. The instrument has three marks and none of them is for desolation. A participant who practiced faithfully and heard nothing is scored at the floor of the primary sign, by construction, and the design's discrepancy engine is blind to it. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** Table 2's full form of costly telling is "Telling what God said and what obeying it cost." The growing form is "Offering: something brought unprompted, and an honest report of a lapsed practice is telling with a small price." The marking vocabulary is three-valued: "seen, not yet, or no occasion."

Read those together and a specific person becomes invisible. Consider a participant — a parent or a teenager — who does the practice every week of the year, who tells the truth about it every week, and who hears nothing. The design has generously provided a rung for the person who *stopped* practicing and admits it: that is credited as growing-form telling with a small price. It has provided nothing for the person who did not stop and received silence. The full form of the sign is definitionally unreachable for them, because there is no "what God said" to tell. They cannot be marked "no occasion," because the occasion arose weekly. They will be marked "not yet," every week, for a year — the identical mark given to a person who is indifferent, and to a room that never tried.

The problem then propagates into the design's central analytic move. The article's account of what a finding looks like is: "A teen whose survey reports daily practice but who has never once told; a room that describes itself as 'family' while nobody has yet sought a stray... these gaps are the findings." But desolation does not produce a gap. It produces *concordant flatness*: the pulse is subdued, the observation is not-yet, the room's self-read is muted. All three tiers agree, and agreement is exactly what this architecture reads as untroubled. The one condition the design is least able to detect is the one the tradition has always regarded as among the most spiritually significant.

I take the anticipated objection seriously. The article is pastorally careful in its prose: "no reading of any instrument measures anyone's standing with God, which is complete by grace at every stage"; "where the mirror shows nothing yet, waited for without panic"; "its absence at year's end is not read as failure." I believe those sentences. My finding is not that the author is careless about this. It is that the *care lives entirely in the prose and not at all in the instrument*, and the instrument is what will be exported to other rooms with other shepherds who did not write the prose. Further, the design's own ladder is inverted at this point: the participant who keeps showing up carried by nothing but the container is, on the taxonomy's terms, exhibiting external regulation rather than intrinsic valuing, and would read as *below* the level-two-to-three threshold the year aims at. On the spiritual-direction tradition's terms, and on the terms of the empirical literature on spiritual struggle and meaning-making, persistence without consolation is nearer the summit of the ladder than its middle. The instrument and the theology disagree, and the article does not notice.

**Why it matters.** For this journal, this is the finding that determines whether the design is safe to publish as a template. A ministry adopting this instrument will, at some point in its first year, have a member in a dark night, and the instrument will tell the team that member is failing to progress. The article's most important ethical rule — "No participant is ever told a level or a stage" — protects the member from hearing that verdict, but it does not stop the team from forming it, and a team's diagnostic lens shapes the care it gives.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Two small changes.

First, add a fourth mark. The vocabulary becomes seen / not yet / no occasion / **borne** — the last reserved for a reading where the team judges the sign flat under a season of loss, dryness, or crisis rather than under indifference. It requires no new page: the debrief sheet already carries "the raw mark, the rung it promotes to, and an eight-word note of the occasion behind it," and a fourth mark with its eight words fits the existing field. Marks of *borne* are excluded from both the promotion count and the not-yet count, and are reported separately in June.

Second, and more important, give the full form of costly telling a second face. As Table 2 stands, the top rung has a content requirement that only consolation can satisfy. I would write it as two alternatives at the same rung: "Telling what God said and what obeying it cost" **or** "telling that God has been silent and that one kept practicing anyway." Those are the same act — costly self-revelation about one's actual life with God at a price — and the second is, on any reading I know of the tradition, the more costly of the two in a room full of people reporting that they heard something. This change costs nothing, announces no target, adds no page, and repairs the design's blindest spot. It also has a measurement payoff: it converts the year's most spiritually serious participants from unreadable into readable, which is the difference between an instrument that measures formation and one that measures a good year.

Third, and this follows: Table 3 row 1's disconfirmer, "Any sign still marked not yet in its first form in February," must be stated as read against marks of *borne*, or a room that suffered well and honestly will read as a design failure.

### 5. The prototype mitigation is applied at the individual level; every reading that matters is a property of the room. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The disclosure is admirably full and I want that on the record. But the mitigation offered is a single sentence: "Participants who came through the prototype are marked as such in the readings, since a room holding people who have practiced for eighteen months is not a naive room and no baseline should pretend otherwise."

Marking individuals cannot mitigate a room-level instrument. The article's seventh commitment is "Rooms, never people," and Table 2's signs are all read as properties of the room: the room settles, the room absorbs, someone goes after a stray. There is no arithmetic by which a marked individual is subtracted from a room's rung. If the participant who goes after the person gone quiet, unasked, is one of the four prototype teenagers who have practiced for eighteen months and have already led sessions, the room is promoted on the strength of a member who arrived at the top rung. The mark records who it was; it does not tell anyone what the room's rung then means.

The contamination is worse than additive in the one place the paper's most novel claim lives. The trust-vocabulary condition (Table 3 row 5) is read from "unprompted language moving from held toward owned across the three self-reads," gathered in a single conversation in which "the room answers them together, leader first as always." A prototype member who says "these are my people, I could say anything" in week 7 has not merely contributed one owned-language datum; they have put the phrase into the room's air, after which no later use of it by anyone is unprompted in the sense the condition requires. The design has no way to mark this, because the vocabulary is a property of a conversation, not of a person.

The denominators are also strained by the disclosure in a way the article does not reconcile. The cohort is "three to six families, six to fifteen participants in all." The prototype family is "Both parents and their four teenagers" — six people. If that family enrolls whole, it is the entire minimum cohort and forty percent of the maximum, and it is one family of three to six. Layer on "The prototype group also previews each session before the cohort meets it," and this family is not a participant group at all; it is a co-design team whose spontaneous behavior in any given session is a second running.

I note that the author already knows individual marking is insufficient, because Table 3 row 4 does the right thing: it carves prototype teens out of the condition entirely — "counted only where the teen had not led before." That carve is applied to exactly one of six conditions. My finding is that the author's own best move has been made once and not generalized.

**Why it matters.** Every group-level reading in the year — the room's rung on all three signs, the settle-time trend, the vocabulary migration, the practice-survival counts, the self-read — is a reading of a room that may be up to forty percent pre-trained and that contains a subgroup which has previewed the session. Reported without carves, these are not readings of the design; they are readings of the design plus its longest-running participants.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Generalize row 4's move to every condition, and do it before the year runs. For each of the six conditions in Table 3, state in advance (a) whether prototype-origin participants are included or carved, (b) what the condition is reported as if the carve empties the sample, and (c) for room-level readings that cannot be carved, a stated rule for reporting the occasion's source — the debrief page's eight-word note already records the occasion, so recording whether the person who produced it was prototype-origin costs one character. Second, report every room-level trace twice where possible: as observed, and with prototype-produced occasions excluded. Third, and separately: the prototype family's pre-series survey is not a baseline and should not be pooled with the naive families' in the pre/post comparison. Say so in the analysis plan, not in June. Fourth, the previewing role should be named for what it is in the design's own vocabulary — this family is functioning as part of the team, not as part of the cohort — and the article should state whether their five pages are being counted as participant data at all.

### 6. Table 3 row 4 requires two failures to fail, may have an empty sample, points at sessions the schedule does not contain, and creates a documented reason not to rescue a struggling sixteen-year-old. (HIGH)

**What is wrong.** Four independent problems in one row. The condition reads: predicted, "By each second running, the structure lives in the members enough to survive a teen leader"; shown wrong by "An adult taking over a block the teen was leading, at both spring second runnings, counted only where the teen had not led before."

(a) *It requires both.* One adult takeover is a pass. A design condition that survives half its own failures is not a threshold.

(b) *The sample may be empty.* The carve is "counted only where the teen had not led before," and the article tells us "in the first year several of them have led before, through the prototype," and separately that "The teen leaders are volunteers, so it runs on the willing." Volunteering will correlate with prior experience. It is entirely possible that both spring second runnings are led by prototype teens, in which case the condition has an n of zero and is untestable — and it does not qualify for the article's own "reported untested" exemption, which is reserved for a condition "written about a part of the curriculum that then changed." The curriculum will not have changed; the sample will simply not have existed.

(c) *"Both spring second runnings" has no referent in Table 1.* The body text says "at least five sessions across the year are second runnings led by teens." Table 1's measurement column names three: "teen-led second running wk 8" and "teen-led runnings wks 14–15." The fourth quarter (weeks 18–22), which is the only stretch of the year a reader would call spring, lists no teen-led running at all; its arc is "consolidates, sends, and commissions." So the condition's occasions cannot be located on the published schedule, and a reader cannot check in June whether it was read at the sessions it named.

(d) *The condition creates an incentive against care.* This is the part I would press hardest. The team knows this threshold — the article publishes it, and the team is trained inside its vocabulary. The condition is failed by "An adult taking over a block the teen was leading." That means that at the moment a first-time teen leader is visibly foundering in front of their own parents and the other families, the adult in the room who steps in to help is the adult who fails the design's condition. The article's general rule "where pastoral care and measurement rigor pull against each other, care wins" is the right rule, but it is stated at the level of the design's ethics and is not applied to this specific case anywhere in the text, and this is the one case in the whole paper where the pull is sharp, immediate, unrehearsed, and falls on a minor.

**Why it matters.** The subject of the harm is a teenager, in front of their parents, in a design that has already placed them in the least powerful position in the room. It is not enough for the paper's ethics to be sound in general; here the disconfirmation architecture points the other way and the paper has not noticed.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** First, restate the threshold as a single failure, not two, and define "taking over" operationally (an adult assuming leadership of a block for more than some pre-stated interval). Second, publish the fallback: if no first-time teen leader runs a spring second running, the condition is reported *not sampled*, with the reason, and the design says so now rather than discovering it in June. Third, place the teen-led runnings on Table 1 at their actual weeks so a reader can locate them. Fourth — and this is the one I would insist on — add an explicit precedence rule in the text: an adult may step in at any time and the covenant's "care wins" clause governs absolutely, and every takeover is recorded with the reason it was judged necessary. Then treat the *reason* as the reading. A takeover because a teen was overwhelmed is a care event and reports as such; a takeover because the structure did not hold reports against the condition. That preserves the condition's information, removes the incentive, and costs one line on the debrief sheet the design already prints for each second running.

## MED-HIGH

### 7. Nothing in the family-facing corpus asks any participant whether they could say a true thing. The design adopts Edmondson's construct and rejects her level of measurement without argument. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The article claims a close convergence with the psychological-safety literature: "the convergence is striking, practice for practice: our confession-and-restoration night is Edmondson's 'mistakes' item lived liturgically; our costly telling is her 'hard issues' item with God in the sentence; our standing welcome for the honest skeptic is her 'different' item given a chair."

Edmondson's items are *items*. They are administered to individuals, privately, and aggregated to the team; the construct is defined as a shared belief, and the design's own limitations paragraph knows this: "Psychological safety is defined in its own literature as a belief shared across a team and measured at team level, while this design reads the room as a whole and never separates the readings by generation, so a room can register as safe while the subgroup least positioned to say otherwise, the teenagers, seated beside their own parents in a room where parents speak first, is never heard on its own."

That is my finding, stated by the author. What I am filing is that it is stated and then not acted on, and the design already contains the fix. The five family-facing pages are individually filled, by permission, never handed out or collected in front of the room, and explicitly available to a teen independent of their parent: "a young person who would rather not fill a page does not have to, and the room is never told who did." That is a private, individually answered, permitted channel — and not one of its questions asks about the room. The pre-series survey asks "practice frequency, whether anyone outside the family knows their interior life, hopes and worries for the year." The pulses ask "how present have I been, what has surprised me, what do I want to do differently." All three pulse questions point the respondent at themselves. Across a whole year, no participant is ever asked, in private, whether there was something they wanted to say and did not.

**Why it matters.** The design's entire epistemology is that a room is not opaque to its members. It then declines to ask the members the one question that would test whether the room is what it looks like from the front. And the person whose answer would differ most from the room's public self-read is the person the design has structurally silenced: the teenager sitting beside their parent, in a round where the parent has already spoken, in a school community their family belongs to.

**Remedy A (constraint-respecting).** Add two items to the quarterly pulse — a page that already exists, already has permission, is already private, and is already excluded from the room. Both must be phrased about the *room*, not the person, so that the "Rooms, never people" commitment is honored and no individual is rated: "Was there anything this quarter you wanted to say in the circle and did not?" (yes/no, with an optional and explicitly not-required line) and "In this group, is it safe to say a hard thing?" Report only as a room-level distribution. Because a room of six to fifteen makes small-cell reporting deanonymizing, route these two items to the outside adult the design already names — "one adult outside the Companion team whom any participant can reach without going through a Companion or a parent, named aloud in the first session" — who returns to the team a room-level summary only, with a floor below which nothing is reported. This uses two existing structures, adds no paperwork to the one-page rule, announces no target, and gives the teenager a line that does not run through their parent. Additionally, the design already records the pulse return rate — "Pulse pages from the quarter's close come back to a basket at the door, unchased. [Count recorded]" — and the corpus is admittedly not anonymous. Report that return rate split by generation. It is free, it is behavioral, and it is the closest thing the design currently has to a teen-voice indicator.

**Remedy B (this argues explicitly against Constraint 6).** I want to make this argument on its own ground, and the author is free to reject it.

The commitment "a family is never divided" is warranted in the paper by parental primacy (Deut 6:6–7; Eph 6:4), by the conviction that "A program that quietly takes a family's formation over has failed even where it appears to succeed," and by a field finding that parents-first unlocks teen honesty. I accept all three. None of them, as far as I can see, entails that in twenty-two weeks there is no hour in which any participant speaks outside the hearing of their own household.

Parental primacy is a claim about who bears responsibility for a child's formation and who a program serves. It is not a claim that the parent must be within earshot of every sentence the child speaks about God. On the contrary, the design already accepts the principle at its sharpest point: it names an outside adult reachable "without going through a Companion or a parent," which concedes that there exist true things a young person may need to say that cannot be said in front of their parent. Having conceded that, the design has provided one emergency door and no ordinary one.

The concrete proposal is modest and, I think, compatible with the conviction rather than against it: one scheduled hour in the year — I would place it in the second quarter, after the container is built and before the outward turn — in which the cohort splits by generation for a single practice, in the same room, at the same time, in sight of each other, with both groups told in advance exactly what the other group is doing, with the same practice run in both, with nothing reported from either group to the other, and with the practice going home to the kitchen table afterward as every practice does. The family is not divided in any sense the conviction cares about: the parents are present, the content is shared, the formation returns home. What changes is that for one hour the teenager whose true answer would embarrass their family, and the spouse whose true answer concerns their marriage, have somewhere to put it.

I note also the case the constraint handles least well: the article observes that "only households with an adult free at that hour could come at all," so a substantial share of families will attend with one parent. For that household, "in every practice the parents start" means one adult opens, every week, for twenty-two weeks, in front of their own children, with no peer step. The reciprocal-disclosure warrant the design cites (Altman and Taylor, which the author very properly limits) assumes matched steps between parties; a lone parent has no matching party. The generation hour is also the only relief this design offers that person.

### 8. Which parent goes first is never specified and never rotated, so a household's disclosure ceiling can be set by one adult and the design cannot see it happen. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The rule appears five times and never with an actor: "in every practice the parents start"; "the parents tell first, and tell something real"; "breaks into family pairs to practice it on a second passage, parents reading first"; "each person tells one thing they heard, a parent opening"; "A parent who goes first with something true licenses a depth the teen would never volunteer cold."

The design's own theory of why this works is a depth-licensing theory: the first speaker sets the level, and everyone after calibrates to it. That theory implies that whoever opens sets the household's ceiling for the evening. The design then leaves the identity of that person entirely to habit, specifies no rotation, and records nothing about it. In a two-parent household in a small Christian school community, the likeliest stable equilibrium is that the same parent opens every week for twenty-two weeks. Where that is the father — which in this population is the likelier default, and the design has no mechanism that would tell us otherwise — the mother's telling is always second, always calibrated to a level she did not set, and the depth she is licensed to reach is a function of what her husband was willing to say in front of their children and three to five other families from their school.

The sharpest version is the one the design cannot reach at all: a woman whose honest telling concerns her marriage has, under "a family is never divided," no circle that is not her husband's circle. The design's answer to the parallel objection about teens is "time and absence... practices that survive a break nobody supervises, a teen-led night no adult carries, and a stray sought by someone nobody pointed." None of those three is available to a spouse.

**Why it matters.** This is the version of the power question that a methods panel will not file and that this journal's readership will encounter in every room that adopts the design. It also has a measurement consequence: if one parent always opens, then the household's observed "costly telling" across the year is substantially a single adult's trace with the rest of the family arranged beneath it, and the room's rung inherits that.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Two lines, no new instrument. First, a rotation rule: in two-parent households the opening parent alternates week to week, stated in the handbook and spoken aloud when the practice is taught, so that it reads as the design's normal shape and not as a correction of anyone. This divides no family, adds no page, announces no target, and preserves the "parents first" commitment intact — it constrains only *which* parent, not the order of generations. Second, record it: one character on the debrief sheet per household per session. Whether a household ever lets the second parent open is a trace of exactly the thing the design says it is measuring, and it is currently thrown away. For single-attending-parent households, see Remedy B under Finding 7; at minimum the article should acknowledge that its reciprocity warrant does not extend to them and say what the design offers instead.

### 9. Table 3 row 5 is immunized against the outcome it most needs to catch, and the paper's most novel claim is read by the people who proposed it, unblinded. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** Row 5's disconfirmer: "The third self-read carrying no more known or owned language than the first, **while sign readings advance**."

The conjunct is fatal. If the vocabulary flatlines *and* the sign readings also flatline, the condition is not disconfirmed. That is, the scenario in which the design has failed completely is the scenario this condition is written to survive. Only a partial failure — signs up, words flat — can trip it. A disconfirmation condition should be hardest to escape in the worst case, and this one is easiest.

Second, the reading procedure. This row carries the article's single most speculative claim, and the article says so: "The identification stage may be internalization by another name, visible at group scale; that equation is this project's proposal rather than the literature's finding." The article also concedes the coding hazard with unusual honesty: "all three house words can be heard along a single dimension of felt warmth, so vocabulary by itself cannot separate a room that calculates from a room that predicts from a room that identifies, and a room that merely grows warmer will appear to migrate." And elsewhere: "there is no blind coding, no second coder, and no inter-rater check." So the highest-stakes novel proposal in the paper is coded by its author's own team, unblinded, on transcripts whose chronological order is known, against a hypothesis they know and hope for.

**Why it matters.** The trust-stage-equals-internalization equation is the theoretical bridge on which the group-level half of the companion article's argument rests. If it is going to be offered to the field, its one empirical test in this pilot should be run in the one way that costs nothing and would make it credible.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Delete the conjunct: the condition should read that the third self-read carries no more known or owned language than the first, full stop, and the state of the sign readings should be reported alongside as context rather than as a gate. Then blind the coding using structures that already exist. The scribe already takes verbatim notes at all three self-reads; the design already recruits "a reader from outside the Companion team" once a quarter. De-identify the three transcripts (the self-read is about the room, so names are not the unit and their removal costs nothing), strip dates, present them in random order to the outside reader, and ask a single question: sort these three by how much the language sounds like people who have become each other's people. Publish the sort, whatever it is. Two hours of one outsider's time converts the paper's weakest evidential claim into its most defensible. This does not touch the covenant — names stop where they already stop — and it does not show the room what the team wrote.

### 10. Named longitudinal records on minors with no retention limit, no destruction date, no subject access, no stated review, and a blanket year-long consent with no withdrawal clause. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The article states: "Families are also told in the same conversation that the team keeps named notes about participants across the year, because permission that conceals what is kept is not permission." Good. And: "Names appear on the team's side only, under a signed measurement covenant described below, in a single secured location." Also good, and better than most.

What is absent is everything after collection. There is no retention period, no destruction date, no statement of who inherits the notes if a Companion leaves or the pilot ends, no right of access for the person described — indeed the opposite is stated, "nobody shows the room what the team wrote" — and no mention anywhere in the manuscript of an ethics review of any kind. The consent is a single blanket yes taken once: "One yes covers the year's five pages." The article carefully protects the *initial* refusal — "a 'no' costs nothing, the family remains a full participant" — and says nothing at all about withdrawal, either of future participation or of pages already given.

The population is minors, in a school their families attend, recruited by a founder who is also the researcher, who also holds the pastoral relationship, and who sits with each family personally to ask.

**Why it matters.** I am not raising this as generic boilerplate; I would not file it against a paper that merely narrated a ministry. This paper claims the epistemic goods of research explicitly — it invokes preregistration, publishes disconfirmation thresholds, submits to a peer-reviewed journal, and functions as the field arm of a measurement article. Once those goods are claimed, the corresponding obligations follow. And the design's own third constraint supplies the principle: "Permission is required for pages, never for care." If permission is for pages, then permission over pages is revocable, and the article's own logic requires a withdrawal clause it does not have.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** All of this belongs in the covenant, which is the design's native instrument for exactly this kind of commitment, and none of it violates a constraint. Add: (a) a retention period and a destruction date for the named notes, stated to families in the permission conversation; (b) a successor rule naming who holds the notes if a Companion departs or the pilot ends; (c) a withdrawal clause — a family may withdraw at any time, and may withdraw pages already given, with the pages destroyed and the fact recorded in the decisions log without a reason being required; (d) a sentence in the manuscript naming what review the pilot received and from whom, or stating plainly that it received none and why the author judges that appropriate. On the ecclesial gap the article already names — "none of which is party to the covenant or receives those this year commissions" — I would add the pastoral consequence: the named outside adult is currently the only structure that catches a serious disclosure from a minor, and the article should say who that person is accountable to.

### 11. The team's own shepherding preempts the occasions for the third vital sign, and this cost is missing from the list of costs. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The sought stray's full form is "Seeking: someone goes after a person gone quiet, unasked," and its occasion is "Whenever someone goes quiet." The occasion is thus defined by someone noticing that a person has gone quiet — and the people trained to notice are the Companion team, operating under a forty-eight-hour follow-up rule and a governing commitment that "where pastoral care and measurement rigor pull against each other, care wins."

The consequence is a loop the article does not address. A Companion who sees a teenager going quiet must, under care-wins, go. Having gone, the occasion for the room to go is largely consumed. The better the shepherding, the fewer occasions the third sign gets, and the sign's ascent becomes a measure of the team's slowness rather than the room's maturity. There is also a confound in the other direction: a week marked "no occasion" is indistinguishable from a week in which the team failed to notice anyone going quiet, and the team is the instrument that decides.

The article contains a list of exactly this kind of cost — "Asking permission for every page means the family-facing corpus is not anonymous... Keeping each family in one circle means the subgroup readings a separating design would yield are not available. Sealing the confidential notes... Refusing to announce a target... And never collecting the journals..." — five costs, carefully counted, and this one is not among them.

**Why it matters.** This is the sharpest internal instance of the paper's own thesis running backwards. The article's claim is that forming and observing are the same activity seen from two sides. Here they are not: the forming act destroys the observing occasion. That is worth saying, because it bounds the thesis honestly rather than leaving a reader to find the boundary.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Do not slow the shepherding; that would violate care-wins and would be wrong. Instead, re-specify the sign so that it is a census rather than a race. Record, for each occasion, *who reached the person and how the team learned of it* — including reaches the team learned about afterward, by text, the next day, from the person themselves. A peer who checked in on Thursday counts as fully as one who moved on Wednesday; the sign is about whether the body goes, not whether it goes first. This costs one line in the existing eight-word occasion note. Second, distinguish the two meanings of "no occasion" on the sheet with a single mark: no one went quiet, versus no one the team saw went quiet. Third, add the preemption cost to the cost list in "Care wins, and we count the cost," where it belongs and where its absence is currently conspicuous.

### 12. The article never states whether Companions are parents of enrolled participants, and there is no recusal rule. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** The article is careful about one role ambiguity and silent about a more consequential one. It stipulates: "Team in this article always means those adults, never the group they lead." That sentence shows the author thought about role boundaries. It does not say whether the two-to-four adults who run the year, sign the covenant, keep the named notes, read the signs, and write the debrief pages are also the parents of enrolled teenagers.

The arithmetic makes overlap likely. The cohort is three to six families; the team is two to four adults; the prototype family contributes two adults with eighteen months of experience and every reason to be recruited; and the article's most specific safeguarding provision is "a Companion watching each teenager during a parent's telling."

That last clause is where the silence bites. Under the current text, it is possible — and nothing in the article forbids it — that the Companion assigned to watch a teenager during the parent's telling is that teenager's other parent, and that the named note recording what was seen is written by the same person. It is also possible that a parent on the team reads and marks the signs on their own child's behavior, in a room where they themselves went first.

**Why it matters.** The design's protections against the founder's dual role are stated at length and honestly. The parallel dual role, one layer down and multiplied by the number of Companions, is not stated at all. In a small institution where everyone is everyone's something, naming who holds which role is not bureaucratic tidiness; it is the difference between a structure that can absorb a difficult moment and one that cannot.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Two sentences of disclosure and one rule. Disclose in the manuscript whether Companions are drawn from enrolled families and what the prototype family's relation to the team is. Then add a recusal rule that respects every constraint: a Companion is never assigned as the watching adult for their own child during their own or their spouse's telling, and where a Companion's own child produces the occasion behind a sign reading, the mark is written by a second Companion and the two-adult rule already in the handbook supplies the second. The family is not divided — they remain in the same circle, in the same practice. What changes is only whose pen writes the mark.

### 13. Settle time is the best measurement in the design, and it is unoperationalized, unscheduled, and unpreregistered. This is where the paper claims least. (MED-HIGH)

**What is wrong.** From the Week 7 narration: "[The scribe discreetly notes the time to a truly settled room; at each of the year's three re-entries the same count is taken, and the trend line is one of the pilot's cleanest traces of internalization.]"

That claim is made in a bracket in a narrative section and is nowhere supported. "A truly settled room" has no operational definition anywhere in the article. Settle time appears in none of the three tables. It has no disconfirmation condition. It is taken three times in a year, which is three points, which is not a trend line.

Which is a shame, because it is the strongest instrument in the design and nobody has noticed. It is continuous rather than ordinal; it is behavioral rather than self-reported; it is invisible to participants and therefore essentially non-reactive; it is unaffected by the prototype problem in the way the vocabulary is, since a settled room is a property of everyone present; it does not require the team to make a judgment against a rubric; and it has a built-in within-room baseline. It is, in short, the one reading in this design that a skeptical methodologist would take seriously without argument — and the article makes a strong claim about it ("one of the pilot's cleanest traces") while giving it none of the apparatus that would let the claim be checked. That is an overclaim and an underclaim in the same sentence.

**Why it matters.** The brief asked where this paper claims less than its data could carry, and this is the clearest instance. The fix would materially change what the pilot can report in June.

**Remedy (constraint-respecting).** Three changes, all free. (a) Operationalize "settled" now, before the year, in a form a scribe can apply with a watch: I would propose the elapsed time from the leader's opening word to the first uninterrupted stretch of some pre-stated length in which no one is moving, arriving, or speaking outside the liturgy — the exact rule matters less than that it be written down in advance and printed on the teaching pages with the other worked examples. (b) Take it *every week*, not only at re-entries. The scribe is already present and already timing; twenty-two values instead of three converts three isolated points into a series with roughly nineteen ordinary weeks against which the three post-break values can be read. That is a genuine interrupted-time-series structure and it is the only design in this pilot capable of supporting a claim about trend. (c) Give it a row in Table 3 with a direction and a threshold — for example, that the third re-entry's settle time is not lower than the first, read against the ordinary-week series. Nothing here touches a constraint: no participant fills anything, no permission is required, no target is announced, no paperwork exceeds one page.

## MEDIUM

### 14. The practice-survival threshold conditions on an attendance filter that biases toward the hypothesis, and at this n a one-person change passes it. (MEDIUM)

Table 3 row 3's disconfirmer: "The practice-survival count at the third break no higher than at the first, among participants present for two-thirds of sessions or more."

The filter is defined by whole-year attendance and applied retrospectively to both counts, which means the autumn count is purified in advance of everyone who later drifted away. Since drifting away and letting a practice lapse are the same underlying tendency, this systematically raises the fall baseline's floor by removing precisely the people who would have depressed the spring number. The design's own honest observation that attrition is unread compounds this. Second, "no higher" makes any increase a pass; with six to fifteen participants, minus prototype members, minus those below the attendance filter, a change from four to five confirms the prediction.

**Remedy.** Report the counts three ways: filtered as specified, unfiltered, and with prototype-origin participants excluded, with the denominators printed. Replace "no higher" with a stated minimum increment, or — better at this n — commit in advance to reporting the count as a description rather than as a passed condition, which is the honest thing a case of this size can support and costs the paper nothing it actually had.

### 15. The pre/post design assumes measurement invariance across an intervention built to change what the items mean. (MEDIUM)

The post-series survey "repeat[s] the baseline items so before can face after." But the year is designed to change the respondent's understanding of the very things the items ask about. A participant asked in September how often they practice, and whether anyone outside the family knows their interior life, answers from September's notion of "practice" and "knows." By May, twenty-two Wednesdays have taught them what costly telling actually is, and the same honest person may rate themselves lower on a standard that has risen. This is ordinary response shift, and it can make a successful year look like a null or negative result.

**Remedy.** Add a retrospective-then item to the post-series page: alongside "how often do you practice now," ask the respondent to rate, from where they stand now, how often they were practicing last September. One extra line, on a page that already exists, within the one-page rule. It gives the design a second and better-anchored contrast and it is the standard remedy in the intervention-measurement literature.

### 16. The year's only genuine manipulation is not preregistered. (MEDIUM)

"Two ordinary Wednesdays are run with the founder absent and no substitute for him, the same counts taken. If the room only works when he is in it, that is better learned in November than in year three."

This is the best piece of causal reasoning in the paper and the only place the design deliberately intervenes on a rival explanation rather than naming it. It appears in prose, has no row in Table 3, no stated prediction, no threshold, and no placement rule — and n=2 with unstated placement is easy to place favorably, whether or not anyone means to.

**Remedy.** Give it a row in Table 3 with a prediction and a threshold on readings the design already takes (settle time, the sign marks, participation breadth). Fix the two weeks now, by date, and place them so that neither is a re-entry week, neither is adjacent to a break, and neither is a teen-led second running — otherwise the two effects confound. Preregistering this costs nothing and is the single change that would most raise a methodologist's estimate of the paper.

### 17. The surprise line is the design's only data stream not generated by its own hypothesis, and it is given no reporting commitment. (MEDIUM)

"[A] line only surprise can answer (what did not go as expected tonight, and what the leader did in the moment)... A working theory shows itself most clearly where the plan failed to hold, which is why that line is there; 'none' is a legitimate answer."

The reasoning is right and the article then does nothing with it. Twenty-two weeks of that line is a corpus of anomalies produced by the only mechanism in the design that is not looking for what the design expects — which, given the author's own concession that "Nothing in the design is positioned to notice what all of us take for granted," makes it disproportionately valuable.

**Remedy.** Commit in advance to reporting the surprise corpus in full alongside the results, and add a Table 3 row on the team's own attentiveness: if "none" is recorded in more than some pre-stated fraction of sessions, the team has stopped seeing, which is a finding about the instrument's operators and belongs in the record. This is free and it strengthens the paper's answer to its own designer-independence problem.

### 18. The three signs are asserted to be one construct with no plan to check it. (MEDIUM)

"[T]hree vital signs, three readings of one underlying construct, confidence in God under the load of ordinary weeks." That is a strong structural claim and the design will collect the data that bears on it — three marks per session across twenty-two sessions — without ever asking whether they cohere. If the three signs are readings of one thing, they should move together; if the room's stray-seeking climbs while its telling stays flat, the construct claim is wrong or the domain restriction has fractured it.

**Remedy.** State in advance that the three weekly series will be reported side by side and that their agreement or divergence will be described. No statistic is required at this n, and none should be claimed; a plotted picture and an honest paragraph would do it. This is the cheapest construct-validity evidence available and the design is already collecting it.

### 19. No rule says which series answers which condition, and Table 3's rows are calibrated inconsistently. (MEDIUM)

The article establishes two parallel records — "The raw weekly series is reported beside the promoted rungs, since the ratchet serves the room and the series serves the record" — and then never says which one each condition is read against. Row 1's disconfirmer ("Any sign still marked not yet in its first form in February") appears to be a raw mark; nothing states this. In June the choice will be available, which is exactly the latitude preregistration exists to remove.

The calibration is also uneven. Row 1 as written can be tripped by a single February week — a flu week, a snow week, a funeral week — while row 6's disconfirmer, "A year in which nothing was ever stopped, softened, or withheld on the covenant's account," is passed by one line in a log the team writes itself. One hair trigger and one near-unfailable condition sit in the same table.

**Remedy.** Name the series for each row. Restate row 1 as a settled state rather than a single mark ("a sign whose modal first-form reading across February is not yet"). Give row 6 a form that could actually cost something: pre-commit that the decisions log's covenant-invoked entries will be reported in full, with dates and reasons, so the condition is answered by a published list rather than by the existence of one entry.

### 20. The weep test screens for cruelty, not for self-service, which is the failure mode this design actually faces. (MEDIUM)

"[A] closing personal test, measure nothing you will not weep over. This weep test may be the most radical suggestion of all."

I think it is a good rule and I would adopt it. But it is a test on the measurer's affect, and affect is a poor guard exactly where this design is weakest. A loving shepherd will not weep over a reading that confirms what he hopes for the room he loves. The weep test will stop him from collecting something invasive or humiliating; it will not stop him from marking *seen* on a Wednesday when the evidence was thin, and the article has already conceded that the same team reads the signs, hears the self-read, and receives the pages, so "agreement among them proves less than it looks."

**Remedy.** Add an inverse test in the covenant's own genre and voice — something to the effect of *record nothing you would be relieved not to record*, or, put as a question a Companion can ask at the moment of marking, *would I still write this mark if it broke the design?* One line, in the instrument the design already uses for exactly this purpose, aimed at the failure mode the existing line does not cover.

## LOW

### 21. "At almost no marginal cost" is not what this design costs. (LOW)

The abstract claims the practices "generate the multi-source, level-matched measurement the theory requires, at almost no marginal cost." Against the article's own numbers: one debrief page per session at roughly ten minutes, times twenty-two; a forty-five-minute team call every week, times twenty-two, times two to four adults — on the order of fifty to seventy person-hours of team time before a single session is led; individual permission conversations with every family; a quarterly outside reader at two hours each; and a scribe taking verbatim notes through every self-read. That is a modest cost for what it buys, and I would defend it as such. It is not almost none, and readers deciding whether their ministry can carry this deserve the real figure.

**Remedy.** Replace the phrase with the actual annual time budget, stated in the abstract or early in the measurement section. The honest number is a better argument for the design than the vague one, because it is small enough to be persuasive and specific enough to be trusted.

## Closing note to the author

I want to name what I think is at stake in Findings 3 and 4, because they are the ones I would not let go in a second round.

This design's deepest instinct is right: that a room which learns to tell the truth becomes, in the same motion, a room that can see. But the design as written can only see the truth-telling of people whose year went reasonably well. It has a rung for the person who lapsed and confessed it, and none for the person who did not lapse and heard nothing. It schedules a load test and then schedules school holidays. It has a week for doubt and no week for grief. And the person for whom none of this is provided — the mother in her second year of a silence she has told no one about, the teenager whose faith went dark in October — will produce, every week, the same three marks a bored room produces, and the instrument will call it *not yet*.

The author's own governing sentence is "measure nothing you will not weep over." I would add its companion: build no instrument that cannot see the person you would weep for. Both remedies I have proposed for that — a fourth mark, and a second face on the top rung of costly telling — cost a line each and break none of the seven constraints. I think they would make this a better instrument, and I am fairly sure they would make it a truer one.
