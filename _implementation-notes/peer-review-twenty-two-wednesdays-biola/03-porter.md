# Stage C Review — "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart"

**Reviewer:** Steven L. Porter — editor, *Journal of Spiritual Formation and Soul Care*; philosophy and theology of spiritual formation.
**Two hats.** Each finding below is tagged **[EDITOR]** or **[PHILOSOPHER]**. The editorial hat asks what this journal can responsibly do with this manuscript. The philosophical hat asks whether the inferences hold.

---

## Editorial recommendation

**REVISE AND RESUBMIT — held, resequenced, and reclassified. I will not send this out for external review in its present form.**

Three things are true at once, and they point the same direction.

First, this manuscript is genuinely interesting and I want it in this journal. The thesis — that a curriculum built honestly for formation *is already* a measurement instrument, and that the observing community must be formed rather than recruited — is a real contribution, and the level-matched longitudinal instrument in Table 2 is the best practical idea in the paper. The author's willingness to publish failure conditions before the year runs is exactly the disposition this field lacks. I am not looking for a way to decline this.

Second, the manuscript in its present form is not publishable at this journal for reasons that are mostly not about its quality. It cites, as *forthcoming*, a paper that is in R&R at this desk and whose fate I have not decided (Finding 1). It reports a study on minors with no ethics-review statement of any kind (Finding 2). It runs roughly 10,500 words against an article envelope of 6,000–8,000 including notes (Finding 4). And it is a protocol paper, for which this journal has no standing category (Finding 3).

Third, and this is the finding I would want the author to hear first: the instrument, across the entire range this pilot expects to measure, does not measure anything about God (Finding 10). That is not a fatal objection. It is a repairable one. But it must be repaired *before* the year runs, because a frozen observation protocol freezes the defect too.

**What I would require before external review**, in order:

1. Corrected citation status for the companion, and structural independence from it (Finding 1).
2. A human-subjects statement — IRB, IRB-equivalent, exempt determination, or a reasoned statement of why none applies — plus the assent arrangements for minors (Finding 2).
3. Repair of Table 2 so the signs reference their own construct in the rungs the pilot will actually read (Finding 10).
4. Cut to 7,000 words including notes (Finding 4, with the cut list).
5. Repair or removal of Table 3 rows 1, 2, 4, and 6 (Findings 13–15).

**Sequencing.** This paper cannot appear before, or independently of, the companion. If the companion is accepted, I would consider this one as a **shorter design-and-protocol essay** paired with it in the same issue or the next. If the companion is declined, this paper needs a different frame entirely, because everything above the level of practical detail is downstream of an argument it does not itself make. If the author would rather not wait, the honest alternative is to withhold this manuscript and submit one paper at the end of the year: design, results, and revised design together. I would read that paper eagerly. It is the stronger publication and the author's own closing sentence concedes as much — "the honest claim available at the end of this pilot will not be 'this works' but 'this ran, here is everything it recorded against the predictions it published, and here is the revised design.'" That sentence describes a paper written in June, not one written in August.

## Finding 1 — The companion is cited as *forthcoming* when it is in R&R at this desk

**Severity: HIGH · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** Three places in the manuscript describe the companion article as published-in-waiting:

> Bibliography: "[Author]. 'Measuring Spiritual Maturity in Hearing God: An Affective Domain Taxonomy for Individual and Small-Group Internalization.' *Journal of Spiritual Formation and Soul Care*, **forthcoming**."

and identically in footnotes 1 and 8. In Chicago and SBL usage, *forthcoming* means accepted and scheduled. The companion is in revise-and-resubmit. It is not accepted. I have not decided it. Neither has the author.

This is not a pedantic point about citation form, because the citation is load-bearing at least six times, and in each case the companion is doing warrant work this paper does not do for itself:

> "A companion article, to which this one is a sequel, argued that existing instruments... cluster at the lower levels of Krathwohl's affective taxonomy because self-report meets a structural ceiling."

> "The companion article argued that adequate measurement is domain-indexed: a profile, not a score."

> "it is also, deliberately, the group-level measurement the companion article proposed under the name of the leadership absence test."

> "It is seen, as the companion article argued, in the growth the group itself recognizes in its individual members."

**Why it matters.** Two distinct problems. (a) *Publication ethics.* A reader who cannot see the review file will read "forthcoming" as "this has cleared peer review at a journal," and will therefore not press on the ceiling argument, the domain-indexing argument, or the leadership-absence-test proposal — all of which are still under contest. The manuscript borrows credibility it has not yet earned. (b) *Editorial hazard.* R&R means the companion is expected to change. If the author revises the taxonomy's status, or the ceiling argument, or the community-as-instrument move in response to review, this paper's citations will point at claims that no longer exist in that form, and I will have published a sequel to a paper that was never written.

**Remedy.**
- Change all three instances to "under review at *Journal of Spiritual Formation and Soul Care*." If the companion is accepted before this one goes to press, the citation is corrected at proof.
- More substantively: make this paper survive the companion's decline. Add one tight subsection (300–400 words, funded from the cuts in Finding 4) stating the ceiling argument *and its warrant* in this paper's own voice — the Vazire self–other knowledge asymmetry material in footnote 15 is already doing this work and deserves to be in the body. Then the cross-references become "as argued at greater length in [under review]" rather than load-bearing premises.
- Add an author's note declaring the relationship of the two submissions. This journal needs to see the pair.

## Finding 2 — No ethics review is named anywhere, in a study on minors that retains named records

**Severity: HIGH · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** Searching the manuscript for *IRB*, *institutional review*, *human subjects*, *ethics board*, and *assent* returns nothing. Not one occurrence. What the paper offers in that space is an internal instrument:

> "The team's covenant, signed before the first instrument runs, binds everyone with access to any of it: mirrors, never levers; no scores; consent and daylight; names stop at the shepherd who knows the faces; and a closing personal test, measure nothing you will not weep over."

and it tells us plainly who wrote and signed it:

> "the covenant was written by the designer and signed by a team he selected and formed."

Meanwhile the design does all of the following to minors: keeps named longitudinal records ("Names appear on the team's side only... in a single secured location"; "the team keeps named notes about participants across the year"), collects five self-report pages, records verbatim speech ("The co-companion scribes the answers verbatim"), and reports the aggregate in a peer-reviewed journal.

**Why it matters.** This is a publication requirement, not a methodological preference. I cannot publish results from research involving children without a statement of ethics review or a documented determination that none was required, and I should not send a protocol out for review without knowing that the protocol will clear that bar when the results arrive. This is also the one finding where the author's greatest strength works against him: the covenant is a *better* ethical instrument than most IRB paperwork, and precisely because it is better, it is tempting to treat it as sufficient. It is not sufficient, for a reason the author already knows and states about his own design — an internally written, internally signed instrument cannot notice "what all of us take for granted."

I want to be precise about what I am *not* saying. I am not saying the covenant is inadequate protection. I am not saying the design is unsafe; the safeguarding architecture described (two-adult rule, mandatory reporting named in advance, a settle protocol, an adult outside the team reachable by any participant) is more careful than most church programs and considerably more careful than most published formation research. I am saying the paper is silent on external review, and silence on that point is disqualifying for publication regardless of how good the internal arrangements are.

**Remedy.** Add a short methods paragraph, before external review, stating:
- Whether an IRB or IRB-equivalent reviewed the protocol, and its determination; or, if the pilot was undertaken as ministry rather than research, who made that determination and on what ground — noting that the intent to publish generally converts it.
- The host school's own approval and the authority under which it was given.
- Parental consent *and* the minors' assent, as separate items. The paper currently has a good assent instinct — "a parent's yes opens the door without closing the teen's: a young person who would rather not fill a page does not have to" — but never names it as assent or says how a teen's refusal is registered without the room seeing it.
- Retention and destruction: how long the named notes live, who holds them after the pilot, and what happens to them if a family leaves.

None of this violates any of the seven constraints. It is disclosure, not new apparatus.

## Finding 3 — This journal has no slot for a protocol paper, and the manuscript does not argue for one

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** The manuscript knows it is unusual — "This article is a field report offered before the field has reported" — and defends the choice by appeal to preregistration:

> "The academic literature calls publishing a design and its failure conditions before the results 'preregistration,' and commends it not as a guard against dishonesty but because it separates what was predicted from what was discovered and limits the choices a researcher can make after seeing the data."

That is a correct account of preregistration's rationale. It is not an argument that a preregistration belongs in a print theology journal as an article. In the fields where registered reports work, the register is a repository (OSF, AsPredicted) and the journal's role is *in-principle acceptance* — a two-stage review with a commitment to publish the results whatever they show. This journal has neither the machinery nor the standing category. Our categories are research articles, theological and theoretical essays, and shorter reflective pieces.

**Why it matters.** If I publish this as an article, I create a precedent I have no policy for, and I owe the author nothing at the back end: he will submit the results next June into an ordinary review queue with no commitment from me, which is the worst of both worlds — he has spent his preregistration in a venue that gave him no in-principle acceptance in return.

**Remedy.** Three options, in my order of preference.
1. **Best for the author.** Register the protocol properly (OSF is free and time-stamped, which is what the falsifiability claim actually needs), and submit one paper in June: design, predictions, results against them, revised design. The paper's own closing sentence already describes this paper.
2. **Publishable now.** Reclassify as a *theological-methodological essay* of ~6,500 words whose thesis is the conceptual one — that forming and observing are one activity, and that the observing community must be formed — using the pilot as the worked illustration rather than as the subject. This is a real contribution and it does not depend on results. It requires the cuts in Finding 4 and it requires that Table 3 stop being a preregistration and start being a worked example of *how* a formation program can state its own failure conditions, which is a more useful contribution to this readership anyway.
3. **Least preferred.** I open a "Protocols and Designs" category. I am not willing to do that for a single manuscript, and I would not do it without a two-stage commitment I cannot presently staff.

## Finding 6 — A disconfirmation condition is read on an instrument the paper never describes, and it is the design's only named individual-level record

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** Table 3, row 2, reads its threshold on an instrument that appears nowhere else in the manuscript:

> "The where-they-differ row left empty at all four **profile reads** for more than half the participants | The four **participant profile pages**"

The word *profile* occurs exactly twice in the manuscript: here, and once in a paraphrase of the companion's argument ("a profile, not a score"). The Measurement Architecture section enumerates the instruments carefully — five family-facing pages, the weekly debrief sheet, the quarterly self-read, the outside reader's answer — and the participant profile page is not among them. Table 1 schedules the year's measurement occasions week by week and contains no profile reads. The reader cannot tell what these pages contain, when the four reads happen, who writes them, who sees them, or how long they are kept.

This matters more than an ordinary documentation gap for three compounding reasons.

1. These are the design's **individual-level, named records on minors**. The paper is explicit that individual tracking happens — "each person's progress from wherever they began is tracked through what can be seen across the year" — and equally explicit that "Names appear on the team's side only." The profile page is evidently where that lives.
2. The paper stakes a claim on total instrument transparency: "every card and instrument this article mentions" is published openly. This article mentions the participant profile page. Is it published? The reader has no way to know.
3. The paper's stated epistemic modesty — "nothing here establishes an individual's level; the claims available at year's end are about the room" — sits uneasily beside a per-participant page whose entire function is to compare a named person's self-report against a named person's observed behavior, four times a year. Both statements may be reconcilable. The paper never reconciles them.

**Why it matters.** An editor cannot evaluate an ethics arrangement he cannot see, and this is the instrument closest to the ethical bone. It is also the instrument on which a disconfirmation condition is read, which means Table 3 currently contains a row that a reader cannot check.

**Remedy.** One paragraph in the Measurement Architecture: what the profile page holds, who writes it, the four occasions (add them to Table 1), who may read it, retention and destruction, and whether the blank template is in the published corpus. If the page exists as described, none of this costs a constraint — it is disclosure of an instrument already running, and it strengthens rather than weakens the "permission that conceals what is kept is not permission" principle the paper already holds.

## Finding 7 — Prose, Table 1, and Table 3 disagree about how many teen-led runnings the year contains, and the flagship condition may have no eligible occasions

**Severity: MEDIUM · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** Three counts, three answers.

- Prose: "at least **five** sessions across the year are second runnings led by teens."
- Table 1: teen-led runnings at wk 8, and wks 14–15. That is **three**.
- Table 3, row 4: the condition is read at "**both spring** second runnings," and only "where the teen had not led before."

Then the eligibility filter, from the body:

> "in the first year several of them have led before, through the prototype, which is why the condition attached to it is read on teens leading for the first time."

Combine these. The design's flagship group-level measure — the leadership absence test, which the paper calls the thing "no survey could elicit" — has exactly one disconfirmation condition; that condition is read at two occasions the year-map does not locate; and those occasions must be led by a first-time teen leader in a cohort of six to fifteen people that may contain four prototype teens who have already led.

**Why it matters.** It is entirely possible for this condition to report *untested* through no fault of the year — which is the outcome the frozen-protocol rule prescribes ("where a condition was written about a part of the curriculum that then changed, it is reported untested rather than passed"). But a condition that is *predictably* untestable at the time of writing is not a disconfirmation condition; it is a placeholder. The author cannot know that in June unless he counts eligible occasions in August.

**Remedy.** Before the year runs: (a) reconcile the three counts and put every teen-led running in Table 1 with its week; (b) state the expected number of *first-time* teen leaders given the enrolled cohort, and if it is fewer than two, say so now and either widen the condition (any teen leader, with prototype status recorded and reported separately) or mark row 4 as expected-untested in the first year and reserved for year two. Either is honest. Silence is not.

## Finding 8 — The question the author says the architecture stands on is deferred past the point of risk

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [EDITOR]**

**What is wrong.** The manuscript closes its penultimate section with a striking admission:

> "Who the observing companions are, how they are formed without being credentialed, and who watches the shepherds is the question the whole architecture stands on, and it deserves its own article, which is later."

I agree with the assessment. That is why the deferral will not do. The pilot is described as "now underway." The companions are already selected, already signing the covenant, already keeping named notes on other people's children, already the sole readers of every instrument in the design. The article that answers "who watches the shepherds" arrives after the year in which nobody watched them.

The paper also names, and then leaves standing, an ecclesial gap in the same territory:

> "The pilot also has no ecclesial location: it runs in a school rather than a church, the families belong to several congregations, none of which is party to the covenant or receives those this year commissions, and a formation program that sends without naming who receives has left something unsaid."

That is honestly said. But note what the year nonetheless does: week 22 is "sending, rite" and a "commissioning." The design performs a commissioning — a quasi-ecclesial act, on minors — under an authority constituted by a privately written covenant among a team the designer selected, with no receiving body and no external accountability. The author has correctly identified this as the deepest question in his architecture. He has then scheduled the act and deferred the question.

**Why it matters.** For this journal in particular. *Soul care* names a practice the church has always regulated, and one of the few things the Christian tradition is unanimous about is that the care of souls is an office held under authority, not a competence exercised on one's own recognizance. A design this careful about consent for a one-page survey, and this uncareful about the authorization of the people doing the shepherding, has its scruples in an odd order.

I want to keep the claim narrow, because there is a good rejoinder. Deferring the companion-formation material is entirely defensible as **article scope** — it is a large subject and it would swamp this paper. My objection is that the paper treats "it deserves its own article" as an answer to a different question, namely **who is accountable this year**. Those are not the same question, and the second one has an answer the author already possesses. The paper tells us so:

> "The pilot's handbook carries a developed architecture for this, selection that looks for faithful and able, a clear agreement spoken before anyone joins, a rung-by-rung development path from receiving to leading, and a rule that every companion stands under a named covering of their own."

**Remedy.** Three sentences, not a section, added where the deferral currently stands: name the actual covering in force during this pilot. Who does each Companion answer to by name of office? Who can remove a Companion mid-year, and on what grounds? What happens to the notes if a Companion is removed? And one sentence on the commissioning: who, if anyone, receives those commissioned, or an explicit statement that the rite is a blessing and not a sending, which would resolve the whole difficulty at the cost of one word. This adds ~120 words, violates no constraint, and closes the paper's own most serious admission.

## Finding 10 — In the range the pilot expects to measure, no vital sign refers to God

**Severity: HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The paper names the construct the three signs measure:

> "The companion team watches three vital signs, three readings of one underlying construct, **confidence in God under the load of ordinary weeks**."

Now read the operationalizations in Table 2 against that construct, rung by rung:

| Sign | First form | Growing form | Full form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Costly telling | "when invited, people say something real, however small" | "something brought unprompted, and an honest report of a lapsed practice" | "Telling what **God** said and what obeying it cost" |
| Response to load | "the room lands when the container opens" | "a late arrival or an awkward moment taken in stride" | "Real load carried toward **God** in the room's first ten seconds" |
| The sought stray | "pointed toward someone, somebody goes" | "the missing and the quiet named unprompted" | "someone goes after a person gone quiet, unasked" |

God appears in exactly two of the nine cells, both of them full forms. The third sign never refers to God at any rung, including its full form.

Now set that against the year's stated target:

> "The year aims at the group's level two to three threshold... What lies past it is prepared for, prayed for, and never claimed, and its absence at year's end is not read as failure."

and against the paper's own account of when the full forms become readable:

> "those full forms are high-level competencies, and a young room will not show them for months."

The conclusion follows directly and is checkable line by line: **across the entire range this pilot expects to read, the instrument contains no theological content whatever.** The signs that will actually be marked, week after week, for twenty-two weeks, are: people answer when asked; the room settles; someone goes when pointed; a lapse gets admitted; an awkward moment is absorbed; the quiet get named. Every one of these is a marker of a well-functioning small group. Not one of them distinguishes a room in which God is being heard from a room in which nobody has mentioned God all year.

**Why it matters, and why a methods panel would not file this.** A methods reviewer files this as construct validity and asks for convergent evidence. That is not what is at stake. What is at stake is that the paper's whole argument is that a *formation* curriculum, honestly built, is *already* the measurement instrument the theory needs. If the instrument reads group health, then what the paper has demonstrated is that a formation curriculum honestly built is already a group-health instrument — which is true, valuable, and a much smaller claim than the one on the title page. The author cannot get from "the room is healthy" to "the room is climbing a ladder of internalization *in the domain of hearing and obeying God*" on these readings, because the readings would be identical in a well-run secular grief group, a good AA meeting, or a strong high-school drama ensemble.

The paper very nearly sees this. The conclusion contains the sentence: "belonging is not the point, since a room can be a beloved community and still not be hearing and obeying." Exactly so. But that sentence indicts Table 2, and the paper does not notice.

**Remedy — and it is cheap, and it costs no constraint.** The design already refuses to record *what* was heard ("the content of hearing is never recorded, only that hearing was told"), and I am not asking it to. That refusal is defensible and I would not disturb it. But recording *that the telling had God as its object* is not recording content. The design already does something adjacent on the debrief sheet: "the sheet will record whether telling happened, whether any of it cost anything, and what the room did with the costliest one." One more field, one word wide, and the first and growing forms can carry their own construct:

- **Costly telling, first form:** "when invited, people say something real *about what they heard or failed to hear*, however small."
- **Costly telling, growing form:** unchanged in substance — but mark whether the lapse reported is a lapse *in the practice of listening*, which it already is by design.
- **Response to load, growing form:** "an awkward moment taken in stride" → note whether the room's absorption included any turning toward God (a prayer, a naming), which is a binary observation requiring no content.
- **The sought stray:** this sign needs the most work, because it has no theological content at any rung. Its growing form could specify *what* is noticed: not merely that someone is quiet, but that someone has stopped telling. That is the sign's own domain, it is observable, and it distinguishes the pastoral care of a small group from the specific attentiveness this curriculum exists to form.

One page of the pre-printed pack changes. Constraint 5 (one page per week) survives untouched. Constraint 4 (journals never collected) survives untouched. And the frozen-protocol rule makes this urgent: after week 1, this becomes unfixable for the year.

## Finding 11 — Adding observers does not resolve the ambiguity that motivates the project; only absence does, and the cleanest absence test is measured by self-report

**Severity: HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The paper's founding premise is stated in the introduction:

> "self-report meets a structural ceiling: **above the middle levels, social compliance and transformed character produce the same observable behavior**, and the heart is opaque even to its owner (Jer 17:9)."

And the proposed solution:

> "It is resolved only when self-report is joined by an observing community, which functions both as the multi-source instrument capable of seeing above the ceiling..."

Set these side by side. If compliance and character produce *the same observable behavior*, then observers of behavior — however many, however loving, however well trained — observe the same thing in both cases. A community watching ambiguous behavior does not disambiguate it. It produces a multi-source reading of an ambiguity. The number of witnesses is not the relevant variable; the identity of the observable is.

The paper has the resource to answer this, and states it once, in passing, about a different problem:

> "What separates training from echo is **time and absence**, and the year schedules both: practices that survive a break nobody supervises, a teen-led night no adult carries, and a stray sought by someone nobody pointed."

That is the right answer, and it is the argument the paper should be making everywhere. Compliance requires a structure to comply with; remove the structure and what persists is not compliance. This is a genuine discriminator, it is philosophically respectable, and the design has built three of them.

The trouble is that the paper never elevates this to an inference rule, and its instruments do not honor it. Look where the weekly readings are actually taken: "Every circle"; "Every opening"; "testimony nights carry the full form"; "the room lands when the container opens." These are readings taken *inside* the liturgy, in the leader's presence, under the container, on the occasions the curriculum schedules for exactly that behavior. They are maximally compliance-conducive by construction. "Settling: the room lands when the container opens" is, considered as evidence, a measurement of compliance with a liturgy — which is a perfectly good thing to measure and precisely not evidence above the ceiling.

**And then the sharpest problem.** Of the three absence tests, break survival is the one the paper calls its cleanest trace:

> "one practice per week makes practice survival across breaks a **clean, countable trace**."

It is the basis of Table 3, row 3. Where does the count come from? From the narrated Week 7:

> "The re-entry check-in asks how the break treated everyone's practice, and honest answers include lapsed ones."

The design's cleanest behavioral trace is gathered by **asking people, out loud, in the circle, in front of their parents and the leader** — which is self-report, the very instrument the companion article declared invalid above the middle levels, delivered under maximal social pressure.

And the pressure is not simple. The room has been carefully trained to reward the *admission* of lapse:

> "'Thinned out over the break,' spoken aloud without flinching, is an offering-level tell, and the team marks the sign seen."

So a participant who reports lapsing earns a rung-promotion credit on costly telling. A participant who reports practicing earns a survival count. Both directions of the answer are rewarded, by different instruments, in the same breath. This is not a claim that anyone lies. It is a claim that the response is not diagnostic: whatever a participant says, some instrument in the room reads it as growth.

**Why it matters.** Row 3 of Table 3 is one of only six disconfirmation conditions, and it is the one the paper most trusts. If its input is compromised, the paper's falsifiability is thinner than it presents.

**Remedy.**
1. **State the inference rule explicitly**, in one paragraph in the Measurement Architecture: *what licenses the move above the ceiling is not multiplicity of observers but withdrawal of the structure that would produce compliance.* Then classify every reading in Table 2 as structure-present or structure-absent, and say plainly that structure-present readings corroborate and structure-absent readings discriminate. The paper already applies exactly this discipline to the trust vocabulary ("The vocabulary corroborates; it does not decide"), so this is the author's own move applied where he has not applied it.
2. **Re-base row 3 on the physical trace the design already collects.** The Watching Progress section already counts "how many walked back in with the return card." That is an artifact, not a claim. Make the return-card count the disconfirmation metric and let the spoken check-in corroborate. This respects constraint 4 exactly — the card is counted, never inspected — and it costs nothing to gather because the count is already being taken.
3. **Separate the two credits.** Do not let the same utterance in the re-entry check-in score both costly-telling and practice-survival. Record the lapse admission on the sign sheet and the survival on the card count, from different sources.

## Finding 12 — The one outcome the paper says it designed for is the state its own cited sources name as the counterfeit

**Severity: HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** From "Watching Progress":

> "by spring the cohort will show **the one outcome I actually designed for**, which no instrument elicits but every instrument serves: people who have tasted what a room like this is and have become **unwilling to go back to rooms without it**. That self-recognition is not a side effect. **It is the level-three transition, intrinsic valuing displacing external structure, happening in public.**"

And the conclusion makes it the year's culminating evidence:

> "A room that ends its year able to look into that mirror and say 'these are my people, and I am unwilling to go back', and able to say how it knows, **has given the theory its data**."

Two objects are being identified here. The first is *a preference for this room over other rooms*. The second is *intrinsic valuing of hearing and obeying God*. These are different objects, and the inference from the first to the second is the largest unargued step in the paper.

That people who experience a warm, high-disclosure, well-facilitated group become unwilling to return to cold ones is one of the best-attested findings about small groups, and it obtains whether or not the group's nominal content is doing any work. The paper cites the source that documents it. Footnote 11:

> "On the tendency of church small groups to drift toward comfortable mutual support absent load, Robert Wuthnow, *Sharing the Journey*."

And it cites the second warning too:

> "the sweetest-looking room can be the lowest-trust room in the building, its harmony maintained by hiding differences, the state one classic account names **pseudocommunity**."

So the paper names two distinct counterfeits of its designed-for outcome — Wuthnow's drift toward comfortable mutual support, and Peck's pseudocommunity — and then adopts as its target the affective state that both counterfeits produce most reliably. Worse, it adopts it as the state that *no instrument elicits but every instrument serves*, which places it beyond the reach of the disconfirmation table entirely. Table 3 has six rows and none of them tests it.

**On whether the paper's humility is real or performed.** The brief asks me to test this, and this finding is the test. The conclusion contains the correct sentence — "belonging is not the point, since a room can be a beloved community and still not be hearing and obeying." That sentence is the smaller, faithful claim the paper says it prefers. But it appears once, in a valedictory paragraph, and it is directly contradicted by the body's designed-for outcome, which is stated with emphasis ("not a side effect"), given a theoretical identification ("It *is* the level-three transition"), and made the year's summative criterion in the paper's final paragraph. When a paper says the smaller claim twice and acts on the larger claim throughout, the humility is real as a conviction and unimplemented as a discipline. That is not hypocrisy; it is the ordinary condition of a designer who loves what he built. But it is the thing review is for.

**Remedy.** Two changes, both small, neither touching a constraint.
1. **Sever the identification.** Delete "It is the level-three transition, intrinsic valuing displacing external structure, happening in public." Replace with the ambiguity, stated: *unwillingness to return to rooms without this one is the outcome I hoped for pastorally, and it is also what a merely warm room produces; it is therefore not evidence of internalization on its own, and the design does not treat it as such.*
2. **Restate the target in the paper's own domain.** The defensible version of the same hope is not "unwilling to go back to rooms without it" but *unwilling to go back to a life in which no one ever asks what God said.* That is a claim about the person's relation to the practice rather than to the room, it survives the group ending, and it is the thing the author actually wants. It also becomes checkable at the one place the design has real leverage — the teens leaving for college dormitories, which the paper already names as "the year's longest target."

## Finding 13 — Table 3, row 1 is disconfirmable only by program collapse, and the ratchet makes it monotone by construction

**Severity: HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** Row 1:

> "**The ladder never climbs** | First forms steady by the end of the first quarter, growing forms by midyear, full forms at least occasionally by spring | **Any sign still marked not yet in its first form in February, or no growing form recorded all year**"

Take the disjunction's two arms in turn.

*First arm.* What are the first forms? From Table 2: people say something real when invited; the room lands when the container opens; somebody goes when pointed. For a sign to be "still marked not yet in its first form in February," a room would have to have failed, five months in, to produce any instance of people answering when asked, or of settling when the meeting starts, or of anyone going to help when directed. A cohort in that state has not failed a measurement threshold; it has ceased to exist as a group, and the pilot would have been stopped in October. A condition that can be met only by catastrophe does not discriminate between the hypothesis and its negation.

*Second arm.* "No growing form recorded all year" requires zero instances across twenty-two weeks of: an honest report of a lapsed practice; a late arrival taken in stride; the quiet named unprompted. In a room with three-to-six families and a liturgy that explicitly asks about lapses at every re-entry, the first of these is nearly certain by week 7.

*And the ratchet.* The marking rule:

> "promotes a rung after **three consecutive weeks of seen**"

with, as the paper itself concedes in the limitations, "no path back down." So once a sign is promoted out of its first form in, say, October, it can never again be "marked not yet in its first form," and the first arm of row 1 is permanently unavailable for that sign. Three good weeks in the autumn immunize the year.

**A secondary defect: the threshold's referent is ambiguous.** "Any sign still marked not yet in its first form in February" can mean (a) the room's standing rung for that sign is below first form in February, or (b) some weekly mark in February reads not yet. These give different verdicts — under the ratchet, (a) is nearly unreachable while (b) is nearly certain, since any single bad week produces a not-yet mark. A preregistered threshold cannot carry that ambiguity into June.

**Why it matters.** Row 1 is the condition that tests the paper's central extension — the taxonomy used as a ladder that groups ascend. The paper stakes its intellectual honesty on that extension being falsifiable: "it is made deliberately, and the disconfirmation conditions below are written so that it can fail." Row 1 is where that promise is kept or broken, and as written it is broken.

**Remedy.** Replace a threshold that only collapse can trigger with one a merely disappointing year can trigger. Suggested:
- Disambiguate to the standing-rung reading, and state it.
- Add a downward rule: three consecutive weeks of *not yet* on a sign with occasions present demotes a rung. The paper's own limitations paragraph identifies the absence of one as a flaw; adopting it costs nothing and makes the series interpretable.
- Set a real threshold: *at least two of the three signs standing at growing form by week 12, and at least one instance of any full form logged by week 18.* That is a prediction that a mediocre year genuinely fails, which is what a disconfirmation condition is for.

## Finding 14 — Rows 2 and 6 are satisfied by near-certainties, one of which the paper reports as already past

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** Two rows of Table 3 have thresholds that a team knowing the threshold will meet without effort.

*Row 2, "The sources never disagree."* The failure condition is "the where-they-differ row left empty at all four profile reads for more than half the participants." This tests whether a row on a form got filled in. It is written and read by the same team that predicted disagreement, on an instrument that team designed, with no independent adjudication — a problem the paper elsewhere names precisely: "the three sources are also one perceiver, since the same team reads the signs, hears the self-read, and receives the pages, so agreement among them proves less than it looks." That concession applies with equal force to *disagreement* among them, and row 2 does not notice. There is also a deeper problem: any two imperfectly reliable measures of anything disagree somewhere. Finding gaps confirms nothing. The prediction says "**systematic, informative** gaps," which is the right standard, and then the threshold drops both adjectives.

*Row 6, "The covenant never costs anything."* The failure condition is "a year in which nothing was ever stopped, softened, or withheld on the covenant's account," read in the team's own decisions log. Two observations. First, the paper reports that this has *already* happened, twice, before the year began: "The mechanism was redesigned twice in the field... A colleague's honest reaction, and mine on reflection, was that it felt like being graded." Second, the paper predicts an event that will discharge the condition at no cost: "a page withheld on a no." At three to six families, at least one decline is close to certain, and a decline costs the team nothing it values. Row 6 is therefore effectively pre-satisfied.

**Why it matters.** Both rows produce the appearance of a passed test with no evidential content. In a paper whose distinguishing virtue is that it published its failure conditions in advance, rows that cannot fail are worse than no rows, because they inflate the count of tests survived.

**Remedy.**
- *Row 2:* raise the threshold to the prediction's own standard. Pre-specify what makes a gap *informative* — e.g., a documented case in which a discrepancy changed how the team cared for someone, logged in the decisions log with date and reason. Then the condition tests whether the discrepancy architecture did any work, which is what the design claims for it. And name an adjudicator: the quarterly outside reader can be asked one added question — "reading these four profile pages, do the recorded discrepancies look substantive to you?" — at no cost to the covenant, since profile pages could be shown de-identified.
- *Row 6:* exclude events that predate the covenant's operation and events that cost nothing. Restate as: *at least one instance in which the covenant stopped something the team wanted, recorded with what was given up.* A withheld page on a family's no does not qualify; that is the design working as designed, not the covenant biting.

## Finding 15 — Row 4 makes adult rescue of a struggling teenager the failure marker, against the covenant's own rule that care wins

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** Row 4:

> "**The container collapses in younger hands** | By each second running, the structure lives in the members enough to survive a teen leader | **An adult taking over a block the teen was leading**, at both spring second runnings..."

The recorded failure event is an act of adult intervention, and the people who decide whether to intervene are the same people who wrote the condition, know that intervening scores against the design, and will complete the debrief sheet afterward.

I want to be careful about what I am claiming, because there is an obvious rejoinder: this is a competent team under a covenant whose first commitment is that care wins, and it is uncharitable to suppose they would leave a teenager floundering to protect a data point. I agree, and I am not supposing it. My claim is narrower and it is about the instrument, not the people. It is this: **the design has created a situation in which restraint and result-protection are behaviorally identical, and the record cannot distinguish them.** An adult who holds back because the teen is finding her feet, and an adult who holds back because stepping in would mark the row, produce the same entry on the same sheet. The paper's own governing rule is "where pastoral care and measurement rigor pull against each other, care wins" — and row 4 is the one place in the design where the paper has arranged for them to pull against each other in real time, in front of a sixteen-year-old, with no way to record which won.

Compounding it: the observation is made by adults with no independent witness, in a room where every other person present is a participant, and the event is a non-event — something that did not happen.

**Why it matters.** This is a formation problem before it is a measurement problem, which is why I file it here rather than as a methods complaint. The teen-led night is described as an apprenticeship — "Entrusting leadership now, with a full team present and a family helping, is the apprenticeship for leading later, when no adult will be down the hall." An apprenticeship in which the master has been given a reason not to step in is not the apprenticeship the paper describes.

**Remedy.** Decouple the reading from the intervention.
- Record the container's four conditions (safe, present, clear, intentional) as *held* or *not held* through the teen-led block, judged on the room's behavior rather than on the adults' restraint. That is the construct row 4 actually cares about — whether the structure lives in the members.
- Log every adult step-in in the decisions log with its reason, and state in advance that a step-in taken for the teen's sake is *not* counted as disconfirming. That preserves "care wins" explicitly rather than by hope.
- Drop the conjunction. Requiring failure "at both spring second runnings" means one adequate night rescues the condition; with possibly only one or two eligible occasions (Finding 7), the conjunction may make the row unfalsifiable outright.

## Finding 16 — Row 5 is conceded uninterpretable in the confirming direction, and the paper leaves it in the table anyway

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** Row 5:

> "**The trust vocabulary does not migrate** | Unprompted language moving from held toward owned across the three self-reads | **The third self-read carrying no more known or owned language than the first, while sign readings advance**"

The paper then concedes, in the body, that the positive result is uninterpretable:

> "all three house words can be heard along a single dimension of felt warmth, so vocabulary by itself cannot separate a room that calculates from a room that predicts from a room that identifies, and **a room that merely grows warmer will appear to migrate**."

That concession is admirable and I want to credit it. But notice what it does to the row. If warmth alone produces the appearance of migration, then migration is not evidence for internalization, and the row can only ever return *disconfirmed* or *no information*. The paper's repair — "The stages are therefore read first from behavior the year already schedules... The vocabulary corroborates; it does not decide" — is the right move, and it means the vocabulary should not be carrying a disconfirmation condition of its own at all. Corroborating evidence does not settle questions; the paper says so.

Two further problems compound it. The coding is done by the team, in house words the team invented ("held / known / owned"), from notes taken by the team's own scribe, with — as the limitations concede — "no blind coding, no second coder, and no inter-rater check." And the conjunct "while sign readings advance" means that in a year where the signs do not advance, row 5 returns nothing, so a globally poor year disconfirms less than a mixed one.

**Why it matters.** Row 5 is the only test of the paper's most interesting theoretical proposal — "The identification stage may be internalization by another name, visible at group scale; that equation is this project's proposal rather than the literature's finding, and the disconfirmation condition below is written to break it." The author has correctly identified this as a proposal requiring a test, and then supplied a test that cannot break it.

**Remedy.**
- Either move row 5's threshold onto the behavioral reads the paper says decide the question — *at the third self-read, no instance of a member predicting how another will react and no instance of anyone acting on another's behalf unasked* — or withdraw row 5 and state that the vocabulary claim is not tested this year. Withdrawal is honest and costs nothing; a corroborating instrument that is honest about corroborating is worth more than a failed test.
- If the row stays, get a second coder. This is the one place where the "no outside coder" limitation is cheaply repairable without touching the covenant: the self-read is spoken aloud in the room by design, it contains no confidential disclosure, and its verbatim notes could be coded by an outsider against the three house words with no access to anything the covenant seals.
- Drop the conjunct.

## Finding 17 — "Internalization" carries three unequal extensions; only one is declared, and the undeclared ones are the load-bearing ones

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The paper makes one declaration:

> "Krathwohl's affective taxonomy is a classification of educational objectives ordered by the principle of internalization, and it was never validated as a developmental stage model. This project uses it as a ladder that persons and groups ascend across a school year, which is an extension past what the taxonomy established; it is made deliberately, and the disconfirmation conditions below are written so that it can fail."

That declaration covers one extension: objectives-classification → developmental ladder. Good. But three distinct moves are being made, and the declaration names only the first.

**Extension (i), declared:** objectives → developmental ladder. Fine, and Table 3 row 1 is meant to test it (see Finding 13).

**Extension (ii), undeclared: individual → group.** The declaration slips "and groups" in as though it rode along. It does not. Principle two then treats it as settled — "measure the group and the person on separate ladders... a room begins at the bottom of its own" — and specifies the year's target in person-predicates:

> "The year aims at the group's level two to three threshold, **where intrinsic motivation begins to take over from external structure**."

*Intrinsic motivation* is a property of a psyche. A room does not have one. Either the phrase is shorthand for an aggregate over the persons in the room — in which case the individual ladder is doing the work, and the paper has disclaimed individual-level findings ("nothing here establishes an individual's level; the claims available at year's end are about the room") — or it names a distinct group-level construct, which needs its own theory and has none. The paper's operationalizations (three vital signs, trust vocabulary) measure group-level properties that are well theorized elsewhere: psychological safety, cohesion, trust stage. None of those is internalization. The paper is borrowing a person-predicate to name a group-property and letting the taxonomy's authority follow the word across.

**Extension (iii), undeclared and self-refuting: Organization redefined.** Here the paper does something remarkable and says it plainly:

> "where the taxonomy's Organization names the ordering of competing values into a system, this curriculum reads it as the corporate turn, a body learning to hear together. Restricting the whole ladder to a single domain, hearing and obeying God, is what makes that reading workable and **is also what removes the competing values the taxonomy's own fourth level assumes.**"

Read that twice. Organization *is*, by Krathwohl's definition, the ordering of competing values. The design removes competing values. What remains at level 4 is therefore not Organization; it is a different thing wearing the name. The paper does not argue for the substitution; it reports the removal as an incidental benefit of domain restriction.

**Why it matters, and why this is the Biola desk rather than the methods panel.** Removing competing values is not merely a taxonomic irregularity. It removes what the Christian tradition has always taken formation to *be about*. Augustine's *ordo amoris* is not one topic within formation; it is the account of what formation is — the reordering of rival loves. James K. A. Smith, whom this paper cites twice and leans on for its third principle ("human beings are formed less by what they are taught to think than by what they are repeatedly trained to love"), makes rival liturgies the center of his argument: the mall and the stadium are forming the same person the church is forming, and the contest between them is the subject. This paper cites Smith for the mechanism and drops Smith's problem.

Concretely, in this room: the teenager who has heard something and must weigh obeying it against what it will cost her with her friends is at the exact center of formation, and she is at level 4 by Krathwohl's own definition. This design has restricted the domain so that her conflict is never the object of measurement. That is a defensible pilot-scoping decision. It is not a defensible reading of Organization, and it should not be presented as one.

**Remedy.**
1. Declare extension (ii) as its own extension, in the same paragraph and the same honest register as extension (i). One sentence: *the taxonomy indexes internalization in persons; applying it to a room is a second extension, made deliberately, and its operationalizations are group constructs (safety, trust stage, distributed leadership) rather than internalization measured directly.* Then either give it a disconfirmation condition or say it is untested this year.
2. Stop calling extension (iii) Organization. Name it — "the corporate turn" is already the author's own good phrase — and say explicitly that it occupies the taxonomy's fourth position without being the taxonomy's fourth level, because the single-domain restriction removes the competing values that constitute it.
3. One paragraph, in the limitations, on what the single-domain restriction costs theologically: that the ordering of rival loves is what the tradition means by formation, and that a design which brackets it is measuring one strand of formation and not formation. The paper is generous with its costs everywhere else ("Each of those is a deliberate loss of evidence, taken with open eyes"); this cost belongs in that list and is currently missing from it.

## Finding 18 — Prototype previewing destroys the "unprompted" discriminator for possibly half the room, and only one condition controls for it

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The word *unprompted* carries more inferential weight in this paper than any other. It appears seven times, and each time it is the thing that converts an ordinary behavior into evidence:

- Table 2, growing form of costly telling: "something brought **unprompted**"
- Table 2, growing form of the sought stray: "the missing and the quiet named **unprompted**"
- Watching Progress: "a mistake admitted **unprompted**"
- Trust vocabulary: "the room's **unprompted** vocabulary should migrate"
- And the principle behind all of them: "**unprompted salience**, what a room spontaneously names as worth keeping, is a truer read of what it values than any answer to a supplied category."

Now the disclosure:

> "The prototype group also **previews each session before the cohort meets it**, which is how several of the early plans reached their present form."

and

> "Both parents and their four teenagers ran the practices through the prototype, three of the teens have already led sessions... and **some of them are expected to enroll** in the cohort described here."

A participant who has previewed the session is, by definition, prompted. Six previewers in a cohort of "six to fifteen participants in all" is between 40% and 100% of the room. The behaviors the design reads as spontaneous — naming the quiet, bringing something unbidden, reaching for a particular vocabulary about the room — may be modeled first by people who saw the plan on Sunday.

The paper takes one precaution and it is a good one: "Participants who came through the prototype are marked as such in the readings." But look at where the marking is *used*. Exactly one disconfirmation condition controls for it — row 4, "counted only where the teen had not led before." Rows 1 (ladder climbing), 3 (break survival), and 5 (vocabulary migration) do not exclude, stratify, or otherwise handle prototype participants, and the paper states no analysis rule for them.

There is also a second-order effect the paper does not consider. Modeling is contagious in exactly the way this design intends — "the second running is led by a teenager," "vulnerability answered with vulnerability across a generation line." A previewer who names a quiet person in week 9 teaches a naive participant to do it in week 11, and the second act is genuinely unprompted by the previewer's standard and genuinely trained by the room's. The design has no instrument that separates these, and the marking rule cannot help, because the naive participant's act is marked naive.

**Why it matters.** The paper's honesty about the prototype is exemplary; that is not the issue. The issue is that disclosure has been mistaken for control. Marking a participant does not handle a contaminant unless an analysis rule says what the mark does.

**Remedy.** Before the year runs, state the analysis rule for prototype-marked participants in each condition, in Table 3 itself:
- *Row 1:* report the rung series twice — whole room, and with prototype-originated instances excluded from the "seen" marks. If the two series diverge, that is itself a finding.
- *Row 3:* restrict the break-survival comparison to non-prototype participants, as row 4 already does for teen leaders. The paper has already accepted this logic; apply it consistently.
- *Row 5:* record who spoke each coded phrase in the scribe's verbatim notes (this is already being captured) and report the migration with and without previewers.
- Suspend previewing for at least one session per quarter, chosen in advance, and note in the debrief which sessions were previewed. This is one line in the decisions log and it gives the year a within-design comparison it currently lacks — at zero paperwork cost, honoring constraint 5.

## Finding 19 — The pneumatology and the disconfirmation logic are inconsistent, and the line between them is drawn without argument

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The Holy Spirit appears exactly once in this manuscript, in the conclusion:

> "Nothing in the apparatus forms anyone. **The Holy Spirit does that**, and the instruments read only the traces He leaves in a room that has agreed to be looked at."

I take this to be sincere and I take it to be right. But a claim of that magnitude, introduced in the last paragraph, is in tension with what the rest of the paper does — and the tension is precise, not vague.

If the Spirit is the agent of formation, then a year in which the ladder does not climb is not straightforwardly evidence against the design. It may be evidence about God's timing, or about a season of dryness, or about nothing the year can name. Yet Table 3, row 1 treats non-climbing as *disconfirming the design*. So growth is being treated as design-attributable when it falls below the threshold and Spirit-attributable when it rises above it:

> "The year aims at the group's level two to three threshold... **What lies past it is prepared for, prayed for, and never claimed, and its absence at year's end is not read as failure.**"

That sentence draws a line. Below level 3, absence of growth counts against the design. Above level 3, absence of growth counts against nothing. This is an enormous theological commitment — that the lower reaches of internalization are within the competence of a curriculum and the upper reaches are reserved to grace — and the paper states it as a scoping decision.

**Why it matters, and why this journal in particular.** This is precisely the question of what warrants claims about spiritual growth, and it is the question this journal exists to keep honest. The tradition has an answer available and the paper does not use it: the means of grace are ordinary and appointed, God works through them reliably enough that we practice them and freely enough that we cannot compel the result. That framing would let the author say what he plainly believes — the practices are means, not causes — and it would repair the inconsistency, because a means-of-grace account expects the ordinary practices to bear ordinary fruit while denying that the fruit is manufactured. It would also tell him what a null year means, which his current framework does not.

I want to state the claim narrowly so it is checkable: **the paper asserts divine agency in formation, makes non-formation up to level 3 a disconfirmation of the design, exempts non-formation above level 3, and never states the principle that divides the two cases.** Each clause is checkable against the text.

**Remedy.** One paragraph, in the Design Commitments section rather than the conclusion — the placement is the substance of the fix, because a pneumatology stated in the last paragraph cannot shape a design stated in the first ten pages. It should say what the practices are (means, appointed, ordinary), what the instruments read (traces, not causes), and — this is the part the paper needs most — **what a null year would and would not mean.** The author already has the vocabulary for it: "where the mirror shows nothing yet, waited for without panic." That sentence, moved forward and turned into a rule for reading Table 3 in June, would do the work. Note that this also protects the author pastorally: without it, a year in which the ladder does not climb is a year in which a shepherd who loves these families must read his own instruments as a verdict on them.

## Finding 20 — "Permission for pages, never for care" draws the line at paper rather than at use; I argue against the constraint on its own ground

**Severity: MED-HIGH · [PHILOSOPHER]**

The brief names this as a settled commitment, so I state at the outset that I am arguing against it deliberately, and on the ground the author himself supplies.

**What the paper says.**

> "Permission is required for pages, never for care. Everything a family fills out exists only on their yes, but the Companion team never asks permission to pay attention, because a shepherd watching a flock with love is not running an instrument, and converting ordinary pastoral attentiveness into a consented procedure would make care itself feel clinical. **The line between the two is physical: if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for; if it is a shepherd's eye held under a signed covenant, it is care.**"

**Where I agree, entirely.** Ordinary pastoral attentiveness requires no consent, and demanding it would be a category error that damages the thing it means to protect. If the author's claim were only this, I would have no finding. A shepherd who notices that a teenager has gone quiet does not need a signed form, and a ministry that thinks otherwise has confused itself with a clinic. The author is right about this and right for the right reasons.

**Where the line fails.** What the pilot does with the shepherd's eye goes past ordinary attentiveness in four specific, textually documented ways. The observation is (a) recorded on a pre-printed instrument — "the sheets are pre-printed for their weeks"; (b) scored against a fixed rule — "marks each sign seen, not yet, or no occasion... promotes a rung after three consecutive weeks of seen"; (c) retained by name for a year — "the team keeps named notes about participants across the year"; and (d) analyzed against published predictions and reported in a peer-reviewed journal.

At the point where (a)–(d) obtain, the thing being done is research on identifiable human subjects, some of them children, and it is research whether or not the subject touches paper. The physical criterion — *did a participant's hand touch the form* — has no moral significance. What has moral significance is **use**: whether an observation of a person is retained, coded, and published. The author's own covenant supplies the principle that decides this, and it decides against the physical line:

> "Families are also told in the same conversation that the team keeps named notes about participants across the year, **because permission that conceals what is kept is not permission**."

Note what that sentence achieves and what it stops short of. It achieves *disclosure* — the families are told. It stops short of *permission* — the design explicitly does not ask. The author has already granted that concealment vitiates permission; the remaining step, that disclosure without asking is notification rather than consent, is one he has not taken but has no principled reason to refuse.

**Why it matters here more than elsewhere.** The subjects include minors who did not choose the setting, seated beside their own parents, in a room whose own limitations paragraph identifies them as the least-positioned to object:

> "a room can register as safe while the subgroup least positioned to say otherwise, the teenagers, seated beside their own parents in a room where parents speak first, is never heard on its own."

The design asks a fourteen-year-old's permission before handing her a one-page survey about her hopes, and does not ask before keeping a named file on her behavior for nine months and publishing the aggregate. The scruple and the exposure are inversely proportioned.

**Remedy — one sentence, in a conversation that is already happening.** The permission conversation exists and is already held family by family in the gap week. Add to it a request for permission for the **research use** of the team's observations — not for the watching, which stays unasked, exactly as the constraint requires. Something like: *We watch because we're your shepherds and we'd do that anyway. Separately: we write some of what we see on a sheet, we keep it for the year, and we'll report what it added up to. May we?*

This preserves every constraint. The shepherd's eye remains unconsented (constraint 3's actual concern). No new paperwork is created (constraint 5). No scores, no names past the shepherd (constraint 1). The family is not divided (constraint 6).

One honest complication the author must handle rather than finesse: the sign readings are room-level, and a room-level count cannot exclude a family that is present. The remedy therefore has to include the disclosure: *if you say no, your family's individually attributable notes stay out of the record, and we'll tell you plainly that the room-level counts include everyone who was in the room, because we can't count a room minus one.* That is a harder sentence to say. It is also the true one, and this author has shown throughout that he prefers the harder true sentence — the whole "Care wins, and we count the cost" paragraph is built out of them.

## Finding 21 — The Week 7 narration is written in the present tense and unmarked as report or plan, in a paper whose own preregistration rationale forbids exactly that

**Severity: MEDIUM · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The paper states the norm it will be judged by:

> "preregistration... **separates what was predicted from what was discovered** and limits the choices a researcher can make after seeing the data."

It then acknowledges that its own evidential base is mixed:

> "Observation did not begin with this article: the design was prototyped with one family across eighteen months, two versions of the quarterly self-read were built and abandoned in the field, and **some of what follows reports behavior already seen**."

But it never marks *which*. And its central section — the best writing in the manuscript, and the section a reader will remember — is narrated in the present tense throughout, in specific detail, indistinguishable from a report:

> "At 4:00 the school day ends and the door opens on a used classroom. The Formation Companion team and a crew of teenagers organize the room into being: chairs to a circle, the screen up, families arriving into the middle of the work and some of them joining it."

> "The re-entry check-in asks how the break treated everyone's practice, and honest answers include lapsed ones. ['Thinned out over the break,' spoken aloud without flinching, is an offering-level tell, and the team marks the sign seen.]"

And it closes with a verdict:

> "Nothing in the evening looked like measurement, and nearly every minute of it measured, **which is the design working exactly as intended**."

Week 7 is the seventh session of a twenty-two-week pilot. Whether or not the pilot has begun, the reader is given no way to tell whether this evening happened, whether it is a composite of prototype evenings, or whether it is a plan written in the present tense. The final clause reads as a result claim about a session the paper has not established occurred.

**Why it matters.** This is not a small stylistic point in this particular paper, because the paper's distinctive claim on our attention is that it separates prediction from discovery *in advance*. Its most vivid section is the one place where the separation is not visible, and it is the section that will do the most work on a reader's judgment.

**Remedy.** Trivial and costless. Add one bracketed line under the section head: *This session has not yet run. The evening is composed from the prototype's eighteen months and the session plan; bracketed observations marked [P] have been seen in prototype, and those marked [D] are designed and not yet observed.* Then tag the brackets. Two dozen characters, and the section becomes the strongest illustration in the paper instead of its softest evidential seam. If, alternatively, the session *has* run, say so and date it — in which case it is data and belongs in the results paper, and the reader is owed the date either way.

Also revise the closing clause. "Which is the design working exactly as intended" should become "which is what the design intends," a change of two words that converts a result claim into a design claim.

## Finding 22 — Goodhart is doing work Goodhart does not do: non-disclosure of the hoped-for movement is over-warranted

**Severity: MEDIUM · [PHILOSOPHER]**

**What is wrong.** The paper justifies keeping its target from the room by appeal to the measurement literature:

> "no target is ever announced, including the movement hoped for above, which is never kept as a scorecard, **because the moment a mirror becomes a goal it stops being a mirror**; the measurement literature has its own name for the corruption of measures that become targets, and churches are not exempt from it."

Footnote 15 states the underlying claim accurately — Goodhart's observation is "that a statistical regularity tends to collapse once **pressure is placed upon it for control purposes**." The citation is careful and correct. My objection is to the inference drawn from it, not to the citation.

Goodhart's law concerns *control pressure*: what corrupts a measure is that people are steered by it, rewarded against it, held to it. Disclosure is not control pressure. Telling a room "we hope you'll move about a level this year, and we are not going to hold you to it" is not the mechanism Goodhart describes. The paper has conflated *not steering by a target* — which is right, and which the covenant secures by other means ("mirrors, never levers; no scores") — with *not saying what you hope for*, which Goodhart does not license.

**Two things follow, and the second is the one that matters.**

First, an internal tension. The target is not, in fact, unannounced. It is in this article — "the hope... is an average movement of about one level" — and this article's project materials are, by the author's own commitment, "published openly." The secrecy holds only as long as the families do not read what their shepherd publishes.

Second, and more seriously: the design asks these families for consent, and the paper makes a great deal of the asking ("The permission conversation converts measurement from something done to a room into something a room agreed to"). Consent is consent to a described thing. A family that says yes without being told what the year hopes for has consented under a partial description — and the partial description was withheld on a warrant that does not support it.

**I am arguing against constraint 2 here, and I should say why the argument is worth having.** The paper's deepest commitment is the one it names as an absence: "Nothing in the year is demanded." That commitment is about treating people as adults who can be told things and left free. Withholding the hope is the one place in the design where the room is managed rather than told — and it is managed for its own good, which is exactly the paternalism the rest of the design is built to refuse. The author's own "do I want this room to feel measured?" test cuts this way: a room told plainly "here is what we hope, and nobody is being graded against it" is less measured-feeling, not more, than a room that senses something is being watched for and has not been told what.

**Remedy.** Choose one of two, and say which.
- **Preferred:** restate the constraint as *no target is used to steer the room*, which is what Goodhart actually supports and what the covenant already enforces. Then say the hope out loud in the permission conversation, in the same breath as "we are grading the curriculum, never the family." One sentence added to a conversation already scheduled; constraint 5 untouched.
- **Or:** keep non-disclosure, and warrant it pastorally rather than psychometrically — that a stated hope becomes a burden in a room where some families will not move, and that the shepherd chooses to carry the hope alone. That is a real argument, it is this author's kind of argument, and it is honest. What it is not is Goodhart's law.

## A closing note to the author, which he may read or not

The three findings I would most want you to act on before September are 10, 13, and 19, and they are one finding wearing three coats.

Finding 10 says your instrument does not mention God in the range you will actually read it. Finding 13 says your central failure condition can only be triggered by the program collapsing. Finding 19 says you have asserted that the Spirit does the forming and then built a table that blames your curriculum when forming does not happen. Underneath all three is the same thing: **you have built a beautiful instrument for watching a room become a room, and you have labeled it an instrument for watching a room learn to hear God.** The first is a real achievement and I would publish it. The second is the claim on your title page, and your instrument does not yet reach it.

That is fixable, and it is fixable cheaply, and it is fixable only before the observation protocol freezes. After week one it is a year too late.

On the question the brief put to me — whether this paper's epistemic humility is real or performed — my honest answer is: real, and unevenly implemented. The humility is genuine wherever you are writing about your *limitations*; the limitations paragraph in "How This Design Could Be Shown Wrong" is one of the most searching self-assessments I have read in a submission to this journal, and it names things no reviewer would have caught. But the humility does not reach your *predictions*. The failure conditions are gentler than the limitations, and in three places (rows 1, 2, 6) they are conditions that a disappointing year would pass. The pattern is recognizable and forgivable: a man is more willing to say what he cannot know than to describe the specific way his year could break his heart. The remedy is to write Table 3 with the same pen you wrote the limitations with.

And the sentence I would keep above all others, because it is the one your whole architecture should be arranged to protect: *"measure nothing you will not weep over."* I would want the year to test it. Row 6, as written, will not.

— S.L.P.
