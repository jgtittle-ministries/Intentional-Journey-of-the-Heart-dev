# The Properties of Spiritual Law

The rest of this volume describes the laws one at a time. This short chapter steps back and asks a
different question: once you have a whole table of them, what do they have *in common* — not in
subject matter, but in the way they *behave*? Physical laws have shared properties of this kind. They
are consistent, they can be tested, they run in describable directions. The laws in this volume turn
out to have shared properties too — and the most useful thing about naming them is this: the ways
these laws *resemble* physical laws are what let us honestly call them "laws" at all, and the ways
they *differ* all point, every time, back to the same place — to the fact that behind a spiritual law
is not a mechanism but a Person.

So begin there, because everything else hangs on it.

## First, the thing that is not like physics at all

A physical law is an impersonal regularity. Gravity does not love you; it does not move first; it
does not care which way you fall. A spiritual law is not like that. Every one of them is a
description of how a faithful God reliably acts — the grain of His covenant faithfulness, not the
gears of a device. That is why none of these laws is a lever you pull to make God do something.
They are the settled, trustworthy ways a Person relates to the people He made. Read every property
below in that light, or you will turn the whole thing into a machine — which is exactly what it is
not.

## The ways they are like physical laws (why we dare call them laws)

- **They are consistent.** The same input tends to the same outcome, across very different lives and
  centuries apart. That steadiness is real; it is what lets Scripture state them as "whoever does X
  will find Y."
- **They are testable — but over a lifetime, not in an afternoon.** You cannot run a spiritual law
  in a controlled lab. You test it the way you test a claim about character: across decades, across
  many lives, watching for the pattern. The evidence is the convergent witness of Scripture's many
  writers, the agreement of the great traditions, and the testimony of lives observed over time.
- **They run in describable directions.** Many run *both* ways — sow good or sow ill, draw near or
  turn away, obey God or obey sin — and the dark direction is usually the same machinery in reverse.
- **They work at every size.** A whole family of them (the scale-invariant ones) holds the same for
  one person, a community, and a generation — the way some physical laws hold from the small to the
  very large.
- **They have thresholds.** Some are gateways (something must be in place first); some build to a
  tipping point past which the ordinary means no longer work ("suddenly broken beyond healing,"
  Prov. 29:1). Spiritual life is not perfectly smooth and linear any more than the physical world is.

Those five are enough to earn the word *law*. This is not loose metaphor; it is a real, describable
regularity. (That is the honest answer to anyone who asks whether these are "really" laws.)

## The ways they are *not* like physical laws (and where each one points)

Here is the striking part. Every place a spiritual law breaks from a physical one, the break points
back to the personal God behind it.

| | Physical law | Spiritual law | …which points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | immediate or precisely clocked | long and variable — seasons, decades | the patient timing of a *Gardener*, not a stopwatch |
| **Reversibility** | symmetric | easy down, hard up — you drift down, you climb up only with help | a *Redeemer* inside the system, supplying the energy to reverse it |
| **Initiative** | not applicable | God moves first; your part is response | a *relationship*, not a transaction you start |
| **The observer** | independent of the observer | your posture is part of it — faith, motive, a whole heart | a *Person* on the other side, not a button |
| **Moral weight** | morally neutral | one direction is life, the other death | a law that runs *toward or away from Someone* |
| **Visibility** | directly measured | hidden; read by its fruit, in hindsight, or by another's eyes | a work done *in* you, deeper than you can watch |
| **What it acts on** | the single event | the steady direction of a whole life | a God who reads the heart's long arc, not a tally of acts |

Two of these deserve a closer look, because they are the ones people feel.

**Timing: the delay is where faith lives.** Drop a stone and it falls now. Sow generosity, or begin
to renew your mind, and the harvest can be seasons or years away. The delay is not the law failing;
it is the law's normal shape. And it is precisely *because* the feedback is delayed that these laws
ask for faith: you act before you can see the result. A stopwatch faith would not be faith at all.

**Asymmetry: easy down, hard up.** A heart hardens, or a habit enslaves, by simple drift — no effort
required, the way a room gets dusty on its own. Coming back up is never that easy; it takes help from
outside — a word, a community, the Spirit's particular work, sometimes a hard jolt. This looks at
first like the way the physical world runs down toward disorder and stays there. But here is the
difference that changes everything: in the physical world, the running-down always wins in the end.
In the spiritual world it does not, because there is a Redeemer *inside* the system, and He supplies
the very energy the climb requires. That is why every one of these laws, when it describes the way
back up, describes it as rescue — never as a self-made escape. The asymmetry is real, and grace is
the answer to it.

## Why naming all this is worth the trouble

It does two jobs at once.

To anyone who doubts these are *really* laws — that they are just pious wishes dressed up — the
family resemblances are the answer: they are consistent, testable over time, directional,
scale-invariant, and threshold-shaped. That is what a law is.

To anyone who fears the opposite — that calling them "laws" turns God into a vending machine and
faith into technique — the differences are the answer: they run on a Person's timing, they wait on a
Person's first move, they involve the heart's posture, they are reversed only by grace, and they are
weighted toward life or death because they run toward or away from Someone. No machine behaves like
that.

So the same set of properties that makes them genuinely law-like is what keeps them from ever being
mechanical. They are laws — the lawful, faithful, utterly reliable ways a loving God deals with His
children. That is the best of both: as dependable as gravity, and personal all the way down.
