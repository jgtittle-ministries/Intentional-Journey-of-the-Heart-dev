# v5_6_0_14 Pre-Pass Candidate Roster — Attachment-Theory Lens on the Trust-and-Obey Cluster

This document is the v5_6_0_14 setup deliverable per the v5_6_0_13 Q-anticipation. The pass's exploratory lens — ratified at the prompt-construction step — is attachment theory: Bowlby and Ainsworth's secure-base and internal-working-models concepts, the four adult attachment patterns (secure, anxious-preoccupied, dismissive-avoidant, fearful-avoidant/disorganized), and the attachment-to-God research line (Kirkpatrick, Granqvist, and successors) distinguishing correspondence and compensation pathways.

The lens is used as a candidate-surfacing heuristic, not as a source of doctrinal authority. Every candidate the lens surfaces must clear the inclusion bar on its own scriptural footing — multi-author support across at least two biblical authors, broad acceptance across mainstream Christian traditions, directional in form, principle-level — independent of whether attachment theory predicts it. The lens is a flashlight; the warrant is scripture. Candidates that are interesting only because attachment theory makes them interesting are demoted at this pre-pass stage rather than admitted to the stress-test pipeline.

This pre-pass discussion presents fourteen candidates with brief operational descriptions, anchor scriptural references with the multi-author check made explicit, and preliminary inclusion-bar evaluations. Your steering response — indicating priorities, excluding candidates that don't warrant the pass's attention, or surfacing additional candidates — will set the v5_6_0_14 pass's working scope. The stress-test and finalist articulation work follows your response.

## Candidate Roster

### P0/GII — Scale-Invariant Heart (Primary Target)

**Candidate 1: The Trust-Substrate Law**

Operational claim: A heart's experienced trust in God's reliable presence and faithful character operates as the substrate condition under which obedience, perseverance, intercession, sacrifice, and difficulty-bearing become structurally possible; absent the trust-substrate, the same activities are performed as self-protection, performance, or compliance rather than as response to God, with the substitution operationally observable in their fruit. The principle holds scale-invariantly — individuals, dyads, communities, and generations each operate by the same trust-substrate condition for the obedience-of-faith outputs the catalog's GV laws name.

Anchor scriptures: Prov. 3:5–6 (trust in the LORD with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding); Isa. 30:15 ("in returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength"); Isa. 26:3–4 (perfect peace whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts; trust in the LORD forever, for the LORD GOD is an everlasting rock); Ps. 9:10 (those who know your name put their trust in you, for you, O LORD, have not forsaken those who seek you); Ps. 37:3–5 (trust in the LORD and do good; commit your way to the LORD; trust in him, and he will act); Ps. 56:3–4 (when I am afraid, I put my trust in you); Heb. 11:6 (without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him); Rom. 4:18–22 (Abraham's trust against hope, the substrate for the obedience the chapter narrates); 1 John 4:18 (perfect love casts out fear, for fear has to do with punishment).

Multi-author check: Solomon (Proverbs), Isaiah, the Psalmists (David and others across multiple Psalms), the Hebrews writer, Paul, John. At least six biblical authors across both Testaments — substantially exceeding the two-author threshold.

Preliminary evaluation: Clears all four inclusion-bar criteria. The territory is operationally substantive and lands at an empty cell (P0/GII). The structural relationship to FL.VI Hear-and-Obey (P0/GV) is anticipated as substrate-to-operation: trust-substrate at GII conditions the obedience-operation at GV. The structural relationship to FL.X Faith-Sight (P0/GIV) is anticipated as heart-and-mind faces of the same faith-family at scale-invariant scale: FL.X is the cognitive-instrumental face (faith as substance-of-things-hoped-for), the present candidate is the heart-relational face (trust as substrate-of-trusting-action). The Mirror form is anticipated as constitutive bidirectional or paired-statement (distrust-substrate produces self-protective substitution as the operational mode that displaces obedience). **High likelihood of finalist status.**

**Candidate 2: The Felt-Refuge Law**

Operational claim: A person's felt-sense of God as refuge (the experienced quality of safety in God's presence, distinct from the cognitive proposition that God is safe) operates as the substrate under which the soul can rest, can stop self-protecting, can face difficulty without dissociation or hardening, and can engage God's address without the defensive postures that would distort hearing. The felt-sense is itself a formation outcome, produced by sustained covenant practice and not generated independently of God's address.

Anchor scriptures: Ps. 91:1–2 (he who dwells in the shelter of the Most High... my refuge and my fortress, my God in whom I trust); Ps. 46:1–3 (God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble); Ps. 27:1–3 (the LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear); Deut. 33:27 (the eternal God is your refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms); Heb. 6:18–19 (we who have fled for refuge to hold fast to the hope set before us as an anchor of the soul); Matt. 11:28–30 (come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest); Isa. 32:17–18 (the effect of righteousness will be peace, and the result of righteousness, quietness and trust forever; my people will abide in a peaceful habitation); 2 Cor. 1:3–4 (Father of mercies and God of all comfort).

Multi-author check: Psalmists, Moses (Deuteronomy), Hebrews writer, Matthew, Isaiah, Paul. At least six authors across both Testaments.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong multi-author footing, broad acceptance, clearly directional, principle-level. **The principal question is whether this is operationally distinct from Candidate 1 (Trust-Substrate) or whether the two are heart-cognitive and heart-affective faces of one law.** The attachment-theory frame distinguishes them — cognitive trust and felt security are operationally distinguishable in attachment research — but the IJH bar is whether scripture distinguishes them as distinct laws. The current judgment is that scripture treats trust and felt-refuge as substantially integrated, with the refuge language as the experiential expression of the trust the substrate names. **Moderate likelihood as distinct law; may be absorbed into Candidate 1 as the experiential articulation of the substrate, with the refuge-language scriptures cited in Candidate 1's chapter rather than supporting a separate admission.**

### P1/GII — Individual Heart (Absorption-Risk Cell)

**Candidate 3: The Internal-Working-Model / God-Image Distortion Law**

Operational claim: A person's prior relational history with primary caregivers operates as the default interpretive frame through which God's self-disclosure is received, so that the same scriptural revelation of God's character is heard differently by hearts shaped by different prior relational patterns; without deliberate scriptural correction and corrective community experience, the prior pattern operates as the default God-image and conditions which aspects of God's revelation are received and which are distorted on reception.

Anchor scriptures: Matt. 7:9–11 ("if you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things"); Lk. 11:11–13 (parallel passage); Eph. 3:14–15 (the Father from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named); Ps. 27:10 ("for my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the LORD will take me in"); Ps. 103:13 (as a father shows compassion to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who fear him); Heb. 12:5–11 (the discipline of human fathers as analog and contrast to the Father's discipline); Mal. 4:6 (turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers).

Multi-author check: Matthew, Luke, Paul, Psalmist, Hebrews writer, Malachi. At least six authors.

Preliminary evaluation: The territory is broadly recognized in pastoral and counseling theology, but the principle is more often *inferred* from the scriptures than directly taught by them. The Matt. 7 and Lk. 11 "how much more" arguments presuppose the dynamic without naming it as the principle in question. Ps. 27:10 and Eph. 3:14–15 hint at the dynamic from the corrective side. The candidate may clear the inclusion bar at the Reasonably Inferred tier but not at the Clearly Taught tier; the pre-pass discussion should examine whether the inferential paths together constitute Clearly Taught status or whether the law is admissible only at the Reasonably Inferred tier (paralleling V1.Exp1 Faith-Hearing Generation and V1.Exp3 Faith-Hope-Love Triad at the same cell). **Moderate likelihood; tier-of-admission is the principal stress-test question.**

**Candidate 4: The Double-Minded Internal Division Law**

Operational claim: A heart that simultaneously approaches God and recoils from God — yearning toward and fearing, drawn and self-protected — operates in structural division that makes it unstable in all its ways and prevents the reception of what it requests from God; the division must be healed by the unification of the heart's direction-of-approach toward God, with the healing requiring deliberate work rather than operating by default.

Anchor scriptures: Jas. 1:6–8 (the doubter is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind; a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways); Jas. 4:8 (cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded); Ps. 12:2 (everyone utters lies to his neighbor; with flattering lips and a double heart they speak); Ps. 86:11 ("unite my heart to fear your name"); 1 Kings 18:21 ("how long will you go limping between two different opinions"); Matt. 6:24 (no one can serve two masters); Rom. 7:14–25 (the divided self that wills the good but does the opposite).

Multi-author check: James, Psalmist (multiple), the Kings narrator, Matthew, Paul. At least five authors.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong scriptural footing — James names "double-minded" twice as a direct category. Broad acceptance across traditions on the operational dynamic. **The principal question is overlap with FL.III Heart-Throne and FL.XIV Vanity-of-Substitutes.** FL.III says only one Object can be enthroned; the present candidate names the structural state of the heart that *attempts* to serve both Objects without yet resolving the throne-question. The two laws may stand as related rather than overlapping — FL.III names the architectural constraint (only-one-Object), the present candidate names the operational instability of the heart that has not yet resolved which Object — but the distinction needs care in articulation. **Moderate likelihood; depends on operational distinctness from FL.III at the stress-test articulation.**

**Candidate 5: The Anxious-Approach / Performance-Worship Law**

Operational claim: When the heart's trust-substrate is uncertain or absent, the soul's approach to God organizes around anxious performance — repeated reassurance-seeking, vain repetitions, scrupulous law-keeping, anxious checking of one's standing — with the anxious pattern displacing the trustful approach the gospel offers; the anxious approach itself becomes a structural barrier to receiving the assurance it seeks.

Anchor scriptures: Matt. 6:7 ("when you pray, do not heap up empty phrases as the Gentiles do, who think that they will be heard for their many words"); Lk. 10:38–42 (Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; only one thing is necessary); Phil. 4:6–7 (do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication... and the peace of God will guard your hearts); 1 Pet. 5:6–7 (humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God... casting all your anxieties on him); Isa. 26:3 (whose mind is stayed on you, in perfect peace); Heb. 4:10 (whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works).

Multi-author check: Matthew, Luke, Paul, Peter, Isaiah, Hebrews writer. At least six authors.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong scriptural footing, broad acceptance (rest-vs-restless-religiosity is a standard pastoral theme). **The principal question is whether this is a distinct law or the Mirror form of Candidate 1 (Trust-Substrate).** The anxious-approach pattern is a specific operational expression of trust-substrate failure; it may be the idol-ward face of the substrate dynamic rather than a Foundational Law in its own right. **Moderate likelihood; may be held as the Mirror form of Candidate 1 (anxious-approach as the operational signature of distrust-substrate) rather than as a distinct law.**

### P1/GV — Individual Body & Action (Mirror-Risk Cell)

**Candidate 6: The Self-Protective Substitution Law**

Operational claim: When the heart's trust-substrate is absent or distrusted, the body-and-action dimension organizes around self-protection rather than trusting-response, with the self-protective pattern displacing the obedience-of-faith as the operational mode; the substitution is hard to distinguish from prudence and is frequently rationalized as wisdom, with the substrate question being the diagnostic that distinguishes obedience-from-prudence from self-protection-as-substitute.

Anchor scriptures: Gen. 3:8–10 (Adam hid himself from the presence of the LORD God among the trees); Prov. 29:25 (the fear of man lays a snare, but whoever trusts in the LORD is safe); Matt. 25:24–25 (the buried-talent servant — "I was afraid, and I went and hid your talent in the ground"); 2 Tim. 1:7 (God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control); 1 John 4:18 (there is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear); Heb. 10:38–39 ("my righteous one shall live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him").

Multi-author check: Mosaic, Solomon, Matthew, Paul, John, Hebrews writer. At least six authors.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong multi-author footing, broad acceptance. **The principal question is whether this is a distinct law at GV or the GV expression of Candidate 1's idol-ward face named in the Mirror text.** The substrate is at GII (heart); the operational consequence is at GV (body and action). The relationship parallels the FL.III/FL.XIV pairing or the FL.VI/FL.XVI pairing — same operational mechanism, different scale of articulation. The candidate may admit as a distinct law (GV-action expression of GII-substrate failure) or as part of the Mirror articulation of Candidate 1. **Moderate likelihood as distinct law; depends on whether the GV-expression is sufficiently operationally distinct from the GII-substrate's Mirror articulation to warrant separate admission.**

### P2/GII — Dyadic Heart (Empty Cell, Secondary Target)

**Candidate 7: The Pair-Bond Attachment Substrate Law**

Operational claim: A dyadic pair-bond (marriage primarily; close friendship, discipleship dyad, spiritual-direction pair secondarily) operates on a heart-level attachment substrate — the felt-sense of being known, prized, and reliably held in the other's love — and the substrate's operational health conditions the pair-bond's capacity to bear difficulty without rupture; the substrate is built through specific operations (presence, attunement, repair-after-rupture, consistent loving address) and is eroded by their absence.

Anchor scriptures: Gen. 2:23–25 ("this at last is bone of my bones... and they were both naked and were not ashamed"); Eph. 5:25–33 (husbands love your wives as Christ loved the church; let each one love his wife as himself, and let the wife see that she respects her husband); Song of Songs throughout (the felt-attachment language); 1 Sam. 18:1–3 (the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul); Ruth 1:16–17 (where you go I will go); 1 Pet. 3:7 (live with your wives in an understanding way... so that your prayers may not be hindered); Heb. 13:4 (let marriage be held in honor among all).

Multi-author check: Mosaic, Paul, Song of Songs writer, 1 Samuel narrator, Ruth narrator, Peter, Hebrews writer. At least seven authors.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong multi-author footing, broad acceptance, clearly directional, principle-level. **The principal question is whether this is absorbed by FL.XXXIV Marriage Covenant Architecture Law (P2/GVI) or admits as the GII substrate-partner to FL.XXXIV at GVI.** FL.XXXIV names the *form* of the bond (leave-and-cleave-and-one-flesh) at the structural-frame Group; the present candidate names the *heart-substrate* upon which the form rests, at the Heart Group. The two would stand as parent-child or constitutive-pair across Groups at the same Period — a relationship pattern the catalog has not yet held explicitly. **Moderate likelihood; structural-relationship question with FL.XXXIV is the principal stress-test question, and the answer would teach the catalog about heart-substrate-to-architectural-frame relationships at any Period.**

### P3 — Community Scale (Absorption-Risk Cells)

**Candidate 8: The Body-of-Christ as Corrective Attachment Field Law**

Operational claim: The body of Christ's corporate life — sustained community, shared worship, communal mourning, restoration practice, soul-care for the wounded — operates as the field within which insecurely-attached members' heart-postures toward God are gradually re-formed toward secure orientation; the field is operationally distinct from any individual member's solitary faith-formation work and produces the re-formation through embodied participation in the body's sustained life.

Anchor scriptures: Eph. 4:11–16 (the body builds itself up in love when each part is working properly); 1 Cor. 12:14–26 (if one member suffers, all suffer together; if one member is honored, all rejoice together); Heb. 10:24–25 (stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together); Acts 2:42–47 (the early church's continuing in fellowship, breaking of bread, daily devotion); Gal. 6:1–2 (restore him in a spirit of gentleness... bear one another's burdens).

Multi-author check: Paul, Hebrews writer, Luke. Three authors, qualifies.

Preliminary evaluation: Operationally real and pastorally important. **The principal question is whether the re-formation work is held by the combined operation of FL.XXX Communal Soul-Care for the Wounded, FL.XXXII Communal Worship Heart-Alignment, and FL.XXV Restoration-of-the-Erring already, or whether it warrants a separate Foundational Law.** The attachment-theory framing of "corrective attachment field" appears to describe the integration of these existing laws rather than naming a new operational mechanism. **Low likelihood as distinct law; the territory is likely already held by the combined operation of FL.XXX, FL.XXXII, and FL.XXV taken together. May warrant a structural-relationship note rather than separate admission.**

### P4/GII or P4/GIII — Generational Scale (Adjacent to FL.XVII / FL.XXI)

**Candidate 9: The Generational Relational-Pattern Transmission Law**

Operational claim: Relational patterns — specifically the patterns of trust, fear, presence, harshness, abandonment, secure address, and contempt — transmit from generation to generation through household-modeled relationship and unrepented relational sin, with the pattern persisting across generations unless interrupted by repentance, regeneration, and corrective formation; the pattern transmits whether or not the doctrinal content of the parents' faith also transmits, so that a household can transmit insecure relational patterns alongside orthodox doctrine, or secure relational patterns alongside heterodox doctrine.

Anchor scriptures: Exod. 20:5–6 / Deut. 5:9–10 (visiting iniquity to the third and fourth generation, showing steadfast love to thousands of those who love me and keep my commandments); Lam. 5:7 (our fathers sinned and are no more, and we bear their iniquities); 2 Tim. 1:5 (the sincere faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, dwells in you); Ps. 78:1–8 (telling the next generation, that they should set their hope in God and not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation); Mal. 4:5–6 (turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers); Prov. 22:6 (train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it).

Multi-author check: Mosaic, Lamentations writer, Paul, Psalmist, Malachi, Solomon. At least six authors.

Preliminary evaluation: Strong scriptural footing. **The principal question is overlap with FL.XVII Substitution-Cascade and FL.XXI Household Formation.** FL.XVII names the transmission of the heart's enthroned *Object* through the implicit-learning channel; FL.XXI names the transmission of *covenant-keeping* through deliberate teaching. The present candidate would name a third aspect: transmission of the *relational pattern* (how the heart approaches whatever it enthrones), distinct from both the Object (FL.XVII) and the deliberate covenant-content (FL.XXI). This could form a structural triplet at P4/GII — Object-transmission, content-transmission, pattern-transmission — all operating within the FL.XXVIII architectural envelope. **Moderate likelihood; depends on whether relational-pattern is operationally distinct from FL.XVII's implicit-learning channel content. May admit as a third entry at P4/GII alongside FL.XVII and FL.XXI, may admit at P4/GIII (relational pattern as a soul-integration-transmission), or may be held as a sub-articulation within FL.XVII's implicit-learning channel.**

Operational claim: When a dyadic or community attachment-bond ruptures (through offense, betrayal, neglect, or harm), restoration of the bond operates through a specified pathway — acknowledgment of harm, expressed remorse, accepted accountability, demonstrated changed pattern over time, and the receiving party's confirmed forgiveness; absent the pathway, the rupture either calcifies into permanent estrangement or is papered over without operational repair.

Anchor scriptures: Matt. 5:23–24 (leave your gift at the altar and go be reconciled); Matt. 18:15–17 (the Matt. 18 pathway); Lk. 17:3–4 (if your brother sins, rebuke him; if he repents, forgive); 2 Cor. 2:5–11 (the offender restored after godly grief); 2 Cor. 7:8–13 (godly grief produces repentance); Jas. 5:16 (confess your sins to one another).

**Candidate 13: The Trust-Precedes-Obedience Order Note**
