## PART I — THE CENTRAL CONVERGENCE, ADJUDICATED

*(Osmer 5 + Osmer §5 vs. MM F1 + MM §5. This is the largest claim in the set and it is the one I
press hardest, because it is stated as an absolute in both reviews and the absolute is false.)*

### They are not one finding. They are two, sharing a sentence.

Osmer's failure mode is **closure**: a warm in-group whose good is bounded by its own edge, no love
of the non-member, "no observation anywhere in this architecture that could tell those two apart."

Miller-McLemore's failure mode is **idiom-convergence**: "a room becoming fluent in its leader's
idiom and reporting that fluency back as revelation, with every trace in the architecture rising as
it does."

These have *different tells* and require *different instruments*. Closure would show as flat
outward crossing; idiom-convergence would show as homogenized content. Presenting them as a single
converged HIGH would overstate the evidence. The verbal overlap ("the design cannot see its own
worst failure") is a shared sentence, not a shared argument.

### What in the paper actually discriminates

I tested every candidate the brief named.

| Candidate | Discriminates closure? | Discriminates idiom-convergence? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sought stray** [36] | **No.** A cohesive in-group is *better* at retrieving its own absent. Osmer is right: "a stray *from the room*." It measures boundary maintenance, which is what consolidation looks like. | No. |
| **Break-survival** [18], [56] | **Partly — and decisively on the internalization axis.** It is behavior in the household when the room is not present and no one is watching. A circle that has produced cohesion and nothing else keeps attending and keeps feeling warm while home practice stays flat. Osmer's own §1 concedes this: "the one place in the architecture where a claim about internalization could be embarrassed by data that no one on the team rated." | Partly. |
| **Teen-led nights** [19], [57] | **Yes, on the leader-dependence axis.** A room consolidating *around the founder* fails an absence test. Osmer 13 weakens it (volunteer selection) but does not eliminate it. | **Yes** — the strongest single counter to MM. A room fluent only in the leader's idiom cannot run without him. |
| **Marker 4** [50], "a disagreement processed without the leader" | Weakly. | **Yes, directly.** This is a *counted* marker that idiom-convergence would suppress, not raise. MM's "every trace rises" is false at this line. |
| **Honest-skeptic seat** [17] | Weakly — it is a practice, not a counted trace. Does not carry evidential weight. | Weakly, same reason. |
| **Outward curriculum** [23] Q3/Q4 | **No.** Osmer is factually correct: the third quarter "turns outward" and the fourth "sends, and commissions," and **not one instrument reads outward.** I verified this exhaustively; there is no outward count anywhere in [36]–[38], [50], or [54]–[58]. |—|
| **Pseudocommunity warning** [42] | Osmer's refinement is precise and correct: pseudocommunity diagnoses *hidden difference*, not *closure*. But [42] does pre-commit the team to distrusting harmony, which both reviewers praise in §1 and then argue as though absent. | **Yes, partly** — a team told "the sweetest-looking room can be the lowest-trust room" is conceptually armed against exactly MM's failure. |
| **[41] warmth confound** | — | **Yes, and it is the paper's own answer to MM's mechanism.** "All three house words can be heard along a single dimension of felt warmth... a room that merely grows warmer will appear to migrate. The stages are therefore read first from behavior the year already schedules." Both reviewers praise this paragraph in §1; both then argue as if the paper had not written it. |
| **Outside reader** [59] | **Yes, weakly.** "Judging only by what this program does, what does it appear to believe?" is the one non-room-bounded read in the design, and it is precisely positioned to say "this looks like a warm closed circle." Both reviewers file *separate* findings that it is under-powered (Osmer 12, MM F19) — which concedes it exists. |

**Conclusion.** The absolute claim in both reviews — "no observation anywhere," "every trace rises" —
is refuted by the paper's own text. Four counted traces (break-survival, teen-led absence, marker 4,
outside reader) bear on the discrimination, and [41] is the paper's explicit defense against exactly
the warmth confound MM's mechanism runs through.

**What survives, narrowed to precisely this:**

1. **There is no outward-facing observation.** True, verified, and the curriculum's own Q3/Q4 arc
   makes it a self-inflicted gap. (Osmer's half.)
2. **There is no record of content, so the specific tell of idiom-convergence is invisible.** True and
   verified. (MM's half.)
3. **The paper's crescendo overclaims.** [51] and [63] name in-group self-recognition — "unwilling to
   go back to rooms without it" — as "the one outcome I actually designed for" and identify it with
   "the level-three transition." That is a sociological criterion doing the work of a theological one,
   *on the paper's own terms*, in a paper that elsewhere refuses to overclaim ([59]: "the smaller claim
   is the faithful one"). This is not an imported standard; it tests the paper's own sentence.

**Severity of the surviving core: MEDIUM-HIGH, not HIGH.** The remedies (one outward line on the
existing debrief page; one matching disconfirmation condition at [54]–[58]) are cheap, correct, and
should be adopted. The rhetoric of "the design cannot distinguish success from its worst failure"
should not survive into synthesis.

**Are they reasoning from different premises?** On this item, yes — but not in the way the reviews
present. They converge verbally and diverge substantively, which is the opposite of what a
convergence is worth. Do not weight them as mutual corroboration.

## PART II — FINDING-BY-FINDING VERDICTS

### OSMER

**Osmer 1 — The situation is never described.**
*Restatement:* no tradition, sociology, denominational location, or household context anywhere.
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM** (filed HIGH). Factually correct: the entire context is [8] "a small Christian
school," [5] "releases its classroom at four o'clock," and a cohort size. But this is a protocol paper
with a results paper to follow ([8] commits that "the enrolled number will be reported with the
results"), and the remedy is two paragraphs. Severity down; MM F13 does the same work better, from the
paper's own numbers rather than from sociology the author must go collect.

**Osmer 2 — No occasion where one person is listened to at length.**
*Restatement:* the design has informal and formal attending, and no semi-formal attending; every
category precedes the room.
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Verified: [34] is five short-prompt pages, [37] is a pre-specified rung read,
[40]–[41] is public and leader-anchored, [27] is a margin. The "reading, not listening" formulation is
precise and fair, and the observation that Q3 is the most revealing question *because* it supplies no
categories is a genuine insight into the paper's own evidence. **One caution:** half the remedy
(promote the margin to a named free-text column) duplicates the surprise line already added at [59],
"one thing tonight that did not go as I expected." Keep the permission-conversation half.

**Osmer 3 — The paper never asks why; theory legitimates rather than explains.**
*Restatement:* eleven literatures, all supplying warrants, none asked a causal question.
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM** (filed HIGH). The textural observation is accurate and well evidenced. But the
paper self-identifies at [6] as "a field report offered before the field has reported," at [2] as "a
field protocol," and at [9] as action research — it never claims Osmer's four tasks. Grading a
protocol paper against a framework it does not adopt is the persona-overreach failure mode, and Osmer
half-concedes it in §4 ("I would have written the descriptive and interpretive article first"). **What
survives and is not disciplinary:** a paper making the causal claim at [16] ("A parent who goes first
with something true licenses a depth the teen would never volunteer cold") and citing zero adolescent-
development and zero family-systems literature has a warrant gap — but that is Finding 4's ground, not
a demand for a two-to-three-page interpretive section. **Proportionate remedy is Osmer's own §4.7:**
one paragraph mapping the multi-article program onto its tasks.

**Osmer 4 — The room is theorized as a work team and a stranger dyad; it is neither.**
*Restatement:* Edmondson's and Altman–Taylor's boundary conditions fail for a kinship circle.
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM-HIGH.** The social-penetration half is strong and survives intact: [16] makes a
fifty-year general claim to warrant a rule, from a literature on voluntary, symmetrical, exitable peer
dyads. The Edmondson half is softened — [17] claims *convergence*, not derivation ("I did not design
the container from that literature, but the convergence is striking"), which is the modest claim Osmer
himself correctly identifies in Finding 3; and [59] already concedes the team-level limit. So "the
convergence argument therefore proves less than it appears to" attacks an appearance the paper does
not create. The debrief line proposed ("after a parent's telling, did their own teenager speak next,
and was it their own words or an echo?") is excellent and does triple duty — it serves MM F1's idiom
test and MM F5's obligation test as well.

**Osmer 5 — Normative horizon stops at the room's edge; cohesion and maturity read identically.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM-HIGH.** See Part I. The absolute is refuted; the outward gap, the missing
disconfirmation condition, and the crescendo overclaim survive. Adopt both remedies.

**Osmer 6 — No route by which a young person can say a hard thing outside the parental circle.**
**SURVIVES — HIGH.** See Part III. Flagged as safeguarding.

**Osmer 7 — The ladder shapes care, so an unvalidated scale acts normatively on people who never see it.**
*Restatement:* non-disclosure protects from knowing the judgment, not from receiving its effects.
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** Core survives and is genuinely unaddressed: [12] says "levels are a Companion's
diagnostic lens for shaping care," and nothing anywhere addresses the distributive ethics of that.
**Factual correction:** Osmer cross-wires two scales. The three-consecutive-weeks ratchet at [37] is
the *room's* sign rung ("the team reads the rung the room is on"); the individual Krathwohl levels at
[12] carry no stated ratchet. His sentence "a person read low... promoted by a ratchet that cannot
descend" merges them. The charge still holds on [12] alone. Both remedies (one covenant clause at
[44], one question on the existing call's "look at the flock" section) are free and good.

**Osmer 8 — "Nothing is ever asked" is asserted, never weighed against obedience.**
*Restatement:* the domain contains "obeying"; the tradition summons; the commitment is unargued.
**WEAKENED — LOW.** The announced-target / spoken-call distinction is a genuine conceptual improvement
and the paragraph at [21] does slide from pedagogy to measurement ethics without noticing they are
different arguments. But the commitment is not wholly unargued — [12]'s fifth principle ("a curriculum
that tried would do harm") argues in its direction. Nothing the paper *claims* is threatened. The
Wesleyan-class-meeting demand is a disciplinary preference (Osmer's own "good practice as a normative
source"), not a defect. **Surviving ask: one sentence naming which of the two refusals is meant.**

**Osmer 9 — The one pneumatological sentence bears causal work it cannot carry.**
*Restatement:* "each level change considered a gift of the Holy Spirit" [12] is the entire doctrine of
divine agency, and every behavior read would be predicted by ordinary group process.
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Verified: that clause is the only pneumatological claim in sixty-odd paragraphs.
This is construct validity with a theological name — it bears on whether the paper measures formation
or group process, which is its whole thesis. Osmer concedes the honest answer may be "the Spirit works
through ordinary process," and asks only that it be argued rather than implied. One paragraph.

**Osmer 10 — The fellowship has no ecclesial location.** *(merge with MM F16)*
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Verified: [23] "sends, and commissions," [51] "unwilling to go back to rooms
without it," and the church appears nowhere. Osmer's distinctive contribution is the internal-
consistency form of it — [16] says a supporting work "encourage[s] and equip[s]... never to replace"
of the *family*, and never says it of the *church*. His "what would count as evidence we drew families
away" test is disconfirmation-shaped and fits the paper's register. Take his test, take MM's mechanism.

**Osmer 11 — The room's counsel is coded rather than adopted.**
*Restatement:* [28] maps the room's "do differently" answers to the signs; nothing adopts them.
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM** (filed MEDIUM-HIGH). "Nowhere in the architecture is there a mechanism by which
the room's counsel changes the year" is too strong: [44] says the team is "updating content as the
train is moving as necessary to best serve this group at this juncture." What survives intact, and is
the load-bearing half, is that **nothing closes the loop back to the room** — "received without
correction" [28] is not the same as acted upon and said so. The formulation "the leader recedes in
visibility but never in authorship" is sharp and true. Free remedy.

**Osmer 12 — The outside reader has no feedback loop and no counterpart.** *(merge with MM F19)*
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH.** The counterpart-page point is the sharpest thing in Osmer's pragmatic
section and is a *TAR-internal* charge, not an imported one: the espoused/operant comparison the paper
invokes at [59] and fn 15 requires two statements, and the design has one. His surprise-line sub-point
is verified — [38]'s call liturgy is "look back (the signs first, then the feedback round's echo)" and
contains no digestion of surprises. Both fixes are free.

**Osmer 13 — Volunteer-only teen leadership weakens the absence test as a disconfirmation condition.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Clean and correct. [19] "when they are a willing volunteer" + [23] "at least
five sessions" + a cohort of 6–15 (≈3–8 teens) means the same one or two may lead all five, in which
case [57] tests the most formed teenager rather than the structure. As written the condition "is nearly
unfalsifiable in the direction that matters" — accurate. The remedy is one tick mark, sharpens a
*preregistered* condition, and explicitly does not breach "nothing is ever asked." One of the two
best-constructed findings in Osmer's set.

**Osmer 14 — The individual-movement desire has no matching disconfirmation condition.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH.** Verified: [12] states "each individual will move, on average, one level or
more"; [54]–[58] contain nothing about individual level movement. In a paper whose entire rhetorical
warrant is preregistration ([8], [53] "A protocol that cannot fail is a testimony, not a design"), the
one quantitative statement of intent is the one unguarded claim. The secondary point is also correct
and independent: averaging an ordinal rating the paper has *just declared unvalidated as a stage model*
[12] implies precision the instrument does not have. Not announcing a target to participants is a
different thing from publishing a failure condition to readers. **Costs one sentence; closes the
paper's only asymmetry against its own stated standard.**

### MILLER-McLEMORE

**MM F1 — The design records the shape of hearing, never its content.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM-HIGH** (filed HIGH). See Part IV for the full adjudication. Factual claim verified
and true: nothing anywhere captures content. Diagnostic claim (this is where operant theology lives)
is strong. The severity framing — "the design cannot detect its most likely failure mode," "every trace
in this architecture rises" — is refuted by marker 4 at [50], the teen-led absence test, and [41]. The
remedy is proportionate but needs two repairs (see Part IV).

**MM F2 — The consent unit is "the family"; a minor's yes is absorbed into a parent's.**
**SURVIVES — HIGH.** Verified: [21] "I sit with each family and ask... One yes covers the year's five
pages," and [12] tracks each individual across the year. The paper's own protection — "the room never
learns who answered which way" [21], the unchased basket at [27] — operates at *family* level only,
and her remedy instantiates the identical mechanism one level down (a "*I'd rather skip this one*" line
on each of the five pages, returned to the same basket). She is explicit and correct that this does not
divide a family: "asking each person present is not separating anyone; it is what already happens at
the table, done properly." Cheap, inside every constraint, closes a real exposure. **Stake-worthy.**

**MM F3 — Participants are not told a named, longitudinal written record exists about them.**
**SURVIVES with a narrowed remedy — HIGH.** The catch is real and I could not break it: the permission
conversation [21] covers *the five pages*; [38] describes weekly dated named sheets with follow-ups,
owners and deadlines "in a single secured location"; nothing indicates participants are told. Her
sentence — "Calling it 'a shepherd's eye' is a description of the motive, not of the artifact" — is the
single sharpest line in either review, and it lands because the paper builds a *rule* on the paper/eye
distinction at [46] and that rule mis-sorts its own heaviest artifact. **Defect in her remedy:** "you
can ask any of us what is written about you" collides with [12], which forbids disclosing levels to a
participant or parent, and would open a parent→teen disclosure channel the design has deliberately
closed. **Constraint-respecting version:** disclose the existence, purpose, no-scores, and non-release;
scope or drop the open-access promise.

**MM F4 — Counting cross-generational reciprocity converts teen disclosure into a target.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** See Part V. The Strathern breach is refuted; the concern is real but belongs to
F5/F10, not to the marker; **the remedy is better than the finding and should be adopted.**

**MM F5 — Social penetration does not transfer to a parent–minor dyad, and the central rule rests on it.**
**SURVIVES — HIGH.** The three failed boundary conditions (symmetry, voluntariness, exit) are correctly
stated and genuinely fail. She credits the field evidence properly ("a real finding and I do not dismiss
it") and attacks only the *generalizing warrant*, which is the right target: [16]'s "the disclosure
research has said for fifty years" is a general-warrant sentence with an unmarked transfer. The
parental-self-disclosure / role-reversal literature is a genuinely different and more apposite body than
Osmer's family systems. **Her proposed disconfirmation condition is the best unclaimed instrument in
either review:** the extension fails "if teen disclosure appears promptly and never varies, which is what
obligation looks like from the outside." That is observable, room-level, needs no new instrument, and it
is also a partial answer to Part I. And the structural fix both reviewers independently reach — state the
transfer the way [12] already states the Krathwohl extension — uses the paper's own established habit.

**MM F6 — Break-survival cannot bear the inferential weight [56] puts on it.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** See Part VI for the conflict with the prior round. Instrument survives; the
*wording of the disconfirmation condition* is the real defect, and her fix strengthens it.

**MM F7 — The home is a destination for practices and never a source of data.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** The factual claim holds: in the whole architecture the household appears once,
as a survival count. The asymmetry with [16]'s parental-primacy theology is worth a paragraph. But
three problems. (a) The paper *chose* this and gave a validity argument — [34] "a room that does not
feel surveilled behaves like itself" — which **Osmer explicitly evaluated and accepted** ("these refusals
cost the descriptive-empirical task *very little*"). The two reviewers disagree here; this is not a
convergence. (b) Her one-word remedy is a net loss: [34]'s pulse item "what has surprised me" is
currently *open* and can already be answered about home; narrowing it to "at home" removes the pulse's
only uncategorized question — the very thing Osmer 2 says the design is starved of. Add an item, do not
swap one. (c) "The household is the centre and the room is the periphery" is a disciplinary preference
against a paper whose thesis at [7] is explicitly about the room as instrument; her §4.1 ("I would have
made the household the unit of the study") concedes she is asking for a different study.

**MM F8 — The response to a young person's silence is pursuit, with no stopping rule.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH.** I could not find anything in the paper that answers this, and the handbook
machinery does not either (Settle Protocol and the two-adult rule govern *how* a conversation happens,
not *whether pursuit stops*). The structural asymmetry is genuinely sharp: [36]–[37] make going after
the quiet a *maturity marker* that "matures into seeking," so the healthier the room reads on its own
instrument, the more reliably a withdrawing teenager is pursued, and there is no rung at which "let her
be" is correct. Her aggregate point is also verified — [29]'s "everyone, most, or a few" cannot see a
person who is in the residual every week for twenty-two weeks. **The remedy is superior instrumentation,
not a concession:** "sought-declined" is a *discriminating* datum, since a room that cannot hold a
refusal is a different room from one that can. Downgraded from HIGH only because it is a specification
gap in a protocol rather than a demonstrated harm, and the calibration pages [38] already exist to hold
it. **Flagged secondarily as safeguarding** — see Part III.

**MM F9 — Theological action research's insider half is quoted and left unfilled.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH; remedy requires repair.** The internal-consistency catch is clean and is not
an imported standard: [59] itself says TAR "is a partnership between insiders and outsiders, not a
practitioner reporting alone," and then adds an outsider and a leader-internal surprise line. Her
reading of Cameron is correct — the insiders are the practising community. **Two defects in the remedy
she does not see.** (a) The Thursday call's own liturgy [38] includes "look at the flock (every follow-up
closed or carried, each with a name and an owner)" — that section *is* named confidential material, so
seating participants on that call collides with "names stop at the shepherd" [44]. A separate quarterly
co-readers' half-hour is the constraint-respecting version. (b) Seating a parent and a teenager together
and asking "what are we getting wrong" reproduces the exact asymmetry her own F5 and F10 identify.
Rotate them into different quarters.

**MM F10 — "A family is never divided" leaves a young person with no door.**
**SURVIVES — HIGH.** See Part III. Flagged as safeguarding.

**MM F11 — Leader-first is applied to the one instrument that could revise the design.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** She properly declines to re-file the non-independence concession at [41], and
the new half — that the self-read is also the room's *only* route to revising the year — is real and

formed answer before hearing the leader's. That does not eliminate anchoring but it refutes "essentially
no others." Her remedy — leader last on Q3 only — is well targeted, since Q3 is the one question where
[20]'s modeling rationale is weakest, and it is free.

**MM F12 — "Costly" is not indexed to who pays.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** The diagnosis substantially restates the Stage A limitation at [59] ("this design
reads the room as a whole and never separates the readings by generation, so a room can register as safe
while... the teenagers... [are] never heard on its own"), applied to a second instrument, and she does not
flag the overlap. The extension is legitimate but not novel. **What is new and valuable is the remedy:**
tagging [29]'s costliest-telling line with *adult / young person* is the cheapest available partial
answer to a limitation the paper has already confessed and left standing, and the same tag serves F5's
obligation test and Osmer 4's echo line. Rated MEDIUM on the strength of the remedy, not the diagnosis.

**MM F13 — "Self-selected" names the fact of selection and never the mechanism.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH** (filed MEDIUM; raised). This is stronger than Osmer 1 because every filter is
derived from the paper's own text rather than from sociology the author must go collect. Two observations
are the best unclaimed material in either review: (a) **the Wednesday-at-four filter**, made vivid by the

families producing six-to-fifteen participants is 2–2.5 people per household, so "the parents," plural,
at [16] is already not what many of these households will look like, and "in every practice the parents
start" is under-specified for a single-adult home. Her scope reduction is also fair on the paper's own
terms, since [59] says "the smaller claim is the faithful one." **Stake-worthy.**

**MM F14 — Which parent goes first is never recorded.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** The tick-mark remedy survives and is genuinely free, and F13's denominator makes
it *more* valuable, since the same tally records which households have one available adult. But the
headship prediction ("either *the father always*... or *the mother always*") is sociological speculation
with no support in the paper. The Eph 6:4 point — mandate taken, restraint dropped — is a fair marginal

"no teenager is honest in front of their mother" catch is fair as a one-word style fix, but note the

**MM F15 — The prototype family is undisclosed and may be in the cohort.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM-HIGH** (filed MEDIUM; raised). Underrated by its own author. Verified: [8] and [16]
both cite eighteen months with one family and the relationship is never stated. The confound is concrete
and unnoticed by the paper — if that family is enrolled, they enter in year three of the practice while
everyone else enters in week one, in a cohort where one family is a fifth to a third of the denominator,

raises it:** this is a *preregistration* paper by its own account [8], so the decision to flag their
readings in a separate column must be made *before* the year or it cannot be made honestly afterward.
Costs one sentence and one column; releases nothing; separates no one.

**MM F16 — The families' own congregations are absent from the ecology and the ethics.** *(merge with Osmer 10)*
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Her distinctive contribution over Osmer 10 is the symmetry argument, which turns
the paper's own conviction on itself: [16] commits that a supporting work must "encourage and equip

She is also right that Wuthnow is cited for the wrong half — fn 9 uses him only for small groups drifting
toward comfortable mutual support, not for small groups becoming their members' primary religious
community, which is the apposite finding here. Her mechanism (one question added to the permission
conversation already happening in each home, with a costless no) is the cheaper and better remedy.

**MM F17 — Adolescents figured as adults-in-preparation, never as present theological agents.**
**WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** Three parts, unequal. (a) "There is no channel by which a fifteen-year-old's
theological insight changes the curriculum" overshoots — the channels are the same ones open to adults
(the feedback round at [20], where the room answers the same two questions; the self-read), and the teen
room-build crew [5] and teen-led nights [19] are present-tense agency. The finding reduces to: teens have
no *distinctive* channel and the stated aim for them is future-tense. (b) **The 1 Cor 14:26–29 point
survives cleanly and is her best exegetical catch:** [12] cites it to warrant the corporate turn, and its
logic is Spirit-distribution without regard to standing, while [16] fixes a permanent speaking order by
generation. Naming that tension costs one sentence. (c) The "college dormitories" class script at [19] is
correct and cheap to fix, and connects to F13's income filter. (d) Her session proposal is treated
separately below as a convergence.

**MM F18 — The room never gets to contest the account written about it.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM.** Member checking is a standard participatory norm and the paper claims the
participatory method [59] while doing none of it. The remedy is well-built: it uses the register [12]
already mandates ("story and specific moments"), carries no levels, rungs, or names, releases nothing
confidential, and can be spoken at the last session rather than issued as a family-facing page — which
matters, because the written-page option would itself trigger the permission rule at [46].

**MM F19 — The outside reader is author-chosen, sees one announced session, and is recorded not published.**
**SURVIVES — MEDIUM** (filed LOW; raised). Merge with Osmer 12. Two of her three points are free and
correct (recruitment by a third party; an unannounced visit inside a named window). The third —

## PART III — SAFEGUARDING ADJUDICATION (Osmer 6 / MM F10, with MM F8 secondary)

**FLAGGED SEPARATELY. Both findings SURVIVE at HIGH. This is a safeguarding matter, not a
methodological one, and should be adjudicated on that clock.**

**The paper's state, verified.** [17] promises confidentiality aloud "and its legal limits named
honestly" — that is mandated-reporting disclosure and I credit it. [16] fixes "a family is never divided,
and in every practice the parents start." [59] concedes the generational limit in its own words. Nothing
in the paper describes a route by which a young person initiates a conversation, and the paper does not
say where the sought-stray follow-up happens, who is present, or what is held — Osmer's charge on that is
factually exact.

**Does the handbook machinery already provide the door? No — and the distinction is precise.**

The Settle Protocol (weight *received*), a Companion watching the teen during a parent's telling, and the
48-hour follow-up rule (which is in the paper, at [38]) are all **team-initiated and observation-triggered**.
Every one of them fires on a signal the adults detect. The never-alone two-adult rule and the mandatory-
reporting protocols are **governance of a conversation, not a route to one** — MM's formulation is exactly
right: "mandated reporting is a rule about what a leader does *after* a disclosure. It is not a route to
disclosure."

The young person whose cost is at home is precisely the person most likely to show nothing, because
concealment is the adaptive behavior. Observation-triggered machinery cannot reach her by construction.

**So the machinery is real and materially mitigates the response side. It does not supply the initiation
side, and — decisively — none of it is announced to participants.** A handbook the families never read
cannot tell a fourteen-year-old she may ask.

**The constraint argument is correct.** Both reviewers make the same distinction — a constraint forbidding
the *division* of the circle does not forbid an individually available *door* — and neither proposes a
teen-only session, a separated family, or staged parent-child conflict. The paper already implies such
conversations occur, since the sought stray means someone "goes after them" [36]. The door exists in
practice and is unnamed. Naming it costs one sentence in a script already spoken aloud weekly.

**Where the two remedies differ, and my adjudication.** Osmer's ("any companion") is weaker: a companion is
inside the circle and, in a small Christian school, socially entangled with the parents. MM's (an adult
*outside the circle*, named aloud weekly) is stronger on independence but introduces costs she does not
weigh — a school counselor is a mandated reporter under the *school's* authority rather than the program's,
is not under the measurement covenant, is not formed in the container, and may be unknown to the families.

**Constraint-respecting synthesis I would put to the author:**
1. Name the door aloud in the container script, once, in the same breath as the confidentiality limits:
   any person in this room, of any age, may ask any companion — or [named adult outside the circle] — for a
   conversation at any time, and does not need a reason.
2. Publish in the handbook what already exists and is invisible: the venue, the two-adult rule, what is
   held and what must be passed on, and what happens if what is said concerns someone in the room.
3. Record on the debrief sheet only *that* such a conversation occurred — never content, never a name.
   A year in which no young person walks through that door is itself a reading.
4. **Pair it with MM F8's stopping rule.** These are one item seen from two sides: the design's only route
   *to* a quiet teenager is adult-initiated pursuit, and her only route *out* is nothing. Fixing one without
   the other leaves the asymmetry intact.

**One correction to how both reviewers state it.** "This design has no route" is too strong given the
handbook. The accurate charge — and it is still HIGH — is that **the paper describes no route and no route
is announced to participants**, in an article whose central claim is that the container is safe to tell in.
That reframing matters: this is under-description of an existing safeguarding architecture plus a genuine
gap in participant-initiated access, not a program lacking safeguarding. MM's "non-negotiable for
publication" stance is defensible in that narrowed form.

**Convergence assessment:** real in substance, partly artifact in phrasing. The division/door distinction
appears in near-identical words in both reviews and may be an artifact of how the brief stated the
constraint. But the *reasons* differ genuinely — Osmer argues the door is a condition of the safety claim
the paper makes (epistemic and pastoral); MM argues it is a safeguarding obligation independent of any
claim, and she alone identifies the mechanism that concentrates the risk (parents-first + the reciprocity
marker + no session in twenty-two weeks without him). **MM's version is the load-bearing one.**

## PART IV — MM F1 ADJUDICATED IN FULL

**Is the factual claim true? Yes.** I checked every recording surface. [38]'s debrief page carries "the
three sign readings at the room's current rung, the feedback round's echo, a participation read, the
week's specific counts, and follow-ups." [29] records "whether telling happened, whether any of it cost
anything, and what the room did with the costliest one" — a *response* record, not content. [28] scribes
the self-read verbatim, but that is the room talking about *itself*, not about what God said. [34]'s pulse
asks "what has surprised me." [59]'s surprise line is the companion's own. **Nothing anywhere records what
anyone heard.** For a twenty-two-week curriculum whose sole domain is "hearing and obeying God" [18], and
whose sixth practice step is telling someone what was heard [18], that is a real and striking gap.

**Is the diagnostic claim true?** Partly. Content is where operant theology lives, and it is the one place
idiom-convergence would show as *homogenization*. But her stronger claim — "every trace in this
architecture rises" — is false: marker 4 at [50], "a disagreement processed without the leader," would be
*suppressed* by idiom-convergence, not raised; the teen-led absence test would be suppressed; and [41] is
the paper's own explicit defense against the felt-warmth confound her mechanism runs through. She praises
[42] and [41] in her §1 and then argues in §5 as though they were absent.

**Is the remedy safe? Yes, with two repairs.**

The pastoral-prudence objection is real but smaller than it first looks. The covenant forbids *release*,
not *recording*; the team already holds weekly named sheets and verbatim self-read notes [28], [38]; and
under the paper's own [46] rule a scribed utterance is not "paper a participant touches," so it falls on
the care side. One unnamed line is a marginal, not categorical, increase in the artifact.

**Repair 1 — the third-party problem, which she does not address.** What a person says God told them will
often name or identify someone else ("God showed me I've been harsh with my daughter"). An unnamed
*speaker* does not produce an unnamed *subject*, and a written record of a private spiritual claim about a
non-consenting third party is a different category from a sign reading. **Narrow the remedy to: one
verbatim line, speaker unnamed, excluding anything that names or identifies a third party.**

**Repair 2 — it must land with F3.** Her own F3 argues participants have not been told a written record
exists. Adding a new *category* of record without adopting F3's disclosure clause makes F3 worse. The two
are compatible only in that order: disclose first, then add the line.

**One cost she does not weigh.** [34]'s validity argument is "a room that does not feel surveilled behaves
like itself." A scribe visibly writing down what someone just said God told them could make the tell step
performance-sensitive — which is the exact contamination the paper's refusals exist to prevent. This is
mitigated by the fact that the scribe is *already* visibly writing during the self-read [28], but it is a
real trade the author should weigh, not a free lunch as she presents it.

**Verdict: WEAKENED — MEDIUM-HIGH.** The gap is real, striking, and worth fixing. The remedy is
proportionate with the two repairs. The rhetoric that this is the paper's single largest defect, and that
the design is blind to its most likely failure, should not survive into synthesis.

## PART V — MM F4 ADJUDICATED

**Where she is right.** [50] does list "vulnerability answered with vulnerability across a generation line
(a parent's costly tell answered by a teen's)" among the things a Companion watches for, and the pressure
she describes is real: the room's most powerful adult goes first by rule, with something costly, in public,
beside the child.

**Where the finding fails.** She charges that *counting* it makes the Strathern mistake. Strathern's
mechanism requires the measured party to be under pressure *from the measurement* — "when a measure becomes
a target, it ceases to be a good measure" describes what happens when a measure is announced and pressed for
control. Test the paper against its own commitments: the marker is **not announced** [45], **not scored**
[44], **not disclosed to a participant or a parent** [12], [45], and is read at room level. The teenager does
not know it exists.

**And the causal claim inverts.** She writes that "the parent's disclosure *is* the target, delivered face to
face" — which concedes the case. The pressure is generated by the **parents-first liturgy**, not by the
observation. Delete the marker tomorrow and not one ounce of pressure on that teenager changes. So the
marker is not the mechanism, and the paper's no-targets commitment is **not breached**.

**What survives.** A second-order effect: what a team watches for shapes where its attention and
encouragement go — which is Osmer 7's ground (level shapes care), not Strathern's. And the underlying
concern about reciprocity pressure on a minor is entirely real, but it belongs to **F5** (the warrant) and
**F10** (the door), where it is already filed at HIGH.

**The remedy is better than the finding and should be adopted anyway.** "Did anyone decline or pass, and did
the room take the pass without repair-work?" reads the room's capacity to hold a non-answer, which is
Edmondson's construct correctly applied rather than borrowed. It is the only proposal in either review that
would *detect* coerced reciprocity. I would keep the existing marker and **add** the pass-read rather than
re-point it, since the pass-read is the discriminator and the reciprocity marker is the thing it
discriminates against. Note it pairs with MM F8's "sought-declined": **two independent findings converge on
the fact that the design has no read for a refusal**, which is a real pattern and a better synthesis item
than either finding alone.

**Verdict: WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** Strathern charge REFUTED; concern real but relocated; remedy adopted.

## PART VI — MM F6 vs. THE PRIOR ROUND, ADJUDICATED

**Both sides are right, because they are talking about two different things, and the conflict is largely
verbal.** Her own title concedes it: "cannot bear the inferential weight **[56] puts on it**" — she attacks
the inference, not the instrument.

**The prior round is right about the instrument.** Break-survival is behavioral, unobtrusive, requires
nothing from participants and nothing of observers but counting, is repeated three times within-design, and
is the one trace that can embarrass the theory. Osmer independently reaches the same judgment in his §1:
"the cleverest measurement move in the paper... the one place in the architecture where a claim about
internalization could be embarrassed by data that no one on the team rated." **She does not dispute any of
this.**

**She is right about [56]'s wording, on one specific point.** [56] commits in advance to reading a flat line
as disconfirming "in a cohort that attends faithfully," and she correctly identifies that the qualifier
fails: attendance at four o'clock on a Wednesday is itself a capacity measure (see F13's Wednesday-at-four
filter), so the condition controls for the confound using a proxy for the confound. With three to six
families [8], one hard winter in two households dominates the line.

**Where she overshoots, and it matters.** She ignores the repeated-measures structure stated in the very
sentence she quotes: "The year has three scheduled structure-stoppages, and the same three counts are taken
at each" [56]. Household capacity is largely a between-family, time-stable factor; the design reads
*within-family change across three break points*. That is precisely the structure that handles a stable
capacity confound. The confound bites where she says — a single bad break in a tiny cohort dragging the
trend — and not where she implies, which is the instrument as such. She gives the design no credit for this.

**Adjudication.** The prior round is right that break-survival is the paper's best and most trustworthy
*instrument*; MM is right that **[56]'s disconfirmation wording** is under-specified. There is no real
conflict once instrument and inference are separated. What is wrong is her severity.

**Her remedy is genuinely good and should be adopted**, because it makes the condition *stronger*, not
weaker, and uses material already in the design — [28]'s re-entry check-in "asks how the break treated
everyone's practice, and honest answers include lapsed ones." Rewriting [56] so that a flat line disconfirms
only when the room's own account of the lapse is about *wanting to* rather than *being able to* costs one
sheet line and one clause.

**Verdict: WEAKENED — MEDIUM.** Retarget from the instrument to [56]'s wording. Do not let this be
synthesized as "the paper's best instrument is unsound."

8. **THE CENTRAL CONVERGENCE ITSELF** (Osmer 5 ≈ MM F1/§5). **Largely an artifact of a shared sentence.**

- **Osmer 12 + MM F19** (the outside reader: counterpart page, third-party recruitment, unannounced window,
  verbatim publication) — collectively the cheapest large gain available to the paper.
- **Osmer 6 / MM F10** — but as a safeguarding item on a different clock; see Part III.

### The findings that should not have been filed

- **MM F4's Strathern charge.** Causally inverted. The pressure comes from the liturgy, not the marker;
  removing the marker changes nothing; the paper's no-targets commitment is not breached. *The remedy should
  survive; the finding should not.*
- **The absolute form of Osmer 5 and MM §5** — "no observation anywhere," "every trace rises." Refuted by
  [41], [42], [50] marker 4, [56], and the teen-led absence test, all of which both reviewers praise
  elsewhere in their own §1s.
- **Osmer 3's core demand** — a two-to-three-page interpretive section, graded against a four-task framework
  the paper never adopts, in a document that calls itself "a field report offered before the field has
  reported" [6]. Textbook persona overreach; Osmer half-concedes it in §4 and supplies the proportionate fix
  himself (one genre-mapping paragraph).
- **MM F7's re-unit demand** — asking that the household be the analytic unit is asking for a different study,
  and Osmer independently evaluated and *accepted* the refusals she objects to. Two reviewers disagreeing is

- **MM F14's headship prediction** — sociological speculation with no textual support. The tick mark
  survives; the prediction does not.
- **MM F17(a)** — "no channel by which a teenager's insight changes the curriculum" is refuted by [5], [19],
  [20], and [12]; teens have the same channels adults have. The 1 Cor 14 tension and the class-script fix
  survive on their own.

### Findings whose remedy would damage the paper

- **MM F7's one-word swap.** Changing [34]'s pulse item from "what has surprised me" to "what surprised you
  about how this went at home" *narrows* the only open, uncategorized question on the family-facing corpus —
  which is precisely the thing Osmer 2 says the design is starved of. Net loss. **Add** an item; do not swap.
- **MM F9's Thursday-call seating.** The call's own liturgy [38] includes "look at the flock (every follow-up
  closed or carried, each with a name and an owner)." That section *is* the confidential material, so seating
  participants on that call breaches "names stop at the shepherd" [44]. And seating a parent and a teenager
  together to answer "what are we getting wrong" reproduces the exact asymmetry her own F5 and F10 identify.
  A separate co-readers' half-hour, with parent and teen rotated into different quarters, gets the value
  without the damage.
- **MM F3's access clause.** "You can ask any of us what is written about you" collides directly with [12]
  ("never disclosed to a participant or a parent") and would open a parent→teen disclosure channel the design
  has deliberately closed. The *disclosure* half of the remedy is essential; the *access* half must be scoped
  or dropped.
- **MM F1's remedy without repairs.** Recording content without excluding third-party identifiers creates a
  written record of private spiritual claims about non-consenting named people; and adding a new record
  category without first adopting F3's disclosure clause makes F3 worse. Also weigh the surveillance cost
  against [34]'s own validity argument — she presents it as free and it is not.
- **Osmer 2's margin-to-column.** Duplicates the surprise line already at [59] on a one-page sheet whose
  lightness the paper argues is load-bearing ("a heavy instrument does not get filled out in a real
  ministry" [38]). Take the permission-conversation half only.
- **A general caution for synthesis.** Between the two reviews there are roughly fifteen proposed additions
  to a one-page debrief sheet the paper defends explicitly on grounds of lightness. Each is individually
  cheap; collectively they would destroy the instrument. If the author adopts a set, it must be **ranked and
  capped**. My ranking of the debrief-sheet additions by value per line: (1) MM F4's pass-read + MM F8's
  sought-declined [one line, two findings, and the design's only read for a refusal]; (2) Osmer 5's
  outward-crossing line [the only outward trace available]; (3) MM F12's adult/young-person tag [two
  characters, partially answers a Stage A-confessed limitation]; (4) Osmer 13's who-led-and-was-it-their-first
  [sharpens a preregistered condition]; (5) MM F14's which-parent tick. Everything below that should wait.

**Primary: Osmer 6 / MM F10 — HIGH.** Full adjudication in Part III. Restated accurately: the paper
describes no participant-initiated route and announces none to participants, in an article whose central

**Secondary and inseparable: MM F8 — MEDIUM-HIGH.** The sought stray has no stopping rule, no specified

**Adjacent, not safeguarding but bearing on it: MM F2** (a minor's own assent) and **MM F5** (a disclosure

immediate safeguarding matter; both raise the stakes of the primary one and argue for handling all four
