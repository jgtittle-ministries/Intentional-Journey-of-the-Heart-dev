# Stage B Methodology Review — Case Study Design

**Reviewer:** Robert K. Yin
**Manuscript:** "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart," draft v2.3 (corrected 2026-08-19)
**Assignment:** Evaluate as case study design.

---

A preliminary word, because it governs everything below. This manuscript describes a single-case
study. It does not use the phrase. It calls itself "action research" [9] and a "protocol paper," and
both descriptions are true, but neither one supplies a design. Action research tells a reader where
the investigator is standing. Case study tells a reader how the evidence will be assembled, bounded,
traced, and argued. The author has adopted the stance and skipped the design, and nearly every
finding I file below is a consequence of that one omission.

I want to be exact about my objection, because the field has a bad habit of using "case study" as an
apology for small n, and this author would be entitled to resent that. My position is the opposite
one. A single case can carry a serious argument — can corroborate a theory, can refine it, can
falsify it outright — but only if five design components are settled before data collection: the
study's question, its propositions, its unit of analysis, the logic linking data to propositions, and
the criteria for interpreting findings. This manuscript has the first two, in unusually good shape.
It does not have the third, fourth, or fifth. That is not a small gap. It is the difference between a
year that can answer something and a year that will produce a great deal of sincere material nobody
can adjudicate.

---

## 1. What is methodologically sound here

I do not often get to write a long version of this section.

**The design is published before the results, with its failure conditions attached.** [2]: "The
design is presented before its results, with disconfirmation conditions stated in advance: the ways a
year could prove this design wrong are part of the design." Whatever else I say below about how those
conditions are built, the decision to write them at all puts this study ahead of most published case
research, which reasons backward from what happened and calls the result a finding. Section [52]–[58]
is the manuscript's spine and should survive revision intact.

**The denominator is stated up front.** [8]: "The cohort is designed for three to six families, six
to fifteen participants in all, with a Companion team of two to four; every count below should be
read against that denominator, and the enrolled number will be reported with the results." Field
reports routinely publish counts with the denominator suppressed. This one refuses to, in advance.

**Prior observation is disclosed rather than absorbed.** [8]: "Observation did not begin with this
article: the design was prototyped with one family across eighteen months, two versions of the
quarterly self-read were built and abandoned in the field." Investigators habitually let pilot
experience quietly become findings. This one draws the line and says where it is. I will complain
below that the line is in the wrong place, but the disclosure itself is exemplary.

**The instruments are published in full.** [24] and footnote 8: "a protocol whose instruments cannot
be inspected cannot be replicated, extended, or corrected." This is the substance of a case study
protocol even though the manuscript does not organize it as one, and it is more than most published
case studies make available. Keep it, and see Finding 9 for how to complete it.

**Disagreement between sources is designated as the finding, not the noise.** [32]: "match the
instrument to the level, and treat disagreement between sources as data rather than noise," developed
at [44]: "these gaps are the findings, and in a pastoral setting they are also the prayer list."
This is triangulation used correctly. Most multi-source designs collect several sources hoping they
will agree, which is the one outcome that teaches nothing. The author has understood what
converging lines of inquiry are actually for. My Finding 7 concerns whether the sources are
independent enough to converge or diverge meaningfully, but the reasoning is right.

**The instrument has an explicit missing-data category.** [37]: "marks each sign seen, not yet, or no
occasion (an event-contingent sign with no event is missing data, not failure)." Field instruments
almost never distinguish "did not happen" from "had no opportunity to happen," and the failure to
distinguish them is a standard source of spurious negative findings. The author saw it unaided. See
Finding 12 for the decision rule this category still needs.

**The instrument ships with calibration.** [38]: "worked examples of what each reading looks like at
each rung sit on two teaching pages at the front of the pack, so a companion who has never read a
word of the underlying research can still mark honestly." Training on the protocol before the field
is a requirement my own texts insist on and that practitioners routinely skip. Two pages at the front
of the pack is exactly the right instinct, and Finding 13 shows it can be made to do a second job for
free.

**The team is pre-briefed against two predictable misreadings.** [42]: "open disagreement tends to
arrive before mature trust, not after it... A team that does not know these two findings will misread
its best weeks as its worst and its worst as its best." This is rival-pattern reasoning applied to
the observers, and it demonstrates that the author already knows how to do the thing I will spend
Findings 5 and 6 asking him to do to his own thesis. He has protected his team from misreading the
room. He has not yet protected the study from misreading itself.

**Behavioral traces are counted mechanically and anonymously.** [50]: "whether home practice
survived, how many walked back in with the return card, how many minutes the room took to become
truly present again. We count all three at every re-entry, with no participant named in any count."
These are unobtrusive measures, and in my judgment they are the strongest evidence in the entire
design — stronger than the author appears to know. Finding 7 asks him to promote them.

**One structural probe is designed rather than awaited.** [19]: "When a sixteen-year-old holds the
room and the container survives in a younger voice, the observing community has just observed
something no survey could elicit." A designed test — an event the protocol schedules in order to see
what it exposes — is worth a great deal more than an opportunistic observation, and this one is
scheduled at least five times [23]. It is a genuine methodological contribution and the manuscript
undersells it.

**The limitation section is stated at full strength.** [59]: "the covenant was written by the designer
and signed by a team he selected and formed; the disconfirmation conditions were written by the
designer and will be read through instruments he built... Nothing in the design is positioned to
notice what all of us take for granted." And the outside reader's single question — "judging only by
what this program does, what does it appear to believe?" — is a construct check that requires no
access to protected material and is recorded "whether or not the team recognizes itself in it." That
last clause is a proper protocol rule: it removes a degree of freedom in advance. I would keep the
outside reader and give it more to do (Findings 10 and 5).

**The operational rule at [46] shows the author can write operational definitions.** "The line
between the two is physical: if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for; if it is a
shepherd's eye held under a signed covenant, it is care." That is a definition a stranger could
apply. Finding 12 asks only that he write four more of them, for the constructs that carry
falsification weight.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — The study is a single-case study and does not say so, so its design components are absent

**Severity: HIGH**

[9]: "This is action research: the practitioner stands inside the practice being studied, the inquiry
aims at improving that practice rather than holding it still, and looking and doing alternate in
cycles. That is not a hedge against rigor but a different kind of rigor."

I agree with the sentence and it does not do the work the manuscript needs it to do. Action research
specifies the investigator's relation to the practice. It does not specify a unit of analysis, it
does not require propositions, it supplies no chain of evidence, and it has no doctrine of
generalization. The manuscript is borrowing its methodological warrant from a tradition that does not
issue the particular warrants its claims require. The claim at [2] — that "the ways a year could
prove this design wrong are part of the design" — is a case study claim, and it needs case study
machinery.

The two are entirely compatible. What the author is running is an action-research single-case study,
and naming it that costs nothing and buys the whole apparatus.

**Remedy.** One page at the front of the protocol pack, never shown to a participant, stating the
five design components: (a) the study question, written as a question; (b) the propositions, which
are already drafted at [54]–[58]; (c) the unit of analysis (Finding 2); (d) the logic linking data to
propositions (Finding 6); (e) the criteria for interpreting findings (Finding 4). This is an
afternoon's work and it is the precondition for every other remedy in this review.

---

### Finding 2 — The unit of analysis is never specified, and four candidates are in play at once

**Severity: HIGH**

This is the failure mode that ruins more case studies than all others combined, and the manuscript
walks straight into it because its own second principle puts two units in the design deliberately.

[12]: "Second, measure the group and the person on separate ladders: the group and the individuals in
it carry separate but interdependent measurements of level."

Read across the manuscript, four different entities are being studied:

- **the room** — the group ladder [12], the container [17], the three vital signs read at the room's
  rung [37], the vocabulary migration [41], the re-entry counts [50];
- **the family** — the permission unit [21], the enrollment unit ("three to six families" [8]), the
  practice unit ("breaks into family pairs" [29]), the attrition unit [59];
- **the individual participant** — "each individual's progress from wherever they start will be
  tracked to see the actual change" [12];
- **the curriculum itself** — [21]: "We are grading the curriculum, never the family," which is the
  only place the manuscript states outright what it thinks it is studying.

A fifth, the Companion team, is deferred to a successor article [61] but is doing evidentiary work
here throughout.

These are not interchangeable. Findings at one level do not license conclusions at another, and the
whole design of data collection follows from which one is the case. My reading is that the correct
design is **single-case embedded**: the case is the pilot cohort — one room, one school, one year,
bounded by the twenty-two Wednesdays — with families and individuals as embedded subunits, and the
curriculum as the *theory under test* rather than as the case. The manuscript is built as an embedded
design and analyzed as though it were holistic in some passages and as though the subunits were the
study in others.

The specific hazard in embedded designs is that the investigator slides down to the subunits, does
all the analysis there, and never returns to the case as a whole. That is what [12] does when it
promises average individual movement. The reverse hazard also appears: the room's readings at [37]
are used to speak about individuals' internalization without any statement of how a room-level rung
relates to a person-level level, though [12] insists they are "separate but interdependent." The word
"interdependent" is doing enormous unexamined work. Nothing in the design says what the dependence
is, which means nothing in the data can test it.

**Remedy.** Declare it: *single-case (embedded) design; case = the pilot cohort across twenty-two
Wednesdays; subunits = families and individual participants; the curriculum's level-sequencing theory
is the object of test, not the case.* Then, for each of the five disconfirmation conditions
[54]–[58], write the level at which it is adjudicated. Two of them are room-level ([54], [58]), one
is room-level but built from person-level counts ([56]), one is instrument-level ([55]), one is
event-level ([57]). Writing the level next to each takes ten minutes and prevents a year of
cross-level inference.

### Finding 3 — "On average, one level or more" is a statistical-generalization claim inside a design that cannot support one

**Severity: HIGH**

[12]: "The desire is that each individual will move, on average, one level or more."

Three separate problems sit in that sentence.

First, it averages an ordinal, admittedly unvalidated ladder — the manuscript itself declares at [12]
that Krathwohl's taxonomy "was never validated as a developmental stage model" — as though the
distance from Receiving to Responding were commensurable with the distance from Valuing to
Organization. It is not, and the manuscript's own extension of level four into "the corporate turn"
makes it less so.

Second, it averages across six to fifteen people who are not independent observations. They are
nested inside three to six families, and the families are nested inside one room, and the entire
design rests on the premise that they influence each other — that is what "vulnerability is
reciprocal" [16] means. Averaging non-independent units and reporting the mean as though it
characterized a population is the error that sampling logic exists to prevent, and there is no
sampling logic here.

Third, and this is the deeper point, an average is the wrong *form* of claim for this design. A
single case does not generalize by aggregation. It generalizes by pattern — by predicting a
configuration of observations with enough discriminating detail that the world can decline to supply
it.

**Remedy.** Replace the average with a predicted pattern, stated per person and adjudicable per
person. For example: *we predict that every participant attending at least sixteen of twenty-two
sessions will show movement on at least one sign form, that none will be read lower in May than in
October, and that the first movement observed in any participant will be on the sign the quarter's
practices were designed to enable.* That is falsifiable person by person, requires no arithmetic on
an ordinal scale, and — importantly for this author — is fully compatible with the rule that the
target is never announced and no one is ever scored, since it lives on the team's side of the paper
exactly as the current desire does.

### Finding 4 — The propositions are excellent and have no criteria for interpreting findings

**Severity: HIGH**

The disconfirmation conditions are, as propositions, better than most. But a proposition without a
decision rule cannot be adjudicated, and the fifth design component — criteria for interpreting the
findings — is nowhere in the manuscript. Take them in turn:

- [54] "A cohort still showing not yet on first forms in February, or no growing form all year,
  falsifies either the phasing or the curriculum." This one is nearly crisp. It still needs: *not yet
  on how many of the three signs? In how many of February's weeks? Read at what point in the month?*
- [55] "If a year of five-page self-reports and weekly observation produces essentially no
  discrepancies." "Essentially no" is not a criterion. What counts as one discrepancy? Who declares
  it? How many across the year would satisfy the prediction, and how few would break it?
- [56] "Flat or declining break-survival across the year, in a cohort that attends faithfully,
  disconfirms the claim." There are three break counts. With three points and a denominator of three
  to six families, nearly any sequence is compatible with nearly any story. "Flat" needs a number in
  advance, and "attends faithfully" needs a threshold in advance.
- [57] "If teen-led nights consistently require adult rescue into the spring, the entrust-early
  design is wrong, or the pace is." "Rescue" is undefined (see Finding 12) and "consistently" is
  undefined. This is the condition most likely to be settled by the affection of the person judging
  it.
- [58] "Stagnant vocabulary alongside advancing sign-readings would break the proposed mapping."
  Vocabulary migration is to be read from three scribed self-reads. Coded by whom, against what
  decision rule, and what magnitude of shift counts as migration rather than as three occasions of
  people using warm words?

The manuscript invokes preregistration at [8] specifically because it "limits the choices a
researcher can make after seeing the data." The choices it limits are exactly these. Publishing the
propositions without the decision rules leaves every one of the constrained choices open, and leaves
them open to a solo investigator who, by his own account at [44], loves the room.

**Remedy.** One additional column on the disconfirmation page: for each condition, the observation
that would satisfy it and the observation that would break it, in countable terms, written before
the data exist. Where a number cannot honestly be pre-specified, say so and name who will adjudicate
and on what basis — an honest "the lead companion will judge this and the reasoning will be recorded
in the decisions log" is far better than silence, because it converts an invisible degree of freedom
into a visible one. This is one page, on the team's side, touching nothing a family sees.

### Finding 5 — No rival explanations are entertained anywhere in the design

**Severity: HIGH**

[59] names the study's structural limits honestly — "One room, one year, one school, one founder.
There is no control cohort; families self-selected" — and then stops. Naming the absence of a control
is not the same as identifying the specific alternative accounts a control would have excluded and
deciding what to do about each. The manuscript nowhere asks: *what else, besides this curriculum,
would produce exactly these observations?* That question is the core analytic activity of case study
research, and its absence means the disconfirmation conditions test the design against nothing but
its own null.

I take the serious rivals in order.

**Maturation.** Adolescents change substantially across a school year; families deepen across
eighteen months of shared Wednesdays regardless of what happens in the room. Every one of the sign
progressions at [37] — answering to offering, settling to absorbing, prompted noticing to naming — is
what ordinary acquaintance produces. **The design cannot currently rule this out at all.**

**Self-selection.** Conceded at [59] and then not used. Families who volunteer for a family-integrated
discipleship pilot are already predisposed to the outcome. **Partly addressable now, at no cost:**
the pre-series survey [34] already captures baseline practice frequency and "whether anyone outside
the family knows their interior life." What is missing is the *invitation denominator* — how many
families were approached, how many declined, and (in aggregate, no names) what the decliners' stated
reasons were. That single count converts self-selection from an admitted weakness into a described
one, and it is available on the day the cohort forms.

**The founder effect.** The most serious rival here, because the author is the designer, the
practitioner, and the observer simultaneously — a rival that contaminates the treatment and the
instrument in the same motion. If the room's warmth and candor track the founder's presence rather
than the curriculum, every reading rises together and nothing in the current design would show it.
**Not addressable now; cheaply addressable with one change** (below).

**Novelty and attention.** A new program with a founder's energy and a school's blessing produces
enthusiasm that is real and temporary. Novelty predicts precisely the manuscript's expected
trajectory: strong first quarter, rising engagement, warm spring self-reports. **Cheaply addressable
(below).**

**Seasonality.** The manuscript's predicted trajectory — first forms by the end of the first quarter,
growing forms across the middle, full forms by spring [54] — is confounded almost point for point
with the affective curve of an American school year. February, the month named in the falsification
condition, is the trough of every school calendar. Spring, when the full forms are predicted, is the
calendar's own upswing. **Not addressable by design; addressable by record.**

**Instrumentation.** Treated separately at Finding 13.

**Implementation rather than substance.** Treated separately at Finding 6, because it is a rival to
the paper's thesis rather than to its outcome.

**The cheapest addition against the most serious rival.** The founder effect is the one I would spend
the money on, because it is the only rival that corrupts the instrument as well as the treatment. The
addition is one line in the year's plan: **schedule two ordinary sessions — not teen-led nights, not
quarter re-entries, ordinary middle-of-quarter Wednesdays — at which the author is absent and a
co-companion leads, one in the second quarter and one in the fourth.** The same debrief sheet is
completed by the same team. The founder does not read those two sheets until the year is over. If the
readings are indistinguishable from adjacent weeks, the founder rival is weakened by direct
observation rather than by assurance. If they collapse, the study has learned the single most
important thing it could learn about itself, and learned it in time to say so. The design already
contains this move philosophically — [12]'s fifth principle is that "at the top, the curriculum
recedes," and [19]'s leadership absence test is the same logic applied to teens. Applying it to the
founder is consistent with the author's own convictions, costs two evenings, separates no families,
grades no one, and announces nothing.

**Second cheapest, against novelty.** One additional count, eight weeks after the last Wednesday, in
the summer, with no program running: the same three break-survival questions on the same unsigned
card, returned to the same basket, from families who already said yes to five pages. Novelty predicts
collapse; internalization predicts survival. It is a sixth page against a five-page corpus and it can
be folded into the year's permission conversation at [21] before the first session, so that it is
consented at the same time as everything else.

**Third, against seasonality, costs nothing at all.** The debrief sheet already has "a margin for
exactly this kind of small event" [27]. Add to that margin one contextual clause per week naming what
the school's own calendar was doing — exam week, illness, a sports season, the week before a break.
At year's end the team can at least ask whether its trough weeks were the school's trough weeks.
Without that line the question cannot be asked at all.

### Finding 6 — The direct rival to the manuscript's own thesis is not among the disconfirmation conditions

**Severity: HIGH**

This is the finding I would most want the author to sit with.

The manuscript's thesis is not that a good weekly gathering forms people. It is the stronger and more
interesting claim that a curriculum *sequenced by level of internalization* is simultaneously a
measurement instrument. [12]: "the sessions are one walk through the levels of internalization, each
presupposing the ones before, designed backward from the transitions they intend to enable." [51]
raises the stakes: "It is the level-three transition, intrinsic valuing displacing external
structure, happening in public."

Now consider the rival: **twenty-two Wednesdays of any warm, well-run, family-integrated,
confidentiality-bound gathering with a meal, a room-build, and a leader who receives correction in
public would produce every observation the design will collect.** The sequencing does no work; the
container, the reciprocity, and the weekly commitment do all of it. The author very nearly says this
himself at [5]: "I have stopped being surprised when the constraint turns out to be the curriculum."

None of the five disconfirmation conditions can distinguish the two. [54] tests whether the ladder
climbs, which the warm-room rival also predicts. [56] tests break survival, which sustained
attachment to a valued weekly gathering also predicts. [57] tests structural durability under teen
leadership, which good facilitation training also predicts. The study as designed can confirm its
thesis without ever testing it.

**Remedy — a sixth disconfirmation condition, based on ordering rather than magnitude.** The
sequencing claim makes one prediction the warm-room rival does not: signs should appear *in the order
the ladder predicts*, and specifically each quarter's designed transitions should not appear before
the sessions built to enable them. Write it as: *If the growing forms of the three signs appear
before the sessions designed to enable them — if a room shows offering, absorbing, and naming during
the first quarter's container work, before the second quarter's hearing practices have run — then the
observations are being produced by the gathering rather than by the sequence, and the backward-design
premise is not doing the work claimed for it.* The debrief sheets will already carry the dates. This
costs one paragraph in the protocol and nothing in the field, and it is the single change that would
convert the study from a demonstration into a test.

I would add that a negative result here would not be a disaster for the author. "A warm,
family-integrated, covenanted room produces measurable internalization signs and the level-sequencing
is optional" is a publishable and useful finding. But it must be findable.

### Finding 7 — Construct validity: the design has three sources of evidence above the ceiling and one perceiver

**Severity: HIGH**

[2] promises "the multi-source, level-matched measurement the theory requires." Multiple sources of
evidence is the first tactic for construct validity, and the manuscript reaches for it correctly. But
convergence and divergence are informative only to the degree the sources are independent of one
another, and above the self-report ceiling — which is where the entire argument lives — they are not.

- The debrief sign-readings [37]–[38] are the Companion team's perception.
- The room's spoken self-read [40] is gathered in front of the team, led by the leader answering
  first, scribed by the team, and mapped by the team "on its own side of the paper" [28]. The
  manuscript already concedes it "is not independent" [41] and reads it "as corroboration and never
  as the reading that settles a question" — proper, and I do not re-file it.
- The quarterly outside reader [59] sees "no confidential notes and no verbatim transcripts" and
  answers a question about the program's apparent beliefs, not about the room's state.
- The family pages [34] are the one source that does not pass through the team — and they are, by
  design and correctly, restricted to the lower levels where self-report is valid.

So the evidentiary base for every claim above the ceiling is the Companion team's perception,
appearing three times in different costumes. This is a different and larger problem than the one the
manuscript already names at [59] (no blind coding, no second coder, no inter-rater check). Those are
reliability problems — the same perceiver might not be consistent. This is a construct validity
problem — there is functionally one perceiver, so consistency would prove nothing.

**Remedy, and it is a re-ranking rather than an addition.** The design already contains a class of
evidence that does not pass through anyone's judgment: mechanical traces. Minutes to a settled room
[27]. Return-card counts [50]. Pulse pages returned to the basket unchased [27]. Attendance.
Practice-survival counts. Who led. How many arrived early to build unasked [27]. These are read off a
clock and counted on fingers; a stranger with the same rule would get the same number.

**Designate the mechanical traces as the primary above-ceiling evidence, and the sign-readings as
corroboration** — the exact inverse of the current hierarchy, and the same demotion the manuscript
already applies, correctly, to the room's self-read. Then the study has one genuinely independent
evidentiary line, one judgment-dependent line, and a stated rule about which settles a question. That
is a defensible multi-source design under the covenant. It requires no new instrument, no outsider,
and no additional page; it requires deciding, in advance and in writing, which source wins when they
disagree. Two of these traces (settle time, "truly settled") still need operational definitions —
Finding 12.

### Finding 8 — There is no chain of evidence, because the debrief sheet records verdicts rather than observations

**Severity: HIGH**

A chain of evidence means a reader can move from the conclusions of a report, through explicit
citation, to specific evidentiary records in a database, to the circumstances under which those
records were made — and back again in the other direction. It is a traceability requirement, not a
disclosure requirement, and it is entirely compatible with confidential material. Court records,
proprietary corporate cases, and clinical case studies all maintain chains of evidence over material
that will never be published.

The break here is at the first link. [38]: "One debrief page per session, filled within a day in
about ten minutes, carries the three sign readings at the room's current rung, the feedback round's
echo, a participation read, the week's specific counts."

A "sign reading at the room's current rung" is a conclusion. It is the output of a judgment, stored
without the input that produced it. When the results article says the room reached *offering* in
November, there is nothing behind that sentence but the word *offering* on a sheet. No one — not a
reader, not a future replicator, not the author himself in May — can get back from the verdict to
what anyone actually did. The manuscript's closing promise at [59], "here is everything it recorded
against the predictions it published," cannot be kept by a record that stored only its own verdicts.

**Remedy, and it fits the one-page rule.** For each sign marked *seen*, one clause naming the
occasion. Eight words. "Seen — lapsed-practice report at re-entry, unprompted." "Seen — M. went after
the quiet one without being pointed." No level, no name that leaves the sheet, no grading, nothing a
participant sees. That clause is the entire difference between a database and a scoresheet.

Three more traceability elements, all costless:

1. **Identifiers.** Every record gets one: `W07-2026-10-14-DEBRIEF`, `RE-2-COUNTS`,
   `SELFREAD-Q2-VERBATIM`. The results article cites the identifier, never the content.
2. **A published derived ledger.** The covenant seals "confidential verbatim notes and journals." A
   dated table of sign readings, counts, settle times, and attendance, with no names and no quoted
   speech, is not a verbatim note. Publishing that ledger as an appendix to the results article gives
   external readers the entire quantitative spine of the chain and discloses nothing the covenant
   protects. I recommend the covenant be amended to say so explicitly, because at present it is
   silent, and silence will be read by a future team as prohibition (see Section 4).
3. **Quotation by specific permission.** Where a verbatim line from a self-read would carry an
   argument, ask that person for permission for that sentence. Permission-first is the manuscript's
   own ethic [21], [46]; extending it to quotation is inside the covenant, not a breach of it, and a
   no costs nothing exactly as it does now.

### Finding 9 — There is no case study database and no case study protocol, and one omission inside them is severe

**Severity: HIGH**

**Protocol.** A case study protocol is not the curriculum. It contains: an overview of the study
(question, propositions, readings); field procedures (access, sources, contingencies); the protocol
questions — which are the *investigator's* standing questions to himself, with the source of evidence
named for each, and are asked of no participant; and a guide for the report. The manuscript has
published its curriculum materials generously [24] and mistaken that for a protocol. I emphasize the
third element because I suspect the author will flinch at the word "questions" given [21]'s
commitment that "Nothing is ever asked." Protocol questions are asked of nobody in the room. They are
the investigator's own reminders — *what evidence bears on whether the container held tonight?* — and
the covenant is no obstacle to them whatever.

Missing field procedures that a volunteer team will need in the first eight weeks: what to do when a
family withdraws; when a session is cancelled; when the scribe is absent; when a reading is ambiguous
between two rungs (Finding 12); who fills the sheet when the lead companion is the one absent
(Finding 5).

**Database.** The database is the organized evidence; the report is the argument about it. Here they
are the same object — the debrief sheet is doing both jobs, which is why Finding 8 bites. The minimum
viable database for a volunteer team is four objects in a shared secured folder, and I mean minimum:

1. **Session files.** One per Wednesday, the debrief sheet as filed, dated and identified, *never
   edited after filing* — corrections are appended and dated, not overwritten.
2. **A counts ledger.** One spreadsheet. One row per session. Columns: attendance, settle-time,
   return cards, practice-survival, each sign's raw weekly mark, current rung, teen-led yes/no,
   founder-present yes/no, school-calendar note.
3. **A verbatim file.** Three scribed self-reads, dated, identified, unedited, sealed.
4. **A decisions log.** See below.

**The severe omission is the decisions log.** [44]: "Pastoral but still listening for what works for
this group, updating content as the train is moving as necessary to best serve this group at this
juncture." That sentence is an honest description of action research and it is also a description of
a treatment that changes underneath its own measurements. If the curriculum is modified in week 9 and
again in week 14 and nothing records what changed, when, and why, then no pattern in the twenty-two
weeks of data is interpretable at year's end — not by a reader, and not by the author. Every
disconfirmation condition in [54]–[58] presumes a stable thing being tested.

**Remedy.** One standing line on the Thursday call agenda, whose liturgy at [38] already has three
movements: *what did we change this week, and why.* Dated, one or two sentences, appended to a single
running file. It is thirty seconds of the forty-five-minute call and it is the difference between an
adaptive design and an unreconstructable one. I would put this above every other remedy in this
review for cost-to-value.

### Finding 10 — No key-informant review of a draft case report, though the covenant permits a form of it that is better than the standard one

**Severity: MEDIUM**

The third tactic for construct validity is to have key informants review a draft of the case report —
not to secure agreement, but to surface where the investigator's account of events is factually wrong
or has missed something. The manuscript has no such step. It has an adjacent thing, the quarterly
self-read [40], but the self-read is read *as data*, not as review of the team's account. Those are
different operations: one asks the room what it thinks of itself; the other shows the room the
investigator's account and asks whether that is the year they were in.

The covenant does not forbid this. [45] forbids telling a participant a level or a stage. [12]
prescribes the alternative register: "growth is spoken to people in the language of story and
specific moments." That is exactly the register a key-informant review of this case should be
conducted in.

**Remedy.** At the final session, the team writes a two-page narrative account of the room's year —
story language, specific moments, no levels, no stages, no scores, no counts, no names — reads it
aloud to the room, and asks one question: *is this the year you were in? What did we get wrong, and
what did we miss?* The corrections are the construct-validity check, and they are scribed verbatim.

One refinement, and I file it as a build rather than a re-file of the disaggregation limitation the
manuscript already states at [59]. A read-back conducted aloud, in a room where parents speak first
by design [16], will inherit exactly the asymmetry [59] names — the teenagers are the subgroup least
positioned to contradict the account, and they are seated beside their own parents. So take the
corrections **both ways**: aloud in the circle, and on an unsigned card into the same basket the
pulse pages already use [27]. Unsigned cards into a basket is a mechanism this design already runs,
it separates no families, it stages no conflict between a teenager and a parent, and it is the only
route by which a dissenting teen can correct the record at zero social cost. Give the same two-page
account to the fourth quarterly outside reader as well, and record whether the reader recognizes the
room the team described — under the same rule already written at [59], "whether or not the team
recognizes itself in it."

### Finding 11 — The case is not bounded, and the eighteen-month prototype sits inside the argument and outside the case

**Severity: MEDIUM**

[16]: "A year and a half of prototyping with one faithful family taught me the opposite, provided one
condition holds: the parents tell first, and tell something real."

That is a design conclusion — one of the manuscript's most consequential, since it reversed the
separate-circles architecture — drawn from a body of observation that is disclosed [8] but is not
part of the study's evidentiary base, has no recorded database, and cannot be inspected. At present
it functions as authority. The manuscript's own standard, "a protocol whose instruments cannot be
inspected cannot be replicated, extended, or corrected" (fn 8), applies to it.

**Remedy, and it is an upgrade rather than a concession.** Declare a **two-case design with literal
replication logic**: Case 1, the prototype family, eighteen months; Case 2, the pilot cohort,
twenty-two Wednesdays. Replication logic predicts that a theory holding in Case 1 should hold in Case
2 under similar conditions, and the interest lies in whether it does. The author already has whatever
records the prototype produced. Writing a two-page account of Case 1 against the same five design
components — what was predicted, what was observed, what was revised and when — converts an
unauditable warrant into a case, and gives the pilot year something to replicate rather than merely
to demonstrate. Two cases with an explicit replication logic is a materially stronger design than one
case, and this author already has both.

While bounding the case, state the boundaries explicitly in time (first Wednesday to last, plus the
summer count if Finding 5 is adopted), in space (the room and the homes the practices go to), and in
membership (enrolled families, the Companion team; the school's staff and the outside reader are
context, not case).

### Finding 12 — The constructs carrying falsification weight have no operational definitions, and one coding decision is left entirely to the coder's hope

**Severity: MEDIUM**

The manuscript proves at [46] that this author can write an operational definition when he sets out
to: "if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for; if it is a shepherd's eye held under a
signed covenant, it is care." Four constructs that carry falsification weight lack the equivalent.

- **"Cost"**, in costly telling [36]–[37]. The whole top rung of the primary sign turns on it, and it
  is nowhere defined. Two companions will not mean the same thing by it in November.
- **"Adult rescue"** [57]. This is the trigger for an entire disconfirmation condition. Does a
  co-companion answering a question count? Restarting a timer? Stepping in when the room goes silent
  for twenty seconds?
- **"A truly settled room"** [27]. A stopwatch is being read against an undefined criterion, and this
  trace is one of the strongest independent measures in the design (Finding 7). Name the visible cue
  that stops the clock — the last side conversation ending, or every chair occupied and no one
  standing — and the trace becomes a real measurement.
- **"No occasion" versus "not yet"** [37]. This is the most consequential judgment in the entire
  instrument and it has no rule. "No occasion" is missing data; "not yet" is a negative reading. A
  team that loves its room, reading ambiguity forty times across a year, will drift toward "no
  occasion," and the drift will be invisible, sincere, and fatal to [54].

**Remedy.** The calibration pages at the front of the pack [38] already exist for exactly this
purpose. Add the four definitions there. For the last one, the rule should be asymmetric on purpose:
*a "no occasion" mark requires the sheet to name why no occasion arose. If you cannot name it, mark
"not yet."* One sentence, and it closes the largest quiet degree of freedom in the design.

### Finding 13 — Instrumentation drift is not addressed, and the one reliability check available under the covenant is unused

**Severity: MEDIUM**

Across twenty-two weeks, a team gets better at seeing what it has been trained to see. Sign frequency
then climbs because the observers improved, not because the room did. This is instrumentation, a
classic threat, and it is not among the limitations at [59] — which names blind coding, second
coders, and inter-rater checks, all of which require outsiders and are correctly ruled out by the
covenant. Instrumentation is different: it can be checked entirely from inside.

**Remedy.** The two calibration pages [38] carry fixed worked examples. At the start of each quarter,
before the term's sessions, each companion independently re-marks the same worked examples cold, and
the markings are compared to the same companion's markings from the previous quarter and to each
other's. Two outputs, both free: an **intra-rater stability** trace (has this reader's threshold
moved?) and an **inter-companion agreement** trace among the two-to-four people who are already
inside the covenant. Twenty minutes, four times a year, no transcripts leave the room, no outsider is
involved, and nothing about any participant is touched — the worked examples are fictional. This is
the only reliability evidence this design can produce, and it can produce it at essentially zero
cost.

### Finding 14 — The ratchet collapses the only time series long enough to show a pattern

**Severity: MEDIUM**

I file this as a build on a limitation the manuscript already states, and I want the distinction to be
clear. [59] names "the sign-promotion rule advances a rung after three consecutive readings of seen
while providing no path back down." The manuscript treats that as a limitation of the *promotion
rule*. My point is a different one about the *evidence base*.

A single case with two or three measurement points is a weak design. A single case with a
twenty-two-point series and scheduled interruptions is a genuinely strong one — an interrupted time
series inside a case, where the interruptions (the three breaks) are exactly where the theory predicts
a discontinuity. The manuscript has that series. The ratchet destroys it, because a monotone
non-decreasing sequence contains no pattern to test: it will rise, and it would have risen under every
rival in Finding 5.

**Remedy, and it does not require changing the promotion rule at all.** Keep two columns on the sheet
instead of one: the **raw weekly mark** (seen / not yet / no occasion, ratchet-free, week by week) and
the **current rung** (ratcheted, for pastoral use, exactly as now). The rung goes on doing its
pastoral work; the raw series preserves the twenty-two points, including the dips, and the dips are
where the analysis lives. Two extra ticks a week on a page that already exists, and it multiplies the
analytic value of the year's primary instrument.

### Finding 15 — The manuscript claims substantially less than the design can support

**Severity: MEDIUM**

[44]: "Generalizations and extensions come later." [59]: "The honest claim available at the end of
this pilot will not be 'this works' but 'this ran, here is everything it recorded against the
predictions it published, and here is the revised design.'"

The modesty is admirable and, on the terms the manuscript states it, unnecessary. It rests on an
unstated assumption that generalization means what survey research means by it — inference from a
sample to a population, requiring sampling logic and adequate n. On that definition, three to six
families generalize to nothing, and the author is right to say so. But that is statistical
generalization, and it is not what case studies do.

**Analytic generalization** is generalization to theory, not to populations. A case is not a sampling
unit; it is closer to a single experiment. One experiment can corroborate, refine, or refute a
theoretical proposition, and no one asks how many subjects were in the population it was drawn from.
The relevant question is not "how many families?" but "does the observed pattern match the predicted
pattern, and does it fail to match the rivals' predicted patterns?"

On that standard, here is what this year can legitimately support with n of three to six families,
provided Findings 4, 6, and 8 are adopted:

1. **Refutation.** This is the strongest position the design occupies, and the author appears not to
   know it. If break-survival is flat or declining across three scheduled structure-stoppages [56],
   the proposition that this curriculum moves a room from compliance to valuing takes a real,
   publishable hit — a single case can refute a proposition offered as general. The disconfirmation
   conditions place this study in the one epistemic position where small n is not a handicap at all.
2. **Corroboration and refinement of specific propositions.** That level-matched, longitudinally aged
   sign forms are readable by volunteers with two calibration pages and no research training. That
   trust vocabulary does or does not migrate alongside sign advance [58]. That a room's spoken
   self-read behaves as the manuscript predicts. Each is a theoretical proposition; each is
   adjudicable in one case.
3. **Existence proof.** That a curriculum and a measurement instrument can be the same object, run by
   a volunteer team, at a marginal cost the team will actually pay across twenty-two weeks. Existence
   claims require exactly one case. This is a real contribution and it needs no n whatever.
4. **A revised design and protocol that a second site can replicate.** This is the payoff of
   replication logic, and it is how the accumulation actually happens in this field: case 2 at another
   school either replicates literally or, run under deliberately different conditions, replicates
   theoretically.

And what it cannot support, which should be stated with the same precision: any effect size; any
causal attribution to the curriculum as against the rivals in Finding 5 that remain unaddressed; the
average movement claim at [12] (Finding 3); and any statement about what would happen in a school
without this founder.

**Remedy.** Replace "Generalizations and extensions come later" with a short paragraph distinguishing
statistical from analytic generalization and stating the four claims above as the study's intended
yield. This makes the paper's contribution legible to reviewers who would otherwise stop at "n = 3 to
6" and put it down.

### Finding 16 — Preregistration is invoked as a function but not instantiated as a mechanism

**Severity: LOW**

I note that Stage A corrected the manuscript's account of what preregistration *does*; my point is
about whether it is *done*. [8] now reads: preregistration "separates what was predicted from what was
discovered and limits the choices a researcher can make after seeing the data." Agreed. But nothing
in this study is registered anywhere. Publishing a narrative article containing failure conditions is
not registration: an article in press is revisable, its version history is invisible, and it carries
no timestamp a reader can check against the data collection window.

**Remedy.** The materials are already published openly (fn 8). Add to that same location a dated,
versioned protocol document — v1.0, with the five design components, the decision rules from Finding
4, and the sixth condition from Finding 6 — posted before the year's midpoint, and cite its version
and date in the results article. Any later change is a new version, and the differences between
versions are themselves reportable. An afternoon's work, and it converts a claimed function into an
actual constraint.

### Finding 17 — No report shell, no stated audience, no analytic strategy named in advance

**Severity: LOW**

A guide to the case study report is the fourth section of a protocol, and drafting the report's
skeleton *before* data collection is the most effective discipline available to a solo investigator.
It forces the design components into view, it exposes which tables have no source, and — the point
here — it fixes the shape of the argument before the data can shape it. For an author who is the
designer, practitioner, observer, and analyst, and who has said so honestly at [59], the report shell
substitutes for the colleague he does not have.

**Remedy.** In September, write the results article's section headings and its empty tables: the
sign-reading table with twenty-two blank rows, the three re-entry count tables, the vocabulary
migration table with three blank columns, the disconfirmation table with a verdict cell for each of
the six conditions. Name the audience (the journal's readership, and separately the practitioner who
might run this next year, which may want two reports rather than one). Name the analytic strategy in
one sentence — pattern matching against the predicted progressions, with rival patterns specified per
Findings 5 and 6. Fill the tables in May.

## 3. What my discipline would consider missing

Compressed, in order of consequence, with cross-references above:

1. **The five design components, stated.** Question, propositions, unit of analysis, logic linking
   data to propositions, criteria for interpreting findings. Two of five are present in substance.
   (Findings 1, 2, 4)
2. **A unit of analysis, declared and held.** With the embedded design named as embedded, and the
   level of adjudication written next to each proposition. (Finding 2)
3. **Rival explanations, identified and addressed.** Case study analysis is largely the systematic
   entertaining of rivals; the manuscript entertains none, including the one that rivals its own
   thesis. (Findings 5, 6)
4. **A chain of evidence.** Records that store observations rather than verdicts; identifiers; a
   derived ledger that can be published without breaching the covenant. (Finding 8)
5. **A case study database, distinct from the report — and above all a decisions log,** without which
   an explicitly adaptive design cannot be reconstructed at year's end. (Finding 9)
6. **A case study protocol** in the technical sense: overview, field procedures with contingencies,
   the investigator's own protocol questions with sources of evidence named, and a report guide.
   (Findings 9, 17)
7. **Operational definitions** for the constructs on which falsification turns, and a coding rule for
   the "no occasion" / "not yet" boundary. (Finding 12)
8. **A logic model.** The manuscript's causal chain — practice → proximal change → intermediate
   change → observable sign — is asserted across [12], [37], and [51] but never drawn. Once drawn,
   each disconfirmation condition attaches to a particular link rather than to the whole, and a
   failure tells you *where* the theory broke instead of only that it did.
9. **Explicit replication logic**, which this author can have for the price of writing up material he
   already possesses. (Finding 11)
10. **A key-informant review of a draft case report**, in a form the covenant permits. (Finding 10)
11. **An intra-rater stability check**, which is the one reliability tactic available inside the
    constraints. (Finding 13)
12. **An articulated doctrine of generalization.** Analytic, not statistical — and the manuscript
    currently forgoes claims it has earned. (Finding 15)

On the constraints themselves. I was invited to argue if I believed a constraint was the
methodological problem. I do not, with one qualification. The Measurement Covenant is not a
methodological obstacle in the way the author may fear it is: case study research has always operated
over sensitive material it cannot publish, and its answer has never been disclosure. Its answer is
traceability plus protocol plus published derived records. Every remedy in this review lives inside
the covenant, and most of them are cheaper than the practices the author has already adopted. The
qualification is this: the covenant as summarized at [44] seals content and is *silent* about the
derived, de-identified ledger, and silence in a governing document is read as prohibition by everyone
who inherits it. That silence, not the covenant, is the methodological risk. It should be resolved
explicitly in the covenant's own text — content sealed, ledger publishable — and resolved now, before
a successor team reads the silence the strict way and the study loses its chain of evidence to an
ambiguity nobody intended.

## 4. What I would have done differently

Same convictions, same constraints, same volunteer team. Ten things, in the order I would do them.

1. **Name it, on one page, in September.** "Single-case (embedded) action-research study. Case: the
   pilot cohort across twenty-two Wednesdays. Subunits: families and individual participants. Object
   of test: the level-sequencing theory." Then the five design components underneath. This page never
   leaves the team's side of the paper and is the parent of everything below.

2. **Make it two cases.** Write up the eighteen-month prototype family as Case 1 against the same
   five components. Two cases with stated replication logic, at the cost of two pages, from material
   already in hand.

3. **Draw the logic model.** One page: practice → proximal outcome → intermediate outcome → sign,
   with the quarter in which each link is supposed to fire. Attach each disconfirmation condition to
   a link.

4. **Split the debrief sheet's sign line into two columns and one clause.** Raw weekly mark
   (ratchet-free) | current rung (ratcheted, pastoral) | eight-word occasion clause when marked seen.
   Still one page. This single change fixes the chain of evidence (Finding 8) and preserves the time
   series (Finding 14) simultaneously, and it is the highest-value edit in this review.

5. **Stand the database up as four objects before week 1.** Session files (never overwritten), counts
   ledger (one spreadsheet), verbatim file (sealed), decisions log. Put the decisions log on the
   Thursday call agenda as a standing thirty-second item. A volunteer team can maintain all four.

6. **Add the two founder-absent sessions and the summer count.** Two ordinary Wednesdays led by a
   co-companion, the founder absent and not reading those sheets until the year closes; one unsigned
   card into a basket eight weeks after the last session, consented in the same September permission
   conversation as the other five pages. Together these are the cheapest available purchase against
   the two rivals most likely to be true.

7. **Write the sixth disconfirmation condition — the ordering condition.** This is what converts the
   study from a demonstration of a good room into a test of a sequenced curriculum, and it is the one
   I would insist on if I could insist on only one thing after item 4.

8. **Write the decision rules onto the disconfirmation page.** Numbers where numbers are honest, a
   named adjudicator and a recorded rationale where they are not.

9. **Draft the results article's shell in September, with empty tables.** Then fill it in May and not
   before. For a solo investigator this is the strongest available substitute for the colleague who
   would otherwise ask "is that what you predicted?"

10. **Do the story-language read-back at the final session**, with corrections taken both aloud and on
    unsigned cards into the basket, and give the same two pages to the fourth outside reader.

Nine of the ten fit on paper the team already prints, and the tenth is one evening. None separates a
family, announces a target, grades anyone, releases a confidential word, or adds a page to the
debrief sheet. I would want the author to notice that: the machinery he is missing is not expensive
and it is not in tension with his ethics. It was simply never asked for, because nobody told him he
was writing a case study.

## 5. My single biggest concern

To the editor: this manuscript's distinguishing virtue — that it publishes the conditions under which
it would be wrong — is currently unsupported by the machinery that would let those conditions bite,
and the result is a design that will, in complete good faith, prove unable to fail. There is no
declared unit of analysis, so a room-level reading and a person-level reading can substitute for each
other whenever one is inconvenient; there are no criteria for interpreting findings, so each of the
five failure conditions will be settled in May by the judgment of the one person who designed the
instruments, trained the observers, and loves the room; there is no chain of evidence, because the
debrief sheet stores verdicts rather than the observations that produced them, so no later reader —
including the author — can audit a verdict against anything; and no rival explanation appears
anywhere, so maturation, self-selection, novelty, the school calendar, and the founder's own presence
will all be silently attributed to the curriculum. The author has been more candid about his position
than most researchers ever are, at [59] and throughout, and candor is not a substitute for design: it
tells a reader where the bias lies without giving anyone, the author included, any way to detect it
operating. I would not reject this. The remedies are unusually cheap — a second column on an existing
page, an eight-word clause, one standing line on a call agenda, two evenings when the founder stays
home, and one page naming what the case is — and with them this becomes a genuinely strong
single-case study that a second site could replicate. Without them it is twenty-two weeks of sincere
and unauditable testimony, which is precisely what the author set out not to write.

*— R. K. Y.*
