# Stage B Methodology Review — "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart"

**Reviewer:** Todd W. Hall, Ph.D. — Professor of Psychology, Rosemead School of Psychology, Biola University; co-developer, Spiritual Assessment Inventory (Hall & Edwards, 1996, 2002); developer, Spiritual Transformation Inventory and SpiritPulse; co-author, *Relational Spirituality* (IVP Academic, 2021)

**Standpoint disclosure:** I have spent thirty years building the kind of instrument this paper is trying to escape. I have also watched three of my own instruments fail in exactly the ways I am about to describe, which is the only reason I can describe them. I read this manuscript as a psychometrician and as a believer, and I want to say at the outset that I do not think those two readings conflict here. The paper is unusually candid, and I have tried to honor that by charging it only for things it has not already charged itself.

---

## 1. What is methodologically sound here

**The self-report restraint is correct, and it is correct for the right reason.** [34] keeps self-report to "practice frequency, hopes, worries, felt experience." That is the single most important psychometric decision in the paper, and it is the one most formation programs get backwards. The lesson I took from building the Spiritual Transformation Inventory over a decade is precisely this: people cannot validly rate their own spiritual maturity, but they can report discrete recent behaviors and current felt states with usable accuracy. This author has landed on that distinction without, apparently, having been told it, and has then held the line even at cost — "we would hold back even if it cost us data" [34]. Keep this. Do not let a reviewer talk you into a longer family instrument.

**Level-matched longitudinal observation is a genuine contribution, and it is the best idea in the paper.** [36]–[37]: "the observation instrument is level-matched longitudinally," with each sign given "younger forms" so that "the instrument ages with the room." The author's stated warrant is pastoral — "watched only in full form, a healthy young cohort reads as failing all autumn, which is false, and discouraging" — but the psychometric warrant is stronger than he claims. What he has independently reinvented is item-difficulty matching. Existing spiritual-maturity instruments cluster low not only because of a response-process ceiling but because their items sit near the low end of the trait continuum, so the instrument has almost no information above about one standard deviation. A fixed-difficulty instrument applied to a group whose trait level is moving is guaranteed to produce a floor in autumn and a ceiling in spring. Aging the observation targets with the room is targeted measurement in all but name, and it is the correct structural answer to the problem the companion article identifies. I would ask the author to say this in the psychometric register as well as the pastoral one; it converts a nice practitioner heuristic into a claim other measurement people have to reckon with.

**Discrepancy is treated as signal, not error** [44]: "Every tier above exists so that sources can disagree." This is current best practice in the multi-informant literature and most practitioners still treat informant disagreement as noise to be averaged away. The examples given — "a teen whose survey reports daily practice but who has never once told" — are exactly the configurations that carry information. This is right.

**The paper thinks in configurations rather than scores, at least intermittently.** [41] states the limit of the trust-vocabulary instrument better than most published psychometric papers state theirs: "all three house words can be heard along a single dimension of felt warmth, so vocabulary by itself cannot separate a room that calculates from a room that predicts from a room that identifies." That is a common-method-variance argument, correctly reasoned, and the remedy chosen — anchor the stage read in behavior the year already schedules and let vocabulary corroborate — is the right one. Whoever taught this author to think that way, it shows.

**Reporting two abandoned versions of the self-read instrument** [40] is instrument-development transparency that almost nobody publishes. The failures are more useful than the final design.

**The permission line at [46]** — "if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for; if it is a shepherd's eye held under a signed covenant, it is care" — is a clean, defensible boundary. I would adopt it. It resolves an ambiguity that has haunted every church-based measurement project I have consulted on.

**Weekly one-page burden with a ten-minute fill time** [38] matches what I have learned the hard way from pulse measurement: brief and frequent beats long and rare, and the failure mode of any field instrument is burden, never brevity. "A heavy instrument does not get filled out in a real ministry, and an unfilled instrument measures nothing" is the truest sentence in the measurement section.

**Knowing the group-development findings in advance** [42] — conflict before mature trust, pseudocommunity as the counterfeit — so that the team does not misread its best weeks as its worst. Building known moderators into observer expectations *before* observation is exactly what you do when you cannot blind your observers.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — The Thursday call destroys the independence that the "team, not one person" claim rests on, and the fix costs three minutes a week
**Severity: HIGH**

[36]: "Above the lower levels the architecture turns observational, and the observing is done by a team, not by one person."
[38]: "The pages feed the companion team's one weekly forty-five-minute call, held the day after the session... Its fixed liturgy holds those two to a discipline: look back (the signs first...)."

**The problem.** The epistemic weight of the entire architecture rests on the observation being multi-source. But as described, the sign readings are produced *in* the Thursday call, by a team talking together, led by the person who designed the instrument and predicted its results, in a project whose signature move everywhere else is that the leader speaks first. That is not multi-source observation. It is one reading plus assent. Consensus discussion before independent marking is the classic contamination pathway; it does not merely fail to establish agreement, it manufactures agreement and then makes it unrecoverable, because after the call there is no un-influenced reading left to compare against. [59] names the absence of a second coder and an inter-rater check, and I am not charging the paper for what it named. I am charging it for something different and worse: the design as written makes those checks *permanently unavailable*, and the paper appears to believe the team structure is already supplying what only independence can supply.

**The remedy, entirely inside the constraints.** This does not require an external coder, does not require releasing a transcript, and does not require a longer sheet. Before the Thursday call, each of the two-to-four companions marks the three signs independently — three marks, three seconds, on the sheet they already carry. The call opens by comparing the marks *before* discussing them. Where they differ, the disagreement is recorded and then resolved in conversation as it always was. That yields, at no cost, (a) a real inter-rater record across 22 occasions, (b) a running check on whether the calibration pages are working, and (c) the most pastorally interesting datum in the whole pilot: which signs the companions see differently, and whose eye sees what. If two companions who love the same room disagree about whether the sought stray was seen, that is a finding about the sign, and the current design cannot notice it.

I want to be blunt about the stakes: without this, the primary instrument's output is a single unblinded judgment by an invested designer, restated by colleagues he selected and trained. With this, it is a genuinely multi-observer record. Nothing else in this review is as cheap or as consequential.

### Finding 2 — The rung statistic cannot decline, and a published disconfirmation condition is hung on it
**Severity: HIGH**

[37]: "...marks each sign seen, not yet, or no occasion... and promotes a rung after three consecutive weeks of seen."
[54]: "A cohort still showing not yet on first forms in February, or no growing form all year, falsifies either the phasing or the curriculum."

**The problem.** [59] discloses that "the sign-promotion rule advances a rung after three consecutive readings of seen while providing no path back down," and I will not recharge that. What has not been noticed is that this ratcheted statistic is the *dependent variable* for disconfirmation condition [54]. A monotone non-decreasing statistic observed across 22 occasions will climb under a wide range of nulls, including a null in which nothing whatever is internalizing and the room simply becomes more familiar with the liturgy. Three consecutive "seen" on a sign whose occasions the liturgy itself schedules is a low bar, and once cleared it is permanent. So the direction the paper hopes for is close to guaranteed by construction, and the direction that would falsify — a room that climbed and then fell back — is not representable in the record at all. That is not a limitation alongside others; it means [54] is not answerable as designed, and any reported "climb" carries no evidential weight.

Compounding this: "no occasion" is coded as missing data [37], which is right in principle, but a sign with few occasions needs only three lucky weeks scattered across a semester to ratchet, while the paper's own phrasing ("three consecutive weeks") is ambiguous about whether "no occasion" weeks break the streak. That decision rule is doing enormous work and is not stated.

**The remedy.** Keep the ratchet exactly as it is for its pastoral function — the whole point is that no one gets demoted, and I would not touch that. But separate the pastoral display from the analytic record. The debrief sheet already collects the raw weekly mark. Report *the raw weekly series* — seen / not yet / no occasion, per sign, per week, 22 points — as the pilot's actual data, and let the rung be the care lens it was designed to be. The raw series can fall, can plateau, can oscillate around a break, and can therefore disconfirm. It also costs nothing new: the marks are already being made. And state the streak rule explicitly: does a "no occasion" week break a run of "seen," or bridge it? Write the answer down before Week 1, because after Week 12 nobody can write it down honestly.

### Finding 3 — "One underlying construct" is asserted where it should be discovered, and I will predict the specific way it will break
**Severity: HIGH**

[36]: "The companion team watches three vital signs, three readings of one underlying construct, confidence in God under the load of ordinary weeks."

**The problem.** This is a unidimensionality claim, and at n = 6–15 it can never be tested, which is precisely why it should not be asserted. I have lived this exact error. The Spiritual Assessment Inventory was built on the a priori conviction that spiritual maturity was one thing; what came out of the data were two dimensions — Awareness of God and Quality of Relationship with God — that behave *oppositely* in relation to well-being under certain configurations. High Awareness paired with high Instability is not a mature believer; it is a distressed one. Had I kept the single-construct claim, I would have summed those and reported the mean of a signal and its inverse.

I think the same split is waiting here, and I will name it as a testable prediction so the author can catch me being wrong. **Costly telling and the sought stray will dissociate.** Telling is verbally mediated, personality-loaded (extraversion and verbal fluency carry it), and — critically — *scheduled by the liturgy* [29]: "The tell step follows in clusters." The sought stray is agentic, unscheduled, and costs the observer nothing to fail to perform. A room can be trained to tell beautifully within eight weeks and never once go after anybody. That room is not at a higher rung on a single construct; it is high on one dimension and floor-level on another, and it is also, precisely, the pseudocommunity the paper already knows to fear [42]. The single-construct framing is what would prevent the team from seeing it, because any one sign moving would be read as the construct moving.

There is a pastoral edge to this too, and it is the weep test's business. Under an occurrence-only single-construct read, a teenager who discloses frequently and rawly registers as high on the construct. In my clinical and empirical experience that configuration — high disclosure, low relational quality — is more often a distress signature than a maturity signature. The design as written cannot tell those two teenagers apart.

**The remedy.** Delete the "one underlying construct" sentence and report three separate traces. If they covary across 22 weeks at room level, that is a finding the pilot will have *earned* rather than assumed, and it is genuinely interesting. Then add a seventh disconfirmation condition: *if telling advances while the sought stray does not, the three signs are not one construct and the framework's collapse of them was wrong.* That condition is answerable at this n, it is answerable from data already being collected, and it would be a real contribution either way.

### Finding 4 — An individual-level change claim with no individual-level instrument
**Severity: HIGH**

[12]: "each individual's progress from wherever they start will be tracked to see the actual change. The desire is that each individual will move, on average, one level or more."

**The problem.** This is the only numeric outcome promised anywhere in the paper, and there is no instrument behind it. The family-facing pages [34] measure practice frequency, hopes, worries, and surprise — none of which yield a Krathwohl level. The three vital signs are explicitly read at *the room's* current rung [38]: "the three sign readings at the room's current rung." The self-read is room-level and explicitly non-independent [41]. So the architecture has no procedure that assigns a person a level, no stated baseline occasion at which September levels are recorded, and no rule for what evidence licenses a level assignment. The paper simultaneously and rightly forbids ever telling anyone a level [45]. Fine — but the claim is not that levels are private; the claim is that individual movement "will be tracked."

At year's end, a designer who has spent 22 Wednesdays with six families and hopes for a one-level average will be able to produce a one-level average. I say that with sympathy rather than suspicion: it is what unblinded retrospective judgment does to all of us, and the paper's own [59] concedes the observer problem in general. What it does not concede is that this specific claim is unsupported by any described procedure.

**The remedy, three options, in order of preference.**
1. *Preferred:* drop the average-level claim entirely and report per-person narrative vignettes with dated quoted instances — which is what the covenant's own language of "story and specific moments" [12] already commits to. The paper loses nothing it can defend.
2. If individual levels are wanted, seal a baseline. At Week 3, each companion independently records a level for each participant, with one dated behavioral instance justifying it; the sheets are sealed and not reopened until June, when the same procedure runs again. Independent, pre-committed, and it takes twenty minutes once per year.
3. Whichever is chosen, adopt the instance rule: **no dated, quoted behavioral instance, no level.** A level with no sentence behind it is a memory, not a measurement.

### Finding 5 — Preregistration is invoked, but nothing is preregistered that can adjudicate anything
**Severity: HIGH**

[8]: "The academic literature calls publishing a design and its failure conditions before the results 'preregistration,' and commends it... because it separates what was predicted from what was discovered and limits the choices a researcher can make after seeing the data."

**The problem.** The author has correctly stated *why* preregistration works — it constrains post-hoc analytic choice — and has then preregistered predictions without preregistering a single analytic decision rule. Every one of the six disconfirmation conditions [54]–[59] is qualitative and adjudicated by the person who wrote it. What counts as "growing forms appearing across the middle of the year" — one appearance, or four? On how many of the three signs? "Essentially no discrepancies" [55] — how many discrepancies is not essentially none, out of how many opportunities? "Flat or declining break-survival" [56] — flat against what threshold, with three data points and roughly ten participants? "Consistently require adult rescue" [57] — how many of the five teen-led nights? "Stagnant vocabulary" [58] — how does one code the verbatim notes to decide?

This matters more here than in an ordinary study, because the reader has already been told [59] that "the disconfirmation conditions were written by the designer and will be read through instruments he built." The only thing that can partly discipline that is a threshold written before the data exist. As it stands the falsification apparatus is a statement of hope with the grammar of a hypothesis.

**The remedy.** One page, written before Week 1, never shown to a family, filed in the handbook. For each of the six conditions: the exact statistic, the threshold, the denominator, and who declares the verdict. Example, for [56]: "break-survival = proportion of enrolled participants reporting the current practice continued at least twice during the break, self-reported aloud at re-entry; improvement = the spring count exceeds the autumn count by at least two participants; declared by the full companion team at the June call before any other year-end review." Ugly, specific, and it is the entire difference between preregistration and a preface.

### Finding 6 — The ceiling claim is directionally right and mechanistically underspecified, and the underspecification costs the design its warrant
**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

[6]: "self-report meets a structural ceiling: above the middle levels, social compliance and transformed character produce the same observable behavior, and the heart is opaque even to its owner (Jer 17:9)."
Footnote 13: "For the self-knowledge asymmetries underlying the self/informant design, Simine Vazire, 'Who Knows What about a Person? The Self–Other Knowledge Asymmetry (SOKA) Model.'"

**The problem.** I agree that self-report hits a ceiling — I have hit it repeatedly with my own instruments — but the paper packs at least three distinct mechanisms into one sentence, and because it never separates them it cannot say which ones an observing community actually fixes.

1. *Impression management.* Real, but it is not worst at the top. Extrinsically motivated respondents at the low end have the strongest incentive to look devout; that is where socially desirable responding does its heaviest damage in my data. An observing community helps here, and this is the paper's strongest case.
2. *Item-difficulty restriction.* The dull, measurable, and I think dominant reason existing instruments cluster low: their items are written at the low end of the continuum and carry almost no information above roughly +1 SD. The SAI thins out up there; I know because I wrote the items. This is not a ceiling on self-report as a method — it is a ceiling on *those items*, and it is fixable by writing harder items, not by adding informants. The paper's argument overreaches by treating a fixable item problem as a structural method problem.
3. *The genuinely irreducible one, which the paper does not name and should.* The response process itself inverts near the top. More mature respondents have more accurate self-perception and less self-enhancement, so they rate themselves *lower*; the trait-score relation stops being monotone. No amount of better items fixes a non-monotone response function. Alongside it sits the implicit/explicit divergence: the relational knowledge that actually carries spiritual transformation is procedural and largely non-verbal, and no verbal self-report instrument at any level of maturity reaches it. That is my paradigm's central empirical claim and it is a much stronger warrant for this design than the one the paper is using — but note what it changes. It makes the ceiling a *domain* problem, present at every level, rather than an *altitude* problem present only above the middle. That reframing strengthens the case for an observing community and simultaneously removes the paper's convenient assumption that the lower levels are safely self-reportable.

Second, SOKA is cited in a footnote and never applied, and applying it properly would help *and* hurt. Vazire's model says informants outperform the self on traits that are **high in observability** and **high in evaluativeness**, and the self outperforms informants on low-observability, low-evaluativeness internal states. Spiritual maturity is high on evaluativeness, which is the paper's case. But the same evaluativeness that degrades self-report degrades *invested* informants, and this design's informants are shepherds who love the room, built the instrument, and predicted the result. SOKA does not hand you informant superiority; it hands you a condition, and this design meets one half of it and violates the other. Saying so would sharpen both the warrant and the limit.

**The remedy.** Three sentences in [6] separating the mechanisms, and one paragraph in [41] or [44] applying SOKA's actual conditions rather than its conclusion. No new data collection.

### Finding 7 — Sign frequency is confounded with the curriculum calendar, and the record does not distinguish scheduled from spontaneous occurrence
**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

[37]: "The first forms are readable every single week because the liturgy itself schedules their occasions; the higher forms are event-contingent, and the year schedules their events too: every break is a load test, every teen-led night an absence test, every testimony night an occasion for the full telling."

**The problem.** The author states the confound plainly and then does not treat it as one. If the liturgy schedules the occasions, then observed sign frequency across the year is a function of the *content calendar* — quarter one is story and confession, quarter two is Scripture practice, quarter four is testimony and commissioning — before it is a function of the room. A rise in costly telling in spring may be an internalization signal or it may be that spring has testimony night on it. The design as described cannot separate those, and disconfirmation condition [54] depends on being able to.

The paper is a half-step from the answer already: the aged forms distinguish "answering (when invited)" from "offering (someone brings something unprompted)" [37]. That *prompted / unprompted* distinction is the whole solution, but it is currently buried inside a rung definition rather than recorded as a property of each observation.

**The remedy.** The debrief sheets are already "pre-printed for their weeks" [38]. Add one narrow pre-printed column per sign: *occasion scheduled tonight — Y/N.* It is filled in before the session by whoever prints the pack, so it costs a participant nothing and a companion nothing. Then analyze scheduled and unscheduled occurrences as separate series. Spontaneous occurrence on a night the liturgy did not invite it is the internalization signal; occurrence on a night it was scheduled is, at best, compliance with a well-run liturgy. That single column probably doubles the interpretive value of the whole sign record.

### Finding 8 — The core mechanism has an attachment moderator the design never reads
**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

[16]: "A year and a half of prototyping with one faithful family taught me the opposite, provided one condition holds: the parents tell first, and tell something real. Vulnerability is reciprocal; the disclosure research has said for fifty years that people deepen by matched steps."

**The problem.** The social-penetration warrant is correctly cited, but it is a warrant about *dyads in general* and this design places a specific dyad — an adolescent and their own attachment figure — under public disclosure demand. The attachment literature says something the disclosure literature does not: whether reciprocal disclosure deepens or forecloses depends on the security of that particular dyad. In a reasonably secure parent-teen relationship, parent-goes-first works exactly as the author observed with his prototype family: the teen watches a trusted figure be formed and follows. In an avoidantly-organized dyad, parent-first *raises* the cost, because the teen must now manage the parent's likely response in addition to the room's, and the deactivating strategy that avoidance runs on will produce polite, fluent, contentless participation. That teen will read on the sheet as "answering, seen." He is not answering; he is complying, and the instrument has no way to tell.

The generalization risk is worse than it looks at n = 3–6 families. The design's central mechanism was inferred from a single prototype family described as "faithful," which is a sample of one selected on the outcome. One insecure dyad in this cohort is a sixth of it, and will present to the team as "a quiet teen" rather than as a boundary condition of the design.

I am not proposing that families be separated — that would violate the pastoral constraint and I think the constraint is right. I am proposing that the mechanism's moderator be *observed*, which requires no page and no permission under the paper's own line at [46].

**The remedy.** On the team's side only, for each family, after any teen tell of consequence, code the parent's *next utterance*: does it stay with the teen's experience, or does it move to correction, instruction, reassurance, or self-reference? That single observation is the closest field-readable proxy for the mentalizing and marked-mirroring capacity that determines whether the parent-first mechanism can work in that family. It takes one line, it is a shepherd's eye rather than a participant's paper, and — the point that satisfies the weep test — it is immediately pastorally actionable in a way almost nothing else in the sign set is. It is also the single most likely thing in this design to change a family, if the companion team is willing to have the private conversation the observation opens.

### Finding 9 — Acts are recorded; repair is not, and repair is where transformation actually happens
**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

[29]: "[The tell step is the sixth step of the practice and the first vital sign made liturgical; the sheet will record whether telling happened, whether any of it cost anything, and what the room did with the costliest one.]"

**The problem.** The three vital signs are, as operationalized, act-occurrence records: did it happen, seen / not yet / no occasion. That is behavioral coding with a spiritual vocabulary, and my objection is not that behavior is the wrong level — it is right, and it is a great deal better than asking people to rate themselves — but that occurrence is the wrong *unit*. In the relational-spirituality paradigm, and in the developmental work on implicit relational knowing that underwrites it, what changes a person is not the disclosure. It is what happens in the thirty seconds after the disclosure: whether the bid is met, deflected, corrected, fixed, or left on the floor, and whether a rupture is repaired. Change accrues through repeated experiences of rupture and repair, not through repeated experiences of speaking.

The paper very nearly has this. "What the room did with the costliest one" appears in the Week 7 narration in brackets — but it appears as a piece of atmosphere, not as an element of the sign set, not on the rung ladder, and not in any disconfirmation condition. The one thing most predictive of transformation in my research tradition is present in this design as a marginal note.

**The remedy.** Elevate it. Either make repair the top rung of *response to load* — where "real load borne toward God" currently sits, add or substitute "load met and, when the meeting went wrong, repaired" — or add it as a fourth sign with its own aged forms (first form: the room does not change the subject; growing: someone stays with it; full: a misfire is named and mended in the room). Then record, for the costliest tell of each session, one of four codes: met / deflected / fixed / left. That is one line on a page that already has a line for it, and it converts the sign set from a record of what people did into a record of what happened between them, which is the thing the paper's own thesis is about.

### Finding 10 — Three data points, called a trend line; and a single-case design going unclaimed
**Severity: MEDIUM**

[27]: "[The scribe discreetly notes the time to a truly settled room; at each of the year's three re-entries the same count is taken, and the trend line is one of the pilot's cleanest traces of internalization.]"
[56]: "The year has three scheduled structure-stoppages, and the same three counts are taken at each."

**The problem.** Three points is not a trend line, and with roughly ten participants and no variability estimate, [56]'s prediction — "flat or declining break-survival" — cannot be evaluated in either direction. This is a real loss, because settle-time and practice-survival are the two best quantitative variables in the whole design: behavioral, unobtrusive, uncontaminated by self-presentation, and already being collected by someone who is already in the room.

Underneath this is a larger point about what the paper thinks it is. It presents itself as an underpowered group study apologizing for its n. It is not that. Twenty-two repeated observations of a single unit, with three scheduled interruptions and pre-stated predictions about recovery after each, is an **interrupted time-series / single-case design**, and single-case methodology has established analytic conventions — visual analysis of level, trend, latency, and overlap; replicated probes; pre-specified phase boundaries — that this pilot could actually satisfy. At the room level, 22 occasions is not underpowered at all. The paper is undervaluing its own strongest structural asset by describing it in the vocabulary of a study it isn't.

**The remedy.** Take the settle-time count *every week*, not only at re-entries. The scribe is present, the count takes no additional apparatus, and it converts 3 points into 22, which turns the three break re-entries into interpretable deviations from an established baseline rather than free-floating numbers. Do the same for practice survival: the weekly re-entry check-in already asks how the practice went; record a count every week, not only after breaks. Then name the design in the methods paragraph [9] as a single-case interrupted time series alongside action research, and adopt its reporting conventions. This is the cheapest large gain available in the manuscript.

### Finding 11 — Response-shift bias will corrupt the pre/post comparison, probably in the direction that makes growth look like decline
**Severity: MEDIUM**

[34]: "...and a post-series survey repeating the baseline items so before can face after."

**The problem.** This is the most common lethal error in formation-program evaluation and I have watched it destroy otherwise good data. A year of instruction changes what the *items mean* to the respondent. A participant who in September counted any morning thought about God as "practiced today" will, by June, have been taught a six-step practice with a telling step and will hold a much higher internal standard for the same item. Genuine growth then produces a *lower* post score. The classic terminology is beta and gamma change — recalibration and reconceptualization of the measurement standard — as against the alpha change the design is trying to detect. This design is unusually exposed to it because the whole curriculum is an intensive year of teaching people what the constructs mean.

**The remedy.** Add a retrospective pre-test to the post page: alongside each current rating, "thinking back to last September, how often were you...". Compare then-with-post rather than pre-with-post; retain the original pre for the contrast, because the pre/then gap is itself an estimate of how much the standard moved and is interesting in its own right. This is a well-validated correction, it costs three lines on a page the family has already consented to, it adds no new instrument and no new permission conversation, and it is the difference between a defensible before-and-after and a set of numbers that may be pointing backwards.

### Finding 12 — No adverse-event channel in a design whose engine is escalating vulnerable disclosure
**Severity: MEDIUM**

[17]: "...with confidentiality promised out loud and its legal limits named honestly."
[50]: "...vulnerability answered with vulnerability across a generation line (a parent's costly tell answered by a teen's)."

**The problem.** The design deliberately escalates self-disclosure across a generational power gradient in a group with limited confidentiality, and it counts increasing disclosure as evidence of success. There is no described procedure for the case where a disclosure goes badly — a teen tells something at cost and the parent's response wounds, or the room's does; a family member discloses something they regret by Thursday; a confidence leaks. In my discipline this is not a bureaucratic requirement, it is the price of running an intervention on people's interiors. Right now the only monitoring channel the design has for harm is the same team whose success criterion is more disclosure, which is a straightforward conflict.

I want to be clear that this is not an argument for an ethics board or a consent form. The paper has already given me the tool: at [46], a shepherd's attention is care, not an instrument, and needs no permission.

**The remedy.** Two commitments, both care-side, neither on paper a participant touches.
1. A standing rule that whoever told at cost on a Wednesday gets a personal contact from a companion within forty-eight hours — the follow-up rule already exists [38], so this is a named trigger for machinery already built. One question, asked in private, of the form "how did that sit with you afterward."
2. A pre-committed rule for what happens when a family stops coming, written before it happens: who calls, what single question is asked, where the answer goes. [59] concedes attrition is not currently read as data; my point is narrower and practical — after a family drifts away, nobody wants to make that call, so the decision has to be made in September when it is still abstract.

### Finding 13 — No exposure variable
**Severity: LOW**

Across 22 sessions with 3–6 families, attendance will vary substantially, and dose is the most obvious rival explanation for any individual difference the team observes. Nothing in the described debrief page records who was present. One line: a headcount and a per-family present/absent tick. It is not a form anyone touches, and without it the team cannot distinguish a teen who did not grow from a teen who attended nine Wednesdays.

### Finding 14 — The pilot's stated long-range aim has no measurement and no follow-up
**Severity: LOW**

[19]: "one overall aim of the pilot is that they leave able to lead other teens, so that what they carry into college life is not only a private practice but the capacity to form a room in which other young people learn it."

That is a real outcome claim and nothing in the architecture touches it. The teen-led nights measure whether the container survives *inside this room with the adults present*, which is a different and much easier thing than forming a room where no adult is down the hall. Either state explicitly that this aim is out of scope for the pilot year, or commit now to a single lightweight follow-up — one page, twelve months after the last Wednesday, by permission — asking what if anything they have led. A commitment made now is worth something; a commitment made in June will not survive the summer.

## 3. What my discipline would consider missing

**Any form of inter-rater agreement.** Named as absent at [59], but see Finding 1: an in-house version is available at essentially zero cost and would not violate a single constraint. Its absence is therefore a choice rather than a consequence of the setting, and it should not be filed under unavoidable limitations.

**A codebook with negative exemplars.** [38] describes "worked examples of what each reading looks like at each rung," which is good and more than most field instruments have. What is missing is the other column: what *looks like* the sign and is not. Every coding scheme I have built failed at the near-misses, never the clear cases. Politeness that resembles absorbing. A joke that resembles offering. A parent's story that resembles going first but is actually a lesson. Two pages of "this does not count, and here is why" is worth more than four pages of exemplars.

**Pre-specified decision rules.** Finding 5.

**A missing-data taxonomy.** "No occasion" [37], "absent tonight," and "family has quietly stopped coming" are three profoundly different things and currently share one fate. They need distinct codes, and the streak rule needs to state how each is treated.

**Any implicit or non-self-report measurement of the individual.** Everything is either explicit self-report on five pages or manifest act-occurrence observed by the team. The layer my own research says actually carries transformation — implicit relational knowledge, affect regulation, discourse coherence — is untouched. I recognize the constraint that transcripts cannot leave the team; I note in Section 4 that this does not close off the option as completely as the paper assumes.

**An adverse-event channel.** Finding 12.

**An exposure variable.** Finding 13.

**A statement of what the pilot can and cannot support, in inferential terms.** [59] is candid in narrative terms — "one room, one year, one school, one founder" — but never says plainly what class of claim survives. Let me say it, and I would welcome the paper saying it in these words: at n = 3–6 families with no control and an invested unblinded observer, this design can support (a) **feasibility and fidelity** claims — the liturgy ran, the sheets got filled at rate X, the permission conversation yielded Y of Z families; (b) **existence proofs** — a full-form sign appeared at least once, or never did; (c) a **within-room time series** across 22 occasions with three interruptions, which is a genuine and analyzable design; and (d) **hypothesis generation and instrument development**, which is the real contribution. It cannot support any effect estimate, any individual-level change claim, any statement that the curriculum caused anything, or any comparison to a room that did something else. The paper's own closing formula — "this ran, here is everything it recorded against the predictions it published, and here is the revised design" [59] — is exactly right, and Finding 4 is the one place where the manuscript quietly promises more than that formula permits.

## 4. What I would have done differently

**I would have made the design's instrument-development purpose primary and said so in the abstract.** The most valuable output of this year is not evidence that the curriculum works. It is a set of field-tested, aged, calibrated observational signs with an inter-rater record and a coding manual, ready for someone with sixty families to test. That is a bigger contribution than any outcome this n could yield, and framing it that way would let the author stop defending claims he does not need.

**I would have built independence into the Thursday call from Week 1,** for the reasons in Finding 1. This is the change I would make first if I could make only one.

**I would take the two unobtrusive behavioral counts weekly rather than at breaks,** for the reasons in Finding 10, and name the design as a single-case interrupted time series.

**I would code repair, not just occurrence,** for the reasons in Finding 9. If the author takes nothing else from my paradigm, take this: it is the after, not the act.

**I would add one behavioral index of teen psychological safety, because the survey route is arithmetically closed here.** [59] concedes that psychological safety is never disaggregated by generation. I want to be useful rather than repetitive, so let me point out that the standard remedy fails at this n: with two to four teenagers in the cohort, a subgroup-disaggregated anonymous survey is not anonymous. There is no version of this that protects them. So the read has to be behavioral, and there is a good one available. In a room where parents speak first by design, the single most diagnostic behavioral index of adolescent psychological safety is **whether a teenager ever disagrees with, corrects, or declines an adult in the room** — not disclosure volume, which is confounded with compliance, but non-agreement across the generation line. One tick per session, on the team's side, no names required in the aggregate count. If a room reaches spring with abundant teen disclosure and zero instances of a teen contradicting an adult, that room is not safe; it is well-behaved, and the design would currently score it as a success. This also gives [42]'s pseudocommunity warning an actual measurable signature.

**On riding an existing validated instrument alongside — the practical offer.**

First, what I would *not* do, and I say it as the person who built it: **I would not put the Spiritual Assessment Inventory in this room.** Its diagnostic value lives in the negative-affect dimensions — Instability, Grandiosity, Defensiveness, Realistic Acceptance — and this design has nowhere safe to put that information. Generating a teenager's Grandiosity score in a room where his mother sits three chairs away, under a covenant that forbids ever disclosing a reading to a participant, creates a piece of clinically loaded data that cannot be acted on, cannot be returned, and would require clinical follow-up capacity the pilot does not have. Its norms are also adult and largely college-sample, not thirteen-year-olds. It is the wrong instrument for this room and I would rather say so than flatter my own work.

What I would ride alongside, in order:

1. **The Relational Health Indices, Community subscale** (Liang et al., 2002), 14 items, on the quarterly pulse pages. This is the best fit I know of to what this design actually claims to build. It measures the qualities of a *growth-fostering community* — engagement, authenticity, and empowerment — rather than any property of the respondent, so nothing it produces is a personal diagnosis, and it is therefore fully compatible with the no-scoring covenant. It has been used with adolescents in youth-mentoring research. What it adds that the pilot cannot otherwise get: a **validated, externally comparable, repeated measure of the container** at three time points, so that the migration claimed in [51] — "the room's own words about itself will migrate from safety language toward belonging language" — has a quantitative companion that a skeptical reader outside the project can evaluate. Right now the vocabulary migration is read from verbatim notes by the people who predicted it, using a house vocabulary they invented. Caveats worth stating in print: the RHI was developed on women's samples and later extended, and at n = 6–15 you report a mean and a spread and never a significance test. It rides on pages the family has already consented to.

2. **Edmondson's seven-item psychological safety scale**, at the same three occasions, aggregate only. The paper leans on this construct heavily and by analogy [17]; measuring it directly costs seven lines, is the field-standard operationalization, and would let the pilot report where the room actually sat on the construct it claims to have built practice-for-practice. Two of the items are reverse-scored, which is a useful check on acquiescence in a room trained toward agreement.

3. **The retrospective pre-test correction** described in Finding 11. Not an instrument, but the highest-value three lines available on a page already being administered.

All three fit inside the five-page family-facing budget if the quarterly pulses absorb them, all three are aggregate-reportable with no personal feedback, and all three remain subject to the permission conversation exactly as described. If only one is possible, take the RHI Community subscale; it is the one that speaks directly to the paper's central claim.

**On the relational-spirituality question, since the brief asks it directly.** I should say plainly that this is the most theoretically well-aimed formation design I have reviewed against my own paradigm. The architecture is implicit-first rather than content-first — "the unit of the curriculum is not the lesson but the repeatable practice" [12] — and that is precisely what my research predicts transforms people. The container functions as a secure base and safe haven in the technical sense. "Response to load" is co-regulation, observed in the wild. "The sought stray" is caregiving-system activation. Parents-go-first models affect regulation across the attachment hierarchy, which is a more powerful mechanism than the disclosure-reciprocity warrant the paper actually cites for it. The recession of the leader [12] is a secure-base outcome, not merely a leadership philosophy. My substantive disagreements are the three above — the unmeasured dyadic moderator (Finding 8), occurrence-without-repair (Finding 9), and the assumption that the lower levels are safely self-reportable (Finding 6) — and none of them is a disagreement about the design's fundamental orientation. It has the right theory of change. It needs a record that can catch the theory being wrong.

## 5. My single biggest concern

To the editor: the manuscript's principal instrument is an unblinded, consensus-produced judgment, made by a team the designer selected and trained, in a meeting where the designer speaks first, recorded on a statistic that cannot decline — and the paper's central prediction is that this statistic will rise. Under those conditions a rising rung ladder is not evidence about the room; it is a property of the measurement procedure, and disconfirmation condition [54] cannot fail for reasons that have nothing to do with whether anyone was formed. What makes this worth a revision rather than a rejection is that the two changes that fix it are nearly free and violate none of the author's constraints: have each companion mark the three signs independently before the Thursday call opens, and report the raw weekly seen / not yet / no occasion series as the analytic record while keeping the ratchet for the pastoral purpose it was rightly designed to serve. I would ask for both before publication, and I would ask the author to withdraw or ground the one number the paper promises — "each individual will move, on average, one level or more" [12] — since no described procedure assigns a person a level. The rest of my findings are improvements. Those are the conditions under which the year produces data instead of a well-kept memory.
