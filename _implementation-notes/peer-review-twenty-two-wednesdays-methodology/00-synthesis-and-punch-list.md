---

## 1. What Stage B found

Seventy-seven findings across five reviewers. After verification the picture is narrower than the raw reviews suggest, and more useful.

**The design's instrument craft is genuinely strong.** Hall called the aged sign forms the best idea in the paper and noted the author had independently re-invented item-difficulty matching. Patton credited the liturgy-scheduled unobtrusive traces, the anchored calibration pages, and the reporting of two abandoned instruments. Yin credited the published failure conditions as better than most protocol papers manage. Osmer judged the article competent as practitioner research and as a protocol. None of that should be lost in the punch-list below.

**The weakness is not measurement. It is self-accountability.** Patton's phrase is the accurate one: unusually strong on instrument craft, unusually weak on the machinery that would let the design catch itself. Three reviewers reached that from independent premises and their verified core is this:

> **Three of the five disconfirmation conditions have no threshold, one has an uninformative pass, one is near-certain to pass on any reading, and none of them tests whether the convictions held.**

That sentence, and not "the design cannot fail," is what survived verification.

## 3. Tier 1 — free, and do them regardless

Four changes cost nothing, break no constraint, need no outsider, and repair the most-cited weakness in both stages.

| # | Change | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Report the raw weekly seen / not-yet / no-occasion series** alongside the ratcheted rung. Keep the ratchet for its pastoral purpose; let the raw series be the analytic record. The data already exists by construction, so this is a reporting decision and nothing else. | zero |

## 7. Safeguarding — a separate clock, before September 2

These are adjudicated on different grounds from everything above and should not queue behind the manuscript.

**S1 — No participant-initiated route, and none announced. HIGH, upheld.** Two reviewers argued this against the never-divide-a-family constraint, both drawing the same distinction: the rule forbids a *division*, not a *door*. The handbook's machinery is real and mitigates the response side, but is **entirely observation-triggered** and cannot by construction reach a young person who shows nothing — which is the adaptive behavior in exactly the case at issue, a teenager whose cost is at home. The two-adult rule and mandated reporting govern a conversation; they are not a route to one. Remedy is a **named outside adult**, announced to participants, not separation.

**S2 — No stopping rule on pursuit.** The same item from the other end: the only route *to* a quiet teen is being sought, and their only route out is showing nothing. Adopt with S1, not after it.

**S3 — The compliant teen.** A young person who answers because answering is expected is currently scored *seen* on the primary sign. Reached only by a non-agreement tick or an unsigned card, both currently unnumbered in the reviews.

**S4 — No adverse-event or harm-response protocol in the paper**, in a design that deliberately escalates minors' disclosure and counts it as success. The handbook has the Settle Protocol; the paper does not say so, and a reader would conclude it does not exist.

**S5 — No pre-committed rule for a family that stops coming**, and no data retention or destruction endpoint under a covenant governing minors' material. The debrief pack names where filled sheets live and never when they stop living there.

**S6 — A consent overpromise.** "The room never learns who answered which way" is arithmetically unkeepable at three to six families. Name the limit in the permission conversation rather than promising past it.

## 8. The capacity problem — rank and cap

Both refuters independently warned that the panel proposed roughly **fifteen additions to a one-page debrief sheet whose lightness the paper itself defends as the condition of its survival.** Individually cheap, collectively fatal. The agreed cap, in order:

1. **Pass-read and sought-declined** — one line, answers two findings, and is the design's only read for a refusal.
2. **The outward-crossing line.**
3. **Adult / young-person tag** on the marks.
4. **Who led, and was it their first time.**
5. **Which parent went first.**

Below that, wait. Everything log-shaped belongs on the Thursday Call, not the sheet.

## 9. Remedies that would damage the paper

- Adding validated scales (RHI, Edmondson's seven items) — undoes the twice-abandoned rated criteria the paper already reports failing, and breaks the one-page pulse.
- Parent-mentalizing codes — the proposer's own argument against his instrument refutes it; the clearest weep-test violation proposed.
- Rung surgery to accommodate repair coding.
- Quotation of participants by permission; writing up the prototype family (re-identifiable at n of one).
- Numeric primary-outcome thresholds, which contradict the no-announced-targets commitment.
- The full nine-item deposit; three of its contents collide with the same commitment.
