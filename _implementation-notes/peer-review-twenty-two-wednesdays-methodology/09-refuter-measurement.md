## PART 1 — THE "DESIGN CANNOT FAIL" CONVERGENCE (pressure point 1)

### 1a. Are they one finding or three? — **THREE, by independent routes; the shared headline is partly an artifact**

- **Hall F2** — mechanism-specific: the promotion statistic is monotone, so the DV of [54] is
  ratcheted.
- **Yin F4 + closing** — procedural: no criteria for interpreting findings, so every condition is
  settled in May by the designer's judgment.
- **Patton F5** — categorical: all five conditions are *outcome* conditions; none asks whether the
  covenant held.

Different premises, different evidence, different remedies (report the raw series / write thresholds /
write adherence conditions). Not collapsible. **But the shared conclusion — "the design cannot fail" —
is false as stated and must be narrowed.** All three reviewers are answering the same sentence ([53]
"A protocol that cannot fail is a testimony, not a design"), which produces convergence at the
headline that the mechanisms do not support.

### 1b. Which conditions can actually fail — **the required narrowing**

| Condition | Falsifiable? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **[54] ladder never climbs** | **YES, but a pass is near-uninformative.** [54]'s falsifier is stated in *raw-mark* terms — "still showing **not yet** on first forms in February" — and [37] requires a weekly seen/not-yet/no-occasion mark per sign for the promotion rule to operate at all. The raw series exists by construction. A ratchet can also simply *stay put*: "no growing form all year" is fully representable. | Hall's "not answerable as designed" is **wrong**; "a reported climb carries little evidential weight" is **right**. |
| **[55] sources never disagree** | **Technically yes, practically no.** Any two sources over a year will differ somewhere, so this condition is near-certain to pass. "Essentially no" has no threshold. This is a *low-bar* problem, not a *ratchet* problem — a distinct third mechanism neither Hall nor Patton names. | Weakest of the five. Supports the "cannot fail" worry by a route no reviewer identified. |
| **[56] nothing survives the breaks** | **GENUINELY FALSIFIABLE.** Rests on mechanical counts at three re-entries [50] — practice survival, return cards, settle minutes — not on team judgment. A companion cannot press a family into practising over Christmas. Weaknesses are power (3 points), no threshold, and an unmeasured precondition ("in a cohort that attends faithfully" — Patton F3). | **The "cannot fail" claim does not reach this condition.** |
| **[57] container collapses in younger hands** | **GENUINELY FALSIFIABLE.** Teen-led nights are scheduled events with a visible binary outcome. Weakness: "rescue" and "consistently" undefined (Yin F12). | **Does not reach this condition either.** |
| **[58] vocabulary does not migrate** | **Falsifiable but soft.** Verbatim scribed notes exist; stagnation is representable. But coded by the people who predicted migration, and [41] concedes all three house words load on one warmth dimension. | Falsifiable in form, discretionary in practice. |

**Narrowed claim for synthesis:** *The disconfirmation apparatus is unevenly falsifiable. [56] and
[57] rest on mechanical and event evidence and can genuinely fail. [54] can fail but cannot informatively
pass. [55] is near-certain to pass on any reading. [58] is falsifiable in form and discretionary in
practice. The correct charge is not "this design cannot fail" but "three of five conditions have no
threshold, one has an uninformative pass, and none tests adherence."* That is a defensible sentence;
the reviewers' headline is not.

## PART 2 — VERDICTS

### TODD HALL

**F1 — Thursday call destroys the independence the "team, not one person" claim rests on** — filed HIGH
→ **SURVIVES, HIGH (mechanism corrected).**
The charge holds and is the most consequential in the set. One correction: Hall says the readings are
"produced *in* the Thursday call." [30] says otherwise — "Within a day the lead companion fills one
page in about ten minutes," and the call is the day after. So the readings are produced by **one
person alone** and then assented to, which is Hall's own phrase and a *stronger* version of his point
than the contamination-in-discussion story he tells. [36]'s "the observing is done by a team, not by
one person" is not supported by [30] + [38]. Overlaps Patton F7 and Yin F7 — one remedy, count once.

**F2 — the rung statistic cannot decline and a disconfirmation condition hangs on it** — filed HIGH →
**WEAKENED, MEDIUM-HIGH.**
Refuted in part: [54]'s falsifier is a raw mark, and [37]'s promotion rule presupposes a weekly raw
mark, so the falsifying direction *is* representable in the record. Hall's own remedy concedes it —
"it costs nothing new: the marks are already being made." What survives, and survives well: (a) a rung
climb is near-guaranteed and therefore near-uninformative; (b) the paper commits nowhere to reporting
the raw weekly series as the analytic record; (c) **the streak rule is genuinely unstated** — does a
"no occasion" week break or bridge a run of "seen"? That third point is load-bearing and merges with
Yin F12.

**F3 — "one underlying construct" asserted where it should be discovered** — filed HIGH → **WEAKENED,
MEDIUM-HIGH.**
[36] does assert it ("three readings of one underlying construct"), and at n=6–15 it cannot be tested.
But the practical harm is smaller than filed: the design never aggregates. [37] gives each sign its own
aged forms; [38] carries "the three sign readings" plural; there is no composite anywhere. So the
defect is interpretive (any one sign moving reads as the construct moving), not analytic. Hall's
predicted dissociation — telling advances, sought stray does not — is exactly [42]'s pseudocommunity
with a measurable signature, and his proposed **seventh disconfirmation condition costs nothing**
because the three traces are already recorded separately. Adopt the seventh condition and delete the
sentence; the rest of the finding is theory, not defect.

**F4 — individual-level change claim with no individual-level instrument** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES,
HIGH.**
Verified exhaustively. [12] promises "each individual's progress... will be tracked" and "move, on
average, one level or more." [34]'s five pages yield practice frequency, hopes, worries, surprise —
no level. [38] reads the signs "at the room's current rung" — room-level. [41] is room-level and
non-independent by the paper's own admission. [12] itself says individuals "are **assumed** to be a
mix of one, two, or three." There is no baseline occasion, no assignment procedure, and no evidence
rule. **This is the strongest single finding in the set**, and Yin F3 reaches it independently by a
different route. Hall's remedy 3 — "no dated, quoted behavioral instance, no level" — is the cheapest
and pairs exactly with Yin F8's occasion clause.

**F5 — preregistration invoked with no analytic decision rules** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH, but
DUPLICATE of Yin F4.** Yin's version is more systematic (condition by condition, plus the honest
fallback of naming an adjudicator where a number would be dishonest). Hall's contribution is the
worked example for [56]. **Count once, credit Yin, keep Hall's example.**

**F6 — ceiling claim mechanistically underspecified; SOKA cited but not applied** — filed MEDIUM-HIGH →
**WEAKENED, MEDIUM.**
Scope problem: [6] explicitly *reports* the companion article's argument ("A companion article... argued
that"). Asking this paper to disaggregate three mechanisms of a claim it is citing is a request to
re-argue a different manuscript. Two halves survive with live consequences for *this* design: (i) the
SOKA point — the same evaluativeness that degrades self-report degrades *invested* informants, and
this design's informants built the instrument and predicted the result, so fn 13's citation cuts
against the design as much as for it; (ii) if the ceiling is a domain problem rather than an altitude
problem, [34]'s premise that the lower levels are "safely self-reportable" loses its warrant. Cost:
three sentences.

**F7 — sign frequency confounded with the content calendar** — filed MEDIUM-HIGH → **SURVIVES,
MEDIUM-HIGH, and I would promote its synthesis value.**
Verified: [37] says the liturgy schedules the occasions and the year schedules the events; [23] orders
the quarters thematically (Q1 story/confession = telling; Q4 testimony = telling again). The paper
states the confound and does not treat it. The remedy — one pre-printed *occasion scheduled tonight
Y/N* column per sign, filled by whoever prints the pack — is free, touches no participant, and is
**the instrument that makes Yin's thesis-rival (F6) testable.** Does not merely duplicate the
answering/offering rung distinction: the rung describes the act, the flag describes the night.

**F8 — the parent-first mechanism has an attachment moderator the design never reads** — filed
MEDIUM-HIGH → **DEFECT SURVIVES at MEDIUM; REMEDY REJECTED.**
The defect is real and verified: [16] draws the design's most consequential reversal from "one
faithful family" — n=1, selected on the outcome — and generalizes it to a cohort where one insecure
dyad is a sixth of the sample. But the remedy asks volunteers to code a parent's mentalizing capacity
on paper about a named family. **This is internally inconsistent with Hall's own review**: he refuses
to put the SAI in the room because it "creates a piece of clinically loaded data that cannot be acted
on... and would require clinical follow-up capacity the pilot does not have." His remedy creates that
same class of data by another route, and a filed judgment that a parent does not mentalize is
precisely what the weep test exists to stop. Constraint-respecting version: state the moderator as a
named boundary condition on [16], and add "fluent, polite, contentless participation" to the
calibration pages' **negative exemplars** (Hall's own §3 recommendation).

**F9 — acts are recorded, repair is not** — filed MEDIUM-HIGH → **WEAKENED, MEDIUM.**
Premise partly wrong. Hall says repair "appears as a piece of atmosphere, not as an element of the
sign set" — but [29] states it is a *sheet element*: "the sheet will record whether telling happened,
whether any of it cost anything, and **what the room did with the costliest one**." What survives: the
sheet captures repair-relevant data and the analytic architecture never uses it — not in the sign set,
not on a rung, not in any disconfirmation condition. Adopt the cheap half (a four-code met / deflected
/ fixed / left on the line that already exists). **Reject the rung surgery**: replacing "real load
borne toward God" with a relational-process construct changes a published observation-layer definition
mid-design, which Patton F1's own amendment rule would forbid, and substitutes a psychological apex
for a theological one in a paper whose domain is hearing and obeying God.

**F10 — three data points called a trend line; claim the single-case design** — filed MEDIUM →
**SPLIT: counts remedy SURVIVES MEDIUM-HIGH; naming remedy WEAKENED to LOW.**
The overclaim is real — [27] calls three re-entry counts "the trend line." Taking settle-time and
practice-survival **weekly** rather than three times is near-free (the scribe is already present) and
converts 3 points into 22; this is one of the three best cost-to-value items in the set. But naming the
design an "interrupted time series" overclaims in the opposite direction: there is no pre-intervention
A phase — the series begins with the treatment. It is a repeated-measures within-case series with
three natural interruptions. Depends on Yin F12's definition of "a truly settled room" to be a
measurement at all.

**F11 — response-shift bias will corrupt the pre/post** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES, MEDIUM.**
Real and well-evidenced: [34] repeats baseline items after a year spent teaching people what a
"practice" is; beta/gamma change is the standard failure mode of formation-program evaluation. The
retrospective pre-test stays inside the register the pulse already uses (practice frequency), adds no
page, and the pre/then gap is independently interesting. Distinguish sharply from Hall's §4 add-ons
(rejected below) — this one does not import rated criteria.

**F12 — no adverse-event channel** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES, but reclassified as SAFEGUARDING, HIGH
on that clock.** See Part 5.

**F13 — no exposure variable** — filed LOW → **SURVIVES but DUPLICATE of Patton F3.** Collapse; Patton
states it decisively better.

**F14 — the long-range aim has no measurement** — filed LOW → **SURVIVES, LOW.**
Verified: [19] makes leaving-able-to-lead "one overall aim of the pilot," and nothing touches it.
Hall's supporting observation — teen-led nights test the container "inside this room with the adults
present," a different and easier thing — is **the same insight that powers Yin's founder-absent test,
reached independently.** Cheapest fix is the one-clause out-of-scope declaration.

**Hall §3/§4 items worth carrying into synthesis:**
- **Negative exemplars in the codebook** ("this does not count, and here is why"). Free, lives on the
  two calibration pages, merges with Yin F12. **MEDIUM-HIGH value.**
- **Missing-data taxonomy** ("no occasion" / "absent tonight" / "quietly stopped coming"). Merges with
  Yin F12 and Hall F2c. **MEDIUM-HIGH.**
- **Teen non-agreement tick** (does any teenager ever disagree with, correct, or decline an adult).
  One tick, team-side, no names. Addresses [59]'s stated teen-disaggregation limit, which Hall
  correctly shows *cannot* be fixed by survey at 2–4 teens, and gives [42]'s pseudocommunity a
  measurable signature. **Filed in §4 rather than as a numbered finding; it deserves numbering.
  MEDIUM-HIGH.**
- **Declining to put the SAI in the room**, with reasons. Not a finding, but the most credible passage
  in the review and independent evidence that the paper's constraint set is defensible rather than
  merely pastoral. Worth quoting to the author.

### MICHAEL QUINN PATTON

**F1 — action research and preregistration are load-bearing in opposite directions; two-layer protocol**
— filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH. Best statement of the change-log cluster.**
Tension verified and unqualified in the paper: [8] preregistration "limits the choices a researcher can
make after seeing the data"; [44] "updating content as the train is moving as necessary." Nothing
anywhere restricts what may change. The risk is concrete, not hypothetical: [40] reports the self-read
— an *observation-layer* instrument — being redesigned twice in the field. Remedy is one sentence plus
an amendment rule. **Absorb F2 and F10 into this finding.**

**F2 — conditions written about a curriculum the design intends to change** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES,
MEDIUM as a standalone; it is F1's corollary.** The "report the condition as **untested**" move is a
genuinely good, free addition and should be carried explicitly.

**F3 — a disconfirmation condition contains a precondition the design never measures** — filed HIGH →
**SURVIVES, HIGH. Decisively the best statement of the dose cluster.**
Verified: [56] says "in a cohort that attends faithfully"; [38]'s debrief contents do not include
attendance; [29]'s participation read is "everyone, most, or a few carried it" — breadth in a moment,
not dose across a year; [50]'s three counts are practice-survival, return cards, and settle minutes.
The strength of this finding over Hall F13 and Yin's ledger column is that it is not a discipline
import — it is an **internal inconsistency**, a condition assuming a measurement the design does not
take. Six seconds of writing on the existing page.

**F4 — no primary intended user; the school's spring decision is an unserved intended use** — filed
HIGH → **SPLIT.**
*Half A, "name a primary intended user" (SURVIVES, MEDIUM, downgraded).* Legitimate and in scope —
[9] already declares whose voice is whose, so one more sentence naming who the findings are for is a
methodological disclosure, and the design does serve five audiences at partial strength. But this is a
framing gap, not a claim-support failure; HIGH is inflated.
*Half B, the school's renewal decision (WEAKENED, LOW-MEDIUM).* **The premise is Patton's, not the
paper's.** Nothing in the paper says the school makes a renewal decision or on what calendar; [16]
establishes only a shared conviction about parents as primary teachers. "Somebody at that school will
decide in the spring" is supposition. A JSFSC protocol paper has no obligation to design a host
institution's decision support; that is program management. The harm mechanism Patton names —
"mirrors, never levers" broken from outside by an improvised headcount metric — **is real, but its
well-evidenced version is his own F12**, where footnote 8 demonstrably publishes the instruments. Fold
the harm concern into F12 and keep only the (cheap, harmless) recommendation to pre-specify a
year-end account.

**F5 — the design can fail on its effects but not on its principles** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH.
The one genuinely novel category in the set.**
Nothing else in these three reviews asks the adherence question. It is in scope precisely because the
paper makes the ethic constitutive ([44]–[46], [63]) and argues the ethic is what makes the measurement
possible. One narrowing: [59]'s outside reader ("what does it appear to believe?") is a partial
adherence instrument, and Patton concedes it elsewhere — so "not one condition asks whether the program
kept its own commitments" is true of [54]–[58] specifically, not of the design entire. Adherence
condition 6 (**"the weep test never once stopped anything all year"**) is the sharpest item in the
review: a principle that has never vetoed a decision is decoration. Patton's reframing of [40] and [21]
as the pilot's existing fidelity evidence rather than charming anecdote is free and strengthens the
paper.

**F6 — the covenant is not printed and is not written in evaluable form** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES,
MEDIUM.** No constraint blocks printing a team document; fn 8 already publishes instruments. The
operative half is the keeping/breaking line per commitment, which converts "mirrors, never levers"
from a value into something the record could contradict. **Merge with F12.**

**F7 — no announced targets protects participants and leaves observers exposed** — filed MEDIUM →
**SURVIVES, MEDIUM; remedy DUPLICATE of Hall F1.**
The argument is genuinely distinct from Hall's and correct: Goodhart bites the *measurer*, not only the
measured, and the companions know the desired trajectory ([12] the one-level desire, [54] the expected
phasing). One overread to correct: Patton says the sheet is "pre-printed with the expected phase";
[38] says only that sheets are "pre-printed for their weeks." Remedy is identical to Hall F1 — count
the remedy once, keep both rationales, because they license the change for different reasons.

**F8 — the anonymity promise is arithmetically fragile at this cohort size** — filed MEDIUM →
**SURVIVES, MEDIUM. Precise catch.**
The paper says two different things: [21] "**the room** never learns who answered which way" (true and
keepable) versus [27] "**nobody** knows" (not keepable — the permission conversation is face to face
with the founder [21], so the team already knows who declined, and [27] records a return count).
Patton's remedy strengthens consent rather than weakening it and costs one sentence in a conversation
that already happens. His observation that warm settings produce compliant yeses is fair and unaddressed
by the paper.

**F9 — "mirrors, never levers" conflates two mirrors and is not evaluable** — filed MEDIUM → **SPLIT.**
*The prose half (LOW):* that [63]'s imagery borrows the fellowship-mirror's warmth for the team's
clinical record is a style critique, not a design defect, and [45]/[12] already state plainly that team
readings are never returned.
*The evaluability half (SURVIVES, MEDIUM):* correct and free — [38] already logs every follow-up with
a name, owner and deadline, and a follow-up is by definition an action taken because of a reading.
Circling each *mirror* or *lever* at close is the operational test of the covenant's first clause and
costs three minutes a quarter.

**F10 — minimum viable change log** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES as the best available spec; count under
F1.** Field 3 ("what we expect to see differently next week") is what converts improvisation into a
micro-experiment; field 4 ("what it displaced") is the only thing that catches the one-practice rule
[18] silently becoming a different curriculum. His caution is correct and worth carrying: **[59]'s
surprise line is not a change log** — you can change a great deal without ever being surprised, which
is what drift feels like from inside. Placing it on the Thursday call rather than the sheet is right.

**F11 — the DE label does not by itself supply DE's safeguards** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES, MEDIUM,
with one dangerous sentence flagged.**
Charging the scribe with a standing evaluative function is cheap, inside the covenant, and the three
standing questions (especially "is there any reading here we want to be true?") are useful. But
Patton's closing instruction — "say so in the paper, because it changes what 'practitioner reporting
alone' means" — contradicts his own warning two paragraphs earlier against borrowed respectability. A
covenant-inside companion with a role is not independence. **Adopt the role; decline the write-up
claim.**

**F12 — publishing the instruments without binding the covenant to them** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES,
MEDIUM.** Best-evidenced harm mechanism in Patton's review: fn 8 publishes everything, the rung ladder
is deliberately concrete and portable, and [45] already warns what happens when a measure becomes a
target. Covenant as front matter of the instrument pack, one sentence on page one. Free. **Merge with
F6.**

**F13 — "generalizations and extensions come later" names a contribution this design will never make**
— filed LOW → **SURVIVES, MEDIUM (upgraded).** Verified at [44]. Patton is right that statistical
generalization here is unavailable rather than deferred. His item 2 — principles travel where recipes
do not, and this is the paper's actual scaling strategy — is the most useful practical addition across
the three reviewers' versions of this recommendation. **Merge with Yin F15 and Hall §3.**

**Patton §3 — data retention and destruction point.** [38] says "a single secured location" and names
no term or end. Genuine, cheap, and a covenant matter rather than a methods matter. **LOW-MEDIUM;
route to the safeguarding list.**

### ROBERT K. YIN

**F1 — it is a single-case study and does not say so** — filed HIGH → **WEAKENED to LOW as a standalone.**
The two components with demonstrable consequences are filed separately as F2 and F4 and are judged
there. What remains is a demand that the paper be renamed into the reviewer's tradition — and **Patton
demands the same thing for a different tradition** ("developmental evaluation... I would put that
sentence in the abstract"). Two reviewers each treating their own label as the precondition for
everything else, on a paper that already declares its method at [9] with a warrant, is the persona
tell. The labels also conflict; the author cannot take both as primary. Do not send this to the author
as "two reviewers said the method label is wrong."

**F2 — the unit of analysis is never specified** — filed HIGH → **WEAKENED, MEDIUM.**
Overstated: [12] explicitly declares a two-level design ("measure the group and the person on separate
ladders"), [8] declares the case boundary, and Yin himself concedes the curriculum is the object of
test rather than a fourth unit. So "four candidates in play at once" is not what the paper does. What
survives, and is worth ten minutes: **writing the level of adjudication next to each of [54]–[58]**,
because the one concrete harm Yin names — a room-level and a person-level reading substituting for
each other — is real and is exactly the Hall F4 / Yin F3 problem.

**F3 — "on average, one level or more" is a statistical-generalization claim the design cannot support**
— filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH. Merge with Hall F4.**
Complementary rather than duplicative: **Hall shows no procedure assigns a level (an instrument gap);
Yin shows that even with levels the average is the wrong form of claim (an inference gap)** — an
ordinal, self-declared-unvalidated ladder [12], averaged across non-independent units nested in
families nested in one room whose design premise is mutual influence [16]. Both are true. Yin's remedy
(a per-person predicted *pattern*, adjudicable person by person, living on the team's side exactly as
the current desire does) and Hall's remedy 1 (drop it; report dated vignettes, which [12]'s own "story
and specific moments" already commits to) are alternatives; the author picks one.

**F4 — excellent propositions, no criteria for interpreting findings** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH.
Primary statement of the decision-rules cluster; absorbs Hall F5.**
Verified condition by condition against [54]–[58]. Defeats the obvious overreach objection in advance:
"Where a number cannot honestly be pre-specified, say so and name who will adjudicate and on what
basis" — which converts an invisible degree of freedom into a visible one without imposing false
precision on a pastoral protocol. One page, team-side, touching nothing a family sees.

**F5 — no rival explanations are entertained** — filed HIGH → **WEAKENED in scope, HIGH in severity.**
"Nowhere" overstates: [42] pre-loads two rival *readings*, and [59] names self-selection and the
founder confound. What is true and serious is that **none is operationalized**, and maturation, novelty
and seasonality appear nowhere at all. Sub-verdicts:
- *Invitation denominator* (how many families approached, how many declined, aggregate reasons):
  **SURVIVES, free, available the day the cohort forms.** Converts a conceded weakness into a described
  one.
- *School-calendar clause in the sheet's existing margin* [27]: **SURVIVES, free.** Without it the
  seasonality question cannot be asked at all.
- *Founder-absent sessions*: see 4a below.
- *Summer card*: see 4b below.

**F6 — the direct rival to the paper's own thesis is not among the conditions** — filed HIGH →
**RIVAL SURVIVES HIGH; PROPOSED TEST WEAKENED.** See Part 4c.

**F7 — three sources above the ceiling, one perceiver** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH.**
Not a re-file: [59] names only *reliability* absences ("no blind coding, no second coder, no
inter-rater check"); Yin's charge is *construct validity* — there is functionally one perceiver, so
consistency would prove nothing. His source inventory checks out against [37]–[38], [40]–[41], [59],
[34]. One refinement to the remedy: the mechanical traces (settle time, return cards,
practice-survival, attendance, who led) measure habit persistence and engagement, not "confidence in
God under load," so **"primary" should mean "settles disputes about what happened," not "measures the
construct."** Designate them primary for the behavioural-signature conditions [56]–[57], and keep the
sign readings primary-but-corroborated for the construct claims, with a written rule for which wins on
disagreement.

**F8 — no chain of evidence; the sheet stores verdicts** — filed HIGH → **SURVIVES, HIGH. Highest
value-per-cost item in the entire set.**
Slight overstatement (the sheet does have a margin [27] and does record what the room did with the
costliest tell [29]), but the core is exact: a sign marked *seen* stores a conclusion with no input,
and [59]'s closing promise — "here is everything it recorded against the predictions it published" —
cannot be kept by a record that stored only its own verdicts. **The eight-word occasion clause is free,
fits the one-page rule, releases nothing, and simultaneously supplies Hall F4's instance rule and the
raw material Yin F6's ordering test needs.**
Sub-items: *identifiers* — free, SURVIVES LOW-MEDIUM. *Derived ledger* — SURVIVES, see Part 3.
*Quotation by specific permission* — **WEAKENED/REJECT**: it collides with [21]'s "Nothing is ever
asked," a request for a quotable sentence in a room this small carries the same social pressure Patton
F8 identifies, and the ledger delivers the chain of evidence without it.

**F9 — no case study database, no protocol; the decisions log is the severe omission** — filed HIGH →
**SPLIT.**
*Decisions log:* **SURVIVES HIGH**, merged with Patton F1/F10 — same finding, independently reasoned,
same placement (the Thursday call), and Yin's cost estimate (thirty seconds of a forty-five-minute
call) is credible.
*Four-object database:* **SURVIVES, MEDIUM.** Cheap, sensible, maintainable by volunteers; the
never-overwrite rule on session files is the part that matters.
*"Case study protocol" in the technical sense:* **WEAKENED, LOW-MEDIUM** as a framework demand — but
the **field-procedures list is concretely valuable and cheap**: what to do when a family withdraws,
when a session is cancelled, when the scribe is absent, when a reading is ambiguous between rungs, and
**who fills the sheet when the lead companion is absent** (a prerequisite for his own founder-absent
test). Keep the list, drop the framework framing.

**F10 — no key-informant review of a draft case report** — filed MEDIUM → **SURVIVES, MEDIUM, and I
would raise its value.**
Legitimate build, not a re-file: [59] names the teen-asymmetry limit and offers no remedy, and the
self-read [40] is read as data rather than as review of the team's account — genuinely different

## PART 3 — YIN'S COVENANT ARGUMENT (pressure point 3)

**Verdict: LEGITIMATE reading of a governing document, not a researcher's convenient reinterpretation
— with one guardrail Yin does not supply.**

His claim: case study has always worked over sealed material through *traceability* rather than
disclosure; the covenant seals content and is merely **silent** on a derived, de-identified ledger;
silence in a governing document reads as prohibition to whoever inherits it; therefore amend it
explicitly — content sealed, ledger publishable.

Tested clause by clause against [44] and the brief's constraint list:
- *"No release of confidential notes or journals"* — a dated table of sign readings, counts, settle
  times and attendance is neither a note nor a journal. **No conflict.**
- *"Names stop at the shepherd"* — the ledger carries no names. **No conflict.**
- *"No scores"* — the only friction, and it dissolves on the paper's own gloss. [45] defines the
  prohibition personally: "No participant is ever told a level or a stage, theirs or anyone's." A
  room-level ledger tells no participant anything.
- *"Consent and daylight"* — daylight favours publication.

**The decisive point, which Yin does not make and which should be given to the author:** the paper has
*already promised* this. [8] commits that "the enrolled number will be reported with the results," and
[59] commits to "here is everything it recorded against the predictions it published." A derived,
de-identified ledger is not an extension of the covenant — **it is the only artifact that can keep a
promise [59] already makes.** Yin is asking the covenant to say out loud what the paper has already
undertaken to do. That is document interpretation, not reinterpretation.

**The guardrail Yin missed:** at three to six families in one named school, a published ledger with
per-week rows is deductively re-identifying to anyone in that local community — and [8]'s own
denominator disclosure sharpens the exposure. His counts-ledger spec (F9 item 2) is correctly
room-level per session; the amendment should say so explicitly — **room-level rows only, no per-family
or per-participant rows, host school unnamed at publication.** With that clause the amendment is safe
and should be made now, before a successor team reads the silence the strict way.

**Rejected sub-item:** quotation by specific permission (F8 item 3). It does cross into sealed content,
it collides with [21], and the ledger delivers traceability without it.

## PART 4 — YIN'S RIVALS AND CHEAP TESTS (pressure point 4)

### 4a. Founder-absent Wednesdays — **rival REAL and the most serious; test WEAKENED but worth running**

**Do the scheduled teen-led nights already serve as founder-absent trials? No — and the difference is
exactly the one that matters.** [19] describes the teen-led second running as happening "with a full
team present and a family helping," and names it the *leadership absence test*: it tests whether the
**structure** survives a change of **facilitator** while the founder remains in the room as a
stabilising presence. It cannot test whether the room's readings depend on the founder's presence. Yin
specifies the difference correctly ("not teen-led nights, not quarter re-entries, ordinary
middle-of-quarter Wednesdays"), and **Hall F14 reaches the same distinction independently** — a real
convergence, not a shared framing.

The blinding half of Yin's proposal is the strongest part and nobody else offers anything like it:
**the founder does not read those two sheets until the year is over.** That is a genuine, free removal
of a degree of freedom.

What the test does **not** deliver, and the author should be told: n=2; the absent weeks are also
weeks with a novel leader, so a dip is confounded with leader competence; the team knows he is absent,
which can bias marks in either direction; and a room the founder formed and returns to next week is
not a founder-free room. **It discriminates a within-session presence effect, not the
founder-as-designer confound [59] names.** Cost is two evenings out of twenty-two, it separates no
family, announces nothing, grades no one, and it is consonant with [12]'s own fifth principle that the
curriculum recedes. Run it; do not claim it settles the founder rival. Prerequisite: Yin F9's field
procedure for who fills the sheet when the lead companion is absent.

### 4b. Summer card against the novelty rival — **SURVIVES, MEDIUM; the best outcome test proposed anywhere in this set**

Its real power is greater than the novelty framing suggests. The year's three break-survival counts
test practice persistence across interruptions the room *returns from*. **A count eight weeks after
the last Wednesday, with no room to return to, is the only measurement in the design that asks whether
the effect outlasts the program** — which is what internalization means. Mechanism reuse is clean:
same unsigned card, same basket, folded into the September permission conversation so it is consented
with everything else.
Costs to state honestly: it makes the family-facing corpus six pages, and [21]/[34] both promise five —
so the permission language must change *before* the first conversation, not after. And return rate with
no gathering to return to will be low; a thin return is itself uninterpretable, so pre-commit to
reporting the denominator.

### 4c. The sixth ordering condition against the thesis rival — **RIVAL SURVIVES HIGH; TEST AS WORDED DOES NOT DISCRIMINATE**

The rival is the most important unaddressed threat in the paper: *any warm, well-run,
family-integrated, confidentiality-bound weekly gathering would produce every observation the design
will collect; the level-sequencing does no work.* The paper half-concedes it at [5] ("the constraint
turns out to be the curriculum") and at [17], where the container's convergence with Edmondson is
called "striking, practice for practice." None of [54]–[58] separates the two.

**Yin's proposed test fails on its own terms.** As worded it fires only if growing forms appear
*prematurely* — during the first quarter's container work. But (i) the warm-room rival predicts
later-is-more just as the sequencing thesis does, since warmth also accumulates monotonically with
time, so ordering-in-time does not discriminate; and (ii) [23]'s quarters do not map cleanly onto the
three signs — Q1 (story, knowing, confession) and Q4 (testimony) both target *telling*, while the
sought stray and response to load never get a dedicated quarter. There is no clean sign-by-quarter
mapping for two of three signs.

**The salvageable form is Yin's own F3 wording plus Hall F7's column:** per participant and per sign,
*the first movement observed should be on the sign the quarter's practices were designed to enable*,
and — the actually discriminating instrument — Hall F7's per-occasion **scheduled / unscheduled** flag,
because spontaneous occurrence on a night the liturgy did not invite it is what the warm-room rival
does not predict. **This is the cleanest cross-reviewer synthesis available: Yin supplies the rival,
Hall supplies the instrument that can test it, and neither noticed the other.**

Yin's closing note is correct and should reach the author: a negative result here is publishable —
"a warm, family-integrated, covenanted room produces measurable internalization signs and the
level-sequencing is optional" is a real finding. But it must be findable.
