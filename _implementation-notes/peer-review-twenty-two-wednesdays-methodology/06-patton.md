# Stage B Methodology Review — Michael Quinn Patton

**Paper:** "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart" (JSFSC protocol paper, v2.3)
**Reviewer lens:** utilization-focused, developmental, and principles-focused evaluation

---

A word before the sections, so the author knows where I am standing. I have spent forty years watching
people import laboratory manners into settings that could not hold them, and then apologize for the
setting. This paper does the opposite. It takes the setting seriously and builds the measurement out of
it. The line I would put on the whiteboard is [38]: "a heavy instrument does not get filled out in a real
ministry, and an unfilled instrument measures nothing." That sentence is the whole of utilization-focused
evaluation in twenty-one words, and the author arrived at it by running a room at four o'clock rather
than by reading me, which is the better way to arrive at it.

So my findings are not corrections from outside. They are the questions I would ask if I were sitting in
the Thursday call.

---

## 1. What is methodologically sound here

**The instrument ages with the room [36]–[37].** This is the best single idea in the paper and I want the
author to know it before anything else. "Watched only in full form, a healthy young cohort reads as
failing all autumn, which is false, and discouraging, and (worse) teaches a team to distrust its own
instrument" [36]. I have watched exactly that kill more community-based measurement systems than
any other single cause. Volunteers stop filling out an instrument that always says they are failing. The
rungs — answering / offering / costly telling; settling / absorbing / bearing; prompted noticing / naming
/ seeking [37] — are a developmental rubric with anchored levels, which is what my field would
prescribe and rarely gets. Keep this. It is the transferable contribution.

**"No occasion" as a distinct code [37].** "an event-contingent sign with no event is missing data, not
failure." Most practitioner instruments collapse this into a zero and then read a quiet week as a decline.
Coding absence of opportunity separately from absence of response is a genuinely sophisticated move.

**Unobtrusive, liturgy-scheduled behavioral traces [27], [37], [50].** Time-to-settled at each re-entry,
break-survival counts, return-card counts, teen-led nights as absence tests. These are unobtrusive
measures in the strict sense: they leave no trace on the participant, they cannot be performed for, and
the year's own calendar schedules their occasions rather than the researcher scheduling them. "every
break is a load test, every teen-led night an absence test" [37]. Bolting these on afterward never works;
designing the program so it generates them is the rare thing.

**Calibration built into the paperwork [38].** "worked examples of what each reading looks like at each
rung sit on two teaching pages at the front of the pack, so a companion who has never read a word of
the underlying research can still mark honestly." Anchored exemplars at the point of use. That is how
you get usable data out of volunteers, and it is more than most funded evaluations do.

**Failed instruments reported [40].** Two abandoned designs for the room's self-read, with the reason
each failed — "revealed criteria are still criteria." I want to underline this twice. Nobody publishes their
failed instruments. I will come back to these in section 3, because the author has not yet noticed what
kind of evidence they are.

**Counter-intuitions pre-loaded into the team [42].** "open disagreement tends to arrive before mature
trust, not after it... the sweetest-looking room can be the lowest-trust room in the building." Training
observers in advance against the two misreadings the setting most invites is evaluator training done
properly. Most teams learn these the hard way in year three.

**The permission/care line drawn physically [46].** "if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for; if
it is a shepherd's eye held under a signed covenant, it is care." That is what a working principle looks
like: it decides something, at four o'clock, without a consultant on the phone. Section 3 will ask why
the other principles are not stated this way.

**The outside reader's single question [59].** "judging only by what this program does, what does it
appear to believe?" The author has independently invented a principles-focused evaluation instrument
and filed it as a limitation-patch. It is better than that, and I will say how to sharpen it.

**Self-report kept where self-report is valid [34].** Practice frequency, hopes, worries. Discipline about
instrument scope is rarer than instrument invention.

**And the honest end-claim [59]:** "the honest claim available at the end of this pilot will not be 'this
works' but 'this ran, here is everything it recorded against the predictions it published, and here is the
revised design.'" That is, almost word for word, the claim developmental evaluation authorizes. The
author has reasoned his way to my discipline's conclusion without my discipline's vocabulary. Which
brings me to the findings.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — Action research and preregistration are load-bearing in opposite directions, and the paper lets both stand unqualified — **HIGH**

[8] says preregistration "separates what was predicted from what was discovered and **limits the
choices a researcher can make after seeing the data**." [44] says the pilot is "Pastoral but still listening
for what works for this group, **updating content as the train is moving** as necessary to best serve this
group at this juncture."

Those two sentences describe incompatible obligations, and the paper does not notice. A reader who
takes [8] at its word will read the year's results as testing a specified curriculum. A reader who takes
[44] at its word will understand that the curriculum being tested in April is not the one described in
Table 1. Right now the paper claims the epistemic credit of the first while reserving the operational
freedom of the second.

**Does developmental evaluation resolve this? Partly, and I want to be precise about which part.**

Yes on the legitimacy question. This is an innovation in a complex adaptive setting, run by its
innovator, that intends to change itself while running, in a domain where the right next move is
discovered rather than deduced. That is the situation developmental evaluation was built for, and DE
gives the adaptation a name, a literature, and — this matters more — a *standard*. DE does not say
"change whatever you like." It says: every adaptation is a hypothesis, recorded at the moment it is
made, with the observation that triggered it and the change you expect to see. The evaluand moves,
and the evaluation's job is to track the movement with enough fidelity that the trajectory is legible
afterward. That is real accountability, and it is stricter than what the paper currently promises itself.

No on the tension itself. DE does not dissolve the preregistration problem; it relocates it. The
resolution I would put in the paper is a **two-layer protocol**:

- **The observation layer is frozen for the year and preregistered in the strict sense.** The three signs
  and their rungs, the promotion rule, the five family-facing pages, the three self-read questions, the
  three counts at each re-entry, the disconfirmation conditions. These do not change mid-year. If one
  must change, it is a *protocol amendment*: dated, reasoned, and flagged in the results so that
  readings before and after are reported separately rather than pooled.
- **The content layer is developmental and openly so.** Session order, teaching length, which passage,
  which practice, how the room build runs. These may change freely — under the change log in
  Finding 10.

Then the paper can say, accurately: *"This is a developmental evaluation of the curriculum carried out
under a preregistered observation protocol."* One sentence, and both claims become true. I would put
that sentence in the abstract [2] and in [9], replacing the undifferentiated "This is action research."

Action research is still the right family name; DE is the right first name for what is happening to the
content.

### Finding 2 — The disconfirmation conditions are written about a fixed curriculum the design intends to change — **HIGH**

[54]: "A cohort still showing not yet on first forms in February, or no growing form all year, falsifies
either the phasing or the curriculum, and the debrief sheets will show which weeks broke."

The debrief sheets will show which *weeks* broke. They will not show which *curriculum* broke, because
under [44] the curriculum in February is not the curriculum in October and nothing in the design records
the difference. The same problem runs through [57] ("the entrust-early design is wrong, or the pace is")
— if the pace was adjusted in November because teen-led nights were wobbling, the pace condition
can no longer falsify anything; it has already been fitted to the data.

This is the specific way a well-intentioned adaptive design becomes unfalsifiable without anyone
lying. Each individual adjustment is defensible. The cumulative effect is that no prediction can fail,
because the thing predicted kept changing shape.

**Remedy, inside the constraints.** Two sentences added to [53], and the change log of Finding 10:
each disconfirmation condition is read *against the version of the curriculum that was running when the
reading was taken*. Where a condition's referent changed mid-year, the paper reports the condition as
**untested** rather than passed. "Untested" is a perfectly respectable result and costs nothing but the
temptation to claim more. A protocol that can honestly retire one of its own five conditions is more
credible at year's end, not less.

### Finding 3 — A disconfirmation condition contains a precondition the design never measures — **HIGH**

[56]: "Flat or declining break-survival across the year, **in a cohort that attends faithfully**, disconfirms
the claim that the curriculum is moving anyone from compliance to valuing."

Nothing in the measurement architecture measures attendance. [38] lists what the debrief page carries:
"the three sign readings at the room's current rung, the feedback round's echo, a participation read, the
week's specific counts, and follow-ups." The participation read is [29]'s "everyone, most, or a few
carried it" — that is breadth within a moment, not dose across a year.

More generally, my field would call this the missing implementation measure, and it is the single
cheapest fix in this review. Without a dose record, **a null result is uninterpretable**: you will not be
able to tell whether the curriculum failed or whether it did not happen. A family that came to nine of
twenty-two Wednesdays and a family that came to twenty-one are pooled into the same room-level
reading. If break-survival is flat in spring, the first explanation to rule out is that half the cohort was
present for half the year.

**Remedy.** A strip at the top of the existing one page, no new lines: number present, number of
families present, and a three-box check that the week's core elements ran (container opened and
closed / practice sent home / feedback round held). Six seconds of writing. Nothing family-facing, no
new instrument, no permission required — this is the team recording its own delivery, not observing
anyone. The year-end account then distinguishes "the curriculum did not work" from "the curriculum
did not run," which is the distinction the disconfirmation conditions currently assume and cannot make.

### Finding 4 — No primary intended user is named, and the school's decision is an intended use with no finding designed for it — **HIGH**

My first question of any evaluation is not "is it valid?" It is "who is going to use this, for what
decision, and what would they need to see?" Everything else follows from that answer, and where the
answer is left open, the design serves everyone a little and no one enough.

This paper has at least five audiences and names none as primary: the founder (redesign), the
Companion team (weekly care decisions), the school [16] ("a conviction held jointly by the school
hosting this fellowship"), the families, and future adopters (footnote 8, published materials). The
families are explicitly *not* users of the team's readings: [45] "No participant is ever told a level or a
stage, theirs or anyone's."

The gap that will actually bite is the school. Somebody at that school will decide in the spring whether
twenty-two Wednesdays happen again. That is a real decision, on a real calendar, and the design
produces nothing shaped for it. Here is what I have seen happen a hundred times when a program does
not design its own accountability finding: **the decision-maker invents a metric**, and the metric they
invent is headcount, retention, and parent satisfaction — the three most available numbers in any
school. Which is to say, the covenant's "mirrors, never levers" [44] will be broken from outside by a
well-meaning administrator, because nobody gave them a mirror they could use.

**Remedy, inside the constraints.** Name one primary intended user in [9] — I would nominate the
Companion team, because they make the most consequential decisions most often, and because
naming them makes the weekly sheet the primary report rather than a byproduct. Then, separately,
design the school's year-end account *now*, in the protocol, before results exist: enrollment and
attendance (Finding 3), what ran, the three re-entry counts, the room's own three answers in its own
words, and the revised design. No individual readings, no levels, no rankings — the school is not a
participant and this breaches nothing, and pre-specifying it is what prevents an improvised metric in
May. A page of what the year contained is a better renewal document than a satisfaction survey, and it
is the one the covenant can live with.

**What changes if each user were named primary:**
- *Companion team:* findings must be weekly and actionable within seven days; the rung readings and
  follow-ups are the report; timeliness beats precision every time.
- *Founder:* findings are design-diagnostic; the surprise line [59] and the change log become the
  primary instruments, and the sign readings become context.
- *School:* findings are an account of the year — what ran, for whom, what changed, what the room
  says; no interior readings at all.
- *Future adopters:* findings are principles plus adaptation record plus context; the sign readings
  matter far less than the record of what had to be changed and why (see Finding 13).
- *Families:* findings are already delivered, in the only currency permitted — [12] "growth is spoken to
  people in the language of story and specific moments." That is a legitimate reporting channel and the
  paper should say so rather than leaving families looking like pure subjects.

### Finding 5 — The design can fail on its effects but not on its principles — **HIGH**

This is the finding I most want the author to act on, and it is the one my discipline is best positioned to
supply.

[53]: "A protocol that cannot fail is a testimony, not a design." Correct — and then every one of the five
conditions [54]–[58] is an *outcome* condition. The ladder climbs or it does not; sources disagree or
they do not; practices survive breaks or they do not; the container holds in younger hands or it does not;
the vocabulary migrates or it does not.

Not one condition asks whether the program kept its own commitments. And this program is governed
by commitments far more than by content: [44] "mirrors, never levers; no scores; consent and daylight;
names stop at the shepherd who knows the faces; and a closing personal test, measure nothing you will
not weep over"; [45] no level ever disclosed, no target ever announced; [46] permission for pages,
never for care; [21] "Nothing in the year is demanded"; [16] parents go first and are never replaced.

A program whose identity is its principles, and whose evaluation measures only its effects, is
measuring the wrong thing — or rather, is measuring half the thing and the less distinctive half. If the
three signs all climb but the room got there because a Companion leaned on a quiet teenager to
produce a costly tell, the pilot succeeded on its instruments and failed at what it is.

**Remedy: adherence-disconfirmation conditions, stated in [53] alongside the five outcome ones.** Six
lines, reviewed once a quarter on the existing team call. Concretely, the pilot has broken faith with itself
if, in the judgment of the team reviewing its own quarter:

1. Any family's *no* was re-asked, or a *no* changed how that family was treated or spoken about.
2. Any participant learned a level or stage — including by inference from how they were handled.
3. Any occasion was arranged in order to produce a sign rather than to serve the room. (Staging a
   sought-stray. Prompting a costly tell because a rung was one week from promotion.)
4. Any reading was used to press a person rather than to shape care toward them.
5. The room came to believe it was being assessed, whatever the team intended.
6. **The weep test never once stopped anything all year.** A principle that has never vetoed a decision
   is not operating; it is decorating.

That last one is the sharpest instrument in this review, and here is why. **The strongest available
evidence that a principle is real is a record of what it cost you.** This paper already holds that
evidence and files it as anecdote: [40] "The mechanism was redesigned twice in the field... it felt like
being graded, and revealed criteria are still criteria"; [21] "I adopted this late, after watching my own
instruments through a parent's eyes." Those are two documented cases of a stated conviction
overruling the designer's own methodological preference and destroying his own instruments. In my
field that is *the* premium evidence of principle-adherence and it is nearly impossible to fabricate. The
author should say so explicitly — not as a charming story about failed drafts, but as the fidelity finding
it is — and then commit to recording every subsequent instance in the same register.

### Finding 6 — The covenant is not printed, and is not written in a form adherence could be judged against — **MEDIUM**

[44] refers to "The team's covenant, signed before the first instrument runs" and gives five phrases.
[38] says "Names appear on the team's side only, under a signed measurement covenant described
below, in a single secured location." A reader cannot read the document that governs the entire
architecture. Since it is a team document and not confidential participant data, no constraint in this
project prevents printing it; footnote 8 already publishes the instruments.

Beyond printing it: test each commitment for evaluability. A principle worth the name is prescriptive
(it tells you what to do), and evaluable (evidence could show whether it was followed). Sorting the
current set against that test:

- *"Permission for pages, never for care"* [46] — fully evaluable, because the author gave it a physical
  criterion. This is the model.
- *"No scores"* and *"names stop at the shepherd"* — evaluable, and trivially auditable.
- *"Consent and daylight"* — evaluable via a permission ledger (asked / yes / no / re-asked never).
- *"Mirrors, never levers"* — **not currently evaluable**, because nothing distinguishes the two in the
  record. See Finding 9.
- *"Measure nothing you will not weep over"* — beautiful, and as written a disposition rather than a
  decision rule. It becomes evaluable the moment it is a **gate**: before any new instrument, any new
  line on the sheet, any new occasion, someone asks the weep question aloud and the answer is
  recorded. Then adherence is simply: did the gate run, and did it ever stop anything.

**Remedy.** Print the covenant as an appendix. Beside each commitment, one line: *what would count as
keeping it, and what would count as breaking it.* That is a half-page of writing and it converts a
statement of values into an evaluable framework — and, incidentally, it is what makes the covenant
portable to a second site (Finding 12).

### Finding 7 — "No target is ever announced" protects the participants and leaves the observers exposed — **MEDIUM**

[45]: "no target is ever announced, because the moment a mirror becomes a goal it stops being a
mirror." [12]: "The desire is that each individual will move, on average, one level or more. That desire is
never announced, never kept as a scorecard, and never disclosed to a participant or a parent."

The corruption of measures under target pressure does not require the measured party to know the
target. It requires the *measuring* party to know it. Here the Companions know the desired trajectory
[54] ("first forms steady by the end of the first quarter, growing forms appearing across the middle of
the year"), they love the room, they want it to be growing, and they make the readings by hand a day
later on a sheet that is already pre-printed with the expected phase. Every condition for benign drift is
present. The paper's own [42] warns the team against two misreadings and does not warn it against
this one.

I am aware the paper already names the absence of blind coding and inter-rater checks, and I am not
re-filing that. My point is different: this is not a reliability problem, it is a **principle-integrity** problem,
and it has a cheaper fix than reliability would need.

**Remedy, inside the constraints.** Sequence, not personnel. On any week when two Companions are
present, both mark the three signs *before* the Thursday call and before any discussion of where the
room "should" be; the marks are compared at the call and disagreements are noted in a word, not
adjudicated into agreement. This is not an external coder and requires no one outside the covenant. It
costs perhaps ninety seconds. And its value is not a reliability coefficient — it is that the team will *see*
whether its readings are being pulled by its hopes, which is exactly what the no-targets principle exists
to prevent and currently cannot detect.

### Finding 8 — The anonymity promise is arithmetically fragile at this cohort size — **MEDIUM**

[21]: "One yes covers the year's five pages; a no costs yes, the family remains a full participant, and
**the room never learns who answered which way**." [27]: "Pulse pages from the quarter's close come
back to a basket at the door, unchased. [Count recorded; a family that said no in September walks past
the basket at no cost, and nobody knows.]"

[8] sets the cohort at "three to six families, six to fifteen participants." With four families and a basket
count of nine pages, the team can very often reconstruct who is in and who is out — and the team is the
room's leadership, which is the relevant asymmetry for a family deciding whether a *no* is really free.
"Nobody knows" is a promise small numbers cannot keep, and the design is otherwise so scrupulous
about promises that this one stands out.

Note also that the same permission conversation happens face to face with the founder, in the family's
own home context, after the first session [21]. That is a warm setting, and warm settings produce
compliant yeses.

**Remedy.** This *strengthens* consent rather than weakening it, so it lives comfortably inside the
constraints. In the permission conversation, say the true thing plainly: *"In a group this small, I may be
able to tell who filled pages out and who did not. The room will not, I will not raise it, and nothing about
how your family is treated changes either way."* Then log the permission decisions as a count only, and
report the year's yes/no split in the results. A consent process that names its own limits is more
trustworthy than one that overpromises, and families with any experience of institutions can smell the
overpromise.

### Finding 9 — "Mirrors, never levers" conflates two different mirrors, and the team's mirror is never returned — **MEDIUM**

[44] "mirrors, never levers." [63] "The measurement is a mirror a fellowship holds up to itself with
permission, gently, quarterly, so that what God is growing there can be seen."

There are in fact two mirrors in this design and only one is held by the fellowship:

- The room's quarterly self-read [40] — genuinely a mirror the fellowship holds up to itself. Beautifully
  constructed, and I would not touch it.
- The team's readings — the three signs, the rungs, the trust-stage mapping, the individual level
  diagnostics [12]. These are held by the team, mapped "on its own side of the paper" [28], [41], and
  never returned to anyone in any form. That is not a mirror. It is a clinical record kept under a
  confidentiality covenant, which is a legitimate and honorable thing, but it is a different thing, and the
  conclusion's imagery [63] borrows the first mirror's warmth to describe the second.

I am not proposing that levels be disclosed; [45] is right and I would defend it. I am proposing that the
paper stop describing the team's instrument in the fellowship's language.

And there is a second half. As stated, "mirrors, never levers" cannot be evaluated, because a reading
that shapes care and a reading that applies pressure look identical in the record. The design already
holds the trace: [38] "follow-ups under a forty-eight-hour rule, each with a name, an owner, and a
deadline." Every follow-up is an action taken *because of* a reading. That is the exact population in
which levers would hide.

**Remedy.** Zero new lines on the page: circle each follow-up *mirror* or *lever* when it closes. Once a
quarter, read the quarter's circles aloud on the call and ask whether any of them put pressure on a
person to produce a sign. That is the operational test of the covenant's first clause, and it takes three
minutes.

### Finding 10 — Minimum viable change log (the constructive spec) — **MEDIUM**

[44] authorizes "updating content as the train is moving." Findings 1 and 2 explain why that needs a
record. Here is the record, sized for a volunteer team and a one-page weekly sheet.

**Where it lives: the Thursday call, not the weekly sheet.** The sheet is full and its lightness is a design
achievement I will not spend. The call already has a fixed liturgy of three moves — "look back... look at
the flock... look ahead" [38]. Add a fourth, three minutes: **look at ourselves.** The change log and the
quarterly covenant check both live there. A three-minute addition to a forty-five-minute call is
affordable in a way that a fourth column on the sheet is not.

**Five fields, one line each, only on weeks something changed:**

1. **What changed** — one clause, naming the session element.
2. **What triggered it** — and specifically *which observation*: name the sign, the week, or the moment.
   "It felt flat" is not a trigger; "week 9, the tell step produced two answers from nine people" is.
3. **What we expect to see differently next week** — the prediction. **This field is the one that does the
   work.** Written before the next Wednesday, it converts an improvisation into a small experiment,
   and it is the entire difference between developmental and opportunistic. A year of these is a chain
   of micro-hypotheses and their outcomes, which is a genuine research product.
4. **What it displaced** — the one-practice rule [18] means every addition is a subtraction, and
   unrecorded subtractions are how a curriculum silently becomes a different curriculum.
5. **Keep or revert** — checked at the quarter turn, not in the moment.

**And the amendment rule from Finding 1, stated once in the protocol:** content changes go in the log;
any change to the observation layer is an amendment, dated and reasoned, and its readings are
reported in two segments rather than pooled.

One caution: the paper's existing surprise line [59] — "one thing tonight that did not go as I expected
and what I did in the moment" — is excellent and is *not* a change log. You can be surprised and
change nothing; more dangerously, you can change a great deal without ever being surprised, which is
precisely what gradual drift feels like from inside. The two instruments catch different things and both
are needed.

### Finding 11 — Naming it developmental evaluation does not by itself supply DE's safeguards — **MEDIUM**

I said in Finding 1 that DE is the right name. I owe the author the honest other half, and it bears on the
already-stated limitation at [59] without repeating it.

In developmental evaluation as I have practiced and written it, the developmental evaluator is a
*distinct role* on the innovation team — someone whose independence is of judgment rather than of
position, whose standing job is to ask the evaluative question the team is currently too invested to ask,
and who has been given explicit permission in advance to be unwelcome. Here [59] states the honest
situation: "this pilot as described is the practitioner reporting alone." The paper's remedy is the
quarterly outside reader, which is good and which I will strengthen below. But a quarterly two-hour
visitor cannot carry the weekly evaluative-thinking function, and that function is where DE's credibility
actually comes from. Adopting the label without the role would give the design a borrowed
respectability it has not earned.

**Remedy, inside the constraints.** The role already half-exists: the scribe [28], [41] is a Companion, is
under covenant, sees everything, and is currently a recorder. Charge that person, in writing, with the
evaluative function: at every Thursday call they own the fourth move, they read back the change log,
and they ask the three standing questions — *what did we predict last week and what happened; what
did we change and why; is there any reading here we want to be true?* No new personnel, no
separation of families, no external access to confidential notes. Then say so in the paper, because it
changes what "practitioner reporting alone" means.

**And sharpen the outside reader [59].** Give them the printed covenant (Finding 6) and a second
question: *"Which of these commitments can you see enacted in what you observed, and which can you
not see either way?"* The second category is the useful one. Invisible is not the same as violated, and
invisible is what the team can actually act on. Also: the reader currently answers a question about the
program's *beliefs*, which is inherently a principles-focused inquiry — the author invented it, and
should say that this is what it is.

### Finding 12 — Publishing the instruments without binding the covenant to them is the design's most likely route to harm — **MEDIUM**

Footnote 8: "The complete curriculum, handbook, journal, and instruments are published at [URL]...
Publication of full materials is deliberate: a protocol whose instruments cannot be inspected cannot be
replicated, extended, or corrected." That reasoning is correct and I support the open publication.

But consider what travels. A youth pastor three states away downloads the three vital signs and the
rung ladder. They are wonderfully concrete — that is their virtue. The covenant is a paragraph of
prose in a journal article behind a login. Which of the two do you suppose arrives at the next elders'
meeting? The instrument is portable; the ethic is not, unless it is made portable on purpose. And an
aged-signs ladder used without "mirrors, never levers" and "no announced targets" is a ready-made
discipleship scorecard — the exact artifact [45] warns against, built out of this author's materials.

**Remedy.** Publish the covenant as the front matter of the instrument pack, not as a companion
document, with one plain sentence on the first page: *these signs are not to be used to rate, rank,
report on, or set goals for any person, and a team that will not sign this should not use them.* Say the
same in the paper. This costs nothing and it is the difference between publishing a method and
publishing a lever.

### Finding 13 — "Generalizations and extensions come later" names a contribution this design will never make — **LOW**

[44]: "Generalizations and extensions come later." [59] is more honest: "One room, one year, one
school, one founder... A quarter's worth of readings will be thin; a year's will still be one case."

The trouble with "later" is that it implies a road. There is none. Run this five years and it is still one
founder's rooms; add a second cohort at the same school and it is still one context and one covenant-
author. Statistical generalization is not deferred here, it is unavailable, and promising it later costs the
paper credibility it does not need to spend.

What a single case *can* legitimately contribute, and what I would have the paper claim instead:

1. **An existence proof.** This configuration is possible: measurement embedded in liturgy, at near-zero
   marginal cost, under a consent ethic, in a real volunteer setting. Nobody has to believe it works to
   find that useful. Most of the field does not believe it can be done at all.
2. **Principles, which travel where recipes do not.** Permission before pages. Signs that age with the
   room. The leader stays a sheep. Parents go first. One practice per week. No occasion is not failure.
   These are the transferable unit — not the twenty-two sessions, which are local to a school that
   releases its classroom at four o'clock. A principle is adapted to a new context; a recipe is either
   copied or abandoned. This is why I keep pressing on principles-focused framing: it is not only a
   fidelity tool, it is this paper's actual scaling strategy.
3. **Naturalistic generalization** — the reader's judgment, not the author's. That judgment requires
   context thick enough to reason from: school size and culture, how the families were recruited and
   how self-selected they were [59], the Companions' backgrounds, the founder's prior relationship
   with these families, the eighteen-month prototype family [16]. Some of this is present; most is not.
   The author cannot generalize *for* the reader, but he can and should supply the material with which
   the reader generalizes.
4. **Accumulation across sites, not across years.** State what a second site would need in order to be a
   test rather than a repetition: a different founder, a different school, the same covenant, the same
   three signs and rungs, and the same change log. Then the comparison of two adaptation records —
   what each site had to change, and why — is genuinely new knowledge, and it is knowledge only this
   design produces.

**Remedy.** Replace the one clause at [44] with a sentence naming which of these the pilot claims. My
recommendation: all four, honestly, in place of the one it cannot deliver.

## 3. What my discipline would consider missing

**A use statement.** One sentence: *the primary intended users of this evaluation are X, who will use its
findings to decide Y.* Everything in my field hangs off that sentence, and its absence is why the paper's
architecture serves five audiences at partial strength (Finding 4). It is also, in my experience, the single
most common omission in otherwise excellent practitioner evaluations.

**Any measure of implementation.** Dose, delivery, attendance, whether the liturgy ran (Finding 3).
Every outcome reading in this design is uninterpretable without it, and the paper's own [56] silently
assumes it.

**Fidelity criteria for the principles.** Not fidelity to a manual — the design has rightly refused that — but
adherence evidence for the commitments that constitute the program (Findings 5 and 6). This is the
central gap. The paper is built on convictions and instrumented only for effects.

**A change record.** Finding 10. Without it, the adaptive freedom the paper claims cannot be
distinguished afterward from having fitted the program to whatever happened.

**Contextual description sufficient for a reader's transferability judgment.** Finding 13, item 3.

**A stated exit and harm-watch.** Twenty-two Wednesdays with teenagers telling costly things beside
their parents will occasionally surface something a formation curriculum should not hold: a
disclosure, a family crisis, a young person who begins to unravel in a room built to be safe. The
container's confidentiality has "its legal limits named honestly" [17], which is good, and that is not the
same as a stated protocol for what the team does, who it hands to, and when a participant should be
walked out of the measurement entirely. My field would call this indispensable and I suspect the
handbook has it; if so, one sentence in the paper naming its existence is enough. If it does not, that is a
higher-severity problem than anything else in this review, which is why I raise it here rather than
quietly.

**A named data-retention and destruction point.** [38] "a single secured location." For how long, and
what happens to it when the pilot ends? Under a covenant this serious, the end of the data's life is part
of the covenant.

## 4. What I would have done differently

Same convictions, same constraints, and I would not have touched the room. Nine changes, all inside
the covenant and the one-page rule:

1. **Written one sentence naming the primary intended user**, in the abstract and in [9]. I would name
   the Companion team, which makes the weekly sheet the primary report rather than a byproduct,
   and everything else a secondary product derived from it.

2. **Split the protocol into the frozen observation layer and the adaptive content layer** (Finding 1), and
   said in one sentence that this is a developmental evaluation of a curriculum under a preregistered
   observation protocol. That sentence buys the paper both of its current claims honestly.

3. **Added the fourth move to the Thursday call — "look at ourselves," three minutes** — and put the
   change log there (Finding 10). Not on the sheet. The sheet's lightness is an achievement.

4. **Added a six-second implementation strip to the top of the weekly page**: present, families present,
   three boxes for whether the core elements ran (Finding 3).

5. **Printed the covenant as an appendix, with a keeping/breaking line beside each commitment**
   (Finding 6), and made the weep test a recorded gate rather than a disposition.

6. **Written adherence-disconfirmation conditions alongside the outcome ones at [53]** (Finding 5), and
   claimed the two abandoned self-read designs [40] and the late-adopted permission conversation
   [21] as what they are: the pilot's best existing evidence that its principles bind.

7. **Charged one Companion — the scribe — with the standing evaluative role** (Finding 11), and given
   the quarterly outside reader the covenant plus the "seen / cannot see either way" question.

8. **Designed the school's year-end account now**, in the protocol, before there are results to shape it
   (Finding 4). This is defensive design and I have never regretted it.

9. **Replaced "generalizations and extensions come later" with the four things a single case can
   actually contribute** (Finding 13), and specified what a second site would need to be a test rather
   than a repetition.

What I would *not* have done: separated the families, added a comparison cohort, sent transcripts to
an external coder, or professionalized the observation. Every one of those would buy a small amount of
internal validity at the price of the thing being studied. The author's instinct here is correct and I want
that on the record, because a reviewer from my discipline is exactly who he would expect to say
otherwise.

## 5. My single biggest concern

**To the editor.** This is a program whose identity is its convictions and whose evaluation measures only
its effects. The five disconfirmation conditions [54]–[58] can all be met by a room that was
successfully pressed toward the readings its Companions hoped for, and they can all be failed by a room
that kept every commitment perfectly — because not one condition asks whether the covenant held.
The author has stated a governing ethic with unusual force ("mirrors, never levers... measure nothing
you will not weep over" [44]), has twice destroyed his own instruments to keep it [40], [21], and has
built no way to find out at year's end whether he kept it — while simultaneously reserving the right to
change the curriculum mid-year [44] under a preregistration claim [8] that the change would void.
Those two gaps compound: an adaptive program with no change record and no fidelity record can
produce a year of readings that no one, including its author, can afterward interpret. My
recommendation is revise, not reject, and the revision is small in pages and large in consequence:
name the primary user, split the frozen observation layer from the adaptive content layer, add a five-
field change log to the existing Thursday call, print the covenant with keeping/breaking criteria, and
write six adherence-disconfirmation conditions beside the five outcome ones. Do that and this becomes
what it is very close to already being — the most instructive practitioner protocol I have read in this
literature in some years, and the rare one whose author has arranged in advance to find out he was
wrong.
