# Stage B — Methodology Review · Reviewer Brief

## The paper

John G. Tittle, "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart," a protocol paper submitted to
the *Journal of Spiritual Formation and Soul Care*. It describes a 22-week, family-integrated
discipleship pilot at a small Christian school, designed so that the curriculum and the measurement
instrument are the same thing: the activities that form an observing community are the activities
that observe it. The author is the designer, the lead practitioner, and the observer.

## What Stage B asks

Stage A asked whether the paper's *sources* were used correctly. **You are asked something
different: is the design sound, and does it support the claims made from it?**

Specifically:

1. **Method.** Is what the paper does recognisable as competent method in your discipline? Does it
   name what it is doing accurately?
2. **Inference.** Do the claims the paper makes actually follow from what it will observe? Where
   does it claim more than the design can deliver, and where does it claim less?
3. **Missing machinery.** What would your field consider indispensable here that is simply absent?
4. **What would you have done differently**, given the same convictions and the same constraints?

## Already done — do not re-file these

Stage A ran with the cited authors seated on their own work, and the paper has been corrected. The
version you are reading already includes:

- A declaration that Krathwohl's affective taxonomy classifies educational *objectives*, was never
  validated as a stage model, and is being extended here deliberately.
- Removal of the claim that the room's spoken self-read is "the best instrument in the pilot," and a
  statement that it is not independent informant data.
- Correct attribution of the six safety markers.
- Stated limitations: no blind coding, no second coder, no inter-rater check, ratchet-only sign
  promotion, psychological safety never disaggregated by generation, attrition not read as data.
- A commitment to a quarterly reader from outside the team and a weekly surprise line in the debrief.
- Corrected citation of Strathern, the trust typology's origin, preregistration's actual function,
  and disclosure of when observation began.

Findings that merely repeat the above will be discarded. Build on them instead.

## Constraints that are not negotiable

The author is a practitioner in a pastoral setting, not a laboratory. Several academically standard
remedies are ruled out here, and a finding whose only fix is one of these will be struck:

- **The Measurement Covenant** binds everyone with access to results: mirrors not levers, no scores,
  names stop at the shepherd who knows the faces, and *measure nothing you will not weep over*.
  **Confidential verbatim notes and journals cannot be released to anyone outside the team**, so
  remedies requiring an external coder to read transcripts are unavailable.
- **No target is ever announced** to participants, and nothing about a person's interior life is
  graded, ranked, or disclosed to them or their parents.
- **Nothing family-facing is administered without permission** asked in advance; a family's no costs
  them nothing and is never re-asked.
- **The debrief sheet is one page per week.** Additions must fit.
- Teenagers sit in circles with their own parents. Remedies that separate families, or that stage
  conflict between parents and their own children, are pastorally unacceptable.

Propose remedies that live inside these. If you believe a constraint itself is the methodological
problem, say so explicitly and argue it — that is a legitimate finding, but it must be argued rather
than assumed.

## Required output format

### 1. What is methodologically sound here
Be specific and cite paragraph numbers in square brackets. If the design does something your field
would consider good practice, say so; the author needs to know what to keep.

### 2. Findings
Numbered. Each with: a short title, paragraph reference with quoted text, the methodological problem,
and a remedy that fits the constraints above. Severity **HIGH** (the design cannot support a claim it
makes), **MEDIUM** (a real weakness with a workable fix), **LOW** (a refinement).

### 3. What my discipline would consider missing
The indispensable machinery that simply is not here.

### 4. What I would have done differently
Given the same convictions and constraints. Concrete.

### 5. My single biggest concern
One paragraph, to the editor.

## Discipline

- No manuscript edits; findings only.
- Quote the paper before objecting to it. Findings that cannot quote it are not findings.
- Credit what the paper already self-critiques. It is unusually candid about its limits; charging it
  for a limitation it names is unfair and will be struck.
- Speak in your own voice, from your own body of work.
