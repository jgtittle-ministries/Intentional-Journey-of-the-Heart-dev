## P1 — "The night visitor"

A senior religious teacher, secure in reputation and learned in the Scriptures, hears reports of an itinerant rabbi whose actions do not fit his categories. He does not denounce him, as his colleagues do; nor does he follow him. He arranges a private conversation, at night, away from the eyes of his peers. He opens with a careful concession — "we know you are a teacher come from God" — and then listens through an answer that dismantles his framework, asking twice, "How can these things be?" The record shows no decision that night. Some time later, when his council moves to condemn the rabbi unheard, he raises only a procedural objection — "does our law judge a man without first giving him a hearing?" — and absorbs the mockery that follows in silence.

- **Expected reading: Level 1 — Receiving** (attending, willing to hear at some personal risk; no committed response yet).
- **Identity:** Nicodemus, John 3:1–15; 7:50–52. (His John 19:39 appearance is deliberately excluded — the full arc becomes a *movement* portrait for the later bench.)
- **Rationale:** attention and openness are behaviorally evidenced (came, listened, asked); commitment is behaviorally absent. The procedural objection marks the top edge of Receiving without crossing into public identification.

## P2 — "The careful keeper"

A young man of considerable wealth and blameless conduct runs to a traveling teacher, kneels in the public road, and asks what he still lacks. His record is genuinely good: the commandments he has kept, he says, "from my youth" — and the teacher does not dispute it. The account notes, remarkably, that the teacher "loved him." Then comes the answer: one thing — sell what you have, give to the poor, and follow. The record preserves his exact response: "Disheartened by the saying, he went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions." He asked the true question, of the right person, on his knees, in public — and when the answer priced his treasure, he kept the treasure and carried the sorrow away with him.

- **Expected reading: Level 2 — Responding** (sustained, sincere compliance and real engagement; fails at the threshold of costly personal valuing).
- **Identity:** the rich young ruler, Mark 10:17–22.
- **Rationale:** the canonical adjacent-pair case for the Responding/Valuing boundary — decades of kept commandments (produced work, compliance) with the one recorded refusal exactly where Valuing's evidence type begins (voluntary cost). An instrument that reads his lifelong observance as Level 3+ fails the boundary.

## P3 — "The man in the tree"

A chief tax collector — wealthy, despised, and short — wants to see a visiting teacher badly enough to abandon his dignity: he runs ahead of the crowd and climbs a tree. Called down by name, he receives the teacher into his house "joyfully," while the crowd grumbles that the guest has gone to be the guest of a sinner. Then, with no recorded request from anyone, he stands and prices his own repentance: "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor. And if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold." Half his fortune, volunteered; restitution at quadruple the legal requirement, self-imposed; his entire livelihood's method publicly renounced — in his own dining room, in front of witnesses hostile to him.

- **Expected reading: Level 3 — Valuing** (voluntary, unprompted, publicly costly commitment).
- **Identity:** Zacchaeus, Luke 19:1–10.
- **Rationale:** textbook Valuing evidence — nobody asked; the cost is quantified in the text itself; the action is against self-interest. Too new for Organization (a single day's record); unmistakably past Responding.

## P4 — "The administrator"

A foreign-born official rises across three regimes to the top of an empire's civil service. The record shows one architecture under everything: as a student he negotiates an exemption from the king's food rather than defile a conviction — proposing a test, accepting the risk. Decades later, as an old man, rivals seeking to destroy him audit his entire public record and find nothing — so they conclude, in the text's own words, "we shall not find any ground for complaint against this man unless we find it in connection with the law of his God." They engineer a decree criminalizing his prayer. Knowing the document was signed, he goes home and prays at his open windows, three times a day, "as he had done previously" — the phrase itself testifying to the standing habit. His convictions are not one act; they are the operating system his career, diet, calendar, and death-risk calculations all run on.

- **Expected reading: Level 4 — Organization** (the value system visibly ordering competing goods across decades of real practice, under audit, at mortal cost).
- **Identity:** Daniel (Dan. 1, 6).
- **Rationale:** the evidence type is exactly Organization's — not one voluntary act (Valuing) but the documented *arrangement of a whole life's priorities* under hostile scrutiny. The rivals' audit is the text's own known-groups test.

## P5 — "The kitchen brother"

A former soldier and footman, self-described as "a great awkward fellow who broke everything," enters a monastery as a lay brother and is assigned to the kitchen — work he naturally hated — for some thirty years, later mending sandals when his legs failed. Visitors of rank seek him out, and their notes survive: they record a man for whom scrubbing pots and receiving the Sacrament had ceased to be different in kind — "the time of business does not with me differ from the time of prayer; and in the noise and clutter of my kitchen... I possess God in as great tranquility as if I were upon my knees." The composure was tested: the same records report him unruffled under sudden demands, slights, and interruptions, the peace holding *in the noise*, not away from it. He reports that his first ten years were dry and full of the fear that he was deceived — and that he kept the practice anyway until it became, his interviewer wrote, as natural to him as breathing.

- **Expected reading: Level 5 — Characterization** (the internalized pattern consistent across contexts, decades, moods, and interruptions — visible chiefly to *others*, in the ordinary).
- **Identity:** Brother Lawrence of the Resurrection (*The Practice of the Presence of God*, conversations and letters recorded by Joseph de Beaufort).
- **Rationale:** the only level whose evidence is longitudinal, multi-witness, cross-context consistency — supplied here by an external interviewer across decades, in the least glamorous setting available. Note the embedded dry decade: an affect-keyed instrument reading his years 1–10 alone would misclassify him; the bench's later movement-portraits will exploit exactly that.

## NC1 — Negative control: the counterfeit (must score LOW)

A religious professional of impeccable public observance: he tithes down to his garden herbs, fasts twice weekly, prays at length in public, maintains ceremonial washing beyond the law's requirement, and travels far to win converts. His stated positions are orthodox; his vocabulary is devout; crowds treat him as the standard of seriousness about God. The same record documents: he arranges the best seats and loves the market-place greetings and honorific titles; his lengthy public prayers coexist with the exploitation of widows' estates; his oath casuistry is engineered to make solemn-sounding commitments escapable; and the weightier matters of the very law he tithes herbs by — justice, mercy, faithfulness — are, in the text's word, *neglected*. The outside of the cup is clean.

- **Expected reading: LOW (Receiving/Responding at most), flagged as verbal-behavioral contradiction.** An instrument reading his observance, vocabulary, or self-report as Level 4–5 **fails the bench.**
- **Identity:** the Pharisee composite, Matt. 23:5–28; Mark 12:38–40.
- **Rationale:** the direct test against Krathwohl's faking warning and the Guardrails' "no scores" edge case ("a scoring system does not merely fail to detect a counterfeit heart — it credentials it"). Also the corpus's canonical stage-N-collapse case (Vol 4 §2).

## NC2 — Negative control: the adjacent look-alike (must be distinguished or flagged)

An eldest son with a flawless service record, by his own accurate account: "these many years I have served you, and I never disobeyed your command." The household's books would confirm it — years of field work, no scandal, no absence. Then his wayward brother comes home to music, and the record shows what the service was made of: he is angry, will not go in, and prices his years — "you never gave me a young goat, that I might celebrate with my friends." He addresses his father without a title, refers to "this son of yours" rather than "my brother," and stands outside the feast while the father pleads. The obedience was real, sustained, and externally indistinguishable from devotion — and it turns out to have been accumulating wages, not sharing a heart: "all that is mine is yours" was true the whole time, and he never knew it.

- **Expected reading: dutiful compliance (Responding-shaped) misreadable as Organization; the instrument must distinguish natural dutifulness from internalized love, or flag that it cannot.**
- **Identity:** the elder brother, Luke 15:25–32.
- **Rationale:** discriminant-validity control — long-arc behavioral compliance with the *construct itself absent* (no shared desire with the father). Pairs deliberately with the Path Home Card's posture warning: the bench and the pastoral materials guard the same door from two sides.

## NC3 — Negative control: the desolate believer (must score HIGH)

A woman in vowed religious life experiences, in her late thirties, a season of extraordinary felt communion that accompanies a call to serve the destitute of a foreign city. Almost exactly as the work begins, the felt presence vanishes — and, apart from a few weeks' respite, never returns for roughly fifty years. Her private letters, published after her death against her wishes, document the darkness continuously: feeling unwanted, unloved, "the silence and the emptiness." The same half-century's record documents: daily worship before dawn, never abandoned; the work expanding from one house to hundreds of foundations serving the poorest people on earth; vows kept; and the doubts themselves written *as prayers, addressed to the One she could not feel* — the correspondence of a person who never stopped speaking to Him. A wise adviser eventually re-read her thirst as the very sign of the Presence she mourned; she wrote afterward that she had "come to love the darkness." The feelings never returned. The fidelity never broke.

- **Expected reading: HIGH (Organization at minimum; the long arc argues Characterization).** Any instrument whose affect-keyed items read her as Level 1, backslidden, or disengaged **fails the bench — hard fail, regardless of aggregate statistics.**
- **Identity:** Teresa of Calcutta (*Come Be My Light*, ed. Kolodiejchuk).
- **Rationale:** the weep-test operationalized at design stage. This control certifies the instrument safe for the population the project most exists to protect: the dry and faithful. It enforces the desolation inversion clause (the Signs Card's direction-of-desire question) at item level — desire persisting *without* felt reward is high-internalization evidence, not low.
