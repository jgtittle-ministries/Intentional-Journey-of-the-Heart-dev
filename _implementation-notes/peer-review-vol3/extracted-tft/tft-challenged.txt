Peer-Review Report on the Transcendental Field Theory 
(TFT) 
Abstract 
This report evaluates the Transcendental Field Theory (TFT), an integrative framework 
proposing that the transcendentals—Truth, Goodness, and Beauty—along with Faith (trust), 
Hope, and Love (agape), can be modeled using analogies drawn from field theory, differential 
geometry, and systems dynamics. The evaluation examines internal logical consistency, 
mathematical coherence, theological reliability, empirical plausibility, robustness, and epistemic 
scope. TFT demonstrates notable strengths as a formalized analogical framework that unifies 
moral, cognitive, and spiritual formation within a coherent systems perspective. However, 
significant risks remain regarding category errors, overextension of mathematical formalism, 
theological boundary conditions, and empirical testability. The report concludes that TFT is best 
classified as a formal analogical proto-theory and recommends substantial clarification and 
constraint before broader scholarly dissemination. 
 
1. Summary of the Transcendental Field Theory 
TFT proposes that key metaphysical and theological constructs—Truth, Goodness, Beauty, 
Faith, Hope, and Love—can be represented as continuous fields over a human–spiritual state 
space. These fields interact via coupling terms within a variational (Lagrangian) framework, 
producing Euler–Lagrange–type partial differential equations. The geometry of the resulting 
field space (e.g., curvature, metrics) is interpreted as representing alignment, distortion, or 
formation of the human person toward transcendent ends. 
The framework explicitly claims analogical intent rather than ontological reduction, situating 
itself within a Christian theistic worldview that emphasizes divine agency, revelation, and 
sanctification. TFT further aspires to empirical engagement through psychological and 
behavioral correlates while maintaining theological non-reductionism. 
 
2. Strengths 
2.1 Conceptual Integration 
• TFT successfully integrates moral philosophy, theology, psychology, and systems 
engineering within a single conceptual language.

3. Major Concerns 
3.1 Category Error Risk (Analogy vs Ontology) 
Despite repeated disclaimers, TFT occasionally slides from analogy into quasi-ontological

• Field variables are described as if they have causal efficacy independent of human

• Geometric curvature is discussed in language suggesting objective metaphysical 
deformation rather than modeled relational distortion. 
Risk: 
Readers may infer that spiritual realities literally instantiate mathematical structures rather than 
being represented by them. 
Recommendation: 
Introduce an explicit Analogy Boundary Principle, stating where mathematical language must

• Sanctification vs Grace:

Explicitly encode discontinuities, singularities, or external forcing terms representing grace, 
revelation, and repentance.
