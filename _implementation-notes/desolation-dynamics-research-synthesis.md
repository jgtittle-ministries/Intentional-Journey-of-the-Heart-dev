# Desolation Dynamics — Research Synthesis (the dark stretch of the road)

*Working artifact for a proposed Vol 3 companion chapter — the shadow-side sibling of "Testing Spiritual Time Against the Revival Record." Compiled 2026-07-06 from eight parallel research passes: (1) Scripture, (2) John of the Cross + Teresa, (3) Ignatius, (4) modern struggle psychology, (5) documented cases, (6) acedia, (7) Young-Eisendrath *The Resilient Spirit*, (8) Schön *The Reflective Practitioner*. Lanes 7–8 were John's additions from his counseling shelf. Distinguishes primary-source from interpretation; flags what is verified vs. needs book-in-hand checking.*

## The problem the chapter solves

By every instrument Vol 3 owns, the saint in a dark night and the soul in a slow drift produce the same readings: low felt force, flat dτ/dt, prayer like talking to the ceiling. The tradition insists they are opposites — one among the most formative seasons of a life, the other genuine loss. A model that cannot distinguish them will wound the person it most needs to help. The chapter's job: the **dynamical signature** that separates them — and the finding of this research is that the tradition, Scripture, and the modern literatures all converge on the *same* signature.

## The six-fold convergence: direction of desire, not intensity of darkness

The single strongest result. Six independent witnesses give the same discriminator between God-hiding-in-love and every counterfeit:

1. **Scripture** — Isa 50:10–11 in one oracle: the darkness-walker who *presently* fears and obeys is told to trust; the self-lit-fire crowd is condemned. In every judgment-hiding text (Deut 31:16–17, Isa 59:2) the *turning precedes the hiding*; in every love-hiding text (Ps 42, Job 23, Isa 50:10) desire is intact and starving. The ache is blessed (Ps 42:1, 63:1, Matt 5:6); the *absence* of ache is Laodicea ("I need nothing," Rev 3:17) and the broken cisterns (Jer 2:13).
2. **John of the Cross** — the second sign (DN I.9): "the memory is ordinarily centred upon God, with painful care and solicitude, thinking that it is not serving God" — because "lukewarmness is not solicitous." (First sign: no pleasure in God *and none in creatures either* — the drift finds the world tasting fine.)
3. **The desert fathers** — acedia is desire *inverted with a pious alibi* (the "elsewhere" fantasy, Cassian Inst. 10.2); Aquinas: acedia = "sorrow about spiritual good" (ST II-II q.35) — the drifter feels no sorrow about it at all.
4. **The modern direction literature** — Chloe Lynch, *Presence* 28:1 (2022): "**Desire is the diagnostic.**" Acedia: desire inverted, prayer abandoned, pleasure elsewhere intact. Dark night: desire persists, prayer persevered in, emptiness covers all domains.
5. **Mother Teresa's confessor** — Neuner (1961): "The sure sign of God's hidden presence in this darkness is **the thirst for God**, the craving for at least a ray of His light."
6. **The Ignatian school** — Lonsdale (*The Way* 41/4): the persisting desire for God amid felt abandonment "**is consolation even at the heart of the felt desolation**" — the naked intent of the will.

## The mechanism, from the primary texts

- **DN II.5 (the crown jewel):** the dark night IS "an inflowing of God into the soul, which purges it from its ignorances and imperfections." The same light is felt as darkness because (a) it exceeds the soul's capacity (Pseudo-Dionysius's "ray of darkness") and (b) the soul's impurity makes contact painful. **Not absence — overload plus impedance mismatch.** God has not withdrawn; the inflow is present but unregisterable by the receiving apparatus. This *exceeds* the chapter's proposed claim and should replace it.
- **DN II.10 (log in the fire = latent heat):** fire first dries the wood, then makes it "black, dark and unsightly, and even to give forth a bad odour" — before kindling and transforming it into itself. The same energy does the blackening and the union; the observable state gets worse during the bond-breaking phase; the fire also exposes moisture/vices the wood didn't know it had (misread as regression). The Engine chapter's latent-heat picture has a 400-year-old primary source.
- **DN I.1 (the weaning):** the mother "puts bitter aloes upon her sweet breast, sets down the child from her arms and makes it walk upon its feet" — withdrawal of sweetness as developmental promotion, purifying love of its dependence on reward.
- **Ascent II.9:** faith "alone is the proximate and proportionate means whereby the soul is united with God" — so stripping felt experience is not a detour from the means of union; it is the *installation* of the means.
- **What the night produces (DN I.12):** self-knowledge first ("as from a foundation"), reverence, humility, love of neighbor, teachability.
- **Deut 8:2–3, 16 — Scripture states the mechanism first:** *led* (divine agency) + *humble* + *test* + "to know what was in your heart" + hunger permitted so a deeper feeding could be known + "**to do you good in the end**." The wilderness pattern is Spirit-initiated (Mark 1:12 — the Spirit *drove* Him; the sequence voice-then-silence: wilderness immediately after "This is my beloved Son").
- **Timescales (DN I.14, II.1, II.7):** no fixed duration; the called stay long; the night of spirit "if it is to be really effectual, will last for some years"; **intermittent by design** — "He gives them refreshments of sense so that they may not fall away"; a plateau of "even years" between the nights.
- **Bounding (DN I.8):** night of sense "common and comes to many"; night of spirit "the portion of very few." Do not universalize the deep night; do not prescribe desolation.

## The four-way differential (chapter centerpiece)

| | **Dark night** | **Drift (backslide)** | **Clinical depression** | **Acedia** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signature | anguished fidelity with nothing to show | untroubled distance noticed too late | global weighted sorrow indifferent to location | restless aversion with a pious alibi |
| Desire for God | intact and starving | rerouted; world tastes fine | flattened with everything else | inverted; sorrow *about* spiritual good |
| Pleasure in created things | also dark (first sign) | intact | anhedonia across the board | intact or heightened (Lynch) |
| Practice | persists in anguish | quietly lapses | may collapse with all function | avoided/abandoned — or fled *into* churn (Snell: acedia as overwork) |
| The tell | painful solicitude about not serving God | no ache at all | May's markers: humor, compassion, functioning, sense of self all impaired; self-loathing verdict | the "elsewhere" whisper — "I would serve God better somewhere else" (Cassian 10.2: the exit costumed as devotion) |
| Right response | wait, persevere, be accompanied (Teresa: works of charity + wait for mercy) | wake and turn (negligence, not night) | **treat medically wherever found** (May's rule) — and keep spiritual practice gentle | stay in the cell; stability, work, measure, tears, brief fierce prayer, memento mori (Evagrius/Cassian/Norris) |
| Prognosis note | resisted acedia is followed by "deep peace and inexpressible joy" (Praktikos 12) — a battle with a far side; the night ends in union; drift has no far side, only distance | | co-occurs with the night (May) — treating one is not treating the other | dryness–acedia r = .72 but factorially separable (Büssing) |

**Why the differential matters practically (Gallagher):** desolation is "a trap of the enemy" to be *resisted*; the dark night is "a gift of God" to be *received*. **Misdiagnosis inverts the correct response** — fight what should be received, or surrender to what should be fought. (Note the live scholarly fork: Gallagher's school separates them sharply; FitzGerald/Lonsdale see overlapping territory — people formed in Ignatian prayer eventually hit an experience "not specifically treated by Ignatius" and need John. Both sides agree on the internal differential-diagnosis requirement; John himself names "melancholy" as a confound in DN I.9.)

**A fifth construct worth acknowledging inside the depression quadrant: demoralization** (Kissane/Clarke, palliative psychiatry) — hopelessness + loss of meaning + subjective incompetence *with pleasure capacity intact*. Responds to meaning-restoring accompaniment rather than antidepressants. Much pastoral "desolation" may be this.

## The Ignatian operational layer

- **Definitions (Rules 3–4, Exx 316–317):** desolation defined by contrariety, and the definitional core is the *as-if*: "as if separated from his Creator and Lord."
- **Rule 5 — the hard lockout:** "In time of desolation never to make a change" — in desolation the enemy counsels, "with whose counsels we cannot take a course to decide rightly." (Scope caution per Yavarone: binds spiritual proposals made in tranquility, not practical adjustments to new objective facts.)
- **Rule 6 — change yourself, not your decision:** more prayer, meditation, examen, suitable penance.
- **Rule 7:** the Lord "has taken from him his great fervor… leaving him, however, **grace enough** for eternal salvation" — sufficient grace always remains, unfelt.
- **Rule 9 — the three reasons God permits desolation:** (1) our negligence (corrective — the honest overlap with drift); (2) testing — do we serve God or the consolations of God; (3) humility — "that we may not build a nest in a thing not ours."
- **Rules 10–11:** prepare in consolation; in consolation humble down, in desolation strengthen up — both movements resist the feeling of the moment.
- **Rule 13 — disclosure breaks the grip:** the enemy "wants to be secret and not revealed" — the structural warrant for companioning. *Corpus link: this is the Tell-step / communal-soul-care mechanism (FL.XXX, V2.Exp9A) in 1548 dress.*
- **Second Week rules:** the enemy shifts tactics as the soul matures — from open discouragement to **counterfeit good** ("angel of light," Rule 4); the trajectory test (Rule 5: beginning-middle-end; the counterfeit is usually a *downgrade disguised as devotion*); the serpent's-tail retrospective audit (Rule 6); even authentic consolation does not authenticate the plans made in its afterglow (Rule 8).
- **Gallagher's modern protocol:** be aware / understand / take action — with the triage category of *nonspiritual* desolation (tiredness, illness, depression) that is "a springboard for spiritual desolation… lowers the drawbridge."
- **Young-Eisendrath convergence:** her transcendent-function counsel — hold the tension without prematurely labeling the situation good or bad, let meaning emerge — is a strikingly close secular analogue of Rule 5. Two traditions, same counsel.

## The bifurcation, empirically specified

- **Pargament's own framing:** "Spiritual struggles are a **fork in the road** — to decline / to growth." And his own discipline: "Studies do not show a consistent link between spiritual struggles and growth… **Don't sentimentalize spiritual struggles.**"
- **The default:** Bockrath et al. 2021 meta-analysis (32 longitudinal studies) — struggles significantly predict *decline*; pooled effect for growth **non-significant**. Decline is the statistical drift of unheld struggle.
- **The selector (what routes the fork):** acceptance-not-suppression (Dworsky 2016 — experiential avoidance is the toxin; Krause 2009 — suppressing doubt harms, engaging it neutralizes); meaning found *in* the struggle (Desai & Pargament 2015; Wilt 2016); benevolent reframing; support (stigma data: struggle unspeakable in community is struggle suppressed); and **resolution vs. chronicity** — it is *getting stuck*, not struggling, that predicts harm. Jung/Cowden 2022: the same struggles predicted small increases in *both* depression and growth at 6 months — the fork, longitudinally photographed.
- **The stakes:** Pargament 2001 (*Arch Intern Med*, N=596): religious struggle predicted 22–33% greater 2-year mortality in hospitalized elders ("Wondered whether God had abandoned me," RR 1.28). How desolation is held is not a soft question.
- **Spiritual dryness is its own construct:** Büssing's SDS program (N=425 and N=3,824 priests; N=626 devout laypeople): ~46% occasional, ~12% often/regular; best predictors are specifically *spiritual* (low perception of the sacred) while social support, self-efficacy, stress, and neuroticism drop out — **dryness is not reducible to psychology**. Devout laypeople report more of it than priests.
- **PTG honesty:** self-perceived growth outruns verifiable growth (Frazier 2009; Jayawickreme 2023 — "most self-reports of PTG are greatly exaggerated"). Corroborated-growth studies exist (Park 1996/2004; Haugen 2011) but are modest. State the growth branch as real-but-conditional, never automatic.
- **"Roots grow in the dark":** the thinnest evidence base of all eight lanes. Exline et al. 2023 name long-term growth studies a *future horizon* ("some sources of growth may take time to emerge, and the pain of an ongoing struggle may overshadow thoughts about growth"). Tier as: the tradition's testimony, *consistent with* but not yet proven by longitudinal science.

## The case record

- **Mother Teresa (the anchor; involuntary primary documentation — she asked that the letters be destroyed).** Six months of felt union (1946–47), then darkness beginning **as the work began** (1948–49: "from the time the work has started — nothing"), lasting ~49 years with one ~5-week respite (Oct 1958). "Just that terrible pain of loss, of God not wanting me, of God not being God (Jesus, please forgive my blasphemies)" — the doubts written *as prayers*. Neuner's reframe (1961) turned resistance into embrace: "For the first time in this 11 years — I have come to love the darkness." Fruit across the same decades: one Calcutta house → ~600 foundations, ~4,000 sisters, ~125 countries; Nobel 1979 — the "silence and emptiness" letter written three months before it. Practice unbroken (4:30 a.m., daily Eucharist). **Answer Hitchens on evidence, not authority:** persistent longing, unbroken practice, doubts framed as prayer — atheists don't write "I miss God" letters. (McAllister 2024: fails the clinical-depression profile; the darkness "promoted virtue.")
- **The onset-at-commissioning pattern:** Teresa's night began with the work; Thérèse's with her first hemoptysis (Easter 1896 → death, the "night of nothingness," the table of sinners); Brainerd's deepest discouragement immediately preceded the Crossweeksung revival; Spurgeon verbatim: "**This depression comes over me whenever the Lord is preparing a larger blessing for my ministry; the cloud is black before it breaks.**" The records place desolation at the threshold of fruit, not after failure.
- **Resolution is interpretive, not experiential (major refinement):** Teresa's feeling never returned — the 1961 change was interpretive; Thérèse died inside the night, willing in it ("My God, I love you!"); Spurgeon's episodes recurred to the end, reframed as "a prophet in rough clothing." Only Lewis shows a felt reopening — and to "a silent, certainly not uncompassionate, gaze," not restored consolation. Accumulated τ becomes visible mainly as changed interpretation + sustained obedience.
- **Subtypes the record supports:** reparatory/charism-linked (Teresa); solidarity-with-unbelievers (Thérèse's table of sinners); grief-desolation — short, resolving (Lewis; the door "slammed in your face… bolting and double bolting on the inside"); constitutional melancholy entangled with the spiritual (Spurgeon — who also *de*-spiritualizes: "dyspepsia has been mistaken for backsliding"; Brainerd, whom Edwards taught to distinguish "spiritual desertion" from "the disease of melancholy"); formative assault (Luther's Anfechtung — *tentatio* as the third maker of the theologian; "by living, nay by dying and being damned one becomes a theologian").
- **Counter-evidence, honestly:** Templeton (collapse was *doubt-led* — longing cooled into settled conclusion; arguably confirms the taxonomy via a different pathway — offer tentatively, and note the Strobel "I miss him!" anecdote is single-sourced with Alzheimer's confound); Lobdell (moral injury from observed hypocrisy). **Survivorship caveat:** those who quietly collapsed didn't leave 40-year letter archives; the documentation selects for perseverers.
- **Image convergence for the writing:** Lewis's "bolting and double bolting on the inside" (1961) and Spurgeon's "iron bolt which so mysteriously fastens the door of hope" (1875) — same image, independent. Both answered by Rule 7/DN II.5: the bolt is felt, not real; sufficient grace remains inside.

## The practitioner layer (Schön + the two secular sources, under Powlison/Trentham)

- **The differential is problem-*setting*, not problem-solving** (Schön RP p. 40): real problems "do not present themselves as givens — they must be constructed from materials which are puzzling, troubling, and uncertain." Naming-and-framing is the skilled work — the swampy lowland where "the problems of greatest human concern" live.
- **"The Patient as a Universe of One"** (RP ch. 4): theory — Sanjuanist, Ignatian, clinical — supplies *repertoire* (exemplars a new case is seen-as), never verdicts. The companion "constructs a new theory of the unique case."
- **How companions misdiagnose — frame-lock:** "selective inattention, junk categories, situational control" (RP ~p. 69 — verify wording in the physical book). The companion with only one category ("it's always spiritual" / "it's always clinical") junk-categorizes the anomalies. Klein's pattern-anomaly work corroborates: the depression that *doesn't* respond like a dark night should register as pattern-violation, not be explained away. The pastor-vs-psychiatrist standoff over one directee is a Schön–Rein *frame conflict*.
- **Discernment probes as move-testing experiments:** does the darkness lift with rest/medication? does desire for God survive beneath the dryness? is there an "elsewhere" whispering? does anything hurt at all? Each probe's back-talk discriminates among frames.
- **The appropriation boundary (IJH's contribution):** Schön's "conversation with the situation" has exactly two parties; spiritual direction confesses **three** — the Spirit is the true Director, and the back-talk includes *given* tests (Gal 5:22; 1 John 4; the Ignatian rules) the practitioner did not construct. Same structural move as "no will overridden" (SIR models) and Spirit-fervency (CVF). Also: ministry absorbed Schön frictionlessly because CPE's action-reflection *predates him by ~50 years* (Boisen) — the borrowing runs the honest direction.
- **Formation link:** *Educating the Reflective Practitioner* (1987) — coached practicum, "hall of mirrors" (supervision enacts what it teaches) — maps directly onto FC1 Companion formation / Leader Lab. A Companion-formation design with exemplar cases of all four quadrants is a straightforwardly Schönian practicum.
- **Young-Eisendrath (*The Resilient Spirit*, 1996; hardcover title *The Gifts of Suffering*):** her selector between growth and destruction — hold the tension (transcendent function) without premature labeling; see one's own contribution; let meaning emerge over time; turn toward compassion/transmission (her four exemplars all teach out of their wound — 2 Cor 1:4 in secular dress). **Convergence:** hold-the-tension ≈ Rule 5. **Divergence to name:** her Buddhist frame reads desire as the root of suffering (dukkha to dissolve); the dark night's signature is desire *intact and starving* — the thirst is not the disease but the evidence of health being purified. Her renewal is *made*; the chapter's is *received* (1 Cor 3:7). Her frame has no Giver, no enemy, no given telos. Quote-verification note: her direct quotes are preserved-fragment grade — verify against John's copy before print.

## The safeguarding rail (required element)

1. **May's co-occurrence rule:** dark night and depression "so often coexist" that the differential must never function as a triage gate withholding clinical care — **identify and treat depression wherever it exists, dark night or no**; treating it does not abort the night.
2. **May's markers** (dark night vs depression): humor intact / compassion intact / functioning maintained / sense of self intact / obscure sense of meaning — vs. collapse of all five and the self-loathing verdict. His clinician's tell: no countertransference frustration with dark-night souls.
3. **Munitiz's hard line:** "Nobody in a state of depression should be encouraged… to undergo the Exercises." Screen for fatigue, burnout, bereavement, trauma, depression *before* applying desolation rules (Lonsdale).
4. **Referral triggers** (practitioner-grade consensus): any suicidal ideation or harm content; psychotic symptoms; persistent functional impairment; suspicion of diagnosable condition; no improvement despite accompaniment. Corroborating systematic review (Unto 2023, low-tier but consistent): the depressed show desire to end suffering quickly; dark-night experiencers frame suffering as purgative and are willing to endure it.
5. **Demoralization** as the middle construct (meaning-collapse, hedonic capacity intact) — meaning-restoring accompaniment, not (only) medication.
6. **The anti-romanticism guard (both directions):** don't spiritualize treatable depression (harm by delay); don't medicalize genuine formation crises (harm by flattening — Durà-Vilà). And don't *prescribe* desolation: Pargament — "don't sentimentalize"; John — the deep night is for "very few."
