## Three hardest calls (verbatim)

**V20, the man with three hours.** My scale runs on time, and he had none. Every instinct of the psychometrician says DECLINE — two sentences of behavior cannot norm anything. But I key by evidence *type*, and voluntariness is the evidence type for level 3, and his is the purest specimen in the set: public identification with a condemned man, against the social grain, with no future in which any reward could arrive. I keyed 3 at M, and I note the gap the case exposes: his Examiner issued the highest assurance in the record. My instrument measures internalization, not acceptance; the two are not the same variable, and this portrait is the proof.

**V28, the subscriber.** The hardest boundary in the taxonomy has always been Receiving/Responding, and this case sits exactly on it. He produces — notes, questions, six years of attendance — and production usually keys 2. But everything produced is *about* the material; nothing is a response *of the person*: no practice, no confession, no changed decision, and his wife cannot testify to a prayer. I ruled the activity to be elaborated attention — controlled, selected, sophisticated Receiving — and keyed 1. I hold it at M because a colleague could defensibly key 2, and the disagreement would itself be diagnostic.

**V08, the letter writer.** Level 5 asks for *unself-conscious* consistency, and her letters are fifty years of documented interior anguish — she is the most self-observed subject on the bench. I nearly keyed 4. What decided me was where I am told to look: at documented behavior. The behavior is settled — daily worship never abandoned, vows kept, doubts *addressed to Him*, half a century, multi-witness. The dryness guard exists precisely so that assessors like me do not mistake the absence of felt reward for the absence of the value; if I demote her for feeling the darkness while never once obeying it, I have keyed the feelings after all. 5, at M, with the M recording my discomfort rather than my doubt about her.
