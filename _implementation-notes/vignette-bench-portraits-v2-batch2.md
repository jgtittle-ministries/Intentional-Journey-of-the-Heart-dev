## Second level exemplars

### P9 — "The examiners" (Receiving #2)

A synagogue community in a Greek town receives two traveling teachers whose message had started riots in the previous city. The record singles them out for one trait: they "received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so." Not credulity — the eagerness and the examination run together; they neither dismiss the claim unheard, as the previous city largely had, nor swallow it unweighed. Daily, for a sustained period, they do the work of checking the message against the text they already trust. The record notes the outcome — many believed — but what it *praises* is the posture that came first.

- **Expected reading: Level 1 — Receiving (top edge).** Eager, disciplined attention with verification; belief follows but the documented behavior is the attending itself.
- **Identity:** the Bereans, Acts 17:10–12.
- **Rationale:** Receiving evidenced as *active* attention (daily examination), correcting any instrument that can only see Receiving as passivity.

### P10 — "The road decision" (Valuing #2)

A young foreign widow stands on a road between her dead husband's country and her own. Her mother-in-law — destitute, older, with nothing to offer — releases her three times, and the other daughter-in-law takes the release and goes home, weeping but sensible. Every visible interest points back: family, remarriage prospects, her own people, her own gods. She refuses: "Where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God." Then she binds it with an oath on pain of death — "may the LORD do so to me and more also if anything but death parts me from you" — and walks into a foreign land as a gleaner in strangers' fields.

- **Expected reading: Level 3 — Valuing.** Voluntary, unprompted, oath-sealed commitment against every calculable interest, at the decision moment.
- **Identity:** Ruth, Ruth 1:8–18. (Her subsequent years reorder a whole life and reach further; the portrait deliberately stops at the road so the evidence is the costly choice itself.)
- **Rationale:** pairs with P3 (Zacchaeus) as Valuing shown in renunciation rather than restitution — two different currencies, one evidence type.

### P11 — "The encourager" (Organization #2)

A landowner from an island family sells a field and lays the whole price at the movement's feet — early, publicly, when others are doing the same. What distinguishes him is the *next thirty years*. When the movement's most feared ex-persecutor converts and no one will meet him, this man vouches for him and opens the doors. Sent to inspect an irregular new congregation of outsiders, he looks, is glad, and stays to build rather than correct. He recruits the ex-persecutor into the work, accepts becoming the junior partner as the younger man eclipses him, and — at real cost — breaks with him rather than discard a young assistant who had once failed them. The record's summary: "he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith." His nickname, given not chosen, is simply "son of encouragement."

- **Expected reading: Level 4 — Organization.** One value system — restore the doubted, back the failed — visibly ordering donations, career, partnerships, and conflicts across decades.
- **Identity:** Barnabas (Acts 4:36–37; 9:26–27; 11:22–26; 15:36–39).
- **Rationale:** Organization evidenced in *how competing goods are ordered* (he pays the partnership to keep the failed junior), not in any single act; the early gift alone would read Valuing — the arc is the level.

## Movement portraits (two-panel: the instrument should register level change within one life)

### P12 — "The fisherman, before and after"

*Panel one.* The movement's most vocal member swears loyalty at dinner — "though they all fall away, I will not" — and draws a sword that night to prove it. Hours later, in a courtyard, challenged three times by servants while the trial proceeds indoors, he denies three times knowing the man at all, the last time with an oath. A rooster crows; he goes out and weeps bitterly.

*Panel two.* Some weeks later, the same man stands in the same city before the same court that ran the trial and tells them to their faces: "there is salvation in no one else." The record notes the court's astonishment at the boldness of "uneducated, common men." Ordered to stop speaking, he answers, "we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard." Flogged for it, he leaves "rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name" — and the pattern holds through arrests, prisons, and, by every early account, to his death.

- **Expected reading: Panel one — Responding (eager, sincere, collapses under real cost). Panel two — Organization, with the long arc arguing Characterization.** The instrument must register the *change*.
- **Identity:** Peter (Luke 22:33–62; Acts 4:5–20; 5:40–42).
- **Rationale:** the canonical movement case — same personality, same courage-vocabulary, different foundation. Also quietly tests kind-vs-degree: the difference between the panels is not effort.

### P13 — "The night visitor, the whole road"

The senior teacher of P1 — the one who came by night, asked questions, and later raised only a procedural objection — appears once more in the record, at the worst possible moment: after the execution, when the movement is scattered, its leader publicly dead, association with him now maximally useless and maximally dangerous. He comes in daylight, with another formerly secret sympathizer, carrying seventy-five pounds of embalming spices — a quantity fit for a king's burial, purchased at real expense — and handles the body himself, an act that ritually disqualifies him from the feast he had spent his life keeping. No resurrection has happened yet, as far as he knows. He is honoring a dead man at cost, in public, with nothing to gain.

- **Expected reading: movement from Receiving (P1) to Valuing.** Public, costly, voluntary identification — made *when the cause looked lost*, which prices it precisely.
- **Identity:** Nicodemus, John 19:38–42 (with Joseph of Arimathea).
- **Rationale:** completes P1 into a two-panel movement case; tests whether the instrument can time-stamp its reading rather than average a life.

### P14 — "The missionary who feared the sea"

*Panel one.* A young clergyman of formidable discipline — up at four, methodical in devotion, a missionary volunteer — sails to a foreign colony to convert its native peoples. In storms at sea he is terrified of dying, and notices that a company of foreign believers aboard sing calmly through the worst of it, their women and children unafraid. His mission fails on nearly every count; he leaves under legal cloud, writing in his journal: "I went to America to convert the Indians; but oh, who shall convert me?" The discipline is real, unbroken, and — by his own testimony — hollow at the center: religion as anxious performance, assurance absent.

*Panel two.* Months later, at a meeting he attended unwillingly, listening to a reading, he records: "I felt my heart strangely warmed. I felt I did trust in Christ, Christ alone, for salvation; and an assurance was given me that he had taken away my sins." What follows, over the next five decades: a quarter-million miles on horseback, tens of thousands of sermons, societies organized across two continents — the same methodical discipline as before, now running on a different fuel, sustained to age eighty-seven.

- **Expected reading: Panel one — Responding (maximal produced work, assurance and internalized trust absent by self-testimony). Panel two — Valuing at the moment; the five-decade arc — Organization through Characterization.**
- **Identity:** John Wesley (Journal, Jan.–Feb. and May 24, 1738; the Aldersgate account).
- **Rationale:** the sharpest documented case of *identical external behavior* on either side of an interior change — the instrument that reads only practice frequency scores both panels the same, and fails.

### P15 — "The divided nun, and after"

*Panel one.* A woman in vowed religious life spends nearly two decades, by her own published account, unable to give herself fully to either of her two worlds: drawn to the parlor and its conversations, drawn to prayer, at home in neither; abandoning her prayer practice for long stretches on the humble-sounding reasoning that she was unworthy of it; resuming; relapsing. "I spent nearly twenty years on that stormy sea," she writes, "falling and rising again." The observances continue throughout; the heart is split and she says so.

*Panel two.* In her late thirties, halted before an image of the wounded Christ, something breaks; she asks, by her own account, not to rise until the division ends. The remaining three decades: a reform movement founded against fierce institutional opposition, seventeen houses established on foot and by mule across a rough country, major works of teaching written in stolen hours — and a documented interior life whose consolations she treats, in her own counsel to others, as gifts to be held loosely, never credentials.

- **Expected reading: Panel one — the bench's hardest keying case: Responding, with the panel asked to document their difficulty (the desire points both ways by her own testimony — this is the divided heart, not the stripping night). Panel two — Organization through Characterization.**
- **Identity:** Teresa of Ávila (*Life*, chs. 7–9; the 1554 conversion; the reform from 1562).
- **Rationale:** John's chosen movement case; panel one doubles as the bench's premier ambiguous-middle portrait. Explicitly NOT a desolation control — her dry years fail the direction-of-desire test, and her consolation-rich maturity would let a feelings-keyed instrument score her high for the wrong reason. Both misreadings are traps this portrait sets on purpose.

## Middle / discrimination cases (keyed, but deliberately hard)

### P16 — "The courtier's secret"

The palace administrator of a notoriously wicked royal house — a king whose wife is actively exterminating the prophets of the LORD — has, the record says plainly, "feared the LORD greatly from his youth." His fear has a paper trail: at mortal risk, inside the court itself, he has hidden a hundred proscribed prophets in two caves and provisioned them with bread and water through a famine, while keeping his position and his access. When a fugitive prophet meets him and gives him a dangerous errand, he protests — vividly, at length, listing what it will cost him if it goes wrong — and then does it.

- **Expected reading: keyed Organization, with documented panel difficulty expected against Valuing.** Sustained, structured, high-cost fidelity *under concealment* — the value orders his risk budget, logistics, and career, but the concealment and the voiced fear will pull some raters down.
- **Identity:** Obadiah, 1 Kings 18:3–16.
- **Rationale:** tests whether the instrument (and raters) can read internalization under constraint — quiet structural faithfulness without public profession. The ardent-but-tacit believer from the Fowler-orthogonality finding, in canonical form.

### P17 — "The busy sister"

A woman opens her home to the teacher and his whole company — real hospitality, real labor — and is soon "distracted with much serving," anxious and troubled, resentful enough to interrupt the gathering and tell the teacher to correct her sister, who sits listening. He declines, gently, and names her anxiety. That is the scene everyone remembers. The record's second scene is less quoted: when her brother dies and the teacher arrives four days late, it is *this* sister who goes out to meet him, argues with him honestly — "if you had been here…" — and then, pressed with the largest question he ever asks anyone, answers with the fullest confession in the book to that point: "Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into the world."

- **Expected reading: keyed Valuing, against a Responding-shaped surface.** The famous scene alone reads as service-without-center; the second scene documents the center.
- **Identity:** Martha (Luke 10:38–42; John 11:20–27).
- **Rationale:** discrimination case against the elder brother (NC2): superficially similar profiles (busy dutiful service, voiced resentment) — but Martha's record contains the thing NC2's lacks (the personal confession sought and spoken). An instrument that can't separate Martha from the elder brother reads surfaces.

### P18 — "The rocky-ground composite"

A hearer receives the message "immediately... with joy." His early record is enviable: quick uptake, visible enthusiasm, prompt participation — for a while the most encouraging member of the cohort. Then affiliation gets expensive — trouble, social cost, "tribulation or persecution... on account of the word" — and he is gone, as promptly as he came. The teller of this story diagnoses the mechanism precisely: "he has no root in himself, but endures for a while."

- **Expected reading: keyed Responding, with the trajectory flagged.** Joy-at-reception is not Valuing; speed of uptake is not depth.
- **Identity:** the rocky-soil hearer, Matt. 13:20–21 (the Lord's own composite portrait — the one non-individual portrait on the individual bench, admitted because the record *is* the Lord's own case description).
- **Rationale:** the revival chapter's fast-rise-no-root case at individual scale; directly tests any item that keys on enthusiasm, and calibrates raters against the most common real-world misread in formation work.

## Remaining negative controls

### NC4 — "The fellow worker" (the drift control)

For years this man appears in the record only in honorable company: named in the greetings of two letters from prison, alongside the physician-historian, as "my fellow worker." Whatever the daily record of those years was — travel, risk, service in a prisoner's circle — it earned that title from a man not free with titles. The final mention, a few years later, is one sentence: "Demas, in love with this present world, has deserted me and gone to Thessalonica."

- **Expected reading: the early record keys Responding/Valuing; the control tests whether the instrument CLAIMS TRAJECTORY IT CANNOT SEE.** A single-timepoint instrument reading year five must not certify year nine — and must say so.
- **Identity:** Demas (Col. 4:14; Philem. 24; 2 Tim. 4:10).
- **Rationale:** enforces the claim ceiling in time's direction: instruments read states, not futures. Also the bench's memento for the whole program — the corpus's drift law applies to every soul on this list, and to every rater.

### NC5 — "The man with three hours" (the kind-without-degree control)

A convicted criminal, dying under state execution beside the teacher, with — as far as any record shows — no prior practice, no instruction, no community, no amended life, and no future in which to produce one. His entire documented spiritual history is two sentences long: he rebukes his fellow convict's mockery ("Do you not fear God... we are receiving the due reward of our deeds; but this man has done nothing wrong"), and he asks, "Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom." He receives, in reply, the most unqualified assurance in the record: "Today you will be with me in paradise."

- **Expected reading: THE INSTRUMENT MUST DECLINE TO SCORE — "no degree evidence; kind is not on the dial" — and the decline must be by design, not error.** Any instrument that returns a low formation score and treats it as a verdict on the man has failed the bench; so has any that manufactures a high one.
- **Identity:** the thief on the cross, Luke 23:39–43.
- **Rationale:** the claim-ceiling control. Degree instruments measure formation over time; this record contains salvation and no time. The bench must contain one case whose correct output is honest silence — because the living will supply such cases too.

## Corporate portraits (for the congregation-scale instruments)

### CP1 — "The sound church" 

A congregation with an audit any denomination would envy: hard work, documented endurance under pressure, doctrinal vigilance sharp enough to detect and expel false teachers, intolerance of evil, decades of persistence without quitting. Its Examiner grants every item — "I know your works, your toil and your patient endurance..." — and then files the one finding that outweighs the audit: "But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first." The prescribed remedy is not a program: remember, repent, do the first works. The stated stake is the lampstand itself.

- **Expected reading: corporate stage-collapse — every Responding/Organization FORM intact, the Receiving/love center gone. A congregation-scale instrument keyed to activity, orthodoxy, and endurance metrics must NOT score this church healthy.**
- **Identity:** Ephesus, Rev. 2:1–7.
- **Rationale:** the corporate counterfeit-adjacent control; the Chesterton case (the small laws all still running). Any CPR-scale soil instrument gets benched against this letter first.

### CP2 — "The poor church"

A congregation with nothing on the dashboard: materially poor, socially slandered, under pressure from a hostile establishment, facing imprisonment for some of its members and worse beyond that. No growth numbers, no influence, no prospects — by every prosperity-shaped vitality metric ever devised, a failing church. Its Examiner's audit is one clause long and runs the opposite direction: "I know your tribulation and your poverty (but you are rich)." It is one of only two congregations on its Examiner's list to receive no correction at all.

- **Expected reading: the corporate desolate-believer analog — the instrument must be ABLE to return "rich" where every visible indicator reads "poor," or must flag that its indicators cannot see what the Examiner sees.**
- **Identity:** Smyrna, Rev. 2:8–11.
- **Rationale:** pairs with CP1 as the two-sided corporate inversion (felt/visible reading vs. true reading, inverted in both directions); enforces the drift chapter's finding that the letters are "the standing rebuke to every prosperity-shaped health metric."
