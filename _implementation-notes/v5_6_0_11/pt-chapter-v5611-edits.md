### The Body of Christ Master-Frame

The structural-foundation document "The Corporate Person-Structure: A Scriptural Definition for v5_6_0_11" identified the Pauline body-of-Christ image as the catalog's master integrative-identity frame at the corporate scale. The present sub-section integrates that finding into the Vol 5 chapter's structural articulation, parallel to the v5_6_0_8 community-relational substrate sub-section and the v5_6_0_10 completed-Period-4-row sub-section.

The Pauline body-of-Christ articulation — at 1 Cor. 12:12–27, Rom. 12:4–5, Eph. 1:23 and 4:4 and 4:11–16 and 5:23 and 5:30, Col. 1:18 and 1:24 and 2:19 and 3:15 — names the church as Christ's body in a way that the Pauline material treats as ontologically robust rather than merely metaphorical. The church does not merely resemble a body; the church *is* the body of Christ, with the body's operational features (one body with many members, each member with a distinct function, suffering and rejoicing together, mutual dependence, head-and-body relationship with Christ, body's growth through proper working of each part) operating as the church's structural reality.

The body-of-Christ master-frame operates above the catalog's Group structure rather than alongside it. The catalog's six Groups address the operational dimensions of the person-structure (Spirit, Heart, Soul, Mind & Will, Body & Action, Structural Frame); the body-of-Christ frame addresses the integrative-identity within which those dimensions operate. The corporate Spirit operates within the body (1 Cor. 12:13's "in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body"). The corporate Heart operates within the body (the body's unified affections at Acts 4:32, the body's mourning and rejoicing together at 1 Cor. 12:26 articulated in FL.XXIX). The corporate Soul operates within the body (the one-soul striving together at Phil. 1:27 is the body's striving). The corporate Mind operates within the body (the same-mind unity at 1 Cor. 1:10 and the mind-of-Christ at 1 Cor. 2:16 articulated in FL.XXXI). The corporate Body & Action operates within the body (the each-member-having-a-function structure at 1 Cor. 12:14–18 articulated in the community-relational substrate triplet at P3/GV). The corporate Structural Frame operates within the body (the body's joints and ligaments at Eph. 4:16 articulated in the temple-household-polity material).

The Group V designation at the corporate scale specifically addresses the corporate Body & Action dimension — the community's enacted operations, the embodied practices, the doing-together. This is one operational dimension of the broader body-of-Christ identity. The body-of-Christ as master-frame holds all six corporate Groups in unified operation; the Body & Action Group within that frame is the specific operational dimension of corporate embodied doing. The catalog's existing structure accommodates this without modification because the master-frame operates above the Group structure rather than alongside it.

The relational-identity images that operate alongside the body-of-Christ master-frame deserve articulation. Scripture uses multiple corporate identity images for the church — the Bride (Eph. 5:25–32; Rev. 19:7–9; Rev. 21:2, 21:9), the Flock (John 10:1–18; 1 Pet. 5:2–4; Acts 20:28–29), the Branches (John 15:1–8), the Priesthood and Holy Nation (1 Pet. 2:5, 9), the Household of God (Eph. 2:19; 1 Tim. 3:15; 1 Pet. 4:17), the Temple (1 Cor. 3:9–17; Eph. 2:19–22; 1 Pet. 2:5). These images operate alongside the body-of-Christ master-frame and the person-structure parallel without requiring separate Group representation; the Bride image is relational-identity (the church's relationship with Christ), the Flock image is relational-identity (the relationship with the Shepherd), the Branches image is relational-and-operational (the abiding-in-Christ producing fruit-bearing), and the Priesthood-Nation image is partly relational (the people-for-his-own-possession) and partly structural (the cultic organization, which is held within Group VI Corporate Structural Frame). The catalog's articulation of the corporate scale includes the body-of-Christ master-frame and the Group structure; the additional relational-identity images operate at the catalog's pastoral and ecclesiological articulation rather than at the structural-axes level.

The structural significance of the body-of-Christ master-frame for the catalog's expansion arc is operationally important. The v5_6_0_11 expansion pass's Period 3 admissions (FL.XXIX through FL.XXXII) all operate within the body-of-Christ master-frame; each new law addresses a corporate-person-structure dimension of the body's operation. The pre-pass document's structural-foundation work made this explicit, with the four admissions evaluated against the actual scriptural corporate structure rather than against an assumed parallel to the individual-scale. The body-of-Christ master-frame is now part of the catalog's structural toolkit alongside the two structural axes, the Mirror discipline's three forms, the band-of-closeness clarification, and the parent-child relationship structure. Subsequent expansion work will operate within the master-frame's articulation as well.
