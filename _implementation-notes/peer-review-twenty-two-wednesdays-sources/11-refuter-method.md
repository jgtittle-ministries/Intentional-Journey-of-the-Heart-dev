# Adversarial Verification — Method, Practice, and Citation Set

**Refuter scope:** nosek.md, schon.md, edmondson.md, citation-sweep.md
**Method:** every finding checked against `paper-v2.3.txt` (paragraph numbers as given). Six factual
claims were verified independently against primary sources; the verification log is at the end.

## A. NOSEK

### N-F1 — "keeps a researcher honest" misnames what preregistration is for. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM.**

Verified against the source. Nosek et al. (2018) state: *"Good intentions are not sufficient to overcome
the fallibility of memory, motivated reasoning, and cognitive biases that can occur outside of conscious
awareness or control,"* and frame the practice around prediction-vs-postdiction and constraining
researcher degrees of freedom. So the paper's gloss at [8] does name the wrong mechanism, and the
reviewer's replacement wording is a genuine improvement.

What weakens it: the finding's damage argument rests on reading "keeps a researcher honest" as a
fraud-guard. In ordinary English that idiom means *keeps you from drifting or fooling yourself* — which
is close to the self-deception framing the source actually uses — and the paper's very next sentence
supplies exactly that reading: "I am telling you how I could be wrong before I know whether I am." A
one-clause colloquial gloss in a pastoral journal is a wording defect, not a credibility threat.

### N-F2 — "This is not a preregistration; the paper should not claim the word." Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: WEAKENED (headline partly REFUTED). Severity: MEDIUM.**

The headline attacks a claim the paper does not make. [8] reads: "The academic literature **calls**
publishing a design and its failure conditions before the results 'preregistration.'" The paper never
says it *is* a preregistration, never uses "preregistered," never claims a deposit or a registry, and
never uses the word again anywhere in the manuscript. The abstract [2], which the finding quotes as a
second locus, does not contain the word at all — it says "The design is presented before its results,
with disconfirmation conditions stated in advance," which is simply true.

So: invoking the concept by report, in a theology journal, is not the same act as claiming to have
performed a registered preregistration, and the finding conflates them.

**What survives, restated precisely** — this is the reviewer's own Extension 2, and it should have been
the finding:
- **[8] gives an inaccurate definition of a technical term.** "Publishing a design and its failure
  conditions before the results" omits the registry, the timestamp, and the analysis plan — the
  elements that make the practice work. A spiritual-formation reader will carry that definition
  forward. Real, checkable, fixed by one clause.
- **The temporal disclosure.** [5] reports what has already been observed in this cohort ("The first
  fifteen minutes are now the 'room build' … it turns out that families who build the room together
  settle into it differently … I did not plan that"); [40] reports a mechanism "redesigned twice in the
  field"; [16] reports "a year and a half of prototyping." If the word stays, the start date belongs in
  the text. Survives at MEDIUM.

On the remedy (OSF deposit before the September start): see Section E, item 2. Short answer — a deposit
is cheap and would strengthen the paper on its own merits, but it is **not** what fixes the identified
defect, and three of the nine listed deposit contents collide with the paper's own governing ethic.

### N-F3 — n is never stated. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH.**

Confirmed by exhaustive search of the manuscript: no count of families, adults, teens, or consenting
respondents appears anywhere. Meanwhile the design generates counts throughout — "how many walked back
in with the return card," "how many minutes," participation breadth read as "everyone, most, or a few"
[50], [29] — with no denominator. Nothing in the paper's ethic obstructs the fix: n is a room-level
number, names nobody, and [23] already publishes sessions, quarters, and minutes. Cleanest survivor in
my set and the cheapest fix in the review. (Reached independently by Vazire F8.)

### N-F4 — Preregistration and mid-course adaptation are in tension. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM.**

The tension is real and unnamed: [44] "updating content as the train is moving as necessary to best
serve this group at this juncture" sits beside [53]–[58]'s advance predictions. But the finding
under-credits [59], which already pre-commits the terminal claim to "this ran, here is everything it
recorded against the predictions it published, **and here is the revised design**" — an explicit advance
commitment to report the revisions, which is most of what a change log is for. What remains is that the
paper does not say *which* elements are frozen. The frozen/adaptive two-column fix is cheap, fully
compatible with a pastoral design, and is the most valuable remedy in the Nosek review.

Best-value survivor in the set.

## B. SCHÖN

### S-F1 — Teach-back is not reflection-on-action; "consolidation" is not Schön's claim. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM (downgraded).**

All three sub-parts are accurate on the text. [20]'s hedge is "the learning literature would recognize"
— not "Schön" — but fn 14 is the only citation attached to that clause, so the reader does attribute it;
1(a) holds. The fn 14 gloss ("reflection afterward makes [tacit knowing] available for examination and
teaching") is genuinely more optimistic than the source; 1(b) holds. And the teach-back prompt is
procedural rather than episodic; 1(c) holds.

Downgraded to MEDIUM because the reviewer's own best fix proves the substance is present: cite Schön
for the round's *first two* questions, "what went well and what they would do differently," which are
retrospective and episodic. The defect is one footnote attached one clause too late, not a misuse of the
concept in the design. The observation that the teach-back manufactures espoused theory weekly is the
most useful thing in this review — but it is an offer, not a finding.

### S-F2 — Two traditions sit adjacent and are not distinguished. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW.**
The reviewer concedes the paper does not attribute action research to him ("the author has not borrowed
my prestige for a tradition I do not belong to"), so the finding is a risk of reader inference plus an
under-citation. Under-citation is not a fidelity defect. The citation sweep independently rates [9]'s
action-research gloss "a faithful, non-inflated summary" of Lewin.

### S-F3 — No mechanism can detect espoused theory vs theory-in-use divergence. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM.**

The categorical form is false against the paper's machinery. Four channels exist through which the
program's behavior is reported by someone other than the designer's introspection:

1. **[20] The Leader Feedback Round.** The leader "invites the room to answer the same two questions
   about the evening," weekly. Participants are not calibrated observers of the designer's making;
   they are reporting how the evening landed.
2. **[40]/[41] The quarterly self-read — the strongest counterexample, and the reviewer does not
   engage it.** The mechanism was redesigned twice *specifically to stop supplying the designer's
   categories*: "The second design kept the question but dropped the taxonomy… The final design adds
   nothing at all." [41]: "The team never hands the room a stage to rate itself against; unprompted
   salience… is a truer read of what it values than any answer to a supplied category." [28]:
   "whatever the room concludes about itself is received without correction." That is a deliberately
   built channel for the room to describe the program in the room's own words — structurally the same
   question the reviewer proposes for his outsider ("judging only by what this program does, what does
   it appear to believe?"), asked of insiders instead.
3. **[34] The five family-facing pages** carry participant-authored free text: "what has surprised me,
   what do I want to do differently."
4. **[40] Two designs abandoned after failing in the field** — one on a colleague's reaction (a person
   outside the team), one on how it landed in the room. The reviewer cites this as pointing "the wrong
   way," which is fair for the *round* but selective: it also demonstrates that a design element can
   be killed by outside reaction, i.e. that a channel exists and has worked.

**What survives, narrowed:** no channel is *independent of the designer*. Every one of the above is
framed by him, opened by him going first ([28] "The leader answers first, all three"), and read by him
or by companions he selected and formed [36]. That claim is true, uncontested by the paper, and worth
filing. So is the absence of the espoused/theory-in-use vocabulary, which would name something the
design half-does. Two of the three illustrations are strong — [21]'s structurally undeclinable offer,
and [46] describing a standardized, pre-printed, rung-scored, three-week-promotion instrument as "a
shepherd's eye."

**Accuracy defect inside the finding:** the reviewer writes "in a room of eight or ten families." That
number appears nowhere in the paper — it is invented, and it is exactly the reasoning-from-assumed-facts
failure the brief warns about. (It also sits oddly beside Nosek F3, which correctly reports that no n
exists.)

The remedy — one outsider, two hours a quarter — is cheap, pastorally harmless, touches no participant,
and is the convergence point of four reviewers. Keep it.

### S-F4 — Leader Feedback Round missing three practicum conditions. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW as source fidelity; (b) and (d) are the residue.**
- (a) no coaching leg — accurate on the text, but a design suggestion; the paper never claims the round
  is a practicum.
- (b) **no private channel** — SURVIVES and is the strongest part. The cost is a direct consequence of
  [16]'s family-never-divided rule and the paper does not name it. Five minutes out of the circle after
  each second running costs nothing.
- (c) "the learning bind has been designed out" — REFUTED as filed. It asks a design paper published
  before results to describe an unobserved future state, in a review that elsewhere faults the paper
  for claiming things in advance of evidence.
- (d) **scope note (professionals vs laypeople)** — SURVIVES at LOW, and is the actual fidelity item in
  the finding. Schön's practicum work is professional education with a coach who has mastered the
  practice; the extension to parents and teenagers is undeclared. One clause.

### S-F5 — Unprompted vocabulary is still espoused, and the design has been teaching it. Filed LOW.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM — I am UPGRADING this one.**

This is the one finding in my set that was filed too low. The paper's load-bearing novel instrument is
[41]'s claim that "unprompted salience… is a truer read of what it values than any answer to a supplied
category," and disconfirmation condition [58] rests entirely on it. But the room has heard "safe,
present, clear, intentional" spoken aloud at the opening and closing of every session [17], and the
migration the team is listening for is [51]'s "from safety language toward belonging language" — the
baseline term is literally a word the liturgy trains twice a week for twenty-two weeks. That is a
specific, named confound in the paper's single most important new instrument, not a matter of taste.
The reviewer filed it LOW because [44] supplies the corrective in principle; but [44] does not schedule
the word/deed pairing, and the fix is one column on a sheet that already exists.

### S-F6 — Position of the Schön footnote in the note sequence. Filed LOW.
**Verdict: NOT A FINDING (unverifiable). Severity: LOW.**
The reviewer states himself that he cannot tell whether this reflects the manuscript or the extraction.
Neither can I: the supplied file carries footnotes stripped to the end. There is a real anomaly (fn 14
glosses [20] but sits between notes anchored at [45] and [44]), so it is worth a production query — but
it cannot be filed as a finding against the paper.

## C. EDMONDSON

### E-F1 — "the safety research validates" six markers; it validates two and a half. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH.**

I verified the seven 1999 items verbatim from the article's own Appendix (ASQ 44(2), p. 382) and checked
her allocation marker by marker. It holds:

| [50] marker | 1999 warrant | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| mistake admitted unprompted | item 1 "If you make a mistake on this team, it is often held against you" (reverse); error discussion in the learning-behavior DV | grounded |
| help asked for | item 5 "It is difficult to ask other members of this team for help" (reverse) **and** learning-behavior item 7 | grounded; her claim that it appears in both is exactly right |
| hard thing raised without invitation | item 2 "Members of this team are able to bring up problems and tough issues" — about *ability*, not spontaneity | the half |
| disagreement processed without the leader | absent from both scales | not hers |
| reciprocal vulnerability across a generation line | absent from both scales | not hers |
| leader's presence and guidance reduced | "Team leader coaching" is a separate **antecedent** scale (initiates meetings / available for consultation / ongoing presence) | not hers, and arguably inverse |

**On the press point — does the paper claim all six?** Yes, on the plain reading. [50]: "watch for six
small events that **the safety research validates** and any Companion can see: …" The relative clause
modifies "six small events"; there is no restrictive reading that spares markers 4–6. And the paper
**contradicts itself**: [16] credits reciprocal vulnerability to the disclosure research; [19] owns the
leader-absence marker as "the group-level measurement the companion article proposed under the name of
the leadership absence test." The reviewer's own line — "[50] is where the paper's honest attributions
go to be homogenized" — is the accurate summary. The verb "validates" plus the internal contradiction
makes this the cleanest fidelity defect in my whole set. Fix is four half-lines of prose.

### E-F2 — Psychological safety is a *team-level shared belief*; the design cannot see when it is not shared. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH.**

Verified: the construct definition (ASQ p. 354) is "a shared belief that the team is safe for
interpersonal risk taking"; the abstract adds "held by members of a team." The paper quotes the
definition at [17] and then operationalizes only individual events at [50]; nothing in the five
family-facing pages [34] ever asks anyone whether the room feels safe. The status asymmetry is inside
the unit of analysis by design — parents and their own teenagers in one circle, with [16]'s standing
rule that "in every practice the parents start." Her failure mode (all six markers lit by articulate
adults while the teens hold a different belief) is fully consistent with every instrument in the pilot.
Remedy caveat in Section E, item 3 — the target is right, the delivery needs the paper's own permission
architecture wrapped around it.

### E-F3 — Her scale is a self-report perception measure; it did not validate observable markers. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**

Verified from the Appendix note: all seven psychological-safety items sit on a "7-point scale from 'very
inaccurate' to 'very accurate'" **team survey**; the learning-behavior DV sits on the **Observer
Survey**. So her account of the design is exactly right, and [17]'s "has validated its markers"
misdescribes what was validated. The irony she names — a paper whose thesis is the self-report ceiling
[6] borrowing a self-report-validated construct — is a genuine reviewer point.

One mitigation she does not offer, which narrows the finding to a wording fix: because observer-rated
learning behavior (asking for help, discussing error) was the validated *outcome* of safety, observing
those behaviors is legitimate probabilistic evidence about safety. The inference is defensible; the word
"validated" is what is wrong.

### E-F4 — Safety as precondition vs continuously re-earned. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW.**
She concedes in the finding itself that "the author's own practice is better than his framing," and
quotes the paper doing what she prescribes: "The container is rebuilt every single week" [17], the
re-forming at [27], and "the container holding **without enforcement**" [49]. Under the brief's rule —
if the paper already says what the reviewer demands, downgrade. What remains is that "The container is
the first curriculum" could mislead a practitioner reader into scoring week one as a safety success.

### E-F5 — Safety and trust run as one construct. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
The paper does move between the vocabularies without distinguishing them: [13] "It is seen in trust";
[17] the container's construct is psychological safety; [41] maps the four container conditions onto
calculus/knowledge/identification trust and glosses rung one as "It's safe and well-run" — safety
language sitting on the bottom rung of a trust ladder. Her diagnosis that the seam runs straight through
held → known → owned is precise and matters for the migration instrument. Independently convergent with
Lewicki F2 from a different premise (his: the three stages collapse onto felt safety; hers: safety is a
different construct from trust).

### E-F6 — Leader's declining necessity presented as a safety marker. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Verified: "Team leader coaching" is a separate antecedent scale in the 1999 model, so reduced leader
presence is a reduction in an antecedent of safety, not an indicator of it. Her "arguably in tension
with my findings" is well founded, not rhetorical. And the practical addition is the most useful single
sentence in her review: a quiet teen-led night is a null result that will read as a positive one, and
[57] catches only the loud failure mode. That is a genuine gap in the disconfirmation set.

### E-F7 — The "different" item transports least well; voluntary attendance reads falsely positive. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
The attrition half is the substance and is stronger than she states. Checked: [59] lists "families
self-selected" as a limitation, but nothing in [53]–[59] treats attrition or drifting attendance as a
signal, and [56] actively *filters it out* with "in a cohort that attends faithfully." So the design
discards its single strongest negative signal by rule. This interlocks with Nosek F5(c). The
"difference in a room with a normative telos" half is more speculative; the attrition half carries it.

### E-F8 — Construct imported without its dependent variable. Filed LOW.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: LOW.** Correct; the paper never states what safety is supposed to predict
here, and naming it supplies a further disconfirmation condition at no cost.

### E-F9 — At [42] the mechanism clause is hers and is credited to Tuckman/Wheelan. Filed LOW.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: LOW alone, MEDIUM jointly.**
This is the same defect the citation sweep reaches as MEDIUM-3, from the opposite direction: the sweep
says the causal clause is in neither Tuckman nor Wheelan; Edmondson says it is a safety claim wearing
group-development clothes. Both are right and together they establish that the sentence's warrant is
simply wrong. Merge and rate MEDIUM.

## D. CITATION SWEEP

### CS-1 — Strathern / Goodhart provenance inverted at fn 13. Filed HIGH.
**Verdict: SURVIVES, with one correction to the finding itself. Severity: MEDIUM (downgraded).**

**Independently verified — I did not take the sweep's word for it.** I retrieved the article and
extracted the text. On p. 308 (running head "308 Marilyn Strathern"), the paragraph reads verbatim:

> "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. The more a 2.1 examination
> performance becomes an expectation, the poorer it becomes as a discriminator of individual
> performances. Hoskin describes this as 'Goodhart's law', after the latter's observation on
> instruments for monetary control which lead to other devices for monetary flexibility having to be
> invented."

The sweep's transcription is accurate to the word and its page attribution is correct. Three things
follow, and the sweep has two of them right:

1. The sentence appears **in the article, in Strathern's own unquoted prose**. Elsewhere she marks
   Hoskin's words with quotation marks and "(Ref. 3, p. …)"; here she does neither. So fn 13's
   "popularly summarized as Goodhart's law" does invert the direction — this is not a popular summary
   *of* her paper. **Correct.**
2. **Goodhart's 1975 wording is different**, as claimed: "Any observed statistical regularity will tend
   to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes" (Reserve Bank of Australia
   conference paper, "Problems of Monetary Management: The UK Experience"). Goodhart never wrote the
   measure/target sentence. **Correct.**
3. **The sweep's own fix would install a second inaccuracy.** It says flatly "the famous sentence is
   Strathern's own" and recommends citing her alone for the wording. That is what the article shows on
   its face but it is not the settled attribution: Strathern's Ref. 3 — Keith Hoskin, "The 'awful idea
   of accountability'" (1996) — contains "that every measure which becomes a target becomes a bad
   measure … is inexorably, if ruefully, becoming recognized as one of the overriding laws of our
   times," and current reference sources credit Hoskin with the formulation and Strathern with the
   restatement that carried it into general use. The honest note is three-layered: **Goodhart 1975**
   (different claim, different words) → **Hoskin 1996** (the measure/target formulation and the
   "Goodhart's law" label) → **Strathern 1997, at 308** (the exact sentence now quoted worldwide).

**Severity downgraded to MEDIUM.** The sweep concedes the body text at [45] is clean and names nobody
("the measurement literature has its own name for the corruption of measures that become targets"), so
no argument in the paper rests on the error. It is one gloss inside an omnibus footnote. The real
argument for keeping it — "an economically literate reviewer notices, and it is trivially checkable" —
argues MEDIUM, not HIGH.

### CS-2 — "Fifty years," uncited Shapiro/Sheppard/Cheraskin, LTG-2006 glossed as endorsement. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Verified: Shapiro, Sheppard & Cheraskin, "Business on a Handshake," *Negotiation Journal* 8(4) (1992):
365–377 is the origin of the deterrence/knowledge/identification typology; Lewicki & Bunker (1996)
adapted it and renamed deterrence-based as **calculus-based**, which is the paper's exact vocabulary at
[41]. So "fifty years of that literature describes trust developing through three levels" is off by
roughly twenty years, given that "that literature" is made to index the three-level model by the
sentence's own structure. Independently convergent with Lewicki F1. The LTG-2006 half is the more
useful: its actual burden is that trust development over time remains under-conceptualized and
under-measured — which hands the author a caveat that *strengthens* his own [58] research-trail framing.

### CS-3 — Tuckman/Wheelan: sequence supported, mechanism is the author's. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Verified: Wheelan's IMGD runs Dependency and Inclusion → **Counterdependency and Fight** → **Trust and
Structure** → Work and Productivity, so [42]'s sequence claim is well supported and the paper picked the
right source. The causal clause "because people risk conflict only in a room they believe will hold it"
is in neither source; both explain Stage-2 conflict as counterdependency — resistance to leader and
structure — so the paper's arrow points the other way. And Tuckman 1965 is a narrative review its author
framed as a hypothesis needing test. All three sub-claims check out. Merge with Edmondson F9.

### CS-4 — Altman revised social penetration in 1981; the disconfirmation conditions contradict his later position. Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: WEAKENED — bibliographic half verified, application half REFUTED. Severity: LOW.**

**Verified, as instructed.** Altman, Vinsel & Brown, "Dialectic Conceptions in Social Psychology: An
Application to Social Penetration and Privacy Regulation," in Berkowitz, ed., *Advances in Experimental
Social Psychology* 14 (1981): 107–160 exists, is by Altman himself, and does revise social penetration
away from a unidirectional cumulative model toward a dialectical account in which openness and closedness
cycle and boundaries are continuously renegotiated. VanLear's two longitudinal studies (*Communication
Monographs* 58(4), 1991) supply empirical support for cycling. So the 1973 model does have a second act
the paper does not mention.

**The application does not hold.** The sweep says "the pilot predicts monotonic movement and treats its
absence as disconfirming," citing [54], [58], [37]. Checked one by one:

- **[54]** — the failure condition is "still showing *not yet* on first forms in February, **or** no
  growing form all year." A cohort that opens, closes for a stretch, and re-opens shows growing forms
  and passes. It is a floor, not a trajectory requirement. (Nosek F5(a) criticizes [54] for exactly the
  opposite defect — that it is *only* a floor — and on the text Nosek is right. The two findings cannot
  both stand; this one falls.)
- **[58]** — conjunctive across three time points: "Stagnant vocabulary **alongside advancing
  sign-readings**." A dialectical retreat does not satisfy it.
- **[37]** — a promotion rule with **no demotion clause**. Retreat is not scored as failure at all;
  Vazire F5(d) independently notes the ratchet. The sweep cites [37] as evidence of the opposite.

Only [56] compares spring against fall, and that is an endpoint comparison a cycling cohort can pass.
Further: the load-bearing use of Altman & Taylor at [16] is **reciprocity** — "people deepen by matched
steps, each opening the next level for the other" — which the 1981 revision does not overturn.

**Residue worth one line:** naming the dialectical revision and adding the 1981 chapter to the
bibliography is free insurance against a reviewer who knows the second act. LOW opportunity note. The
sweep's *own* LOW on this source (dyadic evidence base; honestly-declared field extension at [16]) is
better argued than its MEDIUM.

### CS-5 — Move / NCD as "the instrument landscape." Filed MEDIUM.
**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW.**
The characterizations verify (REVEAL/*Move* = congregational segmentation self-report, four segments,

## E. Convergence test (press point 5)

Four reviewers — Vazire, Lewicki, Nosek, Schön — reach "no independent check on the designer's reading."
Tested as instructed.

**They do reason from different premises.** Vazire argues from informant-report psychometrics
(independence of judges, anonymity, effective sample size). Lewicki argues from coding reliability for a
three-observation latent construct. Nosek argues from researcher degrees of freedom and the
confirmatory/exploratory boundary. Schön argues from organizational learning — that a practitioner's
theory-in-use is unavailable to him by introspection *regardless* of procedure. Those are four genuinely
different warrants, and Schön's is the only one that would still bite if every procedural fix were made.

**But the convergence is not four confirmations.** It is one undisputed structural fact —
the designer and his hand-formed team are the sole readers of the pilot's highest-stakes data, which
the paper states plainly at [36]–[38], [41], and [59] — observed from four disciplines, each naming a
different consequence. It should be weighted as **one finding with four consequences and one shared
remedy**, not as four votes. The fact that all four remedies collapse onto the same cheap action (one
person outside the team reads text that already exists) is stronger evidence than the head-count is.

**The shared framing artifact, named.** All four assume the pilot's data are meant to license an
inference for a reader. The paper disclaims exactly that at [59]: "The honest claim available at the end
of this pilot will not be 'this works' but 'this ran, here is everything it recorded against the
predictions it published.'" Under *that* claim standard, blind coding is a quality improvement, not a
validity requirement. Every one of the four reviewers quotes [59] approvingly and then reasons as though
it were not there. That is a real artifact, and it bears on **severity**, not on existence. Note also
that all four were reading the same text under the same "find where the paper exceeds its warrant"
brief, so a vulnerability the paper itself advertises at [59] will be found by all of them — convergence
here measures salience, not independent corroboration.

## H. Findings whose remedy would damage the paper

1. **Nosek F6 and F5(c) — designating numeric primary outcomes and thresholds.** The paper's governing
   ethic is "no target is ever announced, because the moment a mirror becomes a goal it stops being a
   mirror" [45] — warranted, correctly, by Strathern. A team that has committed in writing to
   "home-practice survival must rise by X" will read the room through that number, which is what the
   paper's own cited source predicts. **Safe version:** register the counts and their operational
   definitions, register a *direction* rather than a threshold, and register the all-outcomes reporting
   commitment. Naming which trace is primary is defensible; putting a number on it is the paper arguing
   against itself. The one place a number is unambiguously worth it is "attends faithfully" — because
   there the *absence* of a number is the escape hatch.
2. **Nosek F2's full nine-item OSF deposit, framed as what earns the word.** Depositing the frozen
   elements, the start date, and the reporting commitment is cheap and strengthens the paper on its own
   merits. But items 3, 6 and 7 (a designated primary with operational definitions, an analysis plan, a
   numeric attendance rule) import hypothesis-testing furniture into a twenty-two-week pastoral protocol
   and collide with item 1 above. And the framing is the wrong diagnosis: one clause disowning the
   technical sense fixes the actual defect. Do the deposit for its own sake, not to rescue a claim the
   paper did not make.
3. **Edmondson F2's four safety items on the pulse page.** The most valuable finding in my set, and the
   remedy is nearly right but not free. [21] describes the author adopting the permission ethic "after
   watching my own instruments through a parent's eyes and asking… do I want this room to feel
   measured?" Four items asking a teenager to rate whether the room is safe — on a page a parent may see
   them fill out, in a design where "a family is never divided" [16] — is precisely the instrument that
   ethic was built to catch, and it inherits the demand-characteristic problem Vazire names for the
   group self-read. **Version that preserves both:** unsigned, generation-stamped only (teen / parent),
   returned to the same unchased basket at the door [27], so the *gap* is readable and no individual is.
4. **Schön F4(c)** — see G. His F4(b) (five private minutes with the teen leader after each second
   running) is the cheap, well-aimed half and should be kept.
5. **Citation sweep CS-7's second option** — "pick a different anchor text" for Romans 10:17. Replacing
   the anchor would cost the paper the connection its own sixth "tell" step already makes. The sweep's
   *first* option — make the proclamation connection explicit, since the practice ends with someone
   speaking what was heard to another person — is strictly better and costs one sentence.
6. **Schön F3's outsider, one caution only.** The remedy is right and cheap, but it must be scoped to
   *text the outsider reads* (debrief sheets, scribed self-reads) rather than an outsider placed in the
   room. Putting a stranger with no stake in the room's welfare inside a circle built on [17]'s
   container and [44]'s weep test would damage the thing being measured. Schön's own formulation
   respects this; the synthesis should not loosen it.
