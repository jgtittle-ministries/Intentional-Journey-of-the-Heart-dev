# Source-Fidelity Review — Simine Vazire

**Paper:** "Twenty-Two Wednesdays as a Fellowship of the Heart," JSFSC protocol paper, draft v2.3
**Reviewing for:** fidelity to Vazire (2010), *JPSP* 98(2), 281–300 — the SOKA model — cited in footnote 13
**Date:** 2026-08-19

A framing note before I start, because it affects how everything below should be read. I am cited once, in a
footnote, for "the self-knowledge asymmetries underlying the self/informant design." That is a small
citation carrying a large building. The entire architecture of this paper rests on the proposition that
self-report runs out and observation takes over, and my model is the empirical warrant offered for it. So
I am going to spend most of this review on whether my model actually says that.

Short version: it does not, and in one important respect it says close to the opposite. But the author's
*architecture* is frequently better than his *framing*, and several of my findings amount to "you already
built the right thing, now stop describing it wrong."

---

## 1. What the paper gets right about my work

I want to be specific here, because there is real evidence of careful reading, and it would be unfair to
bury it.

**The discrepancy-as-data principle is exactly right, and it is the most SOKA-consistent sentence in the
paper.** [44]: "Every tier above exists so that sources can disagree" — and then, better, "these gaps are
the findings." That is the correct inference from an asymmetry model. If you take seriously that self and
other have different information and different motives, the *disagreement* is not measurement failure to be
averaged away; it is the signal. Most people who cite me get this backwards and treat informant reports as a
validity check on self-report, with the informant as referee. This paper does not. Credit where it is due.

**Self-report is retained rather than replaced, and retained in roughly the right places.** [6] says the
problem "is resolved only when self-report is joined by an observing community" — joined, not superseded.
And [34] operationalizes it: "Self-report is kept where self-report is valid: practice frequency, hopes,
worries, felt experience." Hopes, worries, and felt experience are low-observability internal states. Those
are the self's home turf under my model, and the author put them there. Whether he did that from my paper or
from pastoral instinct I cannot tell, but it is right either way.

**The reactivity awareness in [34] is genuinely sophisticated.** "A room that does not feel surveilled
behaves like itself, and the behavioral traces that matter (below) stay uncontaminated by performance for the
instrument." That is a measurement argument, not a pastoral one, and it is correct. I will complain in
Finding 4 that the paper then violates it somewhere else, but the principle is stated properly.

**"No occasion" as a distinct code [37]** — "an event-contingent sign with no event is missing data, not
failure" — is a real psychometric move, and a better one than most published observational rubrics make.
Conflating structural zeros with floor readings is one of the most common ways coding schemes lie to their
users. Somebody thought about this.

**[59] is the best paragraph in the paper**, and I say that as someone who spends a lot of time being rude
about how people describe their limitations. "One room, one year, one school, one founder." "The honest claim
available at the end of this pilot will not be 'this works' but 'this ran, here is everything it recorded
against the predictions it published, and here is the revised design.'" That is a more honest closing
statement than I read in most empirical psychology papers, and I would like to see it quoted at people. My
complaint in Finding 9 is only that it is quarantined at the end while other paragraphs write checks it
cannot cover.

**The bibliographic citation of my paper is accurate.** Title, journal, volume 98, issue 2, pages 281–300,
2010. Correct. This is a lower bar than it should be and the author cleared it.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — The "structural ceiling" is not SOKA, and as stated it is self-defeating. **HIGH**

[6]: "existing instruments for measuring spiritual maturity cluster at the lower levels of Krathwohl's
affective taxonomy because **self-report meets a structural ceiling: above the middle levels, social
compliance and transformed character produce the same observable behavior, and the heart is opaque even to
its owner (Jer 17:9)**."

Two problems, and the second is the serious one.

First, SOKA is not a ceiling model. It is a *relative* accuracy model. My argument is that self- and
other-judgments of the same person have different validity profiles depending on properties of the trait
being judged, and that neither source is globally superior. There is no level above which self-report stops
carrying information in my model. There are traits on which the self is the better judge, traits on which
others are, and traits on which both do poorly. "Ceiling" imports a hard boundary that I did not establish
and that my data do not show. A ceiling claim would require demonstrating that self-report validity
coefficients go to approximately zero above some point on a maturity continuum. Nobody has shown that,
including me, and the paper's own companion article — which I have not seen — would need to show it, not
assert it.

Second, and worse: read the ceiling premise and the proposed solution next to each other. The premise is
that "social compliance and transformed character produce **the same observable behavior**." The solution is
an observing community that watches behavior. If the two states are observationally identical, then
observers, whose entire access is observational, are in a *worse* epistemic position than the self, not a
better one. The self at least has access to the internal state that distinguishes them. The argument as
written proves that nobody can measure this, and then recommends observers.

I do not think the author believes the strong version. The architecture suggests he believes something more
defensible: that self-report on morally loaded internal traits is distorted by self-presentation and
self-enhancement, and that adding sources with different distortions is an improvement. That is a claim I
would sign. But that is a *motivational* argument, not an *observability* argument, and the paper is
currently making the observability argument, which cuts its own legs off.

**Fix.** Two sentences. (a) Drop "structural ceiling" or explicitly label it as the companion article's
construct and not a finding from the self-knowledge literature. (b) Replace "produce the same observable
behavior" with something like "produce behavior that is difficult to distinguish from the outside, and that
the actor is motivated to interpret charitably from the inside" — which sets up multi-source measurement
instead of demolishing it.

### Finding 2 — The design runs against the direction of my model on precisely the traits it cares about. **HIGH**

This is the finding I was seated for, so I will be careful and complete.

SOKA's predictions are organized by two trait properties, observability and evaluativeness:

- **Low observability, low evaluativeness** (internal, not ego-threatening — anxiety, private distress):
  *the self is the more accurate judge.*
- **High observability, low evaluativeness** (extraversion, talkativeness): self and others are comparable;
  both do reasonably well.
- **High observability, high evaluativeness** (intellect, charm, dominance): *others are the more accurate
  judges*, because the self's motivational distortion outweighs its information advantage.
- **Low observability, high evaluativeness**: **neither source is a good judge.** The self has the access but
  the strongest motive to distort; the other has the motive but not the access. This is the blind-spot
  quadrant.

Now place this paper's constructs. Its targets are desire, love for God, motivation to obey, internalization
of value — stated throughout, e.g. [12] "form desire through practice," [36] "confidence in God under the
load of ordinary weeks." These are low-observability traits. They are also, in a Christian school community
where families have voluntarily joined a formation cohort, about as high in evaluativeness as a human trait
can get. There is no more socially loaded question in that room than whether you love God.

So the paper's core constructs sit squarely in the fourth quadrant: **the one where my model predicts that
neither self-report nor observer report is accurate.** The paper's architecture assumes that quadrant hands
the advantage to observers. It does not. It hands the advantage to nobody.

And note the sub-point the brief asked me to check directly: to the extent the paper's traits are *purely*
internal and the evaluative loading were somehow low — the quiet interior movement of desire — SOKA predicts
the **self** is the better judge, not the room. The design would then be routing its most important
constructs away from its best available source. Either way the direction of the model does not support the
architecture as framed.

I want to be clear that this does not sink the project. What the fourth quadrant actually calls for is not a
better judge but a *criterion* — objective, behavioral, non-judgmental data. And this pilot **has one**, and
does not seem to know how good it is. See Finding 3's constructive half and Section 5.

**Fix.** State the quadrant honestly, in one paragraph in the measurement architecture section: "The traits
of interest here are low in observability and high in social desirability, which is the condition under
which the self-knowledge literature predicts *both* self- and observer judgment to be weak. This is why the
design does not rest on judgment at all where it can rest on behavioral trace." Then let the break-survival
counts and the tell-step records carry the argumentative weight. That is a stronger paper and a more honest
one.

### Finding 3 — Group-aloud reporting is collective self-report wearing an informant's coat. **HIGH**

[41]: "**Methodologically, this is informant data gathered aloud, and it evades the self-report ceiling** for
a reason the companion article's own argument supplies: a heart is opaque to its owner, but a room is not
opaque to its members."

Calling it informant data does not make it informant data. The informant-report literature earns its validity
from three properties, all of which this procedure removes:

1. **Independence.** The accuracy gain from informant reports comes overwhelmingly from *aggregating across
   judges whose errors are uncorrelated*. That is the whole mechanism. A group answering aloud, in sequence,
   in one room, does not produce independent observations; it produces one observation with several
   signatures on it. Whatever your effective sample size is, it is not the number of people who spoke.
2. **Privacy from the target.** Informants report about a target who is not standing there. Here the room
   reports about the room. Every informant is also a target, and every target hears every report.
3. **Freedom from the target's evaluative stake.** The self-report problem the paper is trying to escape is
   that people manage impressions on socially loaded traits. Non-anonymous public speech in front of the
   people being described, with the group's leader present and listening, is the *maximum* impression-
   management condition, not the minimum. Anonymity was the one lever available and it was given away.

Add the reactivity: [28] tells us "The co-companion scribes the answers verbatim." Participants can see this.
So the family-facing pages are protected from surveillance effects by design [34], and then the group
self-read — the thing [41] calls "quietly the best instrument in the pilot" — is conducted under open
observation and transcription. The protection is applied to the low-stakes instrument and withheld from the
high-stakes one.

To be fair, and the brief tells me to check before I charge: **[41] contains one defensible claim.** "Group
behavior is public behavior; every participant is an eyewitness to whether the circle held the four
conditions (safe, present, clear and intentional) and who was noticed." For *verifiable event-level facts* —
did the container get spoken, did anyone go after the person who went quiet — a public group report is a
reasonable instrument, and social pressure to lie about a fact everyone witnessed is comparatively weak. The
overreach is using the same procedure to index trust *stage* and internalization *level*, which are latent,
evaluative, and exactly the thing a room has a motive to talk itself up about.

**Fix.** Three options, in ascending order of cost, any of which would help:
(a) Rename it. "Collective self-report" or "group self-assessment" is honest, and the paper loses nothing but
an unearned epistemic upgrade.
(b) Keep the aloud version as formation and add a genuinely independent parallel: the same three questions on
an unsigned index card, collected in the same basket as the pulse pages, at the same three occasions.
Cheap, permission-consistent, and it gives you a *comparison* between the public and private answer — which,
under this paper's own discrepancy-as-data doctrine [44], is itself a finding.
(c) If (b) is refused for pastoral reasons, say so explicitly and label the resulting limitation.

### Finding 4 — "Leader first" cannot be a formative lever and a neutral measurement condition at the same time. **MEDIUM**

[40]: "At the re-entry two weeks later, the room answers them together, **leader first as always**, across the
break on purpose so the members who think best in quiet arrive ready."

The paper argues elsewhere, correctly and with citation to disclosure research, that going first *works*:
[16] "A parent who goes first with something true licenses a depth the teen would never volunteer cold." The
whole design is built on the premise that a modeled first answer changes what follows.

You cannot then use the same move at the head of your measurement occasion and treat the resulting answers as
uncontaminated. If the leader's answer licenses depth, it also anchors content, sets the acceptable range,
and demonstrates the register. And for the vocabulary-migration prediction specifically [58], this is fatal
in a quiet way: the coding scheme reads the *words the room reaches for*, and the leader has just supplied a
vocabulary in front of them. If the leader's own language drifts from "held" toward "owned" across the year —
and why wouldn't it, he designed the ladder — the room's language will follow, and the paper will read
leader drift as group internalization.

The paper does not name this tension anywhere. That is the finding: not that the design is wrong, but that a
paper this scrupulous about its other conflicts has not noticed this one.

**Fix.** Name it, and add one cheap control: scribe the leader's answer verbatim too, in the same transcript,
and code it on the same scale. If leader and room migrate together, you know what you have. This costs
nothing and converts a confound into a measured variable.

### Finding 5 — The instrument that carries the whole argument has no reliability check and no blinding. **MEDIUM**

[37]: the team "marks each sign seen, not yet, or no occasion" and "**promotes a rung after three consecutive
weeks of seen**." [36] calls this "what I believe is its most useful contribution to practice."

Everything above the self-report tier depends on these judgments. And every judgment is made by the team that
designed the rubric, wants the rungs to climb, will be encouraged if they climb, and knows what week it is.
There is no second coder, no inter-rater agreement statistic, no blind coding, and no check on drift.

Rater leniency drift over a year, in raters who are emotionally invested in the outcome and who see the
prediction curve in advance ([54] tells them what the expected progression looks like), is not an exotic
risk. It is the default. And the promotion rule is ratcheted — three consecutive "seen" promotes, and nothing
in the paper demotes — so drift accumulates in one direction only.

The remarkable thing is that the fix is already sitting in the design. **The scribe takes verbatim notes**
[28]. That means the highest-stakes data in the pilot exist as text. Text can be coded blind.

**Fix.** (a) Publish the rubric anchors now, before the year closes — the paper says the instruments are
openly published [24], so this may already be done, in which case say so in-text. (b) Have one person outside
the companion team code the three self-read transcripts with timepoint labels stripped and order shuffled.
(c) Have a second companion independently code a sample of debrief sheets and report simple agreement. This
is perhaps four hours of work across a year and it is the difference between "our team felt the room grew"
and evidence. (d) Add a demotion rule, or state plainly that the rung reading is monotonic by construction
and therefore cannot itself falsify anything.

### Finding 6 — "Preregistration" is claimed for something that has none of preregistration's guarantees. **MEDIUM**

[8]: "**The academic literature calls publishing a design and its failure conditions before the results
'preregistration,'** and commends it because it keeps a researcher honest." Cited to Nosek et al. 2018.

I am stepping half a step outside my SOKA remit here, but this is squarely my other job, so I will say it.

Preregistration's value does not come from publishing a design. It comes from a **timestamped, third-party-
hosted, immutable** record that fixes the analytic decisions *before the data exist*, so that undisclosed
flexibility becomes detectable. None of those properties is present here. There is no registry, no timestamp,
no committed analysis plan, and no external record that would let a reader distinguish a prediction made in
August from one added in June. Worse, [8] itself says the pilot is "now underway," and [5] narrates sessions
that have already happened, so some data are already in hand at the time of writing.

What this paper actually is: a *protocol paper with publicly stated predictions*, which is a genuinely good
thing, is rarer than it should be in practical theology, and deserves credit on its own terms. It is not
preregistration and should not borrow the word.

**Fix.** Say: "The pilot began on [date]; the predictions in the following section were fixed on [date] and
are stated before the year's data have been analyzed. This is not a preregistration in the technical
sense — there is no registry entry and no committed analysis plan — but it serves the same purpose it is
commended for." Then, if the author wants the real thing and not the rhetoric, post the document to OSF with
a timestamp. It takes twenty minutes and is free, and it would turn a MEDIUM finding into a strength.

### Finding 7 — Falsifiability audit: two of five conditions are genuinely falsifiable, three are not as written. **MEDIUM**

The brief asked me to assess these one at a time, so:

**[54] "If the ladder never climbs" — WEAK.** "A cohort still showing not yet on first forms in February, or
no growing form all year, **falsifies either the phasing or the curriculum**." Disjunctive escape: whichever
result arrives, one branch survives, and the paper does not pre-commit to which. Combined with Finding 5
(unblinded raters, monotonic promotion rule), the outcome variable is produced by people who know the
prediction. Fixable by pre-specifying which branch a given pattern kills.

**[55] "If the sources never disagree" — WEAK, and this is the one I would call unfalsifiable in practice.**
"Then **either this room's self-reports are unusually valid, or the observational instruments are too dull**
to detect anything self-missed." Both branches leave the framework intact. It does concede the ceiling
argument "loses its practical force," which is more than most papers offer, so this is not empty. But
"essentially no discrepancies" is undefined, there is no pre-specified discrepancy metric, and no n is
given, so there is no denominator. Any observed result can be narrated afterward. Fixable, concretely:
"We will tabulate, for each consenting family member, whether the post-series self-reported practice
frequency is contradicted by the tell-step and break-survival records. We predict contradictions in at least
[X] of [N] cases; fewer than [Y] disconfirms."

**[56] "If nothing survives the breaks" — STRONG. This is the good one.** Behavioral signature, three
scheduled occasions, same three counts each time, direction specified in advance: "**Flat or declining
break-survival across the year, in a cohort that attends faithfully, disconfirms the claim that the
curriculum is moving anyone from compliance to valuing.**" This is falsifiable, it is objective, and it is
the least judgment-dependent measure in the pilot. It needs a denominator and a stated count rule and then it
is real evidence. See Section 5; I think this is the paper's actual best instrument.

**[57] "If the container collapses in younger hands" — WEAK as written.** "If teen-led nights **consistently
require adult rescue** into the spring." Neither "consistently" nor "rescue" is defined, and the judgment is
made by the adults who would be doing the rescuing and who chose the entrust-early design. Fixable in one
sentence: define rescue as an adult verbal intervention that redirects the session, count them, and
pre-specify the threshold.

**[58] "If the trust vocabulary does not migrate" — MODERATE, and rescuable.** The design is right — verbatim
text, three timepoints, direction pre-specified — and the falsification condition is admirably sharp:
"Stagnant vocabulary alongside advancing sign-readings would break the proposed mapping between trust stage
and internalization level." The execution is the problem: three unanchored bins (held/known/owned), coded by
the people who invented them, unblinded, on transcripts contaminated by the leader-first anchor (Finding 4).
Fix with blind coding and published anchors and this becomes the paper's second-strongest test.

**[59]** is not a falsification condition but a limitations statement, and as noted it is excellent.

### Finding 8 — The paper never states n. **MEDIUM**

I searched. There is "a small Christian school" [8], "a crew of teenagers" [5], "one faithful family" in
prototyping [16], "families." At no point does the paper say how many families, how many adults, how many
teens, how many consented to the five pages, or what counts as attendance.

This is not pedantry. Several of the paper's own disconfirmation conditions require a denominator to be
evaluable — [55] "essentially no discrepancies," [56] "flat or declining break-survival," [50] "how many
walked back in with the return card." A count with no denominator is a number, not a measurement. And a
reader cannot calibrate the hedging in [59] without knowing whether "small" means six families or twenty-six.

**Fix.** One sentence in the shape-of-the-year section: N families, N adults, N teens, N consenting to
instruments, and the attendance definition. Also report the consent rate — which, given [21]'s permission
ethic, is itself an interesting datum and arguably a finding about the ethic.

### Finding 9 — The hedging is real but quarantined; the abstract and [51] make unhedged claims. **MEDIUM**

The brief asked whether the hedging is adequate to the strength of the claims made. My answer: [59] is
adequate to almost any claim. The problem is that it sits at the end, and three other places speak with a
confidence [59] would not permit.

- **Abstract [2]:** the design is "built so that its ordinary practices **generate the multi-source,
  level-matched measurement the theory requires**." Present tense, no conditional, no hedge. The abstract is
  what most readers will read and what indexers will propagate. As written it asserts that the requirement
  has been met.
- **[36]:** "Here the pilot adds **what I believe is its most useful contribution to practice**: the
  observation instrument is level-matched longitudinally." The "what I believe" is doing honest work, but
  this is an unvalidated rubric with no reliability data being described as a contribution to practice
  before its first year has closed.
- **[51]:** "That self-recognition is not a side effect. **It is the level-three transition**, intrinsic
  valuing displacing external structure, happening in public." This is not a hedge failure, it is an
  identity claim: an observed behavior *is* a latent construct transition. That is the strongest
  measurement claim in the paper and it appears in the practitioner-markers section with no qualification at
  all. It also commits an ecological inference — the room's collective vocabulary is being read as evidence
  about *individuals'* internalization, when [12] explicitly established that "measure the group and the
  person on separate ladders" is a design commitment. The paper crosses its own ladder here.

**Fix.** (a) Move "one room, one year, one school, one founder" into the abstract, in some form. It costs
twelve words and buys the whole paper's credibility. (b) [51]: "It is" → "we are treating it as a candidate
indicator of." (c) [2]: "generate" → "is designed to generate."

## 3. Where the author goes beyond me, and whether he says so

An extension audit, charitably scored.

| # | The reach | Does the paper say so? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | From "self- and other-knowledge are differentially accurate by trait" to "**self-report meets a structural ceiling above a taxonomic level**" | **No.** [6] presents the ceiling as inherited from the companion article and footnotes me for the underlying asymmetry. A reader will take the ceiling as empirically warranted. It is not, and I did not establish it. This is the undeclared extension that matters. |
| 2 | From "traits vary in observability and evaluativeness" to "**observers are the appropriate source for the highest-level, most internal constructs**" | **No**, and it runs against my model's direction. See Finding 2. |
| 3 | From individual-level self/other asymmetry to **group-level self-assessment as an informant instrument** | **No.** [41] asserts "this is informant data" as a methodological fact rather than as an extension. My model is about judgments of individual persons by individual judges. Nothing in it licenses treating a collective's statement about itself as informant data. This is a genuinely novel move and might be interesting — but it is the author's move, not mine, and should be labeled as such and defended on its own. |
| 4 | From informant *accuracy* to informant *validity under public, non-anonymous, leader-present conditions* | **No.** The conditions under which informant reports earn their validity are never stated, so their removal is never noticed. |
| 5 | From a psychometric asymmetry model to a **theological anthropology of the opaque heart** (Jer 17:9, [6]) | **Partly.** The Scripture is plainly marked as Scripture. But it is fused to my model in the same sentence, so the two claims share a warrant they do not share in fact. My model does not say the heart is opaque to itself; it says self-knowledge is uneven, and in several domains the self is the best judge available. |
| 6 | From "publishing a design" to "**preregistration**" | **No.** [8] asserts the identity directly. See Finding 6. |
| 7 | From "trust develops in three stages" (Lewicki & Bunker) to "**trust stage maps onto internalization level**" | **Yes, well.** [58] explicitly names this as "the proposed mapping" and "a formal research trail," and treats its failure as disconfirming. This is exactly how an extension should be handled, and I would point the author at his own [58] as the model for how to fix items 1–4 and 6 above. |
| 8 | From the group-development literature to **specific expectations about conflict timing and pseudocommunity** | **Yes.** [42] says plainly that these are "built into the team's expectations rather than discovered by surprise." Honest. |

So: one of eight extensions is declared well, one adequately, and six are undeclared. The author clearly knows
how to declare an extension — [58] and [42] prove it. He just did not apply that discipline to the borrowed

## 4. What I would want cited differently

**(a) Move me to where the argument is made.** SOKA belongs at [32]/[34], where the three-tier architecture
is introduced, not in a footnote to the Goodhart paragraph. And it needs one sentence of actual content, not
a gesture. Something like: *"The self-knowledge literature holds that self- and other-judgments have
different validity profiles depending on how observable and how evaluatively loaded the trait is: the self
tends to be the better judge of internal, low-stakes traits, others of visible, highly evaluative ones, and
neither is reliable for traits that are both internal and highly evaluative — which is the category most of
this project's constructs fall into. That is the reason this design does not rest on judgment where it can
rest on behavioral trace."* Adapt as he likes. But state the direction.

**(b) I am the wrong citation for "informants add validity."** If that is the claim being made — and it is
what the architecture actually needs — the correct primary warrants are:
- **Connelly & Ones (2010), *Psychological Bulletin* 136(6), 1092–1122**, the meta-analysis of observer
  ratings of personality. This is the strongest single source for "informant reports have incremental
  validity over self-reports," which is closer to what the paper wants than SOKA is.
- **Kolar, Funder & Colvin (1996), *Journal of Personality* 64(2), 311–337**, for acquaintances predicting
  behavior better than self-reports do.
- **Vazire & Mehl (2008), *JPSP* 95(5), 1202–1216**, if he wants self *and* other reports validated against
  objectively recorded daily behavior. This is arguably a better fit for a project built on behavioral
  traces than the 2010 SOKA paper is.

Cite me for the asymmetry. Cite Connelly & Ones for the upgrade.

**(c) The wrong literature is doing the ceiling's work.** If the claim is that people cannot accurately report
the *causes and motives* behind their behavior, the canonical citation is **Nisbett & Wilson (1977),
*Psychological Review* 84(3), 231–259**, not me. If the claim is that self-reports of socially desirable
traits are distorted by self-presentation and self-deception, that is the socially-desirable-responding
literature — **Paulhus**, and **John & Robins (1994)** on self-enhancement. Those two together get the author
much closer to what he means by "ceiling" than SOKA does, and they do it without the self-defeating
observability premise flagged in Finding 1.

**(d) The group-aloud procedure needs its own citations, and they will not be flattering.** If [41] is going
to keep its claim, it should engage the conformity and public-response literature — **Asch** at minimum — and
the basic psychometric point that aggregation gains require independence (Spearman–Brown assumes it; a
consensus procedure violates it). Right now the procedure's principal methodological hazard is uncited and
therefore unaddressed.

**(e) Two small ones.** Footnote 13 should be split; five unrelated sources in one note makes each of them
unfindable. And "Harper and Row" [fn 13's Bonhoeffer] vs. "Harper & Row" [69] are inconsistent between note
and bibliography — trivial, but a copyeditor will catch it and I would rather he catch it first.

## 5. My single biggest concern

The paper builds its epistemology on the claim that self-report hits a ceiling and that the observing
community sees above it, and it cites me for that. My model does not establish a ceiling, and on the specific
constructs this project cares about — desire, love for God, motivation, all of them internal and all of them
maximally socially loaded in a Christian school community — it predicts that *neither* the self nor the
observers are good judges. The paper has therefore recruited an asymmetry model to license a hierarchy, and
then compounded the problem by gathering its observer data aloud, non-anonymously, in front of the people
being described, with the leader answering first, and calling the result "informant data" [41]. That
procedure removes independence, privacy, and anonymity — the three things informant reports get their

But here is what I actually want the editor to hear, because I do not think this is a rejection. **The paper
already contains the thing that would fix it, and it has misidentified which of its instruments is the good
one.** [41] calls the group's third question "quietly the best instrument in the pilot." It is not; it is the
most contaminated. The best instrument in the pilot is in [56]: three scheduled structure-stoppages, the same
three counts taken at each, direction of change specified in advance, "flat or declining break-survival
across the year... disconfirms the claim that the curriculum is moving anyone from compliance to valuing."
That is behavioral, objective, judgment-independent, pre-specified, and genuinely falsifiable. It is exactly
the kind of criterion the self-knowledge literature says you need when neither self nor observer can be
trusted — and this practitioner built one, in a church basement, on no budget, apparently by instinct. If
this paper reorganized itself so that the behavioral traces carried the argument and the observer judgments
and group self-reads carried the *formation* — which is what they are actually for, and what [63] says the
author values more anyway — it would be both more honest and more persuasive, and it would stop needing my
model to say something my model does not say.
