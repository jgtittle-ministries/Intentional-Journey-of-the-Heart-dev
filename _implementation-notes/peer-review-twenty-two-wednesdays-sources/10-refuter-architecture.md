# Adversarial Verification — Architecture and Measurement Set

**Verifier scope:** Krathwohl, Vazire, Lewicki
**Paper checked:** `paper-v2.3.txt`, all findings verified against the numbered text
**Date:** 2026-08-19

Every HIGH and MEDIUM finding below carries a verdict and a corrected severity. LOW findings are grouped
at the end except where a LOW finding is refuted outright.

## Part 1 — The five load-bearing claims the brief flagged

### L1. Vazire Finding 1(b) — "the ceiling premise is self-defeating"

**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM** (down from HIGH).

The logic Vazire attacks is real at the level of the sentence. [6] reads: "self-report meets a structural
ceiling: above the middle levels, social compliance and transformed character produce the same observable
behavior, and the heart is opaque even to its owner." Note the sentence's actual structure: clause (b), the
opaque heart, is the warrant for a *self-report* ceiling; clause (a), identical observable behavior, is not
— it is a statement about what observation cannot resolve, offered as a reason self-report fails. Clause (a)
is therefore a non sequitur in its own sentence *and* an obstacle to the paper's proposed remedy. Vazire is
right that as written it "proves that nobody can measure this, and then recommends observers."

But the charge does not survive contact with the design, and the distinction the brief proposes is the one
that dissolves it. Compliance is by definition contingent on the presence of the eliciting structure;
transformed character is not. The two therefore diverge exactly where the structure is withdrawn — and this
paper withdraws it, on purpose, three times: [56] "Internalization has a behavioral signature: practices that
continue when the structure stops holding them. The year has three scheduled structure-stoppages." Add the
leadership-absence test [19], the event-contingent full forms [37], and [27]'s note of "who arrived early to
build unasked." The design samples *outside* the compliance-eliciting window. So the premise, read as a claim
about a single observed behavior on a single occasion, is defensible; read as a universal claim about
behavior, it is false, and its falsity is demonstrated by the author's own best instrument.

That is a sharper finding than Vazire's, and I would put it to the author that way: **if [6] were true as
written, [56] could not work.** One clause fixes it ("the same behavior *within the setting that elicits
it*"). What does not survive is the severity and the reach — this is one sentence, appearing once (the
abstract [2] omits the offending clause), with no consequence for the architecture. It is a HIGH-priority
*edit* and a MEDIUM-severity *finding*. The author should treat it as urgent for a separate reason Vazire
does not give: the same premise presumably anchors the already-submitted companion article, where it is
load-bearing rather than inherited.

### L2. Vazire Finding 2 — "SOKA runs the wrong way"

**Verdict: WEAKENED, substantially. Severity: MEDIUM** (down from HIGH).

Vazire's quadrant analysis is accurate to his own model, and his placement of the *underlying construct* is
correct: [36] names it as "confidence in God under the load of ordinary weeks," which is low-observability and,
in a Christian school cohort, maximally evaluative. So far so good.

The finding then fails on the move from construct to instrument. SOKA is a model of **judgment accuracy** —
how well self-ratings and other-ratings of a trait recover a criterion. This design does not ask observers to
rate the trait. It asks them to record whether specified acts occurred: [36] "does anyone tell what God said
and what obeying cost"; "when something hard lands, what does the room do in its first ten seconds"; "when
someone goes quiet, who notices, and who goes after them" — coded [37] "seen, not yet, or no occasion." Those
are act-occurrence records, not trait judgments, and the fourth quadrant says nothing against them. It says
you need a criterion instead of a judge — which is precisely what an act record is. Vazire concedes this
himself two paragraphs later ("What the fourth quadrant actually calls for is not a better judge but a
*criterion* … And this pilot **has one**"), which means the finding contains its own refutation.

What survives, and it is worth keeping at MEDIUM: (a) the paper never states the *direction* of the asymmetry
it borrows Vazire's name for, so a reader cannot check the inference; (b) the paper's framing at [6] and [41]
does imply observers are the superior source for the most internal constructs, which SOKA does not license;
(c) the top rungs genuinely do require judgment, not act-recording — "real load borne toward God" [37] is an
inference about an internal state, and there the quadrant warning bites. The correct remedy is Vazire's own
suggested paragraph plus an explicit distinction between recording an act and rating a trait. The finding as
titled — "the design runs against the direction of my model" — does not hold.

### L3. Krathwohl Finding 2 — "arithmetic on ordinal categories"

**Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM** (down from HIGH).

The psychometric point is correct in principle and the grammar objection is fair: [12] "The desire is that each
individual will move, on average, one level or more" cannot be parsed as either an individual claim or a
cohort claim, and the categories are ordinal with visibly unequal spacing. Krathwohl is also technically right
on the internal contradiction: individuals are said to include "possibly four," and a level-four individual
moving one level lands at Characterization, which the fifth principle says "cannot be produced by a syllabus."

But the severity rests on Krathwohl's premise that "this is the one quantitative commitment in the paper," and
that premise is false. The quantity does no evidentiary work anywhere: it is stated as "the desire," it appears
in none of the five disconfirmation conditions [53]–[58], it is not reported, and [12] immediately adds that
it is "never announced, never kept as a scorecard, and never disclosed to a participant or a parent." A number
that is never computed, never published, and never tested is a phrasing problem, not a methodological one.

The brief asks whether the "never a scorecard" guard answers the objection. **It does not — it answers a
different objection.** The guard is ethical: it protects participants from being graded and forecloses Goodhart
capture. Krathwohl's objection is psychometric: you cannot average ordinal categories *even privately, even in
a notebook nobody sees*. The guard and the finding pass each other without touching. So the core survives at
MEDIUM, and Krathwohl's replacement — a described transition drawn from [37]'s aged forms and [56]'s
break-survival counts — is both cheap and strictly better, since those instruments already exist.

### L4. Lewicki Finding 2 — "all three house words sit on one warmth scale"

**Verdict: SURVIVES, with one sub-claim weakened. Severity: HIGH.**

I checked the three exemplars at [41] against the three glosses in the same paragraph, and the result is more
damaging to the paper than Lewicki's own statement of it, because the defect is an *internal inconsistency*
rather than a misreading of the source. The paper's glosses are largely right: "knowledge-based (I trust
because I know you now)" has the trustor knowing the trustee, which is the predictability direction Lewicki
demands. The exemplar phrase then inverts it: "'they know me there' is known" — passive, the member being
known *by* the room, which is disclosure received. **The paper understands the model and then codes against
its own understanding.** That is a checkable, one-line-fixable defect, and it is the coder-facing half that
matters, since [30]'s "circling one word" is done off the exemplars, not the glosses.

"held" also fails as Lewicki says: "It's safe and well-run" reports felt safety and competent administration —
Edmondson's construct, which [17] names correctly — not a cost-benefit calculation, and the two are
indistinguishable in that phrase. The collision Lewicki spots is real and verifiable: [17] "The container is
the first curriculum" and [49]'s first-quarter win are the design's foundational achievement, and [51]/[58]
code that same language as the stage to migrate away from.

**Weakened sub-claim:** "owned" is not purely a warmth statement. "These are my people" is identity language,
close to the paper's own correct gloss ("your values have become mine"); only the trailing "I could say
anything" drifts to safety. So the scale is two-thirds collapsed, not three-thirds.

**On "confirms its prediction by construction":** partly fair, and the paper's stated falsification condition
does not rescue it. [58] fires only on the asymmetric case — "Stagnant vocabulary alongside advancing
sign-readings." The case Lewicki is worried about is the *symmetric* one, where warmth rises and both
vocabulary and signs advance together; [58] is silent there, so the instrument cannot distinguish "trust stage
advanced" from "room got warmer." Where Lewicki overreaches is in calling this "the pilot's central
prediction." It is not; [56] is the paper's strongest test and the vocabulary read is explicitly labeled
"proposed" and "a formal research trail" [58]. Net: the construct-validity defect is real and HIGH; the
"confirmed by construction" framing should be scoped to the vocabulary instrument alone.

### L5. Convergence test — Krathwohl F6 and Vazire F1(a) on the structural ceiling

**Verdict: genuinely independent in their premises, partly shared in their reading convention. Treat as ONE
finding with two supports, not as doubled evidence. Severity: MEDIUM.**

The premises are genuinely different. Krathwohl argues from Handbook II's own content (difficulty and
distortion-vulnerability were documented; structural unmeasurability was not) and offers a *rival causal
account* of the clustering — institutional reluctance to grade affective outcomes, privacy fear, evaluation
cycles shorter than the phenomenon. Vazire argues from a different property of a different source: SOKA is a
relative-accuracy model with no absolute boundary anywhere in it. Neither argument depends on the other, and
each is checkable against its own literature. That is real convergence.

The shared framing is a reading convention both apply and neither defends: **name-in-adjacent-footnote equals
warrant**. Both concede the body sentence attributes the claim elsewhere — [6] opens "A companion article, to
which this one is a sequel, argued that" — and both then argue from what a reader will infer. And Krathwohl's
specific charge that footnote 1 places the volume "in the same note as the claim, with no separation" is
**factually wrong**: footnote 1 reads "[Author], 'Measuring Spiritual Maturity…' … **The taxonomy is** David
R. Krathwohl, Benjamin S. Bloom, and Bertram B. Masia…" — the words "The taxonomy is" *are* the separation,
scoping Krathwohl to the taxonomy and not to the ceiling.

One further shared limitation neither declares: both are objecting to an argument made in a document neither
has seen. Vazire says so ("which I have not seen"); Krathwohl does not. A source-fidelity review of *this*
paper can legitimately say the inheritance is under-signposted. It cannot say the claim is unsupported.
Remedy is one clause plus splitting footnote 1 — worth doing, not worth a HIGH.

## Part 2 — Remaining findings

### Krathwohl

**F1 — Objectives classified as persons, undeclared. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM** (down from HIGH).
The slide is verifiable ([12] "The individuals are assumed to be a mix of one, two, or three, and possibly
four") and no declaration appears anywhere. But Krathwohl himself rules the extension legitimate ("It is an
*extension*, and one within the spirit of what we intended"), so the entire finding is about signposting. Two
mitigations he does not credit: [12] opens by saying "The curriculum's skeleton was argued at length in the
project's earlier design papers, and I will not rehearse it here," which is a legitimate offload for a sequel,
and [12] later calls levels "a Companion's diagnostic lens for shaping care," which half-marks them as
inference rather than reading. Real, cheap, MEDIUM.

**F3 — "Organization" redefined as a corporate competence. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH.**
This is the cleanest source-fidelity hit in the set. [12] "Fourth, at the level of Organization, make the
corporate turn," and again "the corporate turn at level four" — the identification is made twice, and 4.1/4.2
(Conceptualization of a Value; Organization of a Value System) are intrapersonal and cross-domain by
definition. A different construct sits under a named category from the source. **Weakened sub-argument:** his
claim that the single-domain restriction "structurally breaks" Organization overstates — the restriction is on
what the *instrument* indexes, not on what values participants hold, and a participant can still hit a
genuine conflict between obeying and a job or a friendship. Take his option (a), rename and declare.

**F4 — Hierarchy made to do validated-stage-model work with no statement of its status. Verdict: SURVIVES.
Severity: HIGH.** The strongest Krathwohl finding, because it is entirely verifiable in the text. [12] asserts
the cumulative hierarchy flatly — "each presupposing the ones before" — with no hedge, and [54] puts only two
disjuncts at risk, "falsifies either the phasing or the curriculum," omitting the live third possibility that
the ordering does not describe this domain or this timescale. The internal contrast Krathwohl draws is exact
and checkable: [58] labels its borrowed trust mapping "proposed" and registers it "as a formal research
trail," and the taxonomy gets no comparable register anywhere. His fix improves the paper's own headline
virtue — a third disjunct makes the preregistration posture stronger, not weaker.

**F5 — Group-level application asserted, aggregation rule unspecified. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Verified: [12] commits to "separate but interdependent" ladders and never specifies the interdependence, and
the members-mostly-at-level-two versus corporate-behavior-level-two-like distinction is genuinely absent. He
correctly credits the partial declaration at [14]. Converges with Lewicki F3's referent-shift claim from an
entirely different source — genuine, not shared framing.

**F7 — Subcategories never used; 2.3 is the one needed. Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW** (down from MEDIUM).
Accurate to the source (2.1 acquiescence, 2.2 willingness, 2.3 satisfaction in response) and the observation
that [37]'s answering / offering / costly telling already tracks that progression is genuinely valuable. But
using only the five major headings is normal practice and not a misuse of the volume. This is "this would be
better," which the brief tells me to separate from "this is a misuse of my work." Keep it as the highest-value
optional improvement in the set, not as a defect.

**F8 — "Internalization by another name" equates two constructs. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Verified at [41]. Converges with Lewicki F3 claim one, and this is the strongest species of convergence
available: two proprietors of two constructs each independently denying identity with the other. The paper
supplies its own fix at [58] ("the proposed mapping"); the task is to make [41] match [58].

**F9 — Relation between the two ordinal ladders unspecified. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
The paper never states whether a rung promotion indicates a level change, and given its own governing rule
[32] — "treat disagreement between sources as data rather than noise" — it owes a reading for level-versus-sign
disagreement in the way [58] supplies one for vocabulary-versus-sign. His trailing point on the promotion rule
is also verified and independently important: [37] "promotes a rung after three consecutive weeks of seen" and
nothing anywhere demotes, and no rule covers a two-miss-two pattern. That half reinforces Vazire F5.

### Vazire

**F3 — Group-aloud reporting is collective self-report, not informant data. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity:
HIGH.** [41] asserts a methodological fact — "Methodologically, this is informant data gathered aloud, and it
evades the self-report ceiling" — and all three properties that give informant reports their validity are
verifiably absent: independence (answers given in sequence in one room), privacy from the target (the room
reports about the room), and freedom from evaluative stake (non-anonymous, leader present, [28] "The
co-companion scribes the answers verbatim"). His observation that the surveillance protection is applied to
the low-stakes instrument [34] and withheld from the one [41] calls "quietly the best instrument in the pilot"
is exactly right and follows from the paper's own words. He also concedes correctly that the event-level half
("every participant is an eyewitness to whether the circle held the four conditions") is defensible; the
overreach is using the same procedure to index latent stage and level.

**F4 — "Leader first" cannot be a formative lever and a neutral measurement condition. Verdict: SURVIVES.
Severity: MEDIUM.** Verified: [16] argues that going first "licenses a depth the teen would never volunteer
cold," [40] runs the measurement occasion "leader first as always," and the tension is named nowhere — [59]
lists the designer/shepherd conflict but not this one. The fix costs nothing and converts a confound into a
measured variable. **Note for synthesis:** this shares its mechanism with Lewicki F10's priming half. Same
premise, two voices — count once.

**F5 — No reliability check, no blinding, ratchet promotion rule. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH** (up
from MEDIUM). I am raising this one. The rung reading is the outcome variable in [54], one of the five
published disconfirmation conditions, and it is monotone by construction: three consecutive "seen" promotes,
nothing demotes, and the raters designed the rubric, want the rungs to climb, are unblinded to week, and have
been shown the expected curve in advance by [54] itself. An instrument that cannot go down cannot falsify a
prediction that it will go up. That bites the paper's central selling point. One correction to Vazire: the
paper does have calibration anchors — [38] "worked examples of what each reading looks like at each rung sit
on two teaching pages at the front of the pack" — so the charge is no *agreement check* and no *drift check*,
not no codebook.

**F6 — "Preregistration" borrowed without its guarantees. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
[8] asserts the identity — "The academic literature calls publishing a design and its failure conditions
before the results 'preregistration'" — and none of the properties Nosek et al. commend it for (registry,
timestamp, immutability, committed analysis plan, data not yet in hand) are present; [8] itself says the pilot
is "now underway." The paper's own next sentence supplies most of the honest version: "I am telling you how I
could be wrong before I know whether I am." Overlaps the Nosek review — dedupe at synthesis.

**F7 — Falsifiability audit of the five conditions. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Each sub-verdict checks out against the text: [54]'s disjunctive escape, [55]'s undefined "essentially no
discrepancies" with no metric and no denominator, [57]'s undefined "consistently require adult rescue" judged
by the adults who would be doing the rescuing, [58]'s good design and contaminated execution, and [56] as the
genuinely strong one. **Cross-finding the synthesis must not miss:** Krathwohl F4 wants a *third* disjunct
added to [54], and Vazire F7 objects that [54] already has one too many. Both are right and the remedies
collide. The reconciliation is to pre-commit which observed pattern kills which branch — then a third branch
adds falsifiability instead of subtracting it.

**F8 — The paper never states n. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.** I searched independently and confirm:
"a small Christian school" [8], "a crew of teenagers" [5], "one faithful family" [16], and no participant count
anywhere. [55] and [56] cannot be evaluated without a denominator, and [50] literally counts things ("how many
walked back in with the return card") against no base. The consent rate is, as he says, itself a finding about
the permission ethic.

**F9 — Hedging quarantined; abstract and [51] unhedged. Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: MEDIUM.**
The [51] charge survives cleanly and is the real content: "That self-recognition is not a side effect. **It is
the level-three transition**" is an unhedged identity claim equating an observed behavior with a latent
construct transition, in the practitioner section, with no qualifier. The other two sub-charges do not hold.
The abstract says the pilot is "**built so that** its ordinary practices generate…," which is design-intent
framing, not an assertion that the requirement has been met. And the [36] sub-charge objects to a claim the
paper already hedges in the words Vazire himself quotes — "**what I believe is** its most useful contribution"
— which is the brief's first failure mode. His ecological-inference add-on is also ambiguous rather than
wrong: [12] targets *the group's* two-to-three transition, so [51]'s reading of a room-level self-recognition
as the level-three transition may be on the group ladder rather than crossing it.

### Lewicki

**F1 — "Fifty years" overstates both age and empirical standing. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: HIGH.**
Three separable claims, all checkable, all correct. The arithmetic ([41] "Fifty years of that literature
describes trust developing through three levels" against a 1996 chapter and a 1992 typology) is a plain factual
error, and his migration hypothesis is supported by the text — [16] does say "the disclosure research has said
for fifty years," which is right for Altman and Taylor. The consensus claim is disproved by the paper's own
footnote 10, which carries Mayer et al. (a trustworthiness-perceptions model, not a stage model) and Rousseau
et al. (a definitional synthesis) under a sentence asserting a three-stage consensus. And the empirical-standing
half — that footnote 10's "updated in" implies confirmation where the 2006 review was a stock-taking finding
developmental models under-tested — is the substantive core, and it is the same species of defect as Krathwohl
F4 reached from a completely different literature. That is real convergence and it is what pushes both to HIGH.

**F3 — "Internalization by another name" carries three undeclared claims. Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity:
MEDIUM** (down from HIGH). Claim one duplicates Krathwohl F8 and is correct, but the paper supplies its own
hedge at [58], so a reader of the whole paper is not left with an identity claim — severity down. Claim two,
the dyadic-to-group referent shift, is *not* hedged anywhere and survives on its own; it converges with
Krathwohl F5. Claim three is the mapping [58] already labels proposed. Net: one clause fix at [41], MEDIUM.

**F4 — A single room-level reading masks heterogeneity; the pre-existing parent-child dyads are the acute
case. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.** The best original finding in Lewicki's set and the one most
specific to this design. Parent-child pairs plausibly enter at identification-based trust on day one, and [16]
puts exactly those dyads at the front of every disclosure ("in every practice the parents start"), while [30]
compresses the room to "one word for the quarter's trust read." Nothing in [40]/[41]/[58] separates trust in
the room from trust in kin who happen to be in the room. The fix is nearly free: the third question already
asks members to describe the group to "somebody else," and specifying an outsider who does not know your family
forces the room referent.

**F5 — Scope conditions never stated, and one is inverted. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
The internal contrast is verifiable: [17] does exactly the right thing for Edmondson — "The workplace
literature calls the underlying construct psychological safety" — and [41] introduces the trust model as "the
trust literature" with no scope statement at all, while extending a model of voluntary, exitable, roughly
symmetric adult work relationships to involuntary, non-exitable, permanently asymmetric family ones. His
compliant-reciprocation mechanism is a legitimate rival explanation for the design's central formative move,
and he is scrupulous about its status ("I am not saying that is what is happening in this room"). The paper's
counter-evidence at [16] is n=1 and the author's own read, so it does not close the point.

**F6 — Credit divided in the prose, recombined in the apparatus. Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW** (down
from MEDIUM). The two textual observations are correct — "since" does assert, and footnote 10 does sit under
the vocabulary inference. But the same sentence opens "the trust literature supplies the stages, and **this
project reads** the answers as a staged vocabulary," which is the division of credit Lewicki himself praises
in his section 1. The residual misattribution risk is small and the fix is two words plus one footnote clause.
Real, cheap, low.

**F7 — Progression treated as one-way, sequential and retained. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity: MEDIUM.**
All three sub-claims check out and all three remedies improve the paper. Non-arrival: [58] treats stagnation
as disconfirming, where the source holds that most relationships settle permanently at calculus-based trust —
and the paper already knows the right posture, since [12] says of Krathwohl level four "designed for but never
claimed … its absence at year's end is not read as failure," and gives the trust ladder no equivalent
sentence. Cumulativeness: "displacing" [51] and "migrate" [58] are the wrong verbs, and his practical warning
is the useful part — declining "held" language is ambiguous between internalized container, taken-for-granted
container, and quietly failed container, and the coding scheme cannot separate the three. Regression: verified,
[53]–[58] contain no condition under which the ladder goes *down*, and adding one is a net gain in
falsifiability.

**F8 — The "first fight" claim welds Tuckman's sequence to Edmondson's mechanism with an unearned "because."
Verdict: WEAKENED. Severity: LOW** (down from MEDIUM). The weld charge does not hold on the natural reading.
[42] says disagreement arrives "before mature trust … because people risk conflict only in a room they believe
will hold it" — that is coherent if the threshold for voicing conflict is *some* safety and mature trust is a
later state, which is exactly the paper's own quarter arc. The two propositions are compatible; Lewicki has
manufactured a contradiction out of a compression. What survives: "usually" is an unsupported frequency claim,
the mechanism half is footnoted only to Tuckman and Wheelan (fn 11) when it is Edmondson's, and his caution
that the marker cannot distinguish "the room held it" from "the room appeared to hold it and the dissenter
never spoke again" is genuinely useful and unaddressed. **Overlaps Edmondson's review — dedupe.**

**F9 — The trust read has no codebook, no second coder, three observations. Verdict: SURVIVES. Severity:
MEDIUM.** The within-paper asymmetry is demonstrable: [38] gives the three vital signs aged rungs, a
three-value coding scheme, a promotion rule, and "worked examples … on two teaching pages," while the trust
read gets three exemplar phrases in one sentence of running prose and [30]'s "circling one word." One
correction: "three occasions cannot demonstrate migration through three stages under any coding scheme" is
overstated — three timepoints across three ordered categories is the bare minimum and can show a monotone
trend; what it cannot support is any claim about the *shape* of the trajectory. Partly shares framing with
Vazire F5 (invested unblinded coder, no agreement check) applied to a different instrument.

**F10 — Aloud, leader-first collection undercuts the paper's own "unprompted" claim. Verdict: SURVIVES.
Severity: HIGH** (up from MEDIUM). I am raising this one, because it is the sharpest form of the leader-first
problem and it catches the paper contradicting itself in its own words: [41] rests the instrument on
"unprompted salience, what a room spontaneously names as worth keeping," while [28] has "The leader answers
first, all three, each with a story attached." The design took deliberate care not to hand the room a category
and then hands it a lexicon. The priming half is *partly* mitigated by [40]'s advance questions sent home
"across the break on purpose," and Lewicki knows this — his fix builds on it. The half that is wholly
unaddressed is the serious one: spoken group data is voice data, voice is a function of the safety being
measured, the sample will skew high-trust by construction and skew further as the room warms, **producing the
predicted migration from the sampling process alone**. Combined with F2, the vocabulary instrument's predicted
result is over-determined by three independent mechanisms. Recording who did not speak costs nothing.

### Refuted outright

**Lewicki F11 — "[13] defines trust as disclosure depth." Verdict: REFUTED. Severity: LOW.**
The paper does not define trust as disclosure. [13] reads "It is seen in trust, as the group risks deeper and
more costly self-revelation with one another" — a visibility claim naming an indicator, not a definition. And
the indicator is warranted by a source the paper already cites in the same footnote as Lewicki: Mayer, Davis
and Schoorman define trust as willingness to be vulnerable, and costly self-revelation is willingness to be
vulnerable. Whatever downstream fusion Lewicki is worried about is already carried by his F2, which does not
need this.

### LOW findings, grouped

Krathwohl F10 (terminology drift, "levels" for "categories"), F11 (the fifth category described at [12] but
never named — verified, "Characterization" appears nowhere in the paper), and F12 (one body mention of
Krathwohl at [6] against an architecture running through [12], [18], [37], [41], [51], [54], [56], [63]) all
**SURVIVE at LOW**; each is factually correct and each is a one-line fix. Vazire F10 (footnote 13 carries five
unrelated sources, and characterizes SOKA without stating the direction of the asymmetry) **SURVIVES at LOW**
— verified, the note runs Strathern, Vazire, Bonhoeffer, Hawkins and Parkinson, Schwarz — and the direction
point matters more than the placement point. The "Harper and Row" / "Harper & Row" inconsistency between
footnote 13 and bibliography [69] is real and trivial. On the citation recommendations: I spot-checked the
sources all three reviewers propose — Connelly and Ones 2010, Kolar/Funder/Colvin 1996, Vazire and Mehl 2008,
Nisbett and Wilson 1977, Anderson and Bourke 2000, Anderson and Krathwohl 2001, Shapiro/Sheppard/Cheraskin
1992, Lewicki/McAllister/Bies 1998 — and they are real works, correctly described, and appropriate to the
claims they are offered for. No persona invented its own literature, which is worth stating because it is the
failure mode I was watching for hardest.

## The findings I would stake the review's credibility on

1. **Krathwohl F4** — the cumulative hierarchy is asserted flatly at [12] ("each presupposing the ones
   before") and put beyond risk at [54], while the paper hedges its *other* borrowed framework properly at
   [58]. Entirely verifiable inside the paper; remedy strengthens the paper's own headline virtue.
2. **Lewicki F2** — the house-word exemplars, and specifically that [41]'s gloss of knowledge-based trust is
   *correct* while its exemplar phrase inverts it. An internal inconsistency, checkable in one paragraph,
   affecting the instrument's coder-facing content.
3. **Vazire F3** — group-aloud collection is not informant data; independence, privacy from target, and

4. **Lewicki F10** — voice-selection skew manufactures the predicted vocabulary migration from the sampling

5. **Vazire F5** — the rung reading is monotone by construction with no demotion rule, which means [54]

6. **Krathwohl F3** — the corporate turn is placed under the name of a source category it is not an instance

## The findings that should not have been filed

- **Lewicki F11.** Rests on reading "It is seen in trust, as…" as a definition. It is not one, and the
  indicator it names is warranted by Mayer et al., cited in the same footnote.
- **Vazire F9's [36] sub-charge.** Objects to a claim the paper hedges in the very words quoted ("what I
  believe is"). Brief failure mode 1.
- **Vazire F9's abstract sub-charge.** "Built so that its ordinary practices generate" is design-intent
  framing; the charge that it "asserts the requirement has been met" does not survive reading the clause.
- **Krathwohl F6's footnote half.** Footnote 1 reads "**The taxonomy is** Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia," which
  is precisely the separation the finding says is absent.
- **Vazire F2 as titled.** "The design runs against the direction of my model" is refuted by the distinction
  between recording an act and rating a trait — and by Vazire's own concession that the pilot has the
  criterion his fourth quadrant calls for.
- **Lewicki F8's weld charge.** Tuckman's sequence and Edmondson's mechanism are compatible under the natural
  reading of [42]; the contradiction is manufactured. The "usually" frequency claim survives; the weld does
  not.
- **Krathwohl F7 as a defect.** Using only the five major headings is normal practice, not misuse. Excellent
  as a suggestion, wrong as a finding.

## Findings whose remedy would damage the paper

1. **Vazire F3's fix (b) — unsigned index cards at the three self-reads.** Methodologically the right move and
   the author should not take it as written. [40] reports this mechanism was "redesigned twice in the field"
   precisely because paper-based versions "felt like being graded," and [46] draws the paper's brightest
   ethical line exactly here: "if it is paper a participant touches, it is asked for." An index card at the
   self-read reintroduces the touched page inside the occasion the design spent two revisions getting paper
   *out* of. Take the free substitutes instead — scribe and code the leader's answer (Vazire F4), record who
   did not speak (Lewicki F10), have members arrive with a written line since the questions already go home
   two weeks early — and then state the residual limitation, which is Vazire's own option (c).
2. **Krathwohl F3's option (b) — instrument Organization as genuine value conflict.** Correct to the source
   and pastorally hazardous: it means eliciting, in a room where parents and their own teenagers sit together
   and "a family is never divided" [16], the conflicts between obeying God and a job, a friendship, or a family
   expectation. Krathwohl's own option (a) — keep the corporate turn, name it as the author's construct, place
   it alongside the category rather than inside it — costs one sentence and carries no such risk. The review
   should recommend (a) and say why (b) was declined.
3. **Lewicki F2's fix in its strong form — read the stages off agency substitution instead of vocabulary.**
   The behavioral markers he names are genuinely better indicators, but the two he points at are already
   carrying other loads: the teen-led second running is [19]'s leadership absence test and [57]'s
   disconfirmation condition, and the sought stray is one of the three vital signs. Re-tasking them as the
   trust read confounds three separate instruments and would cost the design more than the vocabulary defect
   does. His own weaker version — "let the vocabulary be corroborating evidence rather than the instrument" —
   gets the benefit without the collapse.
4. **Vazire F5's fix (b) — an outside coder for the self-read transcripts.** The transcripts are verbatim
   records from a room under a confidentiality promise whose legal limits were named aloud [17], and the
   covenant at [44] says "names stop at the shepherd who knows the faces." Handing them to a coder outside the
   companion team breaks the covenant the paper argues is what makes the measurement possible at all. The fix
   is salvageable only as de-identified transcripts or a second *inside* companion coding blind, which is what
   Lewicki F9 proposes and which costs nothing.
5. **A general caution on Krathwohl F1's and F5's remedies.** Both are correct and both call for declarative
   paragraphs about framework status and aggregation rules. Two or three of these are inoculation; five or six
   of them, stacked before the five principles, would turn the opening of a practitioner protocol paper into a
   methods preamble and bury [5]'s four-o'clock room — which is, on the evidence of every reviewer's section 1,
   the thing that makes the paper persuasive. Recommend consolidating all framework-status declarations into
   **one** paragraph, not one per finding.
