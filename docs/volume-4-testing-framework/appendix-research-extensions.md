# Vol 4 Appendix: Research-Grade Extensions and Future Instruments

!!! warning "These testing protocols are proposed, not validated"

    Every instrument referenced in this Vol 4 framework — the Pilot Ready core (SIP, NRP, EWI), the extension instruments described below, and the protocols that integrate them — is a **proposed design**. The instruments themselves have not been individually validated through formal psychometric work; more importantly, the protocols **have not been tested as a group** in actual ministry settings. No pilot study has yet run the integrated framework end-to-end with real participants, real facilitators, real twelve-month timelines, and the cross-instrument convergence checks the framework predicts. The §1 framing — "drafts, ready for piloting and refinement" — applies to the framework as a whole, not only to individual instruments. Readers and future pilot teams should treat the Vol 4 protocol as a thoughtful design ready for testing, not as a battle-tested measurement program. Findings from any pilot use should be expected to revise both the individual instruments and the protocol's structural choices.

(A separate "Appendix A" is referenced elsewhere in Vol 4 — in §3b, §3d, and §5 — as the home for the full draft instrument items themselves, e.g., the actual PSFA items, the actual STI-A items. Those drafts existed in the original Word manuscript but were not pulled through into the published markdown; that publication remains a future task and is unrelated to this appendix. This appendix is *not* the same Appendix A those sections refer to; it is a separate disposition list about which instruments are core versus extension.)

## Why this appendix exists

The Vol 4 testing protocol is moving toward a **Pilot Ready Protocol** — a three-instrument core (the Stage Integration Profile, the Narrative Reflection Protocol, and the External Witness Interview) designed so that a real pastor, facilitator, or small-group leader in a real church can actually run it without being overwhelmed by assessment load. The §4e section already insists that the facilitator is the single most important variable in whether a PROAPT group produces genuine formation; that claim is operationally meaningful only if the protocol leaves the facilitator the time, attention, and presence the role requires.

The instruments collected here were developed earlier in the Vol 4 design as part of a broader twelve-instrument suite. They remain operationally sound *as proposed designs* (see the warning above) and are preserved here for facilitators, research teams, doctoral-level studies, or future programs that want deeper instrumentation in specific dimensions once the framework moves into actual pilot use. They are not part of the Pilot Ready core. Adding any of them to a real-world protocol is an explicit choice with a known cost in facilitator and participant load; the decision should be made deliberately and with awareness of what the core three instruments already capture.

This appendix is the disposition list for the moment. The transition from the earlier full-suite design to the Pilot Ready core happens in three published passes:

- **Pass A (landed).** The appendix exists as an index. The canonical descriptions of the eight extension instruments still live in §3 and §4 as currently written. Each entry below cross-references the canonical location and adds a Pilot Ready disposition note.
- **Pass B (landed in this Vol 4 revision).** The Stage Integration Profile (SIP — both Individual and Group variants) is now published as a new core instrument in [§3d Instrument 5](section-3-individual-spiritual-formation-testing-protocol.md#3d-the-five-assessment-instruments) and in [§4](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#the-stage-integration-profile-at-the-group-level-group-sip-pilot-ready-core-instrument) (Group SIP), alongside the existing instruments. New Outcome Variable 5 (Individual SIP Trajectory) added to §3c; new Group Outcome Variable 6 (Group SIP Trajectory) added to §4d. The existing twelve instruments remain in place; the Pilot Ready core (SIP + NRP + EWI) is now visible in the published protocol but does not yet replace anything.
- **Pass C (forthcoming).** §3 and §4 are restructured around the Pilot Ready three-instrument core. The canonical descriptions of the eight extension instruments migrate into this appendix; §3 and §4 retain brief stub references back here. The Image of God 4Rs becomes a recurring prompt set within the NRP. The Tell Rate is kept at the group level as a single weekly facilitator check rather than as part of the multi-variable Session Observation Protocol.

Until Pass C lands, the canonical text of each extension instrument remains in §3 or §4 at the location noted below.

## The eight extension instruments

### STI-A — Scripture Trust Index (Adapted)

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§3d Instrument 2](section-3-individual-spiritual-formation-testing-protocol.md#3d-the-four-assessment-instruments).

A 12-item self-report instrument adapted from Campbell et al.'s (2021) epistemic trust measure, recalibrated for trust in scripture as the trusted source. Three trust dimensions: trust, mistrust, credulity.

**When to add this instrument:** when a study or pilot wants a dedicated trust-in-scripture measurement that runs independently of the SIP's broader profile. The SIP captures trust-in-scripture as an aspect of Dimensions 1 (engagement with scripture) and 3 (how scripture shapes decisions); a dedicated STI-A is useful when the trust dimension specifically is the research target — e.g., interventions designed to address scripture-trust deficits, longitudinal trust-development studies, or comparative work against Campbell et al.'s original instrument.

### SDFL — Spiritual Disciplines Frequency Log

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§3d Instrument 4](section-3-individual-spiritual-formation-testing-protocol.md#3d-the-four-assessment-instruments).

A weekly log tracking frequency and approximate duration of five spiritual practice categories (personal bible reading, personal prayer, PROAPT participation, corporate worship, service/giving).

**When to add this instrument:** when a study wants quantitative practice-frequency data that can be correlated with formation outcomes. The SDFL answers questions of the form "did formation occur under conditions of high or low discipline frequency?" — useful for studies investigating the relationship between practice intensity and stage progression, or for participants whose own self-understanding benefits from explicit frequency tracking. The Pilot Ready core does not require this data because the formation reading depends primarily on the qualitative texture the NRP captures, not on practice counts.

### SOP — Session Observation Protocol

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§4f](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4f-the-session-observation-protocol).

A facilitator-completed weekly six-variable checklist tracking substantive engagement in the Observe step, personal-cost applications in the Apply step, Tell completion, named upcoming Tells, significant emotional moments, and an overall depth rating.

**When to add this instrument:** when a research team or doctoral study wants session-by-session formation data that can be aggregated across twelve months. Note: the SOP is the single largest facilitator-load item in the previous full-suite protocol — completing it weekly is the kind of task that competes with facilitator presence during the session itself. The Pilot Ready core replaces the SOP with a single-question weekly Tell-Rate check kept at the group level. Studies wanting the richer session-level data should add the full SOP back deliberately, with explicit attention to whether the facilitator has the time and attention to complete it without compromising presence in the room.

### GSFP — Group Spiritual Formation Profile

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§4c](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4c-the-group-spiritual-formation-profile-gsfp).

A facilitator-maintained visual chart showing the distribution of individual AT stages within the group at each quarterly assessment point.

**When to add this instrument:** when a research team wants the individual-distribution view of the group's formation alongside the group-culture view. Group SIP measures the group's collective culture profile across the five AT stages; the GSFP measures which individuals are at which stages at a given point. These are complementary cuts. The GSFP is also useful when the facilitator wants a quick visual reference for how the group's composition is evolving across the twelve-month arc. The Pilot Ready core does not include the GSFP because the individual SIPs at baseline / midpoint / endpoint contain the same individual-stage information in profile form, and the Group SIP captures the collective-culture reading the GSFP was partly designed to inform.

### CSFA — Collective Spiritual Formation Assessment

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§4g](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4g-the-collective-spiritual-formation-assessment).

A 12-item self-report instrument administered anonymously to the group at baseline, Month 6, and Month 12. Three domains: corporate engagement with scripture, corporate trust and safety, outward orientation.

**When to add this instrument:** when a study wants a complementary cut on group culture organized by domain rather than by AT stage. The Group SIP largely covers the same operational territory but organized around the five AT stages with the integration check built in; CSFA's three-domain organization is useful when a study wants to track corporate-engagement / trust-safety / outward-orientation as separately analyzable variables. Alternating quarterly administration of Group SIP and CSFA (rather than both at every point) is one way to retain the CSFA's information without compounding survey load on participants.

### GCS — Group Cohesion Scale

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§4d Group Outcome Variable 2](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4d-group-level-outcome-variables).

An adapted Group Cohesion Scale drawn from the group therapy literature, administered quarterly. Measures the degree to which participants feel genuinely known, safe to share honestly, and supported in formation.

**When to add this instrument:** when a study wants a validated, externally-grounded cohesion measure independent of the AT integration framing. The GCS connects the Vol 4 protocol to the broader group-therapy research literature and provides a comparison point for spiritual-formation small groups against secular therapeutic groups. The Pilot Ready core does not require the GCS because Group SIP Dimensions 3 (welcoming new voices), 4 (prayer life), and 5 (conflict and correction) cover the operational territory the GCS measures, organized within the same instrument and integration-checked.

### Facilitator PSFA

**Pilot Ready disposition:** optional / superseded by Facilitator SIP in the Pilot Ready core.

**Canonical description:** referenced in [§4e](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4e-the-facilitator-as-a-critical-variable) and [§5](section-5-research-program-design-for-spiritual.md#5c-instruments-summary) as the facilitator-applied version of the Personal Spiritual Formation Assessment.

A version of the PSFA completed by the facilitator at the start of the study to document the facilitator's own AT stage as a formal independent variable in group-outcome analysis.

**When to add this instrument:** under Pass C of the Pilot Ready transition, this instrument is superseded by Facilitator SIP — the SIP completed by the facilitator on themselves at the start and end of the study. The Facilitator SIP carries the integration-check advantage the Facilitator PSFA cannot (a closed Stage 4 facilitator would PSFA-rate as Stage 4 while Facilitator SIP would show the asymmetric profile that flags closure). Studies wanting the original PSFA-format facilitator instrument for comparability to earlier work or to the existing literature can use this preserved version.

### OT-5 Supplementary Interview

**Pilot Ready disposition:** situational / research-grade.

**Canonical description:** see [§4h Phase 2](section-4-small-group-spiritual-formation-testing.md#4h-the-testing-timeline-for-small-groups) where the supplementary interview protocol is introduced as a contingent measure triggered by significant formation breakthroughs.

A brief structured interview triggered when any participant experiences a significant formation breakthrough during the study (dramatic conviction, sudden reorganization of values, unexpected surge of higher-stage behavior). Documents the conditions preceding the event — PROAPT engagement, personal circumstances, prayer life, relational context — for the Open Trail 5 question of what precedes instantaneous or dramatic heart change.

**When to add this instrument:** this instrument is triggered by events, not by schedule. It is part of the Pilot Ready protocol only contingently — if such an event occurs, the facilitator should document it via the OT-5 protocol regardless of whether the rest of the extension suite is in use. The instrument is in this appendix rather than in the Pilot Ready core because most studies will not encounter the triggering events; when they do, the protocol is available here.

## How to use this appendix

The recommended practice for any pilot or study:

1. Run the Pilot Ready core (SIP-Individual + NRP + EWI for individuals; Group SIP + Tell Rate + aggregated EWI for groups) without modification. This is the minimum viable protocol designed to be runnable in real church contexts by real facilitators.
2. Identify the specific research question or pastoral need that an extension instrument would address.
3. Add only the extension instruments that address that specific need.
4. Track facilitator load and participant survey load as protocol variables; the addition of any extension instrument is expected to affect both.

The Pilot Ready core was designed to produce sound formation data on its own. Each extension instrument adds a specific capacity at a specific cost. The point of this appendix is not to discourage use of the extensions but to make the cost-benefit decision deliberate.
