# Before You Measure: Earning the Right to Ask

This chapter sits between the epistemology and the instruments on purpose, because it answers a question the sections on either side of it quietly assume: **how do we know these instruments can actually *see* the thing they claim to measure — before a living soul is ever asked to sit under one?**

Section 2 defined the constructs and was honest about their difficulty. Sections 3 through 5 describe protocols, profiles, and a research program. But between defining a construct and deploying an instrument there is a step that this volume, in its earlier form, skipped — the step where an instrument *earns the right to ask*. Every field that has tried to measure the affective domain and been trusted for it has learned the same lesson: an unvalidated instrument pointed at a person is not neutral. It can misread a dark night as backsliding, credential a performer, teach the honest to hide, and — the finding that should sober us most — *destroy the very thing it was built to see*. So this chapter states the discipline plainly: **no instrument in this volume runs on a living participant until it has climbed the ladder described here.** And the whole ladder is ordered by the one-sentence standard this project holds everywhere measurement appears (see the [Guardrails](https://jgtittle-ministries.github.io/a-church-prepared-for-revival-a-pilot-proposed/reader.html#docs/movement-ii-what-is-your-soil/03-the-guardrails.md) and the Vol 6 [Research Register](../volume-6-governance/research-register.md)): *measure nothing you will not weep over.* Applied to design, the principle becomes a rule of sequence: **the more a measure could wound, the smaller and later it deploys.**

## What the fields that went before us learned

We are not the first to try to measure internalization. Krathwohl's taxonomy has been carried into nursing, medicine, pharmacy, engineering, the military academies, and teacher education, and their sixty years of experience — successes and wreckage both — were reviewed at length for this chapter (the working synthesis, with full citations, lives with the project's research notes; the anchors are named here). Four findings govern everything below.

**First: the fields that were trusted met four conditions at once.** Their assessments tied each level to a logically appropriate kind of evidence; their trained raters agreed on real artifacts; their instruments demonstrably separated groups already known to differ; and no single method — least of all self-report — was ever accepted alone. Where any of the four was missing, the record shows either no real assessment at all (a 2025 scoping review of the field found that sixteen of twenty studies never touched assessment, and that "no guidelines describing the assessment of the affective domain were found" — Potgieter et al., *Nurse Education in Practice* 86, 2025) or counterfeit results.

**Second: each level of the taxonomy demands a different kind of evidence.** The clearest published mapping (Yanofsky & Nyquist, anesthesiology education) runs: *Receiving* is evidenced by attention; *Responding* by produced work; *Valuing* by **voluntary, unprompted** expression or action; *Organization* by the value visible in real practice — how a person orders competing goods when it costs something; *Characterization* only by **consistency across contexts over a long time**. An instrument that asks the same kind of question about all five levels — a Likert self-report battery, say — is category-confused above the second level. This gradient is now a design requirement: every item in every Vol 4 instrument must state which level it addresses and why its evidence type is adequate to that level.

**Third: the top of the ladder is invisible to any single-sitting instrument, and the honest fields said so.** Krathwohl himself wrote that formal education "generally cannot reach this level… the maturity and personal integration required at this level are not attained until at least some years after" formal education ends (Handbook II, p. 165). When Australian medical educators built the one instrument that maps directly onto Krathwohl's levels and validated it properly — trained raters, strong agreement — **no student artifact ever evidenced the top level** (Rogers et al., the Griffith University Affective Learning Scale). When civil engineering wrote affective attainment levels into its professional Body of Knowledge (the effort begun by Lynch et al., *Journal of Professional Issues in Engineering Education and Practice* 135, 2009), the committee set the entry-to-practice target at Organization and placed Characterization deliberately beyond what any assessment certifies. West Point assesses the internalization of values by ratings from self, peers, subordinates, and instructors, repeated across four years — a moving portrait painted by many hands, not a test. The consensus of every serious attempt is the same, and Scripture said it first: the settled character of a life is known by its fruit, over time, to those who walk with it (Matt. 7:16). So every Vol 4 instrument now carries a published **claim ceiling**: instruments gather evidence up to Organization; Characterization is affirmed only by longitudinal, multi-witness consistency of life — and the new birth, being a difference of *kind* rather than degree, is on no instrument's dial at all (John 3:8).

**Fourth — and this one is the weep-test, arrived at empirically by people who never heard the phrase: detached measurement destroys what it touches.** The record is specific. Nursing's "intentional rounding" documentation regime made the *record* of caring the target, and caring itself withered — "the task had become the documentation, not the actual conversation or the care." Medical students under empathy assessment learned to *perform* empathy for the evaluators' criteria, leading researchers to the bleak conclusion that a quality assessed inauthentically may be unassessable. Engineering lab rubrics were found to reward writing the "correct" reading over the honest one — a scoring system that punished integrity. And Krathwohl's own warning from 1964 stands over all of it: students "will provide responses to direct questions that correspond to what they believe the instructor wants, rather than what they truly feel." Note carefully what the successful fields did about this. They did not build cleverer detached instruments. They **moved the measurement into relationship**: many observers who know the person, over time, under low stakes. That is the shepherd's scale, discovered by secular necessity. The corpus holds it as a conviction; the literature now supplies it as a finding.

## The validation ladder

Five rungs, in order. Each instrument climbs all five before it touches a congregation, and the record of the climb is kept.

**Rung 1 — the construct audit.** Every item, traced on paper to three things: the specific Affective-Taxonomy level it claims to detect, the evidence-type warrant (from the gradient above), and the scriptural definition behind the level. Three standing checks travel with the audit. The **inversion check**: any affect-keyed item must survive the desolation question — *which way does the desire point?* — or it will misdiagnose the purified dry believer as a backslider; an item that Teresa of Calcutta — whose roughly fifty documented years of interior darkness ran alongside fifty years of unbroken fidelity and expanding fruit — would fail is a defective item, full stop. The **degree-not-kind label**: every item marked as reading degree only, never the new birth. And the **tacit-believer check**: does this item detect internalization, or only fluency in our vocabulary? An ardent but plain-spoken saint must be able to pass it.

**Rung 2 — the vignette bench.** This is the heart of the chapter, and the direct answer to "how would you know *before* testing with an individual?" **You test the instruments against the documented dead before you test them on the living.** The bench is a set of thirty to forty short written portraits of figures whose spiritual condition is already known from the record — Scripture's own extended cases and saints with thick, multiply-sourced biographies — each portrait pegged to one dominant taxonomy level *by documented behavior* (what the person did, repeatedly, at cost), never by label. Raters trained on exemplars apply the draft instruments to the portraits blind, and we check one thing: **does the instrument sort them the way the record already sorted them?** Every misfire is a design flaw found at zero pastoral cost. Nobody weeps over a vignette — which is precisely why the bench comes first.

The method is not an improvisation; it is a disciplined hybrid of three established research traditions: written clinical vignettes validated as proxies for live observation (Peabody et al., *JAMA* 283, 2000 — vignettes tracked real practice quality nearly as well as standardized patients); anchoring vignettes, in which fixed-truth portraits calibrate raters and scales (King, Murray, Salomon & Tandon, *American Political Science Review* 98, 2004); and historiometry, the quantitative study of documented historical lives (Simonton). To our knowledge no one has combined the three to validate a formation instrument against scriptural and hagiographic portraits — the method itself is a contribution this project intends to document to a publishable standard.

Three rules make the bench honest. *The portraits span the whole continuum, including deliberately ambiguous middle cases* — validating only on obvious saints and obvious counterfeits provably overstates an instrument's power. *The scoring key is frozen before any rater trial*: an expert panel assigns each portrait's level in advance, and a portrait on which the panel cannot agree leaves the bench. And *three negative controls are mandatory, and any control failure fails the instrument regardless of its other statistics*:

1. **The counterfeit** — high verbal piety over a contradicting behavioral record (the Pharisee of Matthew 23 is the canonical case). The instrument must score him *low*. This is the direct test against Krathwohl's faking warning.
2. **The adjacent look-alike** — documented natural virtue, temperament, or discipline without the specific thing being measured. The instrument must be able to tell the difference or flag that it cannot.
3. **The desolate believer** — a figure in documented long dryness whose life-arc shows deep internalization (Teresa of Calcutta is the canonical case; the Psalms supply more). The instrument must score her *high*. **A bench without this control cannot certify an instrument safe for the very people this project most exists to protect** — and an instrument that fails this control has failed the weep-test at the design stage, before it ever had the chance to fail it on a living soul.

**Rung 3 — cognitive interviews.** Before scoring anything live, a handful of people like the eventual participants think aloud through every question: *what did you hear this asking?* A question that reads one way at the writer's desk and another way to a wounded reader, a teenager, or a dry believer is a defect no desk review catches. This is the Guardrails' "wounds are not weeds" commitment applied at design time.

**Rung 4 — the leadership cohort.** The first living people to sit under any instrument are the people who chose it. This extends the *leaders-first* principle that governs the whole pilot architecture (the leadership circle walks the formation path before the congregation does): the leadership cohort takes every survey, keeps every tracker, and sits under the observation notes *before any congregational participant does* — discovering what the instruments feel like from the chair rather than the clipboard, under the deepest consent in the house. Inter-rater calibration for observers happens here too, against the bench's exemplar portraits, and the raters' agreement is measured and recorded before their judgments are ever attached to anyone else.

**Rung 5 — congregational deployment, under covenant.** Only now, and only inside the signed governance already built for it: the Guardrails and the Measurement Covenant, with their consent, their shepherd's-scale cap on names, their bad-news channel, and their annual renewal-or-lapse clock. The ladder does not expire at deployment: any instrument that changes materially returns to Rung 1, and every instrument re-justifies its existence yearly or retires.

## What this chapter changes about the rest of the volume

Sections 3, 4, and 5 should now be read as what they actually are: the **deployment half** of a testing framework whose **validation half** is this chapter. Nothing in them is diminished — the SIP profiles, the External Witness Interview, the Tell-Rate, the group protocols all stand — but each now carries three obligations it did not previously state: a construct audit trail (Rung 1), a bench record showing it sorts the known cases correctly (Rung 2), and a claim ceiling published on its face. The External Witness Interview, it is worth noting, comes out of this review strengthened: the literature's most consistent positive finding is that *other-report from people who know the person outperforms self-report* for exactly the constructs we care about — the corpus's instinct that the witness track is senior to self-report is independently confirmed.

And one commitment binds the whole program. The researcher's-formation section that closes Section 2 said the researcher must be on the journey. This chapter adds the measurement corollary and lets it govern: the instruments of this volume exist to serve shepherds who know faces, and they are validated in the one order that honors that — *paper first, then the willing, then the flock; and at every stage, only in hands that would weep.*

***Certainty note.*** *The validation ladder itself is standard research methodology (content validation, vignette/known-groups calibration, cognitive interviewing, staged piloting) applied to this domain — Reasonably Inferred as method, with the applied-AT literature at its back. The vignette bench's specific extension to scriptural and hagiographic portraits is, as far as we can determine, novel — held as a designed-but-untested method until it has run. Nothing in this chapter changes any claim tier elsewhere in the corpus; it governs process, not doctrine.*

> Discussion Questions for Small Groups

**Before You Measure — Discussion Questions**

**1.** The chapter argues you can test a measuring instrument against "the documented dead" — figures like the Pharisee of Matthew 23 and Teresa of Calcutta — before pointing it at anyone living. Pick one biblical figure with a long recorded arc. What would an instrument have to notice to read that person rightly? What would it be likely to get wrong?

**2.** "The more a measure could wound, the smaller and later it deploys." Where else in church life — outside formal measurement — would that rule change how things are done?

**3.** The desolate-believer control requires that an instrument score a person in a fifty-year dark night *high*. Does that surprise you? What does it say about the difference between what a soul feels and what a soul is?

**4.** Krathwohl warned that people give the answers they believe the assessor wants. Where have you seen that dynamic in spiritual settings — and what made honesty safe, where it was safe?
