# Interrogating Reality: Naming What the Body Already Knows

*An IJH-native group protocol for surfacing and working a **corporate knot** — a reality a body already knows but will not say. Inspired by the practice Susan Scott calls "fierce conversations"; re-grounded here in scripture and in the IJH formation framework, and extended past conversation-management into formation: the corporate knot is not merely surfaced and managed, it is brought to the Lord and worked, the way an individual knot is. See the Attribution note at the end for what is borrowed and what is original.*

## What this protocol does

The Tool Map ([V2.Exp6](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-06-tool-map.md)) routes the **individual** knots — grief, shame, fear, anger, sin, idol — to their tools. This protocol addresses a knot that lives one level up: between people, in a body. A **corporate knot** is something the members of a group already perceive but have agreed, without ever deciding to, not to name — the integrity gap, the unspoken fear, the strategy no one believes in, the loss no one has grieved together. Left unspoken it does not dissolve; it converts into story (see the law below). This protocol gives a body a disciplined, safe way to name what it already knows, locate it at the right level, and bring it into the Lord's presence to be worked rather than merely managed.

**Candidate law it rests on (proposed):** *What a body will not say, it does not stop knowing — it converts into story.* Unspoken corporate reality metastasizes into narrative, assumption, and self-protective behavior. (Run through the Vol 1 law-catalog process before canonizing.)

## The corporate knot — and why power is the first problem

A corporate knot has a **level**: it may belong to this group, to a department, to the whole body, or it may actually be about the facilitator and how the gathering is being led. Naming it at the wrong level mis-fires the work (the four-context distinction below).

It also sits inside a **power structure**, and that is what most often defeats the work. People will not name a reality that indicts those with power over them while those people are in the room — the silence is not absence of the knot, it is the knot operating. IJH's answer is not to pretend hierarchy away but to submit the conversation to a deeper reality: in the body, all positional authority sits *under* one Head (1 Cor 12:12–27; Eph 1:22–23). Safety is built not by flattening the room but by every member — including those with power — placing the conversation under Christ's headship and agreeing that what is said here is said before Him. Where that submission is not yet real, the deep naming must wait or move to the dyad (fallback below).

## Safety preconditions (non-negotiable)

These are drawn directly from the failure analysis of a real engagement where a cold "what are the undiscussables here?" question produced only silence:

1. **The container is established and skill has been built first** ([V2.Exp8](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-08-container.md)). The deep naming is the *last* move in a sequence, never the opening one. A group is led into reflective listening and progressively more real exchange across earlier gatherings before it is asked to name a corporate knot.
2. **Bounded power-distance in the room.** As a working rule, no more than two levels of authority present for the deep naming; and never run the deep naming with the very power-holders the knot concerns in the room *unless* the submit-to-the-Head condition above is genuinely in place. Otherwise use the dyad fallback and resurface aggregated and de-identified.
3. **Voluntary.** Participation and disclosure are chosen, never required.
4. **A clean close is possible** (Step 5). Per V2.Exp8, work opened but not closed dissipates rather than integrates. Do not open what the gathering has no time or capacity to close.

## When to use it

- A body keeps "making up stories about X" — recurring sideways questions ("what's really going on with…?") signal a corporate knot.
- A group has built enough container and reflective-listening skill to hold a real exchange.
- Trust and safety themselves are the thing that needs to be examined, and the conditions above can be met.
- As the corporate complement to individual knot-work: a person's repeatedly re-forming knot turns out to be held in place by a corporate dynamic.

## When NOT to use it (the post-mortem, made into rules)

- **The power-holders the knot concerns are in the room and the submit-to-the-Head condition is not real.** This is the classic silence-producing setup. → dyad fallback.
- **The container and the skill have not been built.** Do not ask the deepest question cold or "off script" when the group has not been led there. (This was the single largest cause of the silence in the source engagement.)
- **No clean close is possible** in the time available.
- **The facilitator is the actual locus** and cannot hold non-defensiveness — get an outside facilitator.
- **A member is in acute individual crisis** — that is individual knot-work (the B12 / Tool Map track), not corporate work.

## The protocol

### Step 0 — Build the container and the skill (across prior gatherings)
Before any corporate knot is named, the body practices reflective listening in **trios**: one shares, a partner reflects back *what they heard* (not a response), then they switch. Earlier gatherings move from low-risk to more real material. A brief **one-to-one** with the facilitator before group sessions both prepares each person and gives the facilitator early read on where the knots and the risks are. Step 0 is not optional warm-up; it is the load-bearing precondition.

### Step 1 — Interrogate reality / ground truths
Each member names what is real *for them* about the area in view; partners reflect it back. The frame is the body seeing in part (1 Cor 13:9–12): no one member holds the whole, and the point is to assemble the parts, not to win. And before pressing what others need to hear, each member interrogates their *own* construct first — the version of reality their background, experience, and desire have built, which may differ from the truth (Prov 16:9) — so what is offered is held as *my drawn-out perception* (Prov 20:5), not the final word. *Facilitator framing:* "Each of us is holding one piece of what's true here. We're not deciding yet who's right — we're putting the pieces on the table so we can see the whole."

### Step 2 — Name it, and locate its level
When the container holds, the facilitator invites the corporate knot to be named plainly — the elephant named (Matt 18:15; 2 Sam 12). Then immediately **locate its level**, using the four-context test from the source engagement:
1. the facilitator / how this is being led;
2. *this group's* dynamics;
3. a department or sub-body's culture;
4. the whole body's culture.
Naming the wrong level mis-fires the work. *Facilitator framing:* "Now that it's named — whose is it? Is this ours in this room, or is it bigger than us, or is it actually about how I'm leading this?"

### Step 3 — Let silence do the heavy lifting
After the naming, **do not rush to fill the silence.** Silence is the body metabolizing what was said; the facilitator's discipline is to hold the container open and let it work (Ps 46:10; James 1:19). (In the source engagement the facilitator's own miss was being "taken aback" by silence and moving too fast to recover; this step exists to turn that lesson into practice.) *Facilitator framing:* "Let's stay here a moment. No one needs to fix this yet."

### Step 4 — Bring it to the Lord, not just into the room
This is where the protocol goes past conversation-management into formation. The named, located knot is brought before the Lord by the gathered body (Matt 18:20), inviting Him to speak truth into it — the corporate analog of how an individual knot is worked, where the Spirit, not the technique, is the agent of release. The body listens, tests what surfaces (V2.Exp7a discernment of voices), and receives. *Facilitator framing:* "We've named it and we've felt its weight. Now we bring it to the Lord together and ask Him what is true here, and what He is asking of us."

### Step 5 — Commitments, accountability, and a clean close
Each member names what they will do, with whom, and by when, and may invite the others to hold them accountable (the accountability move that closed the source engagement's strongest session). The work is then closed deliberately (V2.Exp8): name what was received, thank those who spoke into risk, and release the container. *Facilitator framing:* "What is each of us taking from this — what will you do, by when, and what do you want us to hold you to?"

### Fallback — when the group cannot hold it
If safety is insufficient (Step 2 produces only silence, or the power condition fails), **drop to the safe one-to-one.** Re-interview each member privately with two questions — *"why the silence?"* and *"what is the real knot here?"* — then resurface the findings to the body **aggregated and de-identified**, which often restores enough safety for Steps 3–5. (This is exactly the recovery that rescued the source engagement.)

## Companion / facilitator notes

- **The facilitator's non-defensiveness is the instrument.** If Step 2 locates the knot at level 1 (the facilitator), the whole protocol stands or falls on whether they can receive that without defending. Where they cannot, bring in an outside facilitator.
- **Power is a container variable, not a detail.** Re-read the source engagement's first lesson before every deployment: who is in the room changes what can be said.
- **Silence is the most-skipped discipline and the most important.** Practice it.
- **This is corporate knot-work, not group therapy.** The aim is the body hearing the Lord together about a shared reality, not catharsis.

## Materials
- A held space appropriate to group work; trio-capable seating.
- A simple way to capture commitments (Step 5).
- Optional: a one-to-one schedule for Step 0 / the fallback.

## Time
- **Step 0:** spread across several prior gatherings — not a single session.
- **A full naming session (Steps 1–5):** 90–150 minutes. Do not compress Step 3 (silence) or Step 5 (close).
- **Fallback 1:1 cycle:** ~30 minutes per member plus a resurfacing session.

## Certainty
**Reasonably Inferred.** The interpersonal/corporate effectiveness of structured reflective-listening + named-reality work is well attested in the practitioner source and in the facilitator's repeated field use; the *scriptural re-grounding* and the *Step 4 formation move* (bringing the corporate knot to the Lord) are the IJH-original contributions and are held as a working hypothesis for the FotH pilot to test (feeds the OT-6 collective-formation trail). The protocol is built around its known failure modes, which is the project's disconfirmability discipline applied at design time.

**FORMATION DOCUMENT CONNECTION:** locating and holding a corporate knot is a Stage-3+ corporate competency in MSFIG's small-group taxonomy — it is the move from *Information Sharing* into *Genuine Care* / *Hearing as Body*. A group that cannot yet name what it knows is not at a higher level; it is closed (the integration-not-replacement reading).
