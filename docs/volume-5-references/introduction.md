# Introduction

This volume is a companion reference to the four preceding volumes of the Laws of the Spirit (LotS) series. Volumes 1 through 4 draw on a wide range of scholars, theologians, scientists, spiritual directors, ministry practitioners, scripture figures, and personal witnesses in the course of developing and testing a framework for understanding how the spiritual world operates according to regular, discoverable laws. Volume 5 collects those sources in one place, organizes them alphabetically within five categories, and provides a full Turabian-format bibliographic citation along with a structured annotation for each entry.

It also includes the Formation Documents referenced throughout the first four volumes. These are companion papers that work alongside the investigation in Volumes 1–3, exploring the formation dimensions of the laws described there. The Theological Anthropology paper (TA) is the foundational one — it examines what scripture means by heart, spirit, soul, and mind, and argues that each is distinct and trainable; the three taxonomies that follow are built on its framework. The Heart Formation Theology paper (HFT) applies the Affective Taxonomy of Krathwohl, Bloom, and Masia as the framework for heart formation and proposes trust in scripture as the right affective test case for it — the tractable, focused target that lets us begin measuring what has previously been only observed. The Soul and Spirit Taxonomies paper (SST) proposes five-stage developmental taxonomies for both the soul and the spirit, each with explicit connections back to the structural laws in Volume 1. The Model of Spiritual Formation for Individuals and Small Groups (MSFIG) is the most comprehensive of the five, integrating all three inner taxonomies with Old Testament, New Testament, and Pauline formation theology, and extending them to individual, small-group, and church-wide application. The Formation Companion paper (FC) proposes the person who facilitates this formation in others as a synthesis role drawing on four established Christian spiritual care traditions, with a three-level developmental progression and a capstone competency of real-time mode-switching. Three further papers complete the original set — the Four Connects (4Cs), Measuring Spiritual Formation at Scale (MSF), and Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad (ATB). To these is now added a four-part sub-series, **the Hearing-God Papers**, which develops the affective-taxonomy core at publication length: it measures spiritual maturity in hearing God (MSM), prescribes its cultivation (CSM), and then works that cultivation out through the lower levels of internalization — Receiving, Responding, Valuing (CTG) — and the upper — Organization and Characterization (BFP). These papers do not replace this investigation — they run alongside it. The LotS asks how the spiritual laws work; the Formation Documents ask what it looks like, from the inside, to be formed by them. !!! note "Reading the Affective Taxonomy across the Formation Documents — integration, not replacement"

    The Affective Taxonomy as it appears in HFT, MSFIG, and the related Formation Documents is read across the LotS project under the **integration-not-replacement** framing developed pastorally in [V2.Exp7: Reading These Levels as Integration, Not Replacement](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-07-hearing-with-understanding.md#reading-these-levels-as-integration-not-replacement) and at the testing-instrument construct level in [Vol 4 §2: Reading the Affective Taxonomy as Integration, Not Replacement](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement). The five-stage progression is real — it names the order in which the kinds of engagement first become accessible — but the mature pattern is the held integration of all five rather than the replacement of earlier stages by later ones. A Level 5 person whose Level 1 Receiving has died is not at Level 5; they are rigid. The canonical Pharisee-as-stage-N-collapse diagnostic in V2.Exp7 spells this out at each level. This held-stages reading **originates in MSFIG**, which supplies the Pharisee-as-Stage-2-not-Stage-5 counter-example and explicitly frames progress as a *spiral, not a staircase* — apparent regression under suffering being disorientation, not a lost level.

    The same reading applies to MSFIG's five-level small-group taxonomy — the Affective Taxonomy applied to a group, which the LotS names Social Fellowship → Information Sharing → Genuine Care → Hearing as Body → Sent as Body (MSFIG's Compressed Reference Edition gives these as the collective levels Receiving → … → Characterization): a Hearing-as-Body group that has lost the Social Fellowship or Information Sharing capacity of an earlier level is not at the higher level; it is closed. The Formation Document PDFs listed below are not yet revised to reflect this reading — V2.Exp7 and Vol 4 §2 are the canonical sites for the framing, and HFT, SST, and TA should be read through that lens until a future revision pass (MSFIG already embeds it).

    This held-stages reading does not stand alone; it is independently corroborated by the cross-disciplinary developmental research this project engages structurally. The **transcend-and-include** principle common to Spiral Dynamics (Beck/Graves), *Leadership Agility* (Joiner), and Torbert's seven action-logics — three separate research programs — each finds that healthy development *carries its earlier stages forward* rather than discarding them, and that a "higher" stage which has lost a lower capacity is deformed rather than advanced. (See the Beck, Joiner, and Rooke-and-Torbert entries in [Part I](part-i-scholarly-and-academic-sources.md).) These secular models are engaged as corroboration of the developmental *form*, not as sources of doctrine.

!!! note "Reading spirit, soul, and heart across the Formation Documents — distinction of function, not substance"

    Where the Formation Documents speak of spirit, soul, and heart as distinct — most sharply in SST, which calls them "genuinely distinct ontological realities, not just functional labels" — the LotS reads that distinction as one of **function (job), not substance**: the terms name real, distinguishable jobs within one unified person, not separable parts. This is the careful position worked out in [V1.Exp2: My Spirit, Heart, Soul, and Body](../volume-1-laws-of-the-spirit/exploration-02-my-spirit-heart-soul-and.md) — "the difference … is one of job, not of substance." As with the held-stages reading above, the Formation Document PDFs are not yet revised to this framing; read them through the V1.Exp2 lens until a future revision pass.

The annotation format follows the structure used in my academic work: an Author section identifying who the person is or was; a Summary section describing the relevant work, idea, or contribution; and an Application section explaining specifically how this person’s work connects to the LotS project, with volume and exploration references where applicable. The goal is not an exhaustive academic literature review but a working reference document — one that a future researcher, a family member continuing this exploration, could use to locate and evaluate the sources that shaped this project.

Ministry tools with institutional rather than single-author origins are gathered in Part III.
