## What ATB Argues

The starting question is the Sower's question — *why does the same Word land in some hearts as hardened path, in others as rocky ground, in others as thorny soil, and in others as good soil that produces fruit thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold?* The Affective Taxonomy gives one answer: the variance is developmental. The Foundational Laws give another: the variance is structural. ATB proposes a third complementary answer, one that does not replace the first two but enriches them: **the variance is also relational.** The capacity of a heart to receive, to keep, and to bring to fruit the Word God plants in it is shaped — though not determined — by the relational history through which the person learned (or failed to learn) that closeness with a trustworthy other is safe.

Attachment theory — the body of research initiated by Bowlby and Ainsworth and extended by Hazan, Shaver, Mikulincer, Granqvist, and Kirkpatrick — names that relational history with precision. Ainsworth found three infant patterns (secure, anxious-ambivalent, avoidant); Main and Solomon later added a fourth, disorganized. Adult attachment, though, is measured on two dimensions, attachment anxiety and attachment avoidance, along which adults vary continuously and differently from one relationship to another. There is no adult trait called "disorganized." **ATB therefore keeps the four names only as pastoral vocabulary**: words for struggles people recognize in themselves, in some relationships and some seasons. No facilitator types, labels or records anyone by pattern, and the practices are offered to anyone who recognizes the struggle rather than assigned. In the paper, "disorganized" names a moment, not a person: the moment when closeness and fear arrive together and no strategy holds.

Patterns are tendencies, not destinies. Two pathways shape a person's relationship with God. By **socialized correspondence**, people with secure histories tend to take up a trusted parent's faith gradually and steadily. By **compensation**, some people whose human caregivers were unreliable find in God the secure base they lacked, often suddenly and in a season of distress. They are two doors into the same church, with two formation logics. Compensation is well established in the research. That it matures into durable **earned security** (a narrower technical term in the field) is a plausible hope with partial evidence, not yet a demonstrated finding, so the paper mostly speaks of *growing* security.

ATB integrates this body of research with the Reformed/evangelical/charismatic theology of the biblical triad of **faith, love, and hope** (1 Cor 13:13; 1 Thess 1:3; Col 1:4–5; Gal 5:6) and with the Fellowship of the Heart's TPM Whole-Person practice tradition. The paper has three structural moves: **descriptive** (naming the attachment patterns as pastoral vocabulary for struggles that color how a person receives the Word); **theological** (locating the gospel as the ground of growth toward felt security and naming the church as the relational ecology God ordinarily uses to do that work); and **operational** (proposing the FotH practices — TPM, practice cards, Whole-Person integration matrix — as the concrete formation work that operationalizes the theological claim).

## Before the Theory Had a Name

The church did not learn about attachment in 1969, and ATB v2 restores its older literature. Augustine's "our heart is restless until it rests in you" is a secure-base sentence. The Psalms are the grammar of a secure base: they run to God as refuge, go out from him into "a broad place," protest separation without leaving the relationship ("How long, O Lord?"), name compensation outright ("Though my father and mother forsake me, the Lord will take me in," Ps 27:10), and picture settled security in the weaned child resting with its mother (Ps 131:2). Scripture also speaks of God's care in a mother's voice (Isa 49:15; 66:13; Matt 23:37). These images do not rename the God the church confesses as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, but for a believer whose wound is the father, Father-language alone can bar the door the paper means to open. And Julian of Norwich, walled into one room in the fourteenth century, was shown that "in falling and rising we are ever preciously kept in one love."

## The Triadic Spiral

The triad operates as a mutually reinforcing cycle that ATB calls the **triadic spiral** — a grace-driven, relationally formed cycle that moves from encounter to enactment in four phases. *Phase 1:* God's initiating love, preached and sacramentally enacted and made felt by the Spirit, meets the person where they actually are; encountered love produces growing trust. *Phase 2:* Faith works through love (Gal 5:6); stabilized trust frees love-in-action. *Phase 3:* As love is practiced amid suffering, the Romans 5 sequence unfolds (suffering → perseverance → character → hope), and enacted love confirms hope. *Phase 4:* Eschatological hope enlarges the heart's capacity to risk loving again; strengthened hope generates fresh trust and deeper love. The spiral tightens with each turn.

Each attachment struggle tends to enter and resist the spiral in its own way. Where closeness feels uncertain, Phase 1 (encountered love) is often intense while Phase 2 resists (trust does not stabilize without outside reinforcement). Where closeness feels unsafe to need, Phase 1 itself is resisted (encountered love read as pressure), and embodied formation helps most (the Lord's Supper, structured small-group vulnerability, practices of receiving). Where closeness and fear arrive together, Phases 1 and 3 may be vivid while Phase 2 is skipped; predictable structures, trauma-informed care, and collaboration with a competent Christian counselor are usually essential. The spiral does not depend on any one phase succeeding all at once. **Grace is patient with the substrate**, and the Spirit's ordinary mode is incremental. But ordinary is not the only mode: the paper keeps **a door marked extraordinary** for the gift no practice schedules and no protocol earns, received with gratitude and discernment, never demanded, and never counted as failure when it does not come.

## The Sower Soils as Attachment Profiles

ATB's most direct contribution to the IJH framework is the proposal that the four Sower soils — already mapped in [V2.Exp1](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-01-heart-soil.md) to the five Affective Taxonomy stages — have a loose parallel in the attachment struggles, and the v2 table says how closely each cell fits. **Hard path ↔ avoidant closing-down** is a *plausible* fit, with the parable's own agent written back in: the evil one snatches the word away (Mark 4:15), and the enemy exploits what the closing-down leaves exposed ([V2.Exp2B](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-02b-adversarys-role.md)); the substrate never replaces him. **Rocky soil ↔ anxious** is the *tightest* fit, since the parable's own mechanism (quick joy, no root, falling away in tribulation) matches the anxious shape. **Thorny soil ↔ disorganized** is the *weakest*: the parable's thorns are cares and riches, rival loves rather than trauma, and a secure, wealthy man can be thoroughly thorny. **Good soil ↔ secure** is *loose*, by the paper's own evidence: some believers grow in godliness and service for decades while still carrying attachment wounds, which is good soil without a secure pattern. The claim is not that these mappings are one-to-one or causal. The table offers vocabulary for noticing, never a tool for classifying anyone, and it is never read aloud over a person: an attachment wound is usually something done to someone, and the soils' language of failure must not be laid on it.

Three operational implications follow, held with the same caution. (1) The Level-2-to-Level-3 transition — already identified in V2.Exp1 as the most critical and challenging threshold in the entire Affective Taxonomy — is where attachment work may often matter most. (2) The same heart contains multiple soil conditions in different regions; it may also be secure in one relationship and anxious or guarded in another. A person may be invited to notice that for themselves; no one else records it. (3) The Foundational Laws are experienced differently through different attachment struggles — Drawing-Near Reciprocity (V1 FL-VII) feels different where closeness is uncertain than where it is unsafe to need, and Confession and Restoration (V1 FL-II) feels different where closeness and fear arrive together than where trust comes easily. The laws are universal; the relational pathway by which a particular heart engages them varies.

!!! note "Reading attachment across ATB — integration, not replacement"

    The integration-not-replacement reading developed in the LotS project for the Affective Taxonomy ([V2.Exp7](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-07-hearing-with-understanding.md#reading-these-levels-as-integration-not-replacement); [Vol 4 §2](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement)) extends naturally to attachment. **Mature attachment is not "always secure." It is a secure baseline that still holds two older sensitivities open:** anxious sensitivity (alertness to the loss of relational closeness) and avoidant discernment (the capacity to recognize when intimacy is being pressed before trust has been established). Disorganization is not a third sensitivity to keep: in the research it names a breakdown, and the paper no longer dresses it as a virtue. What the tradition honors instead, the capacity to hold longing and fear in the same prayer as the lament psalms do, is a fruit of healing and of being held. A believer whose secure baseline has eclipsed both sensitivities is not mature — they are **insulated**, the relational analog of the Stage-5 Pharisee diagnosed in V2.Exp7. ATB extends the held-tensions discipline (developed pastorally in the [V1 Supplemental on Held Tensions and Shadow Pairs](../volume-1-laws-of-the-spirit/supplemental-held-tensions-and-shadow-pairs.md) and grounded in the Cameron-and-Quinn framework cited in [Part I](part-i-scholarly-and-academic-sources.md)) to the attachment dimension, as a question for a person's own self-examination, not a check one person runs on another.

## Operational Practices: The FotH Whole-Person Integration

ATB's operational core is the work the Fellowship of the Heart community have developed over many years, expressed most recently in the Warrior Monk × Attachment-Informed Christian / TPM packet. Four components map directly onto the theological claims of the paper: **TPM (Transformation Prayer Ministry) as attachment-aware mind renewal** — the EMOTION → MEMORY → BELIEF → TRUTH → TRANSFORMATION MAP is operationally a Spirit-led practice for surfacing the *belief* layer of attachment-derived working models and presenting it to the Lord for re-patterning; **practice cards** — the Reassurance Cooldown (for the urge to ask again), the Receiving Drill (for the difficulty of receiving), and the Exit/Return Plan (for being overwhelmed), each small, repeatable and scripturally anchored, each offered to anyone who recognizes the struggle and assigned to no one; **the Whole-Person Integration matrix** grounded in Willard's anthropology and IJH's [TA Formation Document](source-pdfs/ta-theological-anthropology.pdf){: .pdf-popup data-pdf-label="TA — Theological Anthropology" }; and **group covenant, safety practices, and trauma-informed structure** that together form the reliable, boundaried, attuned community in which security can grow.

In v2 grace is written into the cards themselves. Each says what happens when it fails: whoever asks again anyway has not lost God; whoever deflects a blessing still received it; return to the room is welcome and never required, and a person may leave the work without spiritual penalty. Every card carries one watermark: *this practice cannot make you more kept than you already are.*

Change comes more through surroundings than willpower, so the paper's pastoral emphasis is on **shaping the habitat for everyone** rather than sorting individuals: leaders and groups that are consistent, available, responsive, reliable and predictable; teaching about dry seasons before the first one comes; and more than one way into a group (the freedom to pass, partners, one-on-one contact with a companion) for those who find the circle itself the hard part. One caution travels with this. Matching love to what others return may describe how insecure hearts protect themselves, but it is not the gospel's rule: God loves first (Rom 5:8), and the church is a family one does not choose.

## A Gate Before Any Group Program

ATB brings trauma histories into the room by design, so its safety must be built, not assumed. The paper's safety *practices* are real. The *architecture* around them does not yet exist: screening, a competence floor and supervision for facilitators, named referral triggers and who decides, a protocol for when prayer ministry surfaces more than a lay group can hold, data governance with separate consent, and a complaint path that works when the leader is the rupture. Designing it is reserved for the IJH Council's Character-Prerequisites pass. ATB names the gate and does not design it. Until the Council has passed it, the practices are not run as a structured group program and nothing from them is gathered for research; the ordinary pastoral care a church already gives is unaffected.

## Where the ATB Framework Is Used

The ATB framework runs through the rest of the project as the **relational-substrate complement** to the developmental account of formation:

- **Vol 1, Exp. 8 — *Prayer as Resonance.*** The resonance framing has a creaturely correlate in attachment theory's account of affect co-regulation; ATB strengthens the V1.Exp8 case by supplying a creaturely substrate for the resonance claim.
- **Vol 1 Foundational Laws** (FL-II Confession and Restoration; FL-V Reciprocal Forgiveness; FL-VII Drawing Near Reciprocity; FL-X Ask-Seek-Knock; FL-XXII Endurance-Hope). Each operates through relational substrates that attachment shapes. ATB does not replace the laws; it names the substrate on which they operate.
- **Vol 2, Exp. 1 — *Heart Soil.*** The Sower-soils diagnostic gains a loose attachment parallel as an enrichment of the existing Affective-Taxonomy mapping, with each cell's fit marked.
- **Vol 2, Exp. 2B — *The Adversary's Role.*** Supplies the hard-path correction: the parable names Satan at the point of reception, and the substrate never replaces him.
- **Vol 2, Exp. 8 — *Container.*** The therapeutic-container chapter is operationally the attachment-formative site of the FotH practice.
- **Vol 3, Exp. 4 — *Spiritual Distance.*** ATB strengthens the case that the Affective Taxonomy level is one valid measurement of Truth-axis spiritual distance, and adds that the integration check (V4 §2) properly includes the attachment-substrate check.
- **Vol 3 Open Trails.** The Attachment-Substrate Measurement Extension is a named research trail. Under v2 it would use validated, dimensional self-report completed by the person, never facilitator observation of individuals, and it waits on the Council's safeguarding pass. The v1 proposal to train facilitators to recognize and record patterns is struck.
- **Vol 5 Formation Documents.** ATB sits closest to FC (Formation Companion) in that both name *how* the formation work happens; ATB adds the attachment substrate on which FC's relational competencies operate.

## A Parallel Tradition: The Life Model

*Added November 2026. This section is not part of the ATB paper; it notes a neighbouring tradition the paper does not cite.*

The **Life Model**, developed by Jim Wilder and colleagues at Shepherd's House (*The Life Model: Living from the Heart Jesus Gave You*; later *Joy Starts Here*, *The Other Half of Church*, and *Renovated* with Dallas Willard), works the same ground from the side of relational neuroscience. It joins attachment research, especially Allan Schore's right-brain work, with a Christian account of maturity. Three of its claims run parallel to ATB's. **Joy is relational:** "someone is glad to be with me" is the ground of secure identity, which is ATB's growing security described from within. **Knowing is not experiencing:** truth known but not met in relationship often does not heal, which is ATB's relational answer to the Sower's question. **The community is the healing place:** a joyful identity group restores what was missing, as ATB names the church the relational ecology God ordinarily uses.

The Life Model adds two things ATB does not have. The first is a set of small, teachable **relational skills**, among them *returning to joy* from distress, *quieting*, and *appreciation*, that a room can practise together; they sit naturally beside ATB's practice cards. The second is a **developmental map of maturity** in five stages (infant, child, adult, parent, elder), with gaps where a stage was never completed. That map measures relational capacity rather than the internalization of the Word, so it complements the Affective Taxonomy rather than duplicating it.

Two cautions travel with it. Its brain language ("right brain," "relational circuits") renders real research in popular form and is best cited as a model, not as settled mechanism. And its evidence base is still young: the one outcome study the project has found is a small 2025 pilot (Mouer, Liberty University: fourteen adults, six weekly sessions, no control group) reporting lower anxiety and higher joy that held at one month. A crosswalk of the Life Model against the Affective Taxonomy and ATB's soil-attachment mapping is held in reserve in the [Research Register](../volume-6-governance/research-register.md).

## The Honest Conclusion

Hope, finally, does not rest on achieved secure attachment. It rests on the God who promises *"I will never leave you nor forsake you"* (Heb 13:5), and who will not forget us even if a nursing mother could forget her child (Isa 49:15). That promise transcends the substrate; it transforms the substrate; in the resurrection it will heal the substrate completely. Until then, the Spirit's patient work continues, sometimes by the ordinary means and sometimes by a door only God can open, and the church — ordered by the Word, enlivened by the Spirit, structured with the consistency, attunement, and reliability in which security can grow — bears witness, sometimes haltingly, always graciously, to the love that casts out fear.
