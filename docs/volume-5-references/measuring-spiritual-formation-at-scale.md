# Measuring Spiritual Formation at Scale

The full paper — *Measuring Spiritual Maturity in Hearing God: An Affective Domain Taxonomy for Individual, Group, and Institutional Internalization* — is the seventh Formation Document and is available here [(pdf)](source-pdfs/msf-measuring-spiritual-formation-at-scale.pdf){: .pdf-popup data-pdf-label="MSF — Measuring Spiritual Formation at Scale" }. The summary below names what the paper does, why it matters to the LotS project, and how its framework is used across the volumes.

## What MSF Argues

Churches have spent three decades building instruments to measure spiritual maturity — Natural Church Development, REVEAL, the Discipleship Pathway Assessment, the Faith Maturity Scale, the Spiritual Assessment Inventory, Fowler's Stages of Faith — and no consensus has emerged on which one works. MSF argues that the failure is not methodological but **taxonomic**: existing instruments conflate different *levels* of affective internalization, and most of them operate only at the bottom two levels (Receiving and Responding) while spiritual maturity, as the New Testament names it, requires measurement at the top three (Valuing, Organization, Characterization).

The diagnostic framework is Krathwohl, Bloom, and Masia's *Taxonomy of Educational Objectives: Affective Domain* (1964), adapted for spiritual formation by Paul Bramer (2007). MSF treats Krathwohl's five levels as a diagnostic ruler for the construct of "spiritual maturity in hearing God," and uses that ruler to explain why the field has been unable to agree: each instrument has a valid operating range, but no instrument covers the full spectrum, and none of the popular tools distinguishes a person who performs spiritual behaviors out of social compliance from one whose character has been transformed by the indwelling Spirit. Jesus named this exact problem with the Pharisees.

## The Five Scales

The paper extends the taxonomy across **five organizational scales** — individual, small group, congregation, denomination, and the universal church confessed in the Nicene Creed as "one holy, catholic, and apostolic." The same five-level internalization dynamics show up at every scale: a denomination operating at Level 2 (programmatically active, metrically obsessed, formationally shallow) constrains every church inside it; a small group whose discernment has become real does so by the same path a person does. The Parable of the Sower — path, rocky, thorny, good — maps onto the failure modes at each level: failed Receiving, Responding without Valuing, Valuing without Organization, Organization through Characterization. The Sower diagnostic operates at every scale because soils are not only individual.

## Where Each Tradition Plateaus

One of the paper's most pastorally direct findings is that **no single theological tradition produces full-spectrum maturity.** Reformed theology supplies the content of what God says (and characteristically plateaus at Level 2). Evangelical theology supplies the urgency of response (Level 2–3 transition). Charismatic theology supplies the immediacy of the Spirit's ongoing communication (Level 3). Mystical theology supplies the depth of interior transformation that makes the other three characterological rather than performative (Level 3–4). Each tradition has a characteristic ceiling, and a church drawing on only one tradition will systematically plateau at that tradition's level. The implication is not syncretism but **catholicity** — being formed by the whole church, not by one tradition's slice.

## The TFT Connection

Transcendental Field Theory, developed in Vol 3 and given a complete formulation in MSF's appendix, supplies the underlying mechanism for why the taxonomy works as it does. The five internalization levels correspond to increasing **coupling strength** between the Truth, Goodness, and Beauty fields: from zero coupling (path soil, Level 1) through externally sustained coupling (Level 2) to self-sustaining coupling (Level 3) to full integration (Level 4) to characterological equilibrium (Level 5). Sanctification is the Spirit's progressive restoration of proper field coupling toward the Glory attractor. Krathwohl's taxonomy describes the surface phenomenon; TFT names the structure underneath.

!!! note "Reading the Affective Taxonomy across MSF — integration, not replacement"

    MSF was written with the Affective Taxonomy presented in its original five-stage progression register (Receiving → Responding → Valuing → Organization → Characterization). Across the LotS project the AT is now read as **integration, not replacement** — the five stages name the order in which the kinds of engagement first become accessible, but the mature pattern is the **held simultaneity of all five** rather than the replacement of earlier by later. A Level 5 person whose Level 1 Receiving has died is not at Level 5; they are rigid. The Pharisee is the canonical case of stage-N collapse, not a high-stage achievement.

    The pastoral exposition lives at [V2.Exp7: Reading These Levels as Integration, Not Replacement](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-07-hearing-with-understanding.md#reading-these-levels-as-integration-not-replacement); the construct-level exposition (for testing-instrument design) lives at [Vol 4 §2: Reading the Affective Taxonomy as Integration, Not Replacement](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement). MSF stands as the original articulation of the measurement framework and should be read through that lens; it is not on the revision path.

## Where the MSF Framework Is Used

The MSF framework runs through the rest of the project as the **measurement backbone** for any claim that someone, or some group, has been formed:

- **Vol 2, Exp. 7 — *Hearing with Understanding.*** The pastoral home of the five-level taxonomy applied to hearing and obeying God, with the integration reading and the Pharisee-collapse diagnostic at each stage.
- **Vol 3, Exp. 4 — *Spiritual Distance.*** The Affective Taxonomy is named as the measurement protocol for the Truth-axis of spiritual distance, with the calibration that a measured AT level is a proxy for distance only when paired with the integration check.
- **Vol 4 — *The Testing Framework.*** The Individual and Group Spiritual-Integration Profiles (SIP-Individual, Group SIP) operationalize the integration check directly; §2 carries the construct-level exposition of the reframe; §3 and §4 carry the calibrations.
- **Vol 5 — *References* introduction.** The standing notice that MSF, HFT, MSFIG, SST, TA, and FC are read across the project under the integration framing.

## The Honest Conclusion

The honest conclusion of MSF, which the LotS project carries forward without softening, is that spiritual maturity at its deepest levels may be **partially opaque to measurement by design.** "The LORD sees not as man sees" (1 Sam 16:7). The taxonomy does not solve the measurement problem; it diagnoses why the problem exists, names what adequate measurement would require at each level and scale, and identifies the specific barriers that prevent existing instruments from capturing the full depth of spiritual transformation. We measure what we can measure, name what we cannot, and trust the One who searches hearts to know what our instruments never will.
