# Part I: Scholarly and Academic Sources

## Barna, George
**Author: **George Barna is a widely cited Christian researcher, professor at Arizona Christian University, and founding director of the Barna Research Group and later the Cultural Research Center at Arizona Christian University. He has spent decades conducting empirical studies on the beliefs, practices, and worldviews of American Christians.

**Summary: **In this book Barna examines the results of large-scale survey research into how holding a biblical worldview — defined by affirmative answers to seven foundational questions — produces measurably different life outcomes. He found that only approximately six percent of self-identified born-again Christians answer all seven questions affirmatively, and that this group shows dramatically lower rates of divorce, substance abuse, and incarceration compared to both nominal Christians and non-believers. Barna argues that transformed behavior follows transformed thinking, and that the goal of Christian formation is producing this integrated biblical worldview.

**Application: **The LotS project uses Barna’s biblical worldview framework as the Stage 5 endpoint descriptor of the Affective Taxonomy developmental arc. In Vol 2, Exp. 7 and Vol 4, Section 3, the fully formed Stage 5 Characterization level — in which a person’s values, desires, and behaviors are integrated around scripture — is explicitly described as Barna’s (2003) biblical worldview “fully embodied.” This provides a concrete, research-grounded definition of the formation destination that the entire LotS measurement protocol is oriented toward.

## Bloom, Benjamin S.
**Author: **Benjamin S. Bloom (1913–1999) was an American educational psychologist at the University of Chicago who led the committee that produced the landmark Taxonomy of Educational Objectives in 1956. His work fundamentally restructured how educators think about learning by distinguishing between cognitive, affective, and psychomotor domains. The cognitive taxonomy was revised in 2001 by a group of his former colleagues.

**Summary: **Bloom and his collaborators developed three parallel educational taxonomies — cognitive (1956, revised 2001), affective (Krathwohl, Bloom, and Masia, 1964), and psychomotor (Simpson, 1966) — that together describe the full range of human learning development. The cognitive taxonomy, from Knowledge through Evaluation (revised: Remember through Create), has been the most widely used. The affective taxonomy, which Bloom co-authored, became foundational to the LotS project’s developmental framework.

**Application: **Bloom’s three taxonomies are cited in Vol 3 as expanding the LotS project’s scope considerably, providing a research-grounded framework for thinking about formation across all three human domains. Vol 4 references doctoral research applying all three of Bloom’s taxonomies to spiritual formation practice. The cognitive taxonomy informs the LotS project’s understanding of how scriptural knowledge must ultimately be translated into affective and behavioral transformation — a limitation of purely cognitive engagement with scripture.

## Bowlby, John
**Author: **John Bowlby (1907–1990) was an English psychiatrist and psychoanalyst whose clinical observations of children separated from caregivers during and after World War II led him to formulate attachment theory — the most widely accepted contemporary account of how early caregiving experiences shape relational expectation across the lifespan. He served as deputy director of the Tavistock Clinic in London and authored the three-volume *Attachment and Loss* trilogy (1969–1980). His collaboration with Mary Ainsworth on naturalistic observation and the Strange Situation paradigm produced the empirical foundation that contemporary attachment research still builds on.

**Summary: **Bowlby's central claim, developed across his major works (especially *Attachment and Loss, Vol. 1: Attachment*, 1969/1982, and *A Secure Base*, 1988), is that human infants possess an innate behavioral system whose function is to maintain proximity to a primary caregiver under conditions of threat or distress. The quality of that early caregiving experience produces durable *internal working models* of self, others, and the relationship between them — models that shape emotion regulation, helpseeking, intimacy, and caregiving across the lifespan. The four standard attachment patterns identified by Ainsworth's empirical work — secure, anxious, avoidant, and disorganized — name the characteristic strategies infants develop in response to differently calibrated caregiving environments. Bowlby developed the framework in conscious dialogue with ethology, control-systems theory, and cognitive psychology, marking a decisive break with earlier psychoanalytic drive-theory accounts.

**Application: **Bowlby is the foundational author cited throughout the [eighth Formation Document, ATB — Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad](attachment-theory-and-the-biblical-triad.md). His framework supplies the creaturely-substrate account that ATB integrates with the Reformed/evangelical/charismatic theology of faith, love, and hope, and that the LotS project uses to name the relational dynamics underlying the Sower-soil variances (V2.Exp1), the Prayer-as-Resonance phenomenon (V1.Exp8), and the formation work the Fellowship of the Heart practice tradition operationalizes through TPM and the attachment-style practice cards. The future SIP-Individual and Group SIP extensions named in Vol 3 Open Trails would adapt attachment-pattern dimensions to the LotS testing framework.

## Brueggemann, Walter
**Author: **Walter Brueggemann (b. 1933) is William Marcellus McPheeters Professor Emeritus of Old Testament at Columbia Theological Seminary and one of the most influential Old Testament theologians of the twentieth century. He is particularly known for his work on the Psalms, on prophetic imagination, and on the relationship between lamentation and spiritual formation.

**Summary: **Brueggemann’s framework for reading the Psalms identifies three movements: orientation (settled confidence in God’s order), disorientation (the experience of loss, suffering, or divine hiddenness), and new orientation (a reordering of life around a deeper trust forged through the disorientation). This framework treats suffering and lament not as interruptions to spiritual growth but as its primary engine. The disorientation phase, when navigated with honest prayer addressed to God, functions as a formation crucible.

**Application: **The LotS project draws on Brueggemann extensively across Vols 1–3 to ground the claim that trials and suffering are not obstacles to formation but its primary catalyst (LotS claim LotS-F3). Vol 2 uses Brueggemann’s disorientation framework to explain why the suffering exploration in the Training Plan section belongs at the center of the formation model rather than at its edges. Vol 3 formalizes Brueggemann’s disorientation phase as a formation engine driving stage transitions in the Affective Taxonomy. Vol 4 cites Brueggemann alongside posttraumatic growth research as supporting evidence for the trial-as-formation-catalyst claim.

## Cameron, Kim S., and Robert E. Quinn
**Author: **Kim S. Cameron is the William Russell Kelly Professor of Management and Organizations at the University of Michigan's Ross School of Business and a co-founder of the Center for Positive Organizational Scholarship. Robert E. Quinn is the Margaret Elliott Tracy Collegiate Professor Emeritus at the same school and the originator of the Competing Values Framework (with John Rohrbaugh, 1981). Together they are the most widely cited contemporary researchers on the relationship between organizational culture, leadership, and value creation, with Cameron's positive-organizational-scholarship work and Quinn's deep-change writing each independently shaping a generation of management practice.

**Summary: **The Competing Values Framework (CVF) emerged from Quinn and Rohrbaugh's 1981 empirical question: of the dozens of criteria researchers had proposed for "organizational effectiveness," which ones cohere and which ones contradict? Their finding, replicated many times since, was that the criteria sort onto two underlying dimensions — internal vs. external focus, and flexibility vs. control — yielding four quadrants (Clan/collaborate, Adhocracy/create, Hierarchy/control, Market/compete). Each quadrant names a coherent set of operating values; the four sets sit in **diagonal opposition** (Clan vs. Market, Adhocracy vs. Hierarchy). The framework's central claim is not "here are four kinds of culture" but something structurally deeper: **organizational health is the disciplined holding of paired diagonal tensions, and pathology lives in collapsing the tension toward one pole.** A pure-Hierarchy organization deforms into rigidity; a pure-Adhocracy one into chaos; a pure-Clan one into insularity; a pure-Market one into burnout. The framework's diagnostic power comes from naming, for each pole, the specific failure mode that follows from over-investing in it.

**Application: **Cameron and Quinn's work supplies the **structural lens** for the LotS project's held-tensions reading of the Foundational Laws, developed in [Vol 1's supplemental on Held Tensions and Shadow Pairs](../volume-1-laws-of-the-spirit/supplemental-held-tensions-and-shadow-pairs.md). The author's parallel consulting methodology — the Balanced Scorecard Process (BSCP), built over more than two decades — operationalized the same diagonal-tension discipline in client engagements, and the spiritual-formation reading that runs through Vols 1–4 emerged from carrying that operational practice into the scriptural texts long enough to see the same structure already present in them. The integration-not-replacement reading of the Affective Taxonomy ([V2.Exp7](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-07-hearing-with-understanding.md#reading-these-levels-as-integration-not-replacement) pastoral, [Vol 4 §2](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement) construct-level, [Vol 5 introduction](introduction.md) cross-document) is itself an instance of the CVF discipline applied to a developmental taxonomy: the five stages are not a ladder but a set of capacities to be held in productive tension, and a "Stage 5" person whose Stage 1 Receiving has died is the canonical case of collapsing toward a single pole. Vol 4's SIP-Individual and Group SIP further adapt the **structural pattern** of Cameron and Quinn's Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (the OCAI, introduced in *Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture*) — forced-choice 100-point distribution across multiple value options, scored twice (current and preferred) — applied to the AT's five stages across six personal formation contexts. The specific OCAI items and CVF quadrant content are copyrighted; only the structural pattern is in the public domain and adapted.

## Cameron, Kim S., and Robert E. Quinn — Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture
**Author: **See Cameron, Kim S., and Robert E. Quinn above for full author biography.

**Summary: ***Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture: Based on the Competing Values Framework* (Jossey-Bass, 1st ed. 1999; 2nd ed. 2006; 3rd ed. 2011) is Cameron and Quinn's headline applied-CVF book and the most widely used CVF instrument source in the field. It introduces the **Organizational Culture Assessment Instrument (OCAI)** — a six-dimension self-report measure (Dominant Characteristics, Organizational Leadership, Management of Employees, Organization Glue, Strategic Emphases, Criteria of Success), each dimension scored by allocating 100 points across four quadrant-mapped statements, with the full instrument administered twice: once for the organization's **current** state and once for its **preferred** state in (typically) five years. The gap between current and preferred profiles is the diagnostic and the change agenda. The book also presents a structured organizational-change methodology that uses the OCAI as both the change-readiness baseline and the post-intervention validation instrument.

**Application: **The SIP-Individual and Group SIP in [Vol 4 §3](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-3-individual-spiritual-formation-testing-protocol.md) and §4 explicitly adapt the OCAI's **structural pattern** — not its content — for spiritual formation measurement. The forced-choice 100-point distribution is preserved because it prevents the response-set inflation (everything-is-important checking) that plagues conventional Likert spiritual-formation instruments; the current/preferred dual rating is preserved because the gap is the actionable signal in both registers. In the spiritual-formation adaptation, the current/preferred gap operates as the operational form of the integration-not-replacement check: the preferred profile shows where someone believes they should be, the current profile shows where they actually live, and the divergence between them surfaces both growth direction and the specific stage-N collapse pattern named in V2.Exp7's Pharisee diagnostic.

## Cameron, Kim S., Robert E. Quinn, Jeff DeGraff, and Anjan V. Thakor — Competing Values Leadership
**Author: **See Cameron, Kim S., and Robert E. Quinn above for full Cameron and Quinn biographies. The 2006 volume adds co-authors Jeff DeGraff (Ross School of Business, University of Michigan; founder of Innovatrium and originator of the "Innovation Genome" extension of the CVF) and Anjan V. Thakor (John E. Simon Professor of Finance at the Olin Business School, Washington University in St. Louis; widely cited researcher on corporate culture, governance, and the economics of value creation).

**Summary: ***Competing Values Leadership: Creating Value in Organizations* (Edward Elgar, 1st ed. 2006; 2nd ed. 2014) is the full **leadership and value-creation theory** built on the CVF. Where *Diagnosing and Changing Organizational Culture* gives the framework its applied diagnostic instrument, this book gives it its theoretical reach: each of the four CVF quadrants generates a distinct kind of value an organization can create — Collaborate produces human-capital value, Create produces innovation value, Control produces process value, Compete produces market value — and genuinely effective leadership requires the disciplined capacity to operate across all four quadrants while maintaining the productive tensions between them. Leaders who plateau in a single quadrant, even a "good" one, produce organizations that are characteristically deformed in the diagonal-opposite direction: a pure-Control leader builds a rigid organization that cannot innovate; a pure-Create leader builds a chaotic one that cannot ship; a pure-Collaborate leader builds an insular one that loses the market; a pure-Compete leader builds a burned-out one that cannot hold its people. The book's deepest move is to name **leadership maturity as the held capacity for all four quadrants simultaneously**, not the perfection of any one.

**Application: **This book supplies the structural template for the LotS project's most pastorally direct claim about formation traditions: **no single theological tradition produces full-spectrum maturity.** The tradition-plateau analysis developed in [MSF: Where Each Tradition Plateaus](measuring-spiritual-formation-at-scale.md#where-each-tradition-plateaus) — Reformed at Level 2, Evangelical at the Level 2–3 transition, Charismatic at Level 3, Mystical at Level 3–4 — is structurally the same observation Cameron and Quinn make about leadership styles plateauing at single quadrants. The implication in both registers is the same: the answer is not syncretism but the disciplined catholicity of being formed by the whole — by all four quadrants in organizational leadership, by all five traditions and stages in spiritual formation. The held-tensions supplemental's central claim — that health is "the disciplined holding of paired tensions, with each tension contributing its real good without being allowed to suppress the other" — is the spiritual-formation translation of the leadership claim this book makes for organizations.

## Einstein, Albert
**Author: **Albert Einstein (1879–1955) was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed the special and general theories of relativity, transforming the foundations of physics and demonstrating that Newtonian mechanics, while accurate within certain limits, is a special case of a more general framework. He is widely regarded as the most influential physicist of the twentieth century.

**Summary: **Einstein’s work demonstrated that Newtonian mechanics — which had been the complete and unchallenged description of physical reality for over two hundred years — was not wrong but incomplete. From inside the Newtonian frame, relativistic effects appear impossible or anomalous. From inside the Einsteinian frame, Newtonian mechanics is seen as a valid approximation at low velocities. This expansion of framework without discarding the prior framework is the key conceptual move.

**Application: **The LotS project uses Einstein’s relationship to Newton as a structural analogy in Vol 1’s introduction to explain how new explorations may refine or expand the working axioms without discarding them. The author explicitly models the LotS epistemological stance on this pattern: the four foundational axioms are held at “Clearly Taught certainty” with the explicit acknowledgment that further exploration may refine the general framework just as Einstein refined Newton. This analogy appears in the introduction to Vol 1 and shapes the project’s approach to certainty ratings throughout.

## Feynman, Richard P.
**Author: **Richard P. Feynman (1918–1988) was an American theoretical physicist at the California Institute of Technology, Nobel Laureate in Physics (1965), and one of the most gifted scientific communicators of the twentieth century. His Messenger Lectures at Cornell, published as *The Character of Physical Laws*, addressed fundamental questions about why mathematics so precisely describes the physical universe.

**Summary: **In this work Feynman raises what he calls the ‘unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics’ in describing physical reality — a phrase originated by physicist Eugene Wigner — and candidly admits he does not know the full answer. He explores how physical laws are discovered, how they are structured, and what it means for mathematics to be the language of nature. This question — why does mathematical structure map so precisely onto physical reality? — lies at the heart of Vol 3’s governing premise.

**Application: **Vol 3, Exp. 1 opens with a direct citation of Feynman’s question about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics. The author proposes a theological answer: mathematics describes the physical world so accurately because the physical world was spoken into existence by the Logos, the eternal Word, and the mathematical structure of creation is a trace of the structure of the Logos. If that is correct, the spiritual world — which is more fundamental than the physical world — should have mathematical structure deeper than what physics has yet found. This is the governing premise of Vol 3’s entire quantitative program.

## Friesen, Garry
**Author: **Garry Friesen is a theologian and professor whose 1980 work challenged what he called the ‘traditional view’ of divine guidance — the idea that God has a specific individual will for each decision in a believer’s life which must be discerned before acting. Friesen argued instead for a ‘wisdom view’ in which God’s moral will is revealed in scripture, and within the bounds of that moral will believers are free to make wise decisions without requiring a specific divine communication for each choice.

**Summary: **The Friesen position represents the more rationalist end of a spectrum in evangelical theology about how God guides believers. His view has been influential in Reformed evangelical circles and stands in significant tension with the LotS project’s emphasis on active, real-time hearing of God’s voice as the primary formation mechanism. The debate between Friesen’s position and the position represented by Dallas Willard (and the LotS project) is one of the significant unresolved tensions in evangelical spirituality.

**Application: **The Willard-Friesen debate is explicitly referenced in the LotS Formation Documents (MSFIG) as a key structural claim that requires resolution: the LotS project aligns with Willard’s position that God speaks specifically and personally, while acknowledging Friesen’s concern about subjectivism. The Obedience Channel law (Vol 1, Exp. 6) — which states that obedience to prior revelation opens the channel for further revelation — presupposes the Willardian position. Vol 2 notes this debate throughout the formation document connections.

## Galilei, Galileo
**Author: **Galileo Galilei (1564–1642) was an Italian mathematician, physicist, and astronomer widely regarded as the father of modern observational science. His foundational contributions to kinematics — the description of motion without reference to its causes — provided the empirical and mathematical foundation on which Newton later built his dynamics.

**Summary: **The relationship between Galileo’s kinematics and Newton’s dynamics is the specific analogy invoked in the LotS volumes. Galileo described how objects move; Newton explained why they move by introducing force, mass, and acceleration. Newton’s dynamic laws cannot be defined without Galileo’s kinematic framework beneath them. This hierarchical relationship between descriptive and explanatory frameworks is directly relevant to Vol 3’s program.

**Application: **Vol 3, Exp. 3 (Spiritual Force: The Analytical Core) uses the Galileo-Newton relationship as a structural analogy for the LotS project’s own two-level program: Vols 1 and 2 described the kinematics of spiritual formation (how things move), while Vol 3 attempts to build the dynamics (why they move, with what force, at what rate). The author cites this analogy as evidence that the Vol 3 quantitative program is ambitious but not structurally unprecedented.

## Granqvist, Pehr
**Author: **Pehr Granqvist is Professor of Psychology at Stockholm University and the leading contemporary researcher on the application of attachment theory to religion and spirituality. Building on Lee Kirkpatrick's foundational work, Granqvist has produced three decades of empirical research and theoretical synthesis on how attachment history shapes religious experience, God-images, conversion, and the formation of religious community.

**Summary: ***Attachment in Religion and Spirituality: A Wider View* (Guilford Press, 2020) is the most comprehensive single-volume treatment of the field. Granqvist synthesizes diverse strands of theory and research, examining the connections between early caregiving experiences, attachment patterns, and individual differences in religious cognition, experience, and behavior. He analyzes the function of a deity as an attachment figure, how attachment facilitates the intergenerational transmission of religion, and how the attachment perspective illuminates mystical experiences, connections between religion and mental health, and cultural differences between more and less religious societies. Of particular importance to the LotS project, Granqvist's research documents that "earned security" through religious experience is empirically real — people whose childhood attachment was insecure can develop secure attachment to God through community, healing prayer, sustained Christian relationships, and disciplined formation practice.

**Application: **Granqvist is cited throughout the [eighth Formation Document, ATB — Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad](attachment-theory-and-the-biblical-triad.md) for the empirical foundation of the earned-security claim that ATB locates the gospel as the source of, and the church as the relational ecology God ordinarily uses to do. His correspondence-vs-compensation distinction (developed with Mikulincer and Shaver in their 2010 *Personality and Social Psychology Review* paper) supplies the framework ATB Section 2 uses to describe how attachment history shapes the believer's image of and relationship with God.

## Kirkpatrick, Lee A.
**Author: **Lee A. Kirkpatrick is Professor Emeritus of Psychology at the College of William and Mary and the pioneer of the application of attachment theory to the psychology of religion. His 1992 paper *An Attachment-Theory Approach to the Psychology of Religion* (International Journal for the Psychology of Religion) introduced the framework now standard in the field; his 2005 book *Attachment, Evolution, and the Psychology of Religion* (Guilford Press) developed it at length.

**Summary: **Kirkpatrick's foundational contribution is the identification of two pathways by which attachment history shapes the believer's image of and relationship with God: **correspondence** (secure childhood attachment tends to produce a secure God-image — loving, accessible, responsive — and insecure childhood attachment tends to produce a corresponding insecure God-image — capricious, distant, threatening) and **compensation** (when human caregivers have been unreliable, some people find in God the secure base that human relationships failed to provide, and develop a particularly intimate, dependent relationship with God as a partial healing of the human attachment wound). Empirical research over the subsequent three decades has supported both pathways, with correspondence appearing somewhat stronger in non-crisis contexts and compensation more visible during major life transitions. Kirkpatrick's earlier collaboration with Phillip Shaver (1990, *Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion*) produced the first empirical study connecting childhood attachment history to adult religious experience and conversion.

**Application: **Kirkpatrick is cited throughout the [eighth Formation Document, ATB — Attachment Theory and the Biblical Triad](attachment-theory-and-the-biblical-triad.md), particularly in Section 2 (the orientation to attachment-and-religion research). The correspondence/compensation distinction is one of the load-bearing claims ATB carries into the IJH framework: it supplies the mechanism by which the Sower-soils-as-attachment-profiles parallel mapping (ATB Section 8) operates in the lived experience of believers whose childhood attachment was either secure or insecure.

## Krathwohl, David R., Benjamin S. Bloom, and Bertram B. Masia
**Author: **David R. Krathwohl was a collaborator of Benjamin Bloom and professor of education who led the development of the affective domain taxonomy published in 1964 as the companion volume to Bloom’s 1956 cognitive taxonomy. Bertram B. Masia was also a collaborator in this project. The affective taxonomy describes how people develop in their emotional, attitudinal, and values-based responses to learning content.

**Summary: **The Affective Taxonomy describes five hierarchical levels of affective development: Receiving (bare awareness and willingness to attend), Responding (active engagement and some compliance), Valuing (internalizing a value as one’s own), Organization (integrating multiple values into a coherent value system), and Characterization (the value system becoming the consistent basis for behavior and identity). The transition from Stage 2 to Stage 3 (Responding to Valuing) is identified as the critical transition, representing the point at which external compliance becomes internal conviction.

**Application: **The Affective Taxonomy is arguably the single most important external framework in the LotS project. It is introduced in Vol 1 as an organizing scaffold for the Parable of the Sower, formalized in Vol 2, Exp. 7 as the developmental framework for the entire formation journey, used in Vol 3 as the primary measurement protocol for spiritual distance, and applied as the central assessment dimension in Vol 4’s Individual Testing Protocol (Section 3b). The five-stage LotS formation arc maps directly onto the five Affective Taxonomy levels, providing the project with an empirically grounded, behaviorally observable developmental sequence. The LotS-specific reading of the AT is integration-not-replacement: the five stages name the order in which the kinds of engagement first become accessible, but the mature pattern is the held integration of all five rather than the replacement of earlier stages by later ones (see [V2.Exp7](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-07-hearing-with-understanding.md#reading-these-levels-as-integration-not-replacement) and [Vol 4 §2](../volume-4-testing-framework/section-2-testing-framework.md#reading-the-affective-taxonomy-as-integration-not-replacement)). Krathwohl et al.'s original taxonomy and Bramer's spiritual-formation adaptation remain the upstream sources; the LotS reading is an *interpretation* of the same construct, not a rejection of it.

## Newton, Isaac

## Schwarz, Christian A.
**Author: **Christian A. Schwarz (b. 1956, Germany) is the founder and president of NCD International and one of the most widely read figures in empirical church development research. His NCD framework has been implemented by more than 70,000 churches in 84 countries and 112 denominations. His work is grounded simultaneously in empirical research (survey methodology, longitudinal tracking), the classical philosophical tradition (the three transcendentals of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty), and the Reformed-Evangelical-Charismatic theological tradition. The research finding with the strongest implication for the LotS project: holistic small groups is the single quality characteristic most correlated with overall church health across cultures. The minimum-factor strategy, adapted from Liebig’s Law of the Minimum in biochemistry, identifies the single least-developed characteristic as the one requiring targeted attention first — on the principle that raising the rate-limiting variable produces disproportionate overall gains. Schwarz calls the resulting growth movement “all-by-itself growth”: once the constraint is removed, the system grows by its own internal momentum. Both critiques deserve acknowledgment. Neither invalidates the empirical value of the survey data, and neither addresses the structural convergence between the Trinitarian Compass and TFT’s transcendental framework, which rests on independent philosophical grounds.

**Application: **The NCD framework is most directly relevant to Vols 3 and 4. Vol 3’s TFT framework uses the same transcendental triad — Truth, Goodness, Beauty — as Schwarz’s Trinitarian Compass, a convergence that warrants explicit attention because the two arrived at it independently. ## Schwarz, Christian A. — God’s Energy, Vol. 1: Reclaiming a New Testament Reality
**Author: **See Schwarz, Christian A. (Natural Church Development) above for full author biography.
