# Testing Spiritual Time Against the Revival Record

[The Engine Beneath the Force](the-engine-beneath-the-force-energy-time-inertia.md) proposed an anatomy for a revival: God injects energy, spiritual time (**dτ⁄dt**) spikes so that a weekend can hold years, many souls clear the [activation barrier](exploration-03-spiritual-force.md) at once, the moving body lowers the barrier for those still in the well, and a friction term (**γ**) explains why the tide later goes out. It was offered, honestly, as a *way of seeing* — a schema to be "tested by those who have watched many souls and many awakenings over time."

This chapter takes that invitation at its word and does the testing. It sets the model beside the actual record of revivals — the Great Awakenings, the Welsh outpouring of 1904, the Hebrides, Azusa Street, Herrnhut, Asbury in 2023, the best-measured case of all in the Billy Graham crusades — and beside the scholarship that has studied them: Max Weber on how a fire becomes an institution, Émile Durkheim on the crowd, William James on sudden conversion, and the modern personality science that asks whether the moment that *feels* instant actually *forms* anyone. A genuine test is not a defense. It keeps the evidence that cuts against the model in plain view, and it lets that evidence do two things it turns out to do well: **confirm** the anatomy in its broad shape, and **correct** it at three specific points — one missing term, one unnamed denominator, and one term that needed splitting in three.

## How a schema gets tested — and how honest the verdict has to be

The Engine chapter drew a hard boundary around its own math: these are *conceptual schemas in the shorthand of physics*, carrying ordinal weight — more or less, sooner or later — never a measured number, and all of it governed by **G**, the free self-giving of God, with "no mechanism in what follows." That boundary is exactly what makes a test possible, because it tells us what to test: not whether spiritual time equals so many seconds — it never claimed to — but whether the *shape* of the story is the shape the record shows.

So each part of the model earns its own verdict, and the verdicts are not all the same. Some of the anatomy is strongly corroborated and belongs at the **Reasonably Inferred** tier. One piece of it — the move from kairos-as-a-category to **τ as an integrable rate that can spike** — has no footing anywhere outside this book, and honesty puts it at the **Speculative** tier where the parent chapter already placed the whole. And one distinction has to be held from the start, because nearly everything turns on it: the acceleration of *experience* (a room genuinely changed in a night) is well attested, while the acceleration of *durable formation* (that room still changed a year later) is exactly what the record challenges. The model runs those two together in the bright word "revival." The test has to pull them apart.

## What the record confirms

The anatomy's backbone holds up, and in places it holds up better than the model dared claim.

**Kairos is real, and old.** The distinction between clock-time and God-charged time is not a devotional flourish; it is settled theology. Paul Tillich named *kairos* "the moment... in which the eternal breaks into the temporal," time "shaken" by what is beyond it; Oscar Cullmann built a whole New Testament theology on redemptive time-terms; Mircea Eliade mapped sacred time against the profane. When the Engine chapter set kairos over chronos and read "one day is as a thousand years" (2 Pet. 3:8) as a clock that runs on encounter, it stood on ground the tradition has held for a long time.

**The spike is real.** A community phase-change *does* happen. Jonathan Edwards counted "more than 300 souls" brought in "in the space of half a year" in a town of about eleven hundred — a genuine change of state in a whole population. Cane Ridge in 1801 drew a crowd estimated near a tenth of Kentucky's people. Asbury in 2023 held tens of thousands over sixteen days. William James, studying the phenomenon a century ago, described the self-surrendered conversion breaking "through all barriers and sweep[ing] in like a sudden flood." The model's second step — *dτ⁄dt spikes* — names something every witness to an awakening has seen.

**The injection lands on prepared ground.** The model's first step is a sovereign outpouring "never summoned by technique," and the record both confirms the priority of prayer and sharpens the picture. In the Hebrides, two elderly sisters and a barn full of praying men interceded *before* the preacher came; Herrnhut's Pentecost followed a season of covenant; Azusa followed weeks of prayer. Lewis Rambo's careful stage-model of conversion finds the same: an encounter needs a *prepared context* and a crisis to land in. The one refinement the record presses is worth keeping — in the cases that *lasted*, the ground was not only soft but **organized**: a covenanted, structured community was already in place when the spark fell. Hold that; it becomes the hinge of this whole chapter.

**The cascade is real — and the model is in good company quantifying it.** Step 5 — the moving body lowering the barrier for those still in the well — is the best-attested mechanism of all. Durkheim called it "collective effervescence," the assembly generating "a sort of electricity" in which "every sentiment expressed... resounds without interference in [other] consciousnesses." The vectors change with the century — Azusa's newspaper, Wales's daily press, Asbury's TikTok — but the shape is constant. And here is a thing worth saying plainly to anyone who fears the model over-reaches by daring to quantify a move of God: **it is not alone in the daring.** There is a real, peer-reviewed literature that models religious revival with the mathematics of contagion — multi-strain epidemic equations that reproduce transient waves, cyclical rise-and-fall, even a phase transition, fitted against a century of Swedish church data. The instinct to put an awakening into equations is not a spiritual eccentricity; serious people have done it. What those models *cannot* supply is the very thing the Engine chapter insisted on — that no will is ever overridden. They are agency-blind by construction. That is not the model's weakness against them; it is its one irreducible contribution.

## Where the mathematics is only ours

Confirming the anatomy does not confirm the arithmetic, and the honest line falls right here.

That kairos is a category, the tradition affirms. That kairos is an **integrable quantity** — a τ you can accumulate, whose rate dτ⁄dt rises and falls like a measurable flow — is the model's own invention, and nothing in the theology or the scholarship warrants it. The tradition treats God-charged time *qualitatively*, as a kind of meaning, not as a running total; Eliade's sacred time is even *cyclical and recoverable*, which sits at an angle to the model's forward-accumulating τ. So the picture of spiritual time as a rate that spikes is a genuine act of seeing — it names something believers recognize the instant they read it — but it is an overlay, original to this book, with no support outside it. It stays where the parent chapter's certainty note put it: **Speculative (Frontier)**, a way of seeing offered for testing, never a measurement. The phenomenon is real. The math is ours.

## The contested step — a cascade that compels no one

One step of the anatomy deserves more than a verdict; it deserves an argument the model must actually win. Step 5 claims the awakening propagates *as a cascade* "without overriding wills." The record raises a hard question here. Gustave Le Bon's classic account of the crowd is precisely a story of *agency eroded* — anonymity dissolving into suggestibility until personal responsibility thins out. Cane Ridge's involuntary falling and the notorious "jerks," Finney's frankly engineered "new measures," the whole recurring charge of the *manufactured* revival — all of it presses the same worry: is a cascade just a will overridden by a crowd?

The model cannot wave this away, and it does not need to. Two things answer it. First, modern crowd theory has moved past Le Bon: belonging to a gathered body can *enhance* purposeful agency rather than only erode it — a crowd can be the place a person becomes *more* himself, not less. Second, and decisively, this is the exact seam where the theological claim does work the sociology cannot. The model does not say the cascade is *irresistible*; it says the opposite — that unlike a phase transition in matter, where the parts *must* follow once the critical point passes, "no soul is ever compelled... a body can be warmed all around a person who is not yet ready." That is a falsifiable-in-principle claim about grace, and the record is consistent with it: awakenings are full of the unmoved standing inside the moving room. The honest posture is not to assert non-coercion as a settled fact but to hold it as the thing that distinguishes an outpouring of the Spirit from mere contagion — and to admit that from the outside the two can look alike. It is the contagion of grace, not the overriding of a will; but only the fruit will tell which one a given room held.

## What the record challenges — acceleration is not yet formation

Now the sharpest cut. The model's most quotable line is its most vulnerable: "the fruit of a revival is measured not by its peak but by who is still moving a year later." The record agrees with the *question* and unsettles the *assumption underneath it* — that the spike in experience is itself the formation.

Two findings press in. The first is timescale. Edwards's own awakening unfolded over *months*, not a weekend; William McLoughlin, the historian who studied American awakenings most closely, embeds each one in "cultural transformations... extending over a generation or more." "A weekend holds years" is true as a description of felt time and overstated as a description of formed time; the scholarship situates every spike inside a much longer cycle. The second finding is heavier. A 2024 study in the *Journal of Personality* tracked the Big Five personality traits across religious conversion and found "no evidence for linear post-conversion changes in *any*" of them — concluding that conversion is "better conceptualized as *gradual* processes that unfold long before and after" the moment. The measurable *forming* of a person may be slow even when the *moment* is a flood. This is the model's hardest test: the spike is in the *experience*, and formation may keep its own slower clock underneath. The Engine chapter half-anticipated this — its inertia term already insisted that mₛ "is also lowered the slow way, by the ordinary apprenticeship... most of the Christian life." The correction is to let that admission govern the revival section too: **an outpouring accelerates experience, and often *opens* formation, but it does not compress formation into itself.** The weekend is real. The roots still grow at the speed of roots.

## The missing term — routinization, the wineskin that holds the wine

Here is the one place the model is not merely overstated but *incomplete* — it is missing a whole term. Friction explains why a *single soul* slides back: γ is a drag on a moving person, and when the driving force recedes and γ dominates, the soul that climbed partway slips into the well it came from. But the record forces a second, distinct question friction does not answer: why do some whole *outpourings* leave a lasting body while others evaporate, though the peak looked the same? Friction acts on the individual; this asks about the *container*.

Scripture states the mechanism before any physics can. When three thousand were cut to the heart at Pentecost, the fire did not simply disperse: "they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers" (Acts 2:42). Within the day the outpouring was *lodged in durable practice* — a body with teaching, table, and prayer. And the Lord had already named the law of it: "no one puts new wine into old wineskins... new wine is for fresh wineskins" (Mark 2:22). The wine is the outpouring; the skin is the structure that must hold it. The Sower's rocky soil is the same law read as loss — the seed that sprang up fast "had no root," and "in time of testing" fell away (Luke 8:13). Fast rise, no root, no fruit.

So alongside friction stands a second term — call it **routinization, R**: the mechanism by which the transient kinetic energy of the peak is converted into durable *structure* — offices, doctrine, rhythms, relationships, a body — that holds souls on the Glory trajectory after the outpouring recedes. Where friction *bleeds* energy off, routinization *banks* it. The tide goes out (γ) unless there are banks to hold the water (R). As a fraction, the lasting fruit is roughly the share of the peak's energy that lands in a container:

> **fruit ≈ κ · (energy released at the peak)** — where κ, the capture coefficient (0 ≤ κ ≤ 1), is how much of the outpouring the body's structure actually holds.

This is not a metaphor reaching for evidence; it is the plainest reading of the best-measured revival we have. The Billy Graham crusades were engineered around exactly this term — counselor training, the decision card mailed to a local pastor within a day, referral into a congregation: a *routinization machine*, built because the team knew an unattended decision fades. And the numbers obey. Where the receiving church followed up, retention ran as high as 95–96%; where it did not, a crowd of tens of thousands left fewer than a fifth incorporated into any body a year later. Same peak; different κ. The durable fraction even clustered where relational scaffolding already existed — a friend or relative already in the pew. Max Weber named the general law a century ago: the charismatic outbreak "*must* be transformed into stable, institutionalized structures... or dissipate." The pattern repeats across the record — the founding *event* fades while the *movement* endures: Azusa's own mission was gone by 1931, yet Pentecostalism now counts hundreds of millions; Herrnhut turned its Pentecost into a prayer watch that ran a hundred years; the Vineyard survived its founder's death by planned succession. Durability is not automatic maturation. It is *engineered*, and it is *organizational*.

Below, the healthy wineskin holds a band: loose enough to hold without bursting, whole enough not to sour, between too loose (spills), too rigid (bursts), and too narrow (curdles).](images/wineskin-capture.svg)

And R is dual-valenced, exactly as inertia (mₛ) was — the danger the sociologists name and Scripture named first. Good routinization is the body acquiring *steadfast mass*: the Acts 2:42 devotion, the wineskin supple enough to hold and stretch. But a wineskin can fail two ways. It can grow **rigid** — structure hardens; Weber called its end the iron cage, and Paul named it two thousand years earlier, "having the form of godliness but denying its power" (2 Tim. 3:5); over-routinized, the same mass that once held the wine becomes the brittle skin that bursts at the next filling — the institutional analog of the [hardened heart](../volume-1-laws-of-the-spirit/foundational-law-xv-the-hardening-law.md), high inertia now resisting the *next* move of God. Or it can grow **narrow** — a single stream of the Spirit's life held in isolation, which sours into its own perversion (holiness into legalism, ardor into emotionalism, justice into performance); Richard Foster's *Streams of Living Water* names this failure and its cure, that the streams must converge into one "great river." So the healthy wineskin is both **supple** (not ossified) *and* **whole** (not sectarian): structured enough to hold, elastic enough not to strangle, broad enough not to sour. Too little structure and the flood spills (all fire, no form — the un-followed-up revival); too rigid and the wine bursts the skin (all form, no fire — the dead institution); too narrow and it curdles (one stream alone). Every awakening lives or dies in that band.

***Certainty: Reasonably Inferred.*** *The distinctness of routinization from friction, its dual valence, and the breadth condition are strongly consilient with the record — Weber's routinization of charisma, the Graham follow-up data, Foster's streams, and the founding-event-fades-while-movement-endures pattern of Herrnhut, Azusa, and the Vineyard. The capture coefficient κ is offered as a picture, not a measured quantity — the honest status of all the mathematics in this book.*

## Naming the denominator — which fruit are we counting?

The routinization term forces a question the model left implicit, and answering it dissolves a real confusion. "Who is still moving a year later" has *two* honest denominators, and they give wildly different numbers. Measured as **private steadfastness** — does the person still hold the decision they made? — the Graham figures run high: 70–95% by the organization's own long-range follow-up, and even the sharpest independent critics found that most converts held *some* faith, with only a small fraction fully relapsing. Measured as **visible incorporation into a body** — did they join and stay in a church? — the same crusades yield 15–20%. The gap between "95% still committed" and "15% joined a church" is not a contradiction; it is two different questions wearing one word.

Which denominator does the model want? The harder one. Its whole picture — the cascade, the [Community-as-Amplifier](../volume-2-knowing-to-doing/exploration-09-community-as-amplifier.md), the wineskin — is about a soul held *in a body*, not a soul holding a private resolution alone. And the Graham data deliver the model's own thesis back to it: the body-visible fruit is captured mainly where the relational and institutional scaffolding already existed to hold it. **The body-visible fruit is the captured fruit** — which is simply κ, read from the other side. This is why the fruit-test must say which denominator it means. Private steadfastness reads high, because a soul can hold a true decision alone for a while; body-visible incorporation reads low wherever κ was low. Both are real. Only the second is what the model is actually built to measure.

## Friction, re-read — drag, pruning, and stewardship

The friction term survives the test intact but comes back from it *richer*. The Engine chapter wrote γ as one thing: a drag that bleeds off motion — the world, the flesh, the un-cleared wound, and above all isolation. All of that holds. But the record shows resistance doing two further things that a pure-drag term cannot describe.

The first is **productive friction — pruning**. The Vineyard's history is full of boundary-drawing controversies that, painful as they were, *strengthened* the movement by cutting away excess — the cancelled seminary course, the parting with the Kansas City prophets, the 1995 release of the Toronto congregation. Resistance there was not a brake on formation; it was load-bearing. Scripture already knew this friction by its own name: "every branch that does bear fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit" (John 15:2). The second is **stewardship — a deliberate cap**. The Asbury outpouring in 2023 did not decay; it was *ended*, closed by the university's leadership at sixteen days as an act of stewardship. That is not γ bleeding energy away; it is a hand on the valve. So friction is better read as three modes, not one: **drag** (the fade and backslide, as written), **pruning** (resistance that strengthens by cutting), and **stewardship** (a spike deliberately brought to rest). Only the first is loss. The other two are wisdom.

## The streams within the outpouring

One enrichment the record offers sits slightly to the side of the durability test but earns a place, because it answers a monochrome spot in the model. The Engine chapter's "sovereign injection" comes in a single color. Richard Foster's *Streams of Living Water* argues that renewal has, across church history, come in *distinguishable kinds* — six great streams, each traceable to Christ Himself: the **contemplative** (the prayer-filled life), the **holiness** (the virtuous life), the **charismatic** (the Spirit-empowered life), the **social-justice** (the compassionate life), the **evangelical** (the Word-centered life), and the **incarnational** (the sacramental life). Notably, the very cases this chapter tested are Foster's own exemplars — his charismatic stream runs through Azusa's William Seymour, his evangelical stream through Billy Graham.

Read into this book's language, the six streams are a *spectrum on the injection* — modes, almost frequencies, of the one Spirit's outpouring, a natural rhyme with Vol 3's [resonance](exploration-08-miracles.md) and the Country-of-the-Heart's color for each law-type. Foster is a *witness* to the model's claim that renewal recurs ("the Holy Spirit has a history"), though he shows it by spreading exemplars across the centuries rather than by supplying a mechanism — and he is silent on the time claim entirely, neither confirming nor challenging the τ formalism. His real gift to the durability test is the **breadth condition** already folded into the wineskin above: a stream held *alone* turns pathological, and the cure is integration into the one great river. That is why the healthy container must be not only supple but *whole* — and it is, quietly, a description of this book's own deliberately cross-tradition [Council](../volume-6-governance/research-register.md), an institutionalized refusal to hold one stream alone.

## The counter-evidence, kept in view

A genuine test keeps the disconfirming cases on the table, and they are sobering. The "burned-over district" of upstate New York was named for spiritual *exhaustion* — ground so repeatedly revived it went cold. Charles Finney, the most systematic revivalist of his century, confessed near the end that his revivals were "of much shorter duration" than he had hoped and that "fewer of the converts" became "stable and efficient Christians" — the master of technique troubled by his own backsliders. The Welsh membership rolls *peaked in 1905 and declined continuously thereafter*, and Evan Roberts, the revival's human center, collapsed and withdrew. Asbury 2023 is simply too recent to judge; the durability data do not yet exist. And the personality science, again, cautions that the measurable person may change slowly whatever the moment felt like.

None of this refutes the model — but all of it disciplines it, and in the same direction. Every one of these cases is a story of a peak whose κ was low, or whose wineskin went rigid, or whose fruit was counted in the easy denominator. The counter-evidence does not break the anatomy; it *is the anatomy*, read on its failing side. That the model illuminates its own disconfirmations is a point in its favor — but the failures are real, and this chapter would rather name them than round them off.

## Where this is held

**Certainty: Reasonably Inferred as to the anatomy; Speculative (Frontier) as to the mathematics.** *Tested against the revival record and its scholarship, the shape of the Engine chapter's story stands — the injection onto prepared and organized ground, the spike in experience, the cascade that (the model insists, and the record permits) compels no will, and the fade that follows when nothing holds the energy. The test corrects it at three named points: it adds a **routinization term R** distinct from friction, the wineskin — supple and whole — whose capture coefficient κ decides how much of a peak becomes lasting fruit; it splits **friction** into drag, pruning, and stewardship; and it insists the fruit-test **name its denominator**, private steadfastness or body-visible incorporation, and own that the model is built to measure the second. It separates, throughout, the acceleration of experience (attested) from the acceleration of formation (slower than the spike, and only opened by it). What remains original and unverified is the τ-as-integrable-rate formalism itself — real as a way of seeing, unfounded as a measurement.* These trails are logged in [Open Trails](open-trails-what-vol-3-cannot-yet.md) and the Vol 6 [Research Register](../volume-6-governance/research-register.md), and they move only as the evidence accumulates — never on the strength of a well-argued proposal alone. If this chapter finds you in a season that will not spike, read [If You Come Weary, Read This First](../introduction/if-you-come-weary-read-this-first.md): the roots that grow at the speed of roots are the fruit that lasts, and you are loved all the way down through the slow years.
