# Spiritual Force, Energy, and Power

## Starting to Put Numbers on It
My goal in this section is to begin finding a way to put numbers on the spiritual laws I have identified — to move from "there is a relationship here" to "here is how to describe that relationship more precisely." This is forward-looking, dream-it-first work. This brief discussion is a starting point and a bridge to the fuller discussion in Vol 3.

**One thing has to be said before any of the numbers.** When I speak of spiritual "power" and "force" in what follows, I do not mean a fuel, a current, or a substance I can store and spend. I mean the Holy Spirit Himself — a Person, who comes and works as He wills (John 3:8; 1 Cor. 12:11), never a charge I build up in a tank and switch on by getting the inputs right. Everything below is only my attempt to describe patterns I think I see in how He moves; none of it makes Him move. Keep Him in view as a Person the whole way through, and none of the rest can curdle into a technique.

### Force, Work, and Trust
My working definitions: Spiritual force is the power to get spiritual work done — to move things in the spiritual world, which then shows up in the natural one. Trust (faith, pistis) is the main supply that sets how much spiritual power is on hand. The more genuine trust I have — not just agreeing in my head, but real working trust, the kind that changes what I do — the more power is available.

The mustard seed passages set the minimum: a small amount of genuine trust is enough to produce results that look impossible from where we stand in the natural world. But I want to be careful here — I think the mustard seed is at least about crossing a line (is the trust real, above zero?) more than about amount (is there enough of it?), though it could be some of both — that is still open. This matters for any formula we would try to write.

### Trust — a Gift We Keep Receiving, Not a Tank We Fill
It is tempting to picture trust as a tank I fill and draw down — but that picture puts me at the center, managing my own supply, and that is backwards. Trust is better thought of as a gift I keep receiving with open hands. It does grow over time, it can run thin, and it can be filled again — but the filling is God's doing, not my manufacturing. What fills it is hearing the Word about Christ and being willing to obey (the Exploration 1 chain) — and even the hearing and the willingness are themselves given. What thins it is acting on mere presumption dressed up as faith, and the slow wearing-down of places and voices that feed unbelief. So when trust runs low, the answer is never to squeeze harder; it is to come back, empty-handed, to the One who gives it.

The loop is: more trust → more action in faith → more confirmation → more trust. The negative version: less trust → less action → less confirmation → less trust. The loop turns both ways. This fits Jesus’ statement that "to the one who has, more will be given; and from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away" (Matt. 13:12 ESV). That is a loop that feeds itself.

### The Resonance Connection
From the Eighth Exploration: prayer works like a wave. How often it comes, which way it is aimed, and how long it keeps up all decide whether it builds into resonance (think of a swing pushed again and again in rhythm, going higher each time). From the Authority Exploration: authority is a force multiplier — the wiring that sends the power where it needs to go. These two work on top of the base power available from the trust reservoir.

A rough first formula (very low confidence, offered as a working idea for the community to test): Effective spiritual force = f(trust reservoir) × g(authority alignment) × h(prayer resonance). Here f, g, and h stand for relationships I cannot yet pin down, and h plays out over time rather than in a single instant. This is the start of the math, not the finish. I hold it at about Speculative certainty on the exact form, and Reasonably Inferred on the general idea that these three multiply together rather than simply add up — meaning that if any one of them drops near zero, the whole result drops near zero.

One caution must travel with this formula wherever it goes. It is a *rule of thumb* — a rough way of describing patterns in a believer's own experience of trust, authority, and prayer under grace. It is **not** a calculation of God's response, and God is not a machine or a system that the right inputs can control. He is a Father who hears and freely answers (Matt. 6:8; Luke 11:13; 1 John 5:14), and He remains free to act above, beyond, or against any pattern we think we see. For that reason this formula stays firmly in the speculative, exploring layer and must never slip into the working Foundational Laws, which describe how God has said things work — not recipes for getting results out of Him.

## A Word for the Dry Season
I have to stop and say something here, because I learned it the hard way, more than once. All this talk of reservoirs and channels and force can land cruelly on a person in a dry season — and if you walk with God for long, dry seasons come. There are stretches where prayer feels like talking to the ceiling, where the disciplines that once carried you feel like dead weight, where God seems far off and you cannot feel a thing. When that happens, the language of this chapter can whisper a lie: *your reservoir must be empty; your channel must be clogged; you did something wrong; you failed the mechanism.* Hear me on this — that is a lie. I have believed it myself, and lived to find out it was false.

The dry season is not the scoreboard of your standing with God. Nearly every saint I have read or known has walked through it; the old writers called it the dark night, and they did not mean a punishment — they meant a normal, and even precious, part of the road. The psalmist knew it: "Why are you cast down, O my soul?" (Ps. 42:5); some psalms end in the dark with no sunrise in the lines (Ps. 88). Job sat in it. Paul carried a thorn that never left. The Lord Himself, in the garden, pleaded and felt the silence. Dryness is not the absence of faith; very often it is faith being taught to stand without its props.

And here is what I most want you to keep: God's nearness does not depend on your feeling it. "If I make my bed in Sheol, You are there" (Ps. 139:8). His mercies "are new every morning" (Lam. 3:22-23) — and Jeremiah wrote that line from the middle of a ruin, not a mountaintop. When the channel feels closed, you have not been cut off; you are being carried in a way you cannot feel yet. The answer in the dry season is never to squeeze harder on the machinery — there is no machinery. It is to keep showing up, empty-handed, and let Him hold you until the feeling returns, as in His kindness it always, eventually, does. None of the laws in this book is a verdict on you. They describe how His world runs; they were never meant to measure how loved you are. And you are loved, all the way through the dark, whether you can feel it or not.

## Open Questions
The most important open questions at this point:

- Are there conservation laws? In physics, energy is never created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. If spiritual energy does spiritual work, is there a similar rule about how it carries over and changes form?
- Are there field equations? The physicist Maxwell wrote a small set of equations that describe how electric and magnetic fields behave everywhere in space. Is there a spiritual version — equations describing the "field" of the Spirit that predict how spiritual power spreads out and travels?
- How do time and space come in? The Obedience Channel law brings in time delay as a key factor. Does distance matter in prayer? Is spiritual time different from the time we experience? It sounds like a rhetorical question once you ask it — the centurion’s servant was healed from a distance. Are there limits in space at all, and if so, where?
- How does it add up for a group? The community side of all these laws is mostly unexplored. How does the trust reservoir of a whole congregation add up? Is it simply everyone’s trust summed together, or is there a tipping point where a community’s trust, above a certain level, produces outsized results?
Please see Vol 3 for the extensions of these questions.

## Speculative Laws for consideration and further development
These have not been investigated in any depth and really are just notes for future work.  I actually always have such a set of notes. I have more ideas than solutions, one of the exciting things about this work. I expect the Lord will call me home before it's all done. I also hope my kids, grandkids, and maybe others will find it interesting and take it further.

**The Attention Economy of the Soul**

Scripture again and again treats *what you fix your attention on* as the thing that decides what you become. "Whatever is true, whatever is honorable... think about these things" (Phil. 4:8) is not merely advice — it has the shape of a law. "We all, with unveiled face, *beholding* the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image" (2 Cor. 3:18) makes the change depend directly on steady attention. The possible law: *the soul moves toward whatever it keeps its attention on, and is slowly shaped into its likeness.* This would work the same in both directions — toward God and toward anything else. It would connect to the idolatry warnings in a new way: idolatry isn’t just a moral failure; it’s an attention-machine working exactly as built, but pointed at the wrong thing. Secular research actually agrees: you can measure physical changes in the brain as you train it. Note that 2 Cor. 3:18 (ESV) — “And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another” — makes the change depend directly on steady attention, which is the same thing this law proposes; the connection to that passage is made more fully in the Vol 3 Glory Attractor exploration. Confidence: maybe Reasonably Inferred, but exactly how it works, and how long it takes, are completely open.

**The Gratitude Amplifier**

There is a pattern across Paul's letters that is easy to miss because gratitude looks like a response rather than a cause. But Rom. 1:21 frames ingratitude as the *first* step in the downward spiral — "although they knew God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks to him, and they became futile in their thinking." The order of cause and effect is striking: ingratitude comes before futile thinking, which comes before darkened hearts, which comes before the rest. Running it forward: gratitude may open the channel in the same way obedience does. 1 Thess. 5:18 ("give thanks in all circumstances, for this is the will of God") and the Psalms' constant movement from lament to praise suggest that gratitude is not merely an emotional response to blessing but an *active spiritual practice* that keeps the hearing channel open. Possible law: *steady gratitude keeps the channels of perception open; steady ingratitude slowly closes them, no matter the circumstances.* This would explain why Brueggemann's "disorientation" phase shapes us for good when we go through it with thanksgiving and for ill when we go through it with bitterness. Confidence: Speculative.

**The Confession-Clarity Law**

Jas. 5:16 says, "Confess your sins to one another, and you will be healed" —the community side is explicit, and most treatments tame it down into private confession to God. But the deeper pattern may be this: unspoken sin creates a particular kind of inner fog. Not just the channel-blockage of unconfessed sin before God (the Ps. 66:18 pattern), but a loss of *clear sight of yourself* — the person hiding something from the community slowly loses the ability to see himself accurately. The possible law: *what is named and brought into the light can be changed; what is kept in the dark wrecks your whole inner compass — including your ability to see what is in the dark.* John 3:20-21 has exactly this shape. The practical upshot is that spiritual direction and accountability relationships are not merely useful but actually necessary for growth to continue past a certain point. Confidence: Reasonably Inferred, but exactly how hiding wrecks self-perception is undeveloped.

**The Forgiveness-Debt Transfer Law**

Matthew 18 (the unforgiving servant) has a claim built into it that is starker than it is usually read: the servant who refuses to forgive has his own forgiven debt put back. Jesus is not saying "it would be nice if you forgave." He is describing how something actually works. The possible law: *refusing to forgive puts back the spiritual debt that forgiveness had cancelled — not as a punishment handed down from outside, but as the natural result of refusing to take part in the very mercy that set you free.* This would connect to the emotional knot work in Vol 2: many of the deepest knots have an unforgiveness component, and the reason they resist clearing may be that the debt is being kept alive by the refusal to let it go. The law would also predict a specific loop: unforgiveness → debt put back → more self-condemnation → harder to forgive others → deeper knot. Confidence: Reasonably Inferred on the basic claim; lower on exactly how it works.

**The Worship Alignment Law**

This is the most speculative entry here, and possibly the most interesting. The Psalms treat worship not just as a response to God but as a way of *re-setting what feels real* — when the psalmist moves from lament to praise within a single psalm without the circumstances changing, something in how he sees his situation has shifted, even though the situation itself has not. The possible law: *worship truly aimed at God (not performed for yourself or others) re-sets how the soul sees what is real and what matters most — it resets the baseline that every later judgment and decision starts from.* This would give an account of why Isaiah 6 (worship leading to commissioning) and Revelation 4-5 (cosmic worship as the frame for all that follows) and the Psalms' move from disorientation to orientation are all describing the same underlying thing. It would also explain why worship that never gets past Affective Level 2 (just showing up and going through the motions) does nothing for how we see: the reset only happens when our attention is truly turned outward toward God rather than inward on our own spiritual state. Confidence: Speculative, with how it works almost entirely open.

**The Prophetic Imagination Law**

This one comes from the Prophets and is almost entirely uncharted in the current framework. The prophets don't just predict the future — they *hold open* a different reality in front of a community that has been captured by the imagination of the empire of the day (Brueggemann's language, but the scriptural ground is solid). The possible law: *how much you can act in faith is limited by how well you can picture an alternative to the visible here-and-now — and that ability to picture an alternative can be trained directly, by steady time in scripture's story, especially the prophetic and end-times writings.* This would connect to the Vol 3 eschatological law: the Glory ahead only pulls you toward it if you actually keep it in view, and keeping it in view against the weight of present circumstances is a specific, trainable skill. What this means for formation: prophetic writing may do a different job than teaching-writing — not handing you principles but *stretching what you are able to imagine*, which has to come before faith can act on a large scale. Confidence: Speculative on the basic claim; how it works is entirely open.

**The Generational Transmission Law**

This entry has been split. The *transmission doctrine* it once held has graduated out of this speculative list into the foundational laws, where it is now treated as established. That blessings and curses both pass down a family line beyond genes and culture (Exod. 20:5-6; Deut. 28-30), that Christ breaks the passing-down of the curse (Gal. 3:13-14), and that the blessing is inherited through faith (Rom. 4) are all carried at **Clearly Taught** confidence in FL.XVII Substitution-Cascade (the curse direction and its breaking at the cross) and FL.XXI Household Formation (the blessing direction, handed down through deliberate faith). That doctrine is not speculative and is no longer held here.

What remains genuinely open — and what stays in this list — is the inner-healing *practice* sometimes built on top of that doctrine: that clearing an individual's knots may mean dealing not only with their own knots but with the family patterns, going back generations, that set them up to form. The possible law: *spiritual damage carried in a family's patterns can make some inner-healing work go faster when the generational pattern is addressed, not just the individual one.* This would help explain why some inner-healing work goes faster than other, and why certain sin patterns keep coming back with unusual stubbornness. Confidence: Speculative — and here it is the *practice* that calls for careful testing and real caution, not the settled doctrine above it.

A few observations about this list as a whole. Most of these laws share one thing: they describe *how something works* — things already taken for granted in Christian practice but rarely spelled out as laws with cause-and-effect arrows and self-feeding loops. The attention economy, the gratitude amplifier, the forgiveness-debt transfer — these are taught as virtues or commands, but the IJH project is asking whether they are better understood as descriptions of how things actually work, whether or not anyone obeys them. That shift changes what it means to "apply" them: you aren't just obeying a command; you are either working with or against the way things actually work, whether or not you understand it.

That's where the low confidence is most important. These are proposed ideas, not confirmed ones. The community testing the project calls for — which the Formation Documents make more specific, and the Governance Model (Vol 6) implements as an open-source model — is exactly what these need before they can be moved up.
