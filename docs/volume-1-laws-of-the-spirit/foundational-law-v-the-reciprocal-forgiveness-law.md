# Foundational Law V: The Reciprocal Forgiveness Law

The Reciprocal Forgiveness Law sits inside the Lord's Prayer and is repeated by Jesus more often than almost anything else He says about how the spiritual world works. Here is the heart of it, and the order matters enormously: God forgives us, fully and freely, in Christ — and a heart that has truly received that forgiveness becomes a heart that forgives others. "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you" (Eph. 4:32). Our forgiving is the *overflow* of being forgiven, not the price that buys it. And so the reverse is a warning sign: a person who clings to unforgiveness shows that the grace has not yet done its work in them, and cuts themselves off from the very flow they refuse to pass on. Grace received and grace withheld cannot live in the same heart. The law works both vertically (between us and God) and horizontally (between us and others) at once.

This law is the positive direction of the Forgiveness-Debt Transfer mechanism. Together with FL.II Confession-Restoration and the Sin Blockage Law (V2.Exp4), the four form a complete account of channel-opening at the moral-failure level.

***Luke 6:37 (ESV)***

*“Judge not, and you will not be judged; condemn not, and you will not be condemned; forgive, and you will be forgiven.”*

***Col. 3:13 (ESV)***

## The Mechanism
Three features. First, get the order right, because the wording of the Lord's Prayer ("forgive us… as we forgive") can be misheard as earning. It is not. The ground of all forgiveness is the cross of Christ, and God's forgiveness comes *first* — the servant in Jesus' parable was forgiven his enormous debt *before* he was ever asked to forgive his fellow servant (Matt. 18:32–33). Our forgiving others is the response that flows out of having been forgiven. What Jesus warns about is the opposite case: someone who has supposedly received God's mercy but refuses to extend it. That refusal does not fail to *earn* pardon — it blocks the channel and shows the mercy never truly took hold (the servant's forgiven debt is reinstated, Matt. 18:34–35). Human forgiveness is not the cause of God's forgiveness; it is the evidence and overflow of it, and its absence is a danger sign.

Second, the law is bidirectional in the strict sense FL.I and FL.III are: the positive direction (forgive — be forgiven) and the negative mirror (withhold forgiveness — have forgiveness withheld) are both stated as law, in the same sentence in Matt. 6:14–15.

Third, the law operates at both the vertical and horizontal scales by the same mechanism. Paul’s version (Eph. 4:32; Col. 3:13) frames human forgiveness as participation in the prior divine forgiveness — a single mechanism appearing in two scales.

## Why This Is a Foundational Law
Six scriptural witnesses across at least four authors (Matthew, Mark, Luke, Paul). The directional relationship is uncontested across mainstream Christian traditions. Traditions differ on how to read the severity of Matt. 6:15 — from the Reformed emphasis that the unforgiving heart reveals the absence of true grace, to the more sacramental traditions that emphasize the ongoing operational character of the law, to the Anabaptist readings that emphasize the parable as ethical instruction — but every tradition affirms the directional law itself. Testability in the natural is high: every long-held resentment, every reconciliation, every family-system unforgiveness pattern testifies to it.

**Proposed Law (Operational, Wide Consent): God's forgiveness in Christ comes first and freely; the forgiveness a person then extends to others is the overflow and evidence of having received it (Eph. 4:32; Col. 3:13). Where forgiveness is withheld, the refuser blocks the very flow of mercy they will not pass on, and — as the unforgiving servant shows — the debt that grace had dissolved is reinstated (Matt. 18:32–35; read here, with the Mechanism above, as evidence that the mercy had not truly taken hold — not a pardon earned and then lost). The law operates at the vertical (Person–God) and horizontal (Person–Person) scales at once (tag B), the human direction always downstream of the divine. (Note: this concerns *forgiveness* — releasing the offender from our debt-claim, which we can always do — not necessarily *reconciliation*, the restored relationship, which also depends on the other person.)**

**Certainty: Clearly Taught  ***As high as any law in this volume. Six witnesses across four authors. The remaining uncertainty is in the secondary question of what “forgiveness from the heart” (Matt. 18:35) requires of a person operationally, which Vol 2’s emotional-knot work helps clarify but does not fully settle.*

**A word from your Granddaddy.** This law sounds simple — forgive, and be forgiven — but I've found it has a depth that takes a lifetime to plumb. Let me give you three things I've learned about how deep it goes.

The first is an old saying I heard years ago and have never forgotten: *holding on to unforgiveness is like drinking poison and expecting the other person to die.* That's this law's dark side, said plainly — the one who won't forgive is the one it poisons. The offender often never feels a thing; it's the holder who slowly dies of it.

The second is a distinction I've had to fight for, because getting it wrong does real harm. Forgiveness and trust are not the same thing, and they do not run on the same clock. I once put it as a question, in a hard board discussion about a wrong another group would not own: *when should an abused wife forgive her abuser?* The scriptural answer is plain — right away; she is not to carry the poison. But there is a second question, just as important, that the first must never be allowed to swallow: *when should she trust him again?* And that answer is altogether different: only when there is changed behavior, over time, that actually earns the trust back. Forgiveness you give freely, the moment you are wronged. Trust is rebuilt slowly, and only on proof. Never let anyone use the command to forgive as a lever to force you back into harm's way before the trust has been earned. They are two different things.

The third is the deepest, and I am still learning it. The wrong that calls for your forgiveness did not slip past God; in some way I won't pretend to fully understand, He allowed it. Which means forgiveness is only the floor. The bigger question, once you've forgiven, is the one Joseph finally asked about his brothers' betrayal — *what is God up to in the middle of this, and how does He want me to respond?* (Gen. 50:20). The basics of this law get the poison out of your system. The depths of it are where God turns even the wound into something He can use.
