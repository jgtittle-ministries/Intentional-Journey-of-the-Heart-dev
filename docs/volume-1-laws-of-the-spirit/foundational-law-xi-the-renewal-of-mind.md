# Foundational Law XI: The Renewal-of-Mind Transformation Law

This law is Paul's most direct statement of how the mind is changed by staying steadily soaked in God's truth. Keep renewing your mind with truth, and your whole self is transformed; let yourself be molded by the pattern of the world around you, and the opposite happens. The mind is where the law does its work first, but the change spreads out from there into what you choose, what you feel, and what you do.

This law pairs with the more speculative Attention Economy of the Soul, one building on the other, the same way Ask-Seek-Knock and Prayer Resonance do. Renewal-of-Mind is the close-up: what happens inside one person who keeps their attention fixed on truth. Attention Economy is the wide shot — the bigger claim that the soul drifts toward whatever it keeps paying attention to, at every scale.

## The Scriptural Ground
***Rom. 12:2 (ESV)***

*“Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect.”*

***2 Cor. 3:18 (ESV)***

*“And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit.”*

***Eph. 4:22–24 (ESV)***

*“to put off your old self, which belongs to your former manner of life and is corrupt through deceitful desires, and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness.”*

***Col. 3:9–10 (ESV)***

*“Do not lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator.”*

***Heb. 8:10 (ESV)***

*“For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the Lord: I will put my laws into their minds, and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”*

***John 8:32 (ESV)***

*“and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.”*

## The Mechanism
Four things stand out. First, this is something ongoing, not a one-off. The grammar Paul uses points to a process still underway — be *being* transformed — and 2 Cor. 3:18 puts it as “are being transformed from one degree of glory to another.” It is not a single event. The change builds up from steady, ongoing renewal; one flash of insight won't do it, and someone who renews their mind on Tuesday and then soaks in the world's pattern Wednesday through Monday shouldn't expect the change the law promises. The law works on the steady direction your attention points, not on occasional highs.

Second, the law spells out what goes in and what comes out. What goes in is renewal of the mind — through truth (John 8:32), through knowledge (Col. 3:10), through gazing at the glory of the Lord (2 Cor. 3:18); different words for the same thing. What comes out is transformation, freedom, being remade into God's image, the law written on mind and heart. The flip side in Rom. 12:2 (“conformed to this world”) is the very same process aimed at a different source: keep your attention on the world's pattern and you'll be shaped into the world's pattern. What matters is which way your attention is pointed, not whether you're paying attention at all.

Third, the law runs both ways, just like Sowing-and-Reaping. Rom. 12:2 names both directions in one sentence: do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind. Being molded by the world isn't just a harmless lack of renewal; it's renewal aimed the wrong way, and what comes out is a deformed self. That has a big practical upshot: someone who isn't actively renewing their mind in truth isn't holding steady — they are being shaped by whatever their surroundings keep feeding their attention.

Fourth, the transforming itself is God's work, not a project you pull off by willpower. Paul's verb is passive — you are *being* transformed — and 2 Cor. 3:18 says plainly where the change comes from: “from the Lord who is the Spirit.” Your part is to keep turning toward Him and beholding His glory — the veil lifts, Paul says, “when one turns to the Lord” (2 Cor. 3:16); His part is the actual remaking. This is what keeps the law from shrinking into a self-improvement technique, a regimen of repetition you run to produce your own change. You do not renew yourself by trying harder; you keep your gaze on the truth, and the Spirit does the renewing, received by faith.

## Why This Is a Foundational Law
Six passages from at least three writers (Paul, the writer of Hebrews, and John). Paul carries most of the weight — four of the six are his — but Hebrews and John give the outside confirmation our multi-writer rule asks for, and the Heb. 8:10 covenant passage carries special weight (the law written on minds and hearts is the very heart of the new covenant). The link is uncontested across mainstream Christian traditions: the Reformed stream stresses the Spirit's prior work that makes renewal possible; the sacramental traditions stress worship and the Word as the means that keep it going; and the contemplative traditions stress the gazing part (2 Cor. 3:18) — but no tradition disputes the law itself. It is also easy to see: the difference between a mind kept on truth and a mind molded by its surroundings shows up over the decades, both to the person and to everyone around them.

**Proposed Law (Operational, Wide Consent): Steadily renewing the mind by engaging God's truth transforms the whole person; steadily being molded to the world's pattern deforms it — the very same process aimed at a different source. The change is the build-up of ongoing attention to truth, not the result of a single flash of insight. The law runs between you and God (the truth and the Spirit are what the mind opens onto), and it is the high-confidence, one-person version of the same dynamic the more speculative Attention Economy of the Soul describes on a cosmic scale.**

**Certainty: Clearly Taught  ***As certain as any law in this volume. Rom. 12:2 stating both directions in one sentence, and 2 Cor. 3:18's ongoing “being transformed,” together make the law unusually clear. The one open question is the smaller one of which ways of renewing the mind (study, meditation, worship, contemplation, Scripture reading, teaching) work best at which stages of growth — which Scripture sketches in general but doesn't pin down. Vol 2's Heart Formation work and Vol 3's measurement program both connect into that question.*

**A word from your Granddaddy.** What I have learned about this law, over a long life of it, is that there is no place to just mark time. You do have a choice — make no mistake — but the choice on offer is never "do nothing and stay where I am." That option is not on the table. Every day your mind is being shaped: either it is being renewed by the truth you keep turning toward, or it is being conformed to the world you keep soaking in. One or the other, always. There is no neutral ground, no quiet middle where no harm is done and no foul is called. It simply does not work that way. I used to imagine there was a safe in-between place where I could coast for a season without losing ground — there is not. And the better half, underneath the warning, is this: when you do turn your gaze toward Him, you are not the one who has to manufacture the change. You keep turning toward the truth; He does the renewing. That is the part I most want you to trust — the transforming was never mine to do.
