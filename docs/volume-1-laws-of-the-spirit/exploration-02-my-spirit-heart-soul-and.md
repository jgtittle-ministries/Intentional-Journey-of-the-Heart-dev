# Second Exploration: My Spirit, Heart, Soul, and Body


![](images/image-003.png)

## Why This Matters
Before I can describe the laws that govern how things work in me, I need to understand the parts. Most of us use spirit, heart, soul, and mind as if they all meant the same thing — but if they are actually different, with different jobs, then that sloppiness is costing us a great deal of clarity. This exploration is about getting the layout right.

## The Proposed Structural Relationship
***Mark 12:29-30 (ESV)***

*"Jesus answered, 'The most important is: Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one. And you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength.'"*

My best guess so far is that these are real parts of me. Here is the starting idea: my spirit sits inside my heart, while my heart, my mind, and my body are all parts of my soul. I picture it as a set of layers, with my soul — which, scripture says, is what gets saved — on the outside, holding heart, mind, and body. My spirit is the part of my heart in contact with the Holy Spirit; in fact, I think that’s where the Holy Spirit settles in when He comes to live with me. This is grounded in the question, if God cannot abide sin, how can He hang out with me, certainly a sinner. I see being born again as awakening to the desire to be in communication with God, and that part of my spirit is then holy, which actually means being set apart to God. So that part of me that wants to hear God is where the Holy Spirit comes to hang out. Watchman Nee (The Spiritual Man) does a good job of discussing the tension that arises when my spirit listens to God, my body listens to the world, and my mind is the battleground.

I know this picture is in tension with both main views in systematic theology — the two-part view (a person is a body plus one inner part) and the three-part view (body, soul, and spirit as three things). Grudem works through this carefully in Systematic Theology (Ch. 23) and lands on a practical two-part view: soul and spirit are two names for the same inner, non-physical part of us, used interchangeably in scripture. My picture is different: I propose that spirit, heart, soul, and body each do a real and distinct job, with the soul as the whole that pulls everything together rather than just the spiritual part. The key texts I would ask a systematic theologian to work through with me are 1 Thess. 5:23, which names all three (spirit, soul, body) separately, and Heb. 4:12, which speaks of the Word dividing soul from spirit as if they could be told apart. I hold this picture at Reasonably Inferred partly because I am not sure the theological tradition has fully settled the question this distinction is trying to answer.

If this is right, it matters right away in practice. My spirit is where the Holy Spirit comes to live when I am born again — the innermost holy place, where I am in communion with God. My heart is the next layer out — the real inner self, the soil of the parable of the Sower. The state of my heart decides what happens to the seed of the Word. My body is how I meet the world, and my soul is the part that pulls it all together, the part that is saved and lives forever in His presence.

## What This Explains
This layered picture explains several things that had been fuzzy for me. It explains how I can be "saved" (my spirit is born again and in communion with God) while still needing to "work out my salvation"—there is more of me, in the outer layers, still being shaped into the likeness of Christ. It explains why change works from the inside out: changing your behavior without changing your heart is only skin-deep and doesn’t last.

I also see this in 1 Cor. 3:12-15, where the gold, silver, and precious stones of my life survive the fire (which I take to be me standing before a holy God). The wood, hay, and stubble burn up. As far as my spirit, heart, mind, and body are set apart to God, they are holy and long to be in His presence forever. The thief on the cross had only a thin sliver of that desire to be with Jesus, but he was saved, and that part of him that wanted to be with Jesus loves being with Him.

A word about what I mean when I say the soul is saved. I am using "saved" in the legal sense — in God’s courtroom, the soul is the part of me that stands before God, declared righteous because of Christ. That is settled the moment I am born again. But the soul is also still in process. The same soul that is already declared saved is, at the same time, being made holy — worked on, broken open, healed, and remade by the Spirit over a lifetime. When I talk later about soul-disorder or stages of soul development, I am not questioning that justification is complete. I am describing the lived, formational work that happens inside the already-declared-righteous soul. Both are true. The verdict is final; the inside remodeling is still going on. That is what working out your salvation with fear and trembling looks like from the inside.

What is still an open question is the exact relationship between heart, mind, and soul. I am at about Reasonably Inferred certainty on the overall picture. This needs more work.

**Proposed Law (Structural): The parts of a person are nested inside one another like rings: spirit (innermost) sits inside the heart, while the heart, mind, and body are all parts of the soul. Each outer layer holds — and is shaped by — every layer inside it. Change that does not reach the heart is only skin-deep; lasting change always works from the inside out.**

**The spirit is the part of you that deliberately pays attention and chooses to turn to God. It is where a decision forms before any action follows. It is the part of the heart in living communion with the Holy Spirit, and the part scripture holds you responsible for. The difference between the spirit and the outer layers of the soul is one of job, not of substance: "spirit" names the deliberate paying-attention-and-choosing that the whole soul includes but does not do automatically.**

**Certainty: Reasonably Inferred. **Consistent with the Mark 12 structure and the parable of the Sower. More scriptural support: 1 Thess. 5:23 — “Now may the God of peace himself sanctify you completely, and may your whole spirit and soul and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ” — names all three (spirit, soul, body) separately and backs up the layered picture. The heart-mind-soul question is the least settled part of this exploration. Scripture names the spirit by its **job** — the part that deliberately pays attention and chooses while in living communion with the Holy Spirit. That answers the worry about claiming three separate substances without giving up the practical distinction the work needs (1 Thess 5:23, Heb 4:12). Related scripture: Phil 4:8 (deliberately paying attention — **think on these things**), Rom 12:2 (renewing the mind by deliberate choosing), 1 Tim 4:7–8 (training as repeated deliberate practice), Heb 4:12 (the real but fine line between soul and spirit).

**A word from your Granddaddy.** A great deal has happened to me in this life that I never chose — some of it good, some hard, and some just plain ugly. I’ve come to believe that before the Lord I’m not finally held to account for most of that. I’m held to account for two things: what I *chose*, and where I let my *attention* rest. Those two are mine to answer for; the rest I bring to Him and leave there. And even those two I can’t keep straight for long on my own — it’s His Spirit in me who steadies my choosing and turns my eyes back to what is true.
