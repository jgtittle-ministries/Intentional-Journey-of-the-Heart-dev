# Foundational Law VII: The Drawing-Near Reciprocity Law

This law describes how the relationship between you and God works at its most basic level. Move toward God, and God moves toward you; turn away, and He lets you go. It is the clearest example in Scripture of give-and-take: God responds in kind to which way you turn — toward Him or away.

## The Scriptural Ground
***Jas. 4:8 (ESV)***

*“Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.”*

***2 Chron. 15:2 (ESV)***

*“The Lord is with you while you are with him. If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you forsake him, he will forsake you.”*

***Jer. 29:13 (ESV)***

*“You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart.”*

***Deut. 4:29 (ESV)***

*“But from there you will seek the Lord your God and you will find him, if you search after him with all your heart and with all your soul.”*

***Heb. 11:6 (ESV)***

*“And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.”*

## The Mechanism
Three things stand out. First, the give-and-take matches up in direction — you draw near, you are drawn near to — but the two sides aren't equal in who moves first. God always moves first; your seeking Him is already a response to His invitation. James can say “draw near to God” only because God has already made Himself reachable. This is what lets the Reformed stress on God-goes-first sit comfortably with the law as stated: the back-and-forth is real, and so is the grace that came first and made the back-and-forth possible.

Second, the dark side — turn away, and He withdraws — is put just as bluntly as the bright side in 2 Chronicles 15:2, and it is at least hinted at all through the prophets when God speaks of “hiding his face” from a faithless people. This isn't God lashing out in punishment; it is the same give-and-take running in reverse. But not every season when God seems hidden is this reverse running. Scripture also knows a hiddenness that has nothing to do with turning away — a Father drawing back the surface comforts on purpose, to draw a faithful seeker deeper: “For a brief moment I deserted you… In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion on you” (Isa. 54:7–8). The contemplative tradition called this the dark night of the soul, and the closing word of this chapter takes it up.

Third, the words “with all your heart” in Jer. 29:13 and Deut. 4:29 matter a lot. This isn't a vending machine where you put in a coin and out comes God; the seeking that actually finds Him is whole-hearted, not half-in. That ties straight into the heart taxonomy in Vol 2 (how hearing God develops in stages) and the Vol 5 Heart Formation work — seeking God with your whole heart is itself something that grows over time.

## The Shadow Pair

This is the cleanest example of a held tension in the whole catalog: your drawing near is paired with God's drawing near, matched in direction but not in who moves first (God always moves first; your seeking is already a response to His invitation). The law goes wrong in two opposite ways. (See [Supplemental: Reading the Laws as Held Tensions](supplemental-held-tensions-and-shadow-pairs.md) for the broader framing.)

**The over-pursued shadow: drawing near as anxious chasing.** Someone who hears "draw near to God" and throws themselves into it — hours of prayer, frantic reading, a packed and exhausting schedule of spiritual practices — is sometimes genuinely shaped by it. But just as often, what looks like pursuit is anxious chasing, driven underneath by a fear that God won't really show up. The "whole-hearted" requirement in Jer. 29:13 cuts both ways: it names what real seeking takes, and it exposes the seeking that is really using all those practices to manage worry instead of to draw near. The test: a pursuit that doesn't settle down once God does draw near isn't really pursuit; it is worry borrowing the shape of pursuit to dodge the very meeting the pursuit was supposed to bring about.

**The under-pursued shadow: waiting as avoidance.** The opposite mistake is the person who hears "he will draw near to you" and stops right there. The flip side in 2 Chron. 15:2 ("if you forsake him, he will forsake you") is put just as bluntly as the bright side, but the person in this shadow doesn't feel like they are forsaking God — in their own mind, they are waiting. They are not running from God; they are just letting Him take the lead, which would be fine except that the law deliberately puts the action on our side. The waiting quietly refuses the part of the law that says we move too. The test: spiritual stuckness that gets described in waiting words ("when God wants me to draw closer, He will") is more often this under-pursued shadow than the patience it claims to be.

The law working rightly is the person who draws near with a whole heart and then, when God draws near in return, settles into the closeness instead of rushing on past it. The seeking is real and the receiving is real, and they turn out to be the same movement under two different names.

## Why This Is a Foundational Law
Five passages from at least four writers (James, the Chronicler, Jeremiah, Moses, Hebrews), across both Testaments. The back-and-forth itself is uncontested across mainstream Christian traditions. Reformed, Roman, Orthodox, Anabaptist, and Pentecostal traditions all affirm it, even where they differ on exactly how God's first move works. And it is easy to test: anyone who has lived through a long season of seeking and a long season of distance can vouch for both directions of the law.

**Proposed Law (Operational, Wide Consent): Moving toward God — seeking Him, drawing near, searching with your whole heart — draws God's movement toward you in return (you are found; He draws near); turning away draws His withdrawal. The two sides match in direction but not in who moves first: God moves first, and your seeking is already a response to His invitation. The law runs between you and God, and it is the clearest example in Scripture of give-and-take response.**

**Certainty: Clearly Taught  ***As certain as any law in this volume. The one open question is the smaller one of how “with all your heart” works at different stages of growth: someone earlier on the journey can't seek with the same wholeness a more grown heart can, which raises the question of whether the law works a little differently at different stages. **A word from your Granddaddy.** The hardest part of this law, for me, was never the turning away — it was the seasons when God seemed to hide and I could not point to one thing I had done to cause it. The first time it happened I was truly disoriented; I did not understand it and did not know how to process what was going on. What finally opened it up was John of the Cross and what he called the dark night of the soul — I came to him through Richard Foster — and the truth in it set me free: God was not punishing me. He was drawing back the surface things so that I could go deeper with Him. It has come around several times since, and it has never once felt good — but I learned to turn, right in the middle of the mess, and say, "Lord, this is miserable, and I thank You anyway." Later I found that very posture all through the lament psalms — David's “How long, O Lord?… How long will you hide your face from me?” (Ps. 13:1) — dead honest about the pain, and still turned toward God. Nothing about it is fun, and that turns out to be the whole point. Hanging in there — staying focused on Him and trusting Him when nothing in you wants to — is the pivot the law turns on. As John of the Cross warned, if you will not stay in it, you never grow into the deeper things God has for you. That is the payoff hidden inside the pain.
