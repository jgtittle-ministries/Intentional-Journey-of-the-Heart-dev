# Foundational Law XLVII: The Word's-Efficacy Law

This law is about the power that belongs to God's word itself. Most of the laws nearby are about us — how we hear, receive, abide, obey. This one is about the word: once it goes out from God and is received in faith, it does what He sent it to do. It is one of the newer laws, still being weighed by the wider community, but its scriptural footing is unusually deep.

## The Scriptural Ground
***Isa. 55:10–11 (ESV)***

*"For as the rain and the snow come down from heaven and do not return there but water the earth... so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose, and shall succeed in the thing for which I sent it."*

***Jer. 23:29 (ESV)***

*"Is not my word like fire, declares the Lord, and like a hammer that breaks the rock in pieces?"*

***Heb. 4:12 (ESV)***

*"For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart."*

***1 Thess. 2:13 (ESV)***

*"...you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers."*

## The Mechanism
Two things stand out, and a third has to be said carefully.

First, the word carries its own working power. God says it "shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose" (Isa. 55:11); it is "like fire... like a hammer" (Jer. 23:29), "living and active" (Heb. 4:12), and "at work in you believers" (1 Thess. 2:13). It is not inert information sitting still until we make something of it. When it lands, it works.

Second, this is not magic, and faith is not optional. The power is real, but it is received, not triggered. Hebrews makes the limit plain a few verses earlier: the same good news came to an earlier generation, but the message "did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened" (Heb. 4:2). And Jesus said the word can be "made void" by the traditions we put in its place (Mark 7:13). The word's power is sure; whether it does its work in me turns on whether I receive it as God's word and not merely men's (1 Thess. 2:13).

Third — and this is the deep thing — Scripture uses "word" in two senses, and they belong together. There is the word God speaks and writes, the message and the Scripture; and there is the personal Word, the Logos, who "was with God, and... was God" (John 1:1) — Jesus Himself. The written and spoken word is the word about the Word, and the means by which He comes to us. So the power at work is not an impersonal force: it is the Holy Spirit wielding the word — "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (Eph. 6:17) — and the Word it finally delivers is Christ. Which is why, in the end, to receive the word is to receive Him (this is the deep root beneath the Word → Hearing → Faith chain of the First Exploration).

## Why This Is a Foundational Law
The scriptural backing is exceptional and crosses both Testaments — Isaiah, Jeremiah, the writer of Hebrews, and Paul all state it plainly, with the Gospels and the parable of the Sower standing behind them. No mainstream Christian tradition disputes that God's word is living and powerful and accomplishes His purpose; the traditions differ on the modes and means, not on the thing itself. It runs in a clear direction: word sent and received in faith → it accomplishes what God purposed; received in unbelief or buried under tradition → it does not benefit. And it is a principle, not a single situation: it holds for the word read alone, the word preached, the word remembered in the dark, the word that converts and the word that sanctifies.

Its place in the catalog is among the scale-invariant substrate laws of the Mind & Will dimension: it conditions every other reception law, because it names the power in the thing being received.

**Proposed Law (Operational, newer / still-being-tested): God's word, sent and received in faith, accomplishes the purpose for which He sent it; it does not return empty. Its power is the Spirit's, who wields the word as His instrument, and the Word it delivers is finally Christ Himself — so to receive the word is to receive Him. Received in unbelief, or made void by our own traditions, the same word does not do its work in us.**

***Mirror:*** *The two failure-faces are the word made void — smothered under tradition or system until it can no longer cut (Mark 7:13) — and the word as incantation — its power presumed upon as a formula apart from faith, which Hebrews answers with the message that "did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith" (Heb. 4:2). The bright face is the word received "as what it really is, the word of God," which is then "at work in you believers" (1 Thess. 2:13).*

***Certainty: Clearly Taught.*** *Four writers across both Testaments state it directly (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hebrews, Paul), and the doctrine of the living and active word is held across every mainstream tradition. It enters among the newer laws (still being weighed by the wider community) because its formal place in the catalog — as the word-side counterpart to the human-side reception laws — is recent, not because the truth is in doubt. The operator is the Spirit wielding the word, delivering the personal Word, Christ; the catalog places it by the doctrine of the Logos rather than by any new category.*
