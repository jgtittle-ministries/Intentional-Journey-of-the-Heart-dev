# The Properties of Spiritual Law as Used in This Work

The rest of this volume describes the laws one at a time. This short chapter steps back and asks a different question: once you have a whole table of them, what do they have in common — not in subject matter, but in the way they behave? Physical laws have shared properties of this kind. They are consistent, they can be tested, they run in describable directions. The laws in this volume turn out to have shared properties too — and the most useful thing about naming them is this: the ways these laws resemble physical laws are what let us honestly call them “laws” at all, and the ways they differ all point, every time, back to the same place — to the fact that behind a spiritual law is not a mechanism but a Person.

So begin there, because everything else hangs on it.

## First, the thing that is not like physics at all

A physical law is an impersonal regularity. Gravity does not love you; it does not move first; it does not care which way you fall. A spiritual law is not like that. Every one of them is a description of how a faithful God reliably acts — the grain of His covenant faithfulness, not the gears of a device. That is why none of these laws is a lever you pull to make God do something. They are the settled, trustworthy ways a Person relates to the people He made. Read every property below in that light, or you will turn the whole thing into a machine — which is exactly what it is not.

## The ways they are like physical laws (why we dare call them laws)

- They are consistent. The same input tends to the same outcome, across very different lives and centuries apart. That steadiness is real; it is what lets Scripture state them as “whoever does X will find Y.”
- They are testable — but over a lifetime, not in an afternoon. You cannot run a spiritual law in a controlled lab. You test it the way you test a claim about character: across decades, across many lives, watching for the pattern. The evidence is the convergent witness of Scripture’s many writers, the agreement of the great traditions, and the testimony of lives observed over time. The personal testimonies I have set at the close of many of these chapters are a small sample of that last kind of evidence — one life, watching the same laws hold over decades.
- They run in describable directions. Many run both ways — sow good or sow ill, draw near or turn away, obey God or obey sin — and the dark direction is usually the same machinery pointed in a different direction.
- They work at every size. A whole family of them (the scale-invariant ones) holds the same for one person, a community, and a generation — the way some physical laws hold from the small to the very large.
- They have thresholds. Some are gateways (something must be in place first); some build to a tipping point past which the ordinary means no longer work (“suddenly broken beyond healing,” Prov. 29:1). And what builds toward those tipping points is accumulation: small, repeated inputs compound over time, so it is the steady direction your attention points, not the occasional high, that finally tells. Spiritual life is not perfectly smooth and linear any more than the physical world is.
- They are inescapable. A physical law is never really broken, only demonstrated — the man who steps off the roof does not repeal gravity — and the spiritual laws share the property. Walking away from them is not an exit; it is a change of jurisdiction. G. K. Chesterton compressed it into three clauses: “When you break the big laws, you do not get freedom; you do not even get anarchy. You get the small laws” (*Daily News*, July 29, 1905). And the Lord had made the same diagnosis first: the men who neglected “the weightier matters of the law” did not end up free of law — they ended up tithing mint and dill and cumin (Matt. 23:23), scrupulous about the tiny precisely because they had abandoned the great. Paul names the mechanism underneath both sayings: no one exits service — “you are slaves of the one whom you obey” (Rom. 6:16). So the question a life answers is never *whether* it will live under law, only *which* — the great laws of a loving Father, or the small tyrannies that move in when those are refused.

Those six are enough to earn the word law. This is not loose metaphor; it is a real, describable regularity. (That is the honest answer to anyone who asks whether these are “really” laws.)

## How a passage becomes a law (and how it doesn’t)

There is a prior question hiding under all of this, and it deserves a straight answer: by what right do I move from a verse to a *law* — from “Scripture says this happened, or commands this here” to “this is a settled regularity that holds across times and lives”? That move can be done badly. Pull a single detail out of one story, or one stray sentence, and announce it as a universal law, and you have not found a law of the Spirit; you have proof-texted. So the work runs by a rule, and it is worth stating plainly. A pattern earns the word *law* only when several things line up:

- **Convergence across many witnesses.** The pattern is taught or enacted across multiple biblical authors and, for the highest tier, across both Testaments — not resting on a single verse or a single episode. One instance is an example; a pattern many Spirit-carried writers return to, independently, is something closer to a law.
- **It is asserted, not merely narrated.** Scripture *recording* that something happened once is not the same as Scripture *teaching* that it characteristically happens. The laws are drawn from the teaching, promise, and command forms — “whoever does X will find Y” — not from lifting a descriptive detail out of a narrative and making it normative.
- **Consistency with the whole counsel of Scripture and the character of God.** A candidate pattern has to sit comfortably inside the rest of what God has revealed about Himself. A “pattern” that would require Him to act against His revealed character is disqualified, however many proof-texts seem to line up behind it.
- **Confirmed in the agreement of the great traditions and in lives observed over time** — the same convergent, lifetime-scale testing the *testable* property above describes.

This is exactly why the catalog does not treat every claim as equally settled. The patterns drawn from the widest, most convergent witness are held at the Clearly Taught tier and make up the thirty-eight of wide consent; the ones resting on thinner or more recent ground are marked as newer and still being tested, or carried lower at Reasonably Inferred or Speculative. The confidence tier on each law *is* the honest record of how fully the move from passage to law has been earned in that case. (For the related but different distinction between laws that describe what the spiritual world *is* and laws that describe how it *moves*, see the Introduction’s [A Note on Two Kinds of Laws](../introduction/note-on-two-kinds-of-laws.md).)

## The ways they are not like physical laws (and where each one points)

Here is the striking part. Every place a spiritual law breaks from a physical one, the break points back to the personal God behind it.

|  | Physical law | Spiritual law | …which points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | immediate or precisely clocked | long and variable delay — seasons, decades | the patient timing of a Gardener, not a stopwatch |
| **Reversibility** | symmetric | easy down, hard up — you easily drift down, you climb up only with help. Think of the phrase, highway to hell and stairway to heaven as a comment on the difference in difficulty. | a Redeemer inside the system, supplying the energy to point to the Father. |
| **Initiative** | not applicable | God moves first; your part is response | a relationship, not a transaction you start |
| **The observer** | independent of the observer (except in QFT) | your posture is part of it — faith, motive, a whole heart | a Person on the other side, who both cares and is involved. |
| **Moral weight** | morally neutral | one direction is life, the other death | a law that runs toward or away from Someone |
| **Visibility** | directly measured | hidden; read by its fruit, in hindsight, or by another’s eyes | a work done in you, deeper than you can watch |
| **What it acts on** | the single event | the steady direction of a whole life | a God who reads the heart’s long arc, not a tally of acts |

One caution on that table before going on: when it says your posture is “part of it,” posture is a *condition of the relationship* — how a person stands before God — never a lever you operate or a dial you turn to get a result.

Two of these deserve a closer look, because they are the ones people feel.

Timing: the delay is where faith lives. Drop a stone and it falls now. Sow generosity, or begin to renew your mind, and the harvest can be seasons or years away. The delay is not the law failing; it is the law’s normal shape. And it is precisely because the feedback is delayed that these laws ask for faith: you act before you can see the result. A stopwatch faith would not be faith at all. (There is a deeper layer beneath this one: Scripture says God’s own reckoning of time is not ours — “one day is as a thousand years” (2 Pet. 3:8). How that larger timescale relates to the ordinary delays we feel is a real question, one I follow as an open research trail rather than try to settle here.)

Asymmetry: easy down, hard up. A heart hardens, or a habit enslaves, by simple drift — no effort required, the way a room gets dusty on its own. Coming back up is never that easy; it takes help from outside — a word, a community, the Spirit’s particular work, sometimes a hard jolt. This looks at first like the way the physical world runs down toward disorder and stays there. But here is the difference that changes everything: in the physical world, the running-down always wins in the end. In the spiritual world it does not, because there is a Redeemer inside the system, and He supplies the very energy the climb requires. That is why every one of these laws, when it describes the way back up, describes it as rescue — never as a self-made escape. The asymmetry is real, and grace is the answer to it.

## And they form a system

One more property belongs to none of the laws by itself, because it shows up only when you have the whole table: they interlock. A gateway law opens the door for a whole cluster; Mirror-paired laws describe one dynamic from its bright and its dark side; the output of one law becomes the input of the next — you reap what you sowed, and the reaping becomes the next thing you sow. So these are not a list of separate rules but a connected system, where moving one part moves others. Vol 3 takes this up directly, modeling the laws as interacting, mutually dependent parts — much the way a [systems-dynamics model](../volume-3-quantitative-framework/exploration-07-systems-dynamics-as-modeling-language.md) treats a set of linked sub-models, each one feeding the others.

## Why naming all this is worth the trouble

It does two jobs at once.

To anyone who doubts these are really laws — that they are just pious wishes dressed up — the family resemblances are the answer: they are consistent, testable over time, directional, scale-invariant, threshold-shaped, and inescapable. That is what a law is as I am using it. (Vol 3 makes this same case at more length — on [why these count as “laws”](../volume-3-quantitative-framework/exploration-01-case-for-quantification.md), on [why the measuring question is fair](../volume-3-quantitative-framework/note-before-we-begin.md), and on [standing where Newton stood](../volume-3-quantitative-framework/epilogue-the-invitation-forward.md).)

To anyone who fears the opposite — that calling them “laws” turns God into a vending machine and faith into technique — the differences are the answer: they run on a Person’s timing, they wait on a Person’s first move, they involve the heart’s posture, they are reversed only by grace, and they are weighted toward life or death because they run toward or away from Someone. No machine behaves like that.

So the same set of properties that makes them genuinely law-like is what keeps them from ever being mechanical. They are laws — the lawful, faithful, utterly reliable ways a loving God deals with His children. That is the best of both: as dependable as gravity, and personal all the way down.

**A word from your Granddaddy.** I did not learn any of this from a chart. I learned it slowly, by living long enough to watch the harvests come in — usually much later than I wanted, and never on a stopwatch. I learned the asymmetry the hard way too: how easily I drifted down, and how the way back up always cost more than I had and always came as help from outside, never as something I managed on my own. So when I lay these out like the properties of a law, do not hear a man describing a machine he figured out. Hear a man telling you about Someone he has learned, slowly, to trust over his lifetime — who has been, every single time, as dependable as gravity and as personal as a father.
