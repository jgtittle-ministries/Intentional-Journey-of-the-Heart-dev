# The Scripture-Grounding Standard

*Where the [Proposal Template](proposal-template.md) records how a claim earns its place in the registry, this standard records the bar a claim's **use of Scripture** must clear. It exists because the project makes strong, structured claims about how the spiritual life works — and a strong claim resting on a misused text is worse than no claim at all, because it can wound the very reader it was meant to help. The 2026 cross-volume grounding audit found the catalog already meets this bar in most places; this document names the bar so that every future addition meets it too.*

## Why this standard exists

A recurring fault in formation literature is the **proof-text**: a verse lifted from its setting and pressed to do mechanical work it was never written for. The danger is double. First, it is dishonest to the text — it makes Scripture say what it does not say. Second, it is dangerous to the reader — a verse turned into a mechanism becomes a club. "The Lord would not have listened" (Ps. 66:18) gets read as proof of hidden sin; "ask, and it will be given" (Matt. 7:7) gets read as a vending machine. This project's whole aim — to serve the dry, the wounded, and the honest seeker — fails if its handling of Scripture cuts the people it means to bind up.

The standard below is not new law imposed from outside. It is the discipline the best chapters of this corpus already practice, named here so it can be applied uniformly.

## The five tests

A claim's use of a Scripture passage meets the standard when it passes all five.

**1. Multi-witness.** A load-bearing claim rests on more than one passage — ideally several writers, across both Testaments. A single verse rarely carries a Foundational Law by itself. *(In the catalog: FL.II, FL.VII, FL.X, and FL.XIX each stand on four to six passages from multiple writers.)*

**2. Genre-aware and context-honoring.** Each text is read according to its kind — **narrative is not prescription, parable is not mechanism, wisdom and poetry are not legal code, a polemic is not a general doctrine** — and the claim is either what the text plainly teaches or a *disciplined extension* of it, never a reading its context forbids. Where a passage is commonly misread, the chapter says so and corrects it. *(In the catalog: FL.XIX corrects, in its own text, the reading of 1 Sam. 19 as a "contagion mechanism," and fixes "double portion" to the firstborn's-inheritance sense of Deut. 21:17.)*

**3. Analogy-versus-text honesty.** When a model, mechanism, or analogy is laid *over* a text — as Volume 3 routinely does with its field-theoretic and resonance language — the chapter says so **in plain words**, distinguishing what the text asserts from what the author is illustrating. An illustration must never be allowed to masquerade as exegesis. *(In the catalog, verbatim: Vol 3 Exp. 3 — "The resonance language here is an analogy I am laying over the verse, not a claim the verse makes"; Exp. 6 — "an analogy I am drawing from the parable's outcome, not the lesson Jesus is teaching.")*

**4. Calibrated certainty.** The confidence label matches what Scripture actually carries. **Clearly Taught** is reserved for what the text plainly teaches; **Reasonably Inferred** and **Speculative** carry disciplined extensions and working hypotheses. When a chapter mixes a taught core with an inferred mechanism, **the label is split by claim** rather than averaged or rounded up. *(In the catalog: Vol 3 holds its mechanism chapters at Speculative and says so in its own voice; the Vol 2 Heart-Soil chapter splits "the soils are heart-conditions" — Clearly Taught — from the taxonomy mapping — Reasonably Inferred.)*

**5. Guarded at the edge.** A claim with a sharp pastoral edge carries its guard **at the point of use**, not in a distant chapter, so a true text cannot be turned against a faithful sufferer. The two standing guards are the **sovereignty word** (God gives as He wills and in His timing; a poor outcome is not by itself a verdict on the person) and the **dry-season word** (felt silence or unanswered prayer is not by itself evidence of sin or failure — it may be the dark night the Father sends to deepen a seeker). *(In the catalog: FL.VII distinguishes judicial withdrawal from the dark night; the Vol 3 Force chapter leads with **G** — God's free self-giving — and carries "A Word for the Dry Season" inside the chapter.)*

## How the standard is applied

- **At proposal time.** The [Proposal Template](proposal-template.md)'s four-factor confidence test (sections 3a–3d) already enforces grounding at the platform level. This standard is its Scripture-specific companion: a proposal whose primary texts fail any of the five tests is *returned for strengthening, not rejected* — the same charitable posture the Council brings to every proposal.
- **At review and audit time.** The five tests are the audit rubric. A periodic grounding pass over any volume asks them of every load-bearing citation, flags failures by severity, and proposes the lightest fix that restores the text's own meaning.

## The checklist (operational form)

For each load-bearing citation in a claim, confirm:

1. ☐ Does the claim stand on **more than one** passage (multiple writers / both Testaments where it bears weight)?
2. ☐ Is each text read **in its genre and context** — and is any common misreading named and corrected?
3. ☐ Where a model or analogy is laid over a text, is that **stated plainly**, with text and illustration kept distinct?
4. ☐ Does the **certainty label** match what the text carries — split by claim where a taught core meets an inferred mechanism?
5. ☐ Does a claim with a sharp edge carry its **sovereignty / dry-season guard at the point of use**?

A citation that clears all five is grounded. One that does not is returned for strengthening.
