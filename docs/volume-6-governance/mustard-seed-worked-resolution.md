# The Mustard Seed Question — A Worked Resolution

*This chapter records how one long-standing open question was actually resolved — the backstory and the reasoning, not just the verdict. It is offered to the Council for two reasons: so the resolution can be weighed on its merits, and as the first **worked example** of the method the project intends to use whenever an open question is closed. The full evidentiary trail lives in the repository at `_implementation-notes/mustard-seed-resolution/`; the standing status is tracked in the [Research Register](research-register.md).*

---

## 1. The question, and why it carried weight

When Jesus says that faith "like a grain of mustard seed" can move a mountain (Matt 17:20), He could mean one of two very different things:

- **Threshold (Model A):** the smallest *genuine* faith is enough. The constraint is **quality** — real trust versus mere assent — not amount.
- **Proportionality (Model B):** the mustard seed is a floor, not a ceiling; **more faith yields more** spiritual force.

This was never a merely curious question. The project's central quantitative claim — the Spiritual Force equation (`V3.Exp3`), since revised to lead with God's free self-giving as `F_s = G × [ f(trust) × g(authority) × h(resonance) × i(channel) ]` — cannot take a definite shape until it is settled, because the question bears on the trust factor `f()` *inside* the bracket, and the two readings demand different mathematics there: Model B makes `f()` a continuous, rising curve; Model A makes it a near-vertical step. The question had been flagged since Volume 1 as the cap on the Prayer-as-Resonance law (`V1.Exp8`) and named in the [Vol 3 Open Trails](../volume-3-quantitative-framework/open-trails-what-vol-3-cannot-yet.md) as the one quantitative-side trail a single person could actually work. So it was both ripe and consequential.

## 2. How the question was worked

The method mattered as much as the answer, and it is the part meant to be reusable:

1. **Pre-registration.** Before reading the evidence, the three candidate models (threshold, proportionality, and a synthesis) were written down together with a single decisive **decision rule**: *is there any narrative where, all else equal, a person with more faith receives a larger outcome than a person with less?* If yes, proportionality lives; if no, it falls.
2. **A bounded corpus.** Every miracle and answered-prayer narrative across the Old Testament, the Gospels, Acts, and the Epistles was catalogued — some fifty-nine entries — and coded for *whose* faith was named, what the text says about it (quality or quantity), the outcome, and whether persistence or authority were in view.
3. **An adversarial pass.** The preliminary reading was then deliberately attacked: the strongest possible case *for* proportionality was built and tested against the corpus.
4. **An independent adversarial pass.** Because the same hand that reaches a conclusion is a poor judge of it, a second, independent reviewer — given only the corpus and the models, and **instructed to overturn the result** — repeated the attack from scratch.
5. **A cross-tradition check.** The resulting reading was tested against how the Reformed, Evangelical, Charismatic, and Contemplative streams each take the mustard seed and the gift of faith.

## 3. What the evidence showed

The corpus answered the decision rule cleanly, and the same pattern appeared from every angle:

- **Failures are failures of *quality*, never of *quantity*.** Where a work failed, the text names unbelief, doubt, or counterfeit (Nazareth's unbelief, Mark 6; the disciples' "little faith," Matt 17; the double-minded doubter, James 1) — never "had some faith, but not enough."
- **"Great" and "little" faith are qualitative.** "Little faith" is everywhere a rebuke for *fear*; "great faith" (the centurion, the Canaanite woman) is commended for *precision and tenacity*. Decisively, **"great faith" never buys a bigger miracle**: the centurion's great faith and the trembling, wordless touch of the woman with the hemorrhage both receive a complete healing.
- **No outcome is ever scaled to the amount of faith** — not one case in the whole corpus.
- **A hard outer bound.** Genuine, persistent, aligned faith does not compel a result: Hebrews 11:39 (faith commended, the promise withheld) and Paul's thrice-prayed thorn (2 Cor 12) keep the whole matter under God's sovereign freedom.

The independent reviewer corroborated this — and, valuably, **caught a flaw**: the first corpus had omitted Scripture's strongest *vocabulary* of faith-as-quantity ("increase our faith," Luke 17:5; "the measure of faith," Rom 12:3; "as your faith grows," 2 Cor 10:15; the thirty/sixty/hundredfold). Those texts were then engaged head-on rather than left out — and none of them scales a *miracle* to faith-amount. Two even cut the other way: Luke 17:5's request for *more* faith is answered with the *mustard-seed* (threshold) saying, and the thirty/sixty/hundredfold varies by the *quality of the soil*, not the quantity of faith. The honest summary is the reviewer's: **proportionality has the words; the synthesis has the events — and outcomes are decided by events.**

## 4. The resolution

The evidence retired pure proportionality and established the synthesis (**Model C**):

> Genuine faith operates as a **quality threshold**, not a quantity. A sharp floor of real trust is the entry condition; below it nothing operates, and the smallest genuine faith above it suffices. **Above the floor, the magnitude and timing of the outcome are carried by alignment, persistence, authority, and obediently-prepared capacity — not by the amount of faith** — all bounded by divine sovereignty.

For the Force equation this fixes the trust factor `f()` as a near-step threshold and relocates every genuine "magnitude" effect into the resonance and authority factors `g()`/`h()`. One thread was deliberately *not* closed and is preserved as its own open question (`V3.Exp3.Open2`): whether the sovereignly-given "gift of faith" (1 Cor 12:9) is a kind of proportionality at the level of *capacity* — a question about whom God enables for which works, distinct from the per-act threshold.

## 5. The witness behind it

The resonance reading is not only exegeted but lived; the proposer offers it as personal witness (the proposal's experiential section). He connects the rhythm to *pray without ceasing* (1 Thess 5:17): he wakes each morning with a song, journals it, and lets that **rhythm — not the words** — be his prayer through the day, returning to it and trusting it continues even in the night. It is now shared: he texts the morning's song to a couple who take up the same rhythm — "two or three gathered in the Name of Jesus" (Matt 18:20), the same resonance become corporate. This is the threshold-and-resonance structure as a life: above the floor of genuine trust, what carries the prayer is alignment and persistence, not a quantity of faith summoned in a moment.

Alongside the lived witness, the resolution is **corroborated by the Gospel faith-cases themselves** — recorded here as additional confirmation, not as any change to the resolution. Tested against the same pre-registered rule (*is there any narrative where, all else equal, more faith yields a larger outcome?*), the cases answer in one voice:

- **Faith is never ignored — the floor is cleared.** The centurion ("such great faith," Matt 8:5-13), the Syrophoenician woman ("great is your faith," Mark 7:24-30), and the woman healed on a touch He had not initiated ("your faith has made you well," Mark 5:25-34) each show real trust opening access — none a *larger* miracle for *more* faith.
- **Bounded by His timing.** At Cana (John 2:1-11) faith asks, Jesus answers "My hour has not yet come," then acts on His own timing — the outcome's timing is His, not faith's to force.
- **God acts apart from prior faith.** Lazarus (dead, unable to believe), Saul on the Damascus road (an enemy), and Cornelius (the Spirit falls before any work, Acts 10:44) show faith as the reception God never ignores, not the cause that compels Him.
- **The threshold holds corporately.** At Kadesh-Barnea (Num 13-14) a generation's unbelief fell below the floor and shut a door that stood open; the real faith of Joshua, Caleb, and Moses did not outweigh it.

Every case is consistent with the threshold-bounded-by-sovereignty synthesis, and none satisfies the proportionality rule. The same material is woven into the body of the work at `V1.Exp1` ("How to Get Faith") and the `FL.XLIII` guard — *faith receives, it never compels* — with its pastoral corollary that a dry or unanswered season is no proof of too-little faith.

## 6. How it was governed

The resolution was not simply declared. It entered the project's process: the open question (`V1.Exp8` / `V3.Exp3.Open1`) was put into a 14-day comment period; the Prayer-as-Resonance law's confidence was raised on the strength of the resolution; and the frontier-tier Force equation (`V3.Exp3`) was entered into the **research-program track** under a Council sponsor, so its confidence moves only when testing accumulates, never by argument alone. The threshold-event account of miracles (`V3.Exp8`) was flagged as corroborated. The complete trail — pre-registration, corpus, both adversarial passes, the cross-tradition survey, and the proposal — is preserved so the next steward can audit every step.

## 7. Why this is a template

What makes the conclusion trustworthy is not its eloquence but its discipline: a decision rule fixed *before* the evidence, a bounded corpus rather than a handful of proof-texts, a deliberate attempt to refute the favoured reading, an *independent* attempt to refute it, an honest correction when that attempt exposed a gap, and a check across the traditions. The project commends this shape for every future resolution of an open question. A claim that survives an honest attempt to break it has earned its place; a claim that has never been attacked has not.
