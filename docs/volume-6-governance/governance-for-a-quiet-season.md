# Governance in This Season

What follows is how this work is governed at this stage of the project: one author, a small Council that meets monthly, and a public record. It is deliberately light, because that is the honest shape of the work right now. A fuller governance model, written for a larger season with many contributors, is kept in the [reference section](part-1-governance-model.md) at the end of this volume for when it is needed.

---

## Who decides

During my active tenure I am the steward and editor of the work. I decide what changes, and I say so plainly.

The Council of Stewards is my counsel and covering, not a legislature. Its first job, in [Part 2](part-2-council-fellowship.md)'s words, is its own formation; its second is stewardship of the work. That stewardship takes one concrete form: once a month, at its regular meeting, the Council hears what I have changed and what I intend to change, and it blesses, questions, or asks me to wait.

For the Council's word to mean anything, *wait* has to be real. When the Council asks me to wait, I wait, and the reason is written down. This is the Rule of Life's own line: we do not use votes to end disagreements that have not yet been prayed through. The same applies to blessings; a change the Council has not prayed through is not yet blessed.

---

## Three kinds of change

Every claim in these volumes carries a confidence tier: anchor claims, working claims, and frontier claims. Each tier is handled one way, and the three ways are three verbs.

**Tell.** Working claims, prose refinement, cross-references, new research trails, corrections of fact or citation, readability, site mechanics. I make the change, mirror it to the published site when it is ready, and report it at the next meeting. Most of a year's work lives here.

**Ask.** Anything that changes what the work claims at its foundations: the four axioms and the other anchor claims; adding, removing, or re-tiering a Foundational Law; revising a preserved minority position or closing an open question; anything touching safeguarding; and any change to this governance itself. I bring these to the Council before they are published. The development repository may carry the draft; the published site waits for the Council's blessing. If the Council is divided, the change waits and the division is recorded.

**Wait.** Frontier claims, the force equation, the conservation laws, and the rest held below 65%, do not move on argument; they move on evidence. When evidence exists, the change becomes an Ask. The [Research Register](research-register.md) is where that work is tracked.

When a change is hard to classify, it is an Ask.

## The record

The repository is the record. Every change is a commit with a written reason. The development repository holds work in progress; the published repository holds what has been released; nothing reaches the published site except by a deliberate mirror on my word. That is the whole change-control system.

Two small things sit on top of it. **The [Council Log](council-log.md)**, one entry per meeting: the date, who was present, what was told, what was asked and how it was answered, what was held, and any dissent by name; newest first; public. And **the monthly changes note**, prepared before each meeting in three lists, told, asking, and waiting. It is the agenda for the governance part of the meeting, and afterward it becomes the Log entry.

## The standards every change meets

- **The four-factor test and disconfirmability.** Any substantive change must be able to answer, in the Council's hearing: which scriptures, whose experience, does it cohere, what does tradition say, and what would show it wrong.
- **Preserved dissent.** When a claim is revised, the prior position is kept with its argument, not deleted. When the Council declines a change someone cared about, the argument is kept in the Log. This is what makes the work a living tradition rather than a series of overwrites.
- **The [Scripture-Grounding Standard](scripture-grounding-standard.md)**, which every chapter's handling of Scripture is held to.
- **The [Research Register](research-register.md)**, where open investigations are tracked and sorted by what can be worked now.
- **The claim registry.** Four files index the claims, their tiers, and their dependencies, and a tier audit keeps the chapters, the Master Law Index, the Periodic Table, and the registry in agreement. The registry is a map; the prose is canonical.
- **The license.** CC BY 4.0 for the corpus, and the Developer Certificate of Origin for contributions.

## Contributions from outside

Anyone may open an issue on the repository or write to me. A serious proposal is welcome and will be brought to the Council as an Ask at the next meeting, with the proposer told when that is. The [Proposal Template](proposal-template.md) is available as a courtesy for anyone who wants to be thorough; it is not a gate. The repository's one-page [CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/jgtittle-ministries/Intentional-Journey-of-the-Heart/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) file says the same.

## Spiritual authority

Part 2 says the Council is under a spiritual authority that must be identified and clarified before its work is fully begun. No procedure here answers that, and none could; it is the governance question that matters more than any of the above. The Council is pursuing it step by step, and the Council Log records each step.

## The fuller model

The reference section at the end of this volume holds a fuller governance model, [Part 1](part-1-governance-model.md), written for a larger season: a five-seat Council with terms and elections, contributor status, comment periods and supermajorities, a calibration cycle, and the platform to run them. None of it is in force now, and none of it is discarded. If in any twelve months the project receives three or more substantive outside proposals, or a second regular editor joins the work, or the Council itself asks, the Council may bring any part of it into force.
