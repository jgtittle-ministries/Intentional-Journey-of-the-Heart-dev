# Sixth Exploration: The Tool Map — Matching the Right Tool to the Right Blockage

## Why the Map Matters
I have a long list of tools and processes that I have found effective in various contexts. The list includes GRH, TPM, Inner Healing in the Payne/Bennett tradition, shadow work, voice dialogue, psychodrama, the Wild at Heart materials, Warrior Monk processes, and more. The list is not the problem. The problem is that without a map showing which tool addresses which blockage type, I end up applying tools at random — and a well-intentioned but wrong tool can make things worse. GRH and TPM have explicit mappings of tools, which are helpful. What follows is my mapping as I have used the tools. That caution has an outer limit: when what surfaces is beyond what these tools and a lay Companion can hold — acute crisis, abuse, trauma at depth — the right "tool" is a referral to a licensed clinician, not another process. See the Container chapter's note on the limits of "safe."

![A craftsman's tool cabinet — the right tool, used on the right material, at the right time.](images/07-tool-bench.jpg)

The diagnostic framework from Part I gives us the map. Here is how I currently understand the tool-to-blockage correspondence:

**Surfacing which blockage to work — the Kingdom-gap entry (exploratory).** Before the Map can route a blockage to its tool, the blockage has to surface. One entry I have used is the *Kingdom-gap*: in a quiet dyad, ask three times slowly, *"What would your life look like, right now, if the Kingdom of God were fully in it?"*, then *"What does it look like now?"*, and name the gap between the two. The person then chooses which part of the gap *has energy* for them — an *"If I were to work tonight, I would work on…"* round — and that choice routes into the Map below (a sin to confess, a wound to heal, a doubt to bring to *Any Doubts?*, and so on). The Kingdom-gap is a work-selection lens, not a tool in itself, and it is held as exploratory, founder-developed practice.

## For Grief Knots: Naming and Completing
The primary tool is the Grief Recovery Handbook process. Its key contribution is the Loss Graph — a systematic identification of the losses in my life with ongoing emotional impact. Most people underestimate how many forms of loss they are carrying, because we have a culturally narrow definition of grief (death of a loved one) that excludes losses from divorce, betrayal, career failure, loss of health, loss of a dream, and dozens of other forms. The GRH process names them all and creates specific "completion letters", the unfinished communications that need to be said.

The spiritual completion of GRH work happens through TPM: I bring the named grief to the Lord in the specific memory context and invite Him to speak. His truth about the loss, about the person lost, about what comes next — this is what releases the grief knot. The GRH can identify what needs to be brought; TPM can be the point of access through which the Spirit completes the work.

## For Shame Knots: Recovering the Gold
The primary tool is the Gold and Shadow work from the Band of Brothers material. The shadow is the disowned, ashamed part of the self — the part I have hidden because I believe it is too broken, too weak, too dark to be acceptable. The gold is the God-given strength that got buried with that part of myself. The Shadow Mission work asks: if my God-given strength were taken and twisted in the worst possible direction, what would that look like? The answer reveals both the deepest threat and the deepest gift.

The inner healing complement: once the shadow has been named, I bring it to the Lord in prayer. The specific lie at the root of the shame is identified. The Spirit speaks truth. The gold is recovered. The ashamed part is integrated rather than kept hidden, and in the integration, it loses its power to drain me.

## For Fear Knots: The Somatic Entry
Fear knots require a different entry point. Because the fear response is physically expressed, the somatic entry — breath work, progressive relaxation, body awareness — should come first to bring the nervous system out of the threat-response state before cognitive or spiritual approaches can reach the root. The Garden of Your Heart exercise (described in Exploration 7) serves this function: the guided relaxation creates a physiological opening before the Spirit-encounter is invited. Many other exercises, classic breathing exercises, for example, can work here.

Once the somatic entry has created access, the TPM sequence applies: the fear emotion leads to the originating memory; the memory reveals the lie ("I am not safe," "God cannot be trusted," "I am alone"); the Spirit speaks the truth. The specific truth about God’s presence and power in the specific feared situation is what releases the fear knot. Not generic reassurance — specific truth for this specific fear.

## For Anger Knots: Naming Before Releasing
Anger knots require the injustice to be named honestly before forgiveness can be genuine. Attempting to forgive before naming the full weight of the wrong produces suppression, not release. The sequence: I name what was done, I acknowledge what it cost me, I acknowledge my anger at it, and then — and only then — I bring it to the Lord and release my claim to be the one who settles the score.

The psychodrama and voice dialogue tools are useful here because they provide a safe structure for expressing and witnessing anger before it is released. A man who has never been able to say out loud, in a safe container, "what you did to me was wrong, and it cost me this and this and this" — that man cannot fully release the anger because it has never been fully named. The speaking is part of the completion.

## For Sin Knots: Confession and Community
As established in Exploration 4, the tool for sin blockage is confession — specific, owned, without self-justification. The community complement is James 5:16 — confessing to one another in a safe container. The container protocol from Exploration 8 is the preparation for this kind of confession to be possible.

**Proposed Law (Tool Application): Different hurts need different help. A grief needs to be named and finished; a shame needs to be met with the truth about who I am; a fear often needs the body quieted before the heart can hear; an anger needs to be said out loud before it can be released; a sin needs to be confessed — and underneath any of these there may be a false god still on the throne, which has to be named and dethroned before the rest will stay healed. Matching the right help to the right hurt is itself a work of discernment, not a lookup; and in every case it is the Holy Spirit who heals — the tools only open the door He walks through (John 15:5; Zech. 4:6; 2 Cor. 3:5–6).**

Two guards travel with that law. The Map is a *servant* of discernment, never a substitute for it: a correct diagnosis does not by itself *guarantee* a release, because the Spirit gives as He wills (see the [standing word in Exploration 2](exploration-02-emotional-knots.md)). And when what surfaces is past what these tools and a lay Companion can hold, the right "tool" is the *stop-and-refer* set out in [the Container chapter](exploration-08-container.md), not another process.

Underneath the plain statement, the practice has long been reading three things at once — three *dimensions* a Companion holds together in any real session:

- **What is blocked** (the blockage-type): grief → naming and completing (GRH → TPM); shame → shadow recovery and identity truth (Gold/Shadow → inner healing); fear → somatic entry, then specific truth (relaxation → TPM); anger → full naming before release (psychodrama/voice dialogue → forgiveness prayer); sin → confession ([V2.Exp4](exploration-04-confession-and-restoration.md)); idol → naming the functional savior (Keller's question: *what do I look to for ultimate security, meaning, or identity?*), explicit renunciation, and Spirit-led re-enthronement of Christ (Col. 3:1–4). The tell of an *idol* knot is that the other knots keep re-forming across cycles of clearing, because the occupant of the throne keeps producing them.
- **How the person is reached** (the access-mode): verbal/visual entry is the default the tools above assume; some people are reached body-first (kinesthetic), some through an imagined supportive figure (reparenting work, for tender grief and shame), and some only after a standing interior agreement that binds across several knot-types is renounced first.
- **What governs from upstream** (the governing-axis): a shaming voice that has substituted itself for the Lord's voice must be named before the person can hear His actual blessing across *any* knot-type; a standing interior agreement ([V2.Exp2C](exploration-02c-standing-interior-agreements.md)) produces knots across types and needs its own renunciation; and a heart-throne occupied by anything but Christ ([V1.FL.III](../volume-1-laws-of-the-spirit/foundational-law-iii-the-heart-throne-law.md)) keeps the downstream knots re-forming until He is re-enthroned.

The three are read *together* — any session asks at once *what* is blocked, *how* this person is reached, and *what* governs from upstream — which is why the right entry point can never be reduced to a chart. (For practitioners, the working protocol-code names behind these moves — A24 blessing-receiving with the God-split, A25 breaking the contracts, B11 ideal support figure, B14 kinesthetic metaphor — are catalogued in the Vol 5 practitioner notes; they are labels for the moves above, not steps a reader needs in hand.)

**Certainty: Reasonably Inferred** *The tool-to-type mapping is the author's current best working hypothesis, based on observation. The boundaries are not clean — most knots have multiple types **mixed together**, and the right entry point for a given person **in a given** moment requires discernment that cannot be fully systematized. This is where the community of practitioners does its most important testing work. The three-dimensional structure now articulated names what the practice has been carrying implicitly; the operational confidence is unchanged by making the dimensions explicit, though the discoverability of the Map's structure improves substantially.*

**FORMATION DOCUMENT CONNECTION: The discernment needed to match the right tool to the correct blockage type is itself a formation-level competency — not a skill that can be taught cognitively and applied mechanically. [HFT](../volume-5-references/source-pdfs/hft-heart-formation-theology.pdf){: .pdf-popup data-pdf-label="HFT — Heart Formation Theology" }’s Affective Level 3 is the threshold: a person operating at Level 1–2 (compliance, attendance, following a curriculum) will tend to use tools in the order the curriculum presents rather than in the order the Spirit is guiding. [MSFIG](../volume-5-references/source-pdfs/msfig-model-spiritual-formation.pdf){: .pdf-popup data-pdf-label="MSFIG — Model of Spiritual Formation for Individuals and Small Groups" } emphasizes this in its section on small group leadership: a leader cannot help a group grow into **Stage** 3+ formation if they themselves operate at Level 1/2. The same limitation applies — the discernment this law requires is a **Stage** 4 competency (Organization: integrating what one has learned about the Spirit’s work into a coherent framework for analyzing specific situations). This is not a critique of beginners; it’s an honest reflection of the developmental path the work demands.**
